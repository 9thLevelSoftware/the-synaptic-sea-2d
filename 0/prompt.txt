  ### The question
  
  The brief asks what should stay the same across the deliberate rhythm and the frantic one. The source docs already answer most of it.
  - **Pillar 2 controls the tempo.** The art bible says the breach feeling comes from "more things moving, not from the screen fighting the player" (2.8).
  - **Sanity may not change tempo** (2.9).
  - **Pillar 4's secondary test** rules out sanity as a movement debuff: "−10% move speed vs. a gauge showing a false value → the false gauge."
  
  So the real choice is which pillar the body's fantasy leads with. Each candidate below leads with a different one.
  
  ---
  
  ### Candidate A: "Looking is spending" (Pillar 3 leads)
  
  **Your eyes are in your hand.** You can't see anything you haven't lit, and everything you light can see you back. Moving and turning are mostly about pointing the beam. The beam is the art bible's hero shape #1, "the player's eye, hand and identity" (3.5). The body is just what carries the beam. In the boarding rhythm, turning is the decision: you swing the cone slowly across a doorway, and as the frame resolves you also feel your exposure. In the breach rhythm, the beam is the one steady thing on screen, and running is the expensive choice, because every step is noise the web can hear.
  
  *Anchor:* swinging the beam onto a doorway, when the filaments at the edge of the cone resolve into strands.
  
  *Pillar served:* The Web Watches. "The players' light, noise, power use and restraint raise or lower its attention... Restraint and stealth are real strategies."
  
  *Weakness:* it gives the controller credit for the Emitter State's and Attention's fantasy. The controller aims the light, but it doesn't own what light costs. It also says little about the body itself: stiffness, weight, the cryo-pod steps.
  
  ### Candidate B: "Two rhythms, one body" (Pillar 2 leads), recommended
  
  **The ship changes tempo and the body doesn't.** The Sealed Technician moves the same way in both rhythms: stiff, weighted, upright, competent. The pack breathes, the helmet doesn't bob, and the beam turns as fast as your hand turns it, no faster and no slower. In the boarding rhythm, that steadiness reads as patience: careful steps and a slow sweep. In the breach rhythm, the same steadiness is what you hold onto. Red light sweeps, loops vent, a trail runs down the corridor faster than you can walk, and your body still answers exactly as it did a minute ago. The panic comes from the world. The only thing that changes in the body is what you choose: whether to run, which costs noise, and where to point the light.
  
  *Anchor:* swinging the beam onto the same doorway twice, once while boarding and once in the breach. Same gesture, same speed of turn; only the room has changed.
  
  *Pillars served:* Dread Before Chaos, "This pillar owns tempo and presentation." Also Sanity Rewrites Reality's secondary test, "−10% move speed vs. a gauge showing a false value → the false gauge."
  
  *Weakness:* the body could feel unresponsive in the breach. That risk belongs to Game Feel and Detailed Design, which tune base responsiveness. The fantasy doesn't need breach-only boosts to solve it.
  
  ### Candidate C: "The body that cannot lie" (Pillar 1 leads)
  
  **The crewmate's sprite is "the one instrument in the game that cannot lie" (art bible 5.0).** Your gauges may lie and your eyes may lie, but your body never does. If it staggers, you are really failing. If rime forms on the plates, you are really cold. When you press a key the body answers that same frame, and the ship confirms on the next tick. Leaning on that honesty is the fantasy: a fogged visor in a crewmate's beam is a check that only costs light.
  
  *Anchor:* the first steps out of the cryo pod, when your breath fogs and you realise the room is still getting colder.
  
  *Pillar served:* The World Never Lies. "Pipes leak, frost forms, air hisses; the physical world is ground truth. Only instruments and perception can be wrong."
  
  *Weakness:* it gives the controller credit for the Suit's and vital overlays' honesty. Overlay thresholds belong to the game-designer (art bible 5.3), and the kernel's Section B already owns "there is only one ship." It also has to be worded carefully. The body is honest about vitals but says nothing about sanity ("there is no self-tell", 5.3), so "the body never lies" must not slide into "the body shows your sanity."
  
  ---
  
  ### Recommendation: Candidate B, with A's beam and C's honesty as its constants
  
  **Why B:**
  1. **It's the only one that fully belongs to this system.** Moving, turning and holding the beam are what the controller does. A borrows from Emitter State and Attention. C borrows from Suit and the vital overlays.
  2. **It answers the brief's central question directly**, and it supports the MVP's single hypothesis: "The boarding→breach two-rhythm loop... is tense and absorbing." If the body changes with the rhythm, the player reads the rhythm from their controls instead of from the world, and that weakens the tell Pillar 2 depends on.
  3. **It produces the cleanest falsifiable test** (test 2 below): "no movement or aim parameter reads Attention, breach state or sanity" can be checked in code and in playtest.
  4. **It keeps the other two inside it.** The beam as the eye (A) and the honest body (C) become the things that stay constant across both rhythms.
  
  **Trade-off accepted:** the body never "rises to the occasion." There is no adrenaline burst or breach sprint. This is deliberate: Barotrauma's tempo, but your own legs. If playtests read it as sluggish, the fix is base responsiveness for both rhythms, never a breach-only modifier.
  
  **This is the user's call.** If they want the honesty theme at the front, C is defensible, but its "does not claim" note would need to give vitals and sanity back to Suit and Sanity.

  ```markdown
  ## Player Fantasy
  
  *Direct — the player operates this system every second.*
  
  **The ship changes tempo; the body does not.** The player is the Sealed Technician: a round helmet on a square pack, stiff, weighted, upright, competent and fragile. The body moves the same way in a silent derelict and in a venting corridor. It walks without bounce, the pack rises and falls because the suit is sealed, and the beam in its hand turns exactly as fast as the player turns it. Looking at something means lighting it. The body answers the frame the player presses and the ship confirms a tick later, so the hand never waits for the simulation. In the boarding rhythm that steadiness is patience: a deliberate step, a slow sweep, every door a decision. In the breach rhythm the same steadiness is the one thing the player can still trust. The panic is in the world — red sweeping, loops venting, a trail outrunning you down the corridor — and never in the controls. The player's only levers are their own choices: whether to run, which leans the body forward and is noise the web can hear, and where to point the light.
  
  The anchor is one gesture made twice. The first time is early: the player steps out of the cryo pod, takes a few careful steps and swings the beam onto the doorway out, and the frame comes up out of the black. The second time comes later, with the same doorway or one like it and the alarm sweeping. The player swings the beam at the same speed, through the same arc, with the same hands. The room has changed and the body hasn't, which is how the player knows the danger is out there and not in them. When the body does change, the change is true. A stagger means the suit is failing, and the beam stays in the stumbling hand: its origin rides the hand point, while its aim, shape and reach stay as the player and the light made them. Rime on the plates and a fogged visor are real cold and real bad air, never mood.
  
  In co-op, a body tells the truth about its owner. A crewmate who walks like a technician and carries one of the four beam shapes is a crewmate, even through a hallucinated horror rig, because the rig is driven by their real motion and their real beam.
  
  **Pillars served.** *Dread Before Chaos*: "Every breach is preceded by a tell a careful player could have perceived... This pillar owns tempo and presentation." The body's steadiness keeps tempo in the world, where the tell can be read. *The Web Watches*: "The players' light, noise, power use and restraint raise or lower its attention... Restraint and stealth are real strategies." Running and aiming are the restraint choices the player holds every second. *The World Never Lies*: "Only instruments and perception can be wrong." The body is not an instrument, and its vital states are physical consequences. *Sanity Rewrites Reality*, secondary test: "−10% move speed vs. a gauge showing a false value → the false gauge." Sanity lies through the world, never through the legs.
  
  **Design tests.**
  - If we are debating whether the body or the beam may do something the player's input did not do and the simulation did not cause (aim snap or assist, auto-vault, auto-step, a camera-led turn, a scripted walk, a contextual animation that moves the character or reorients the beam): it may not. Presenting what happened is permitted: render interpolation, the per-facing hand point, quantising facing to 8 directions while the beam rotates 360°, and immediate local acknowledgement.
  - The fantasy has failed if the same input, at the same suit vitals and the same carried load, produces different movement or beam motion in the boarding rhythm, in the breach rhythm or at low sanity. That would mean some movement or aim parameter reads Attention, breach state or sanity level. It is a Pillar 2 violation, because the player would be reading the rhythm off their controls instead of off the world, not a tuning issue.
  
  *What this section does not claim*: the controller does not own what light and noise cost (Emitter State and Attention), when rime, fog or stagger appear (the game-designer, via Suit and vitals), the speeds, acceleration and turn rates that make "stiff" responsive (Detailed Design and Game Feel), or the input bindings (ux-designer). Whether the stagger's hand-point displacement needs a reduced-motion variant is flagged for `design/accessibility-requirements.md`; per CR-11, presentation easing never changes the sim. (Alternatives considered: "Looking is spending," a beam-first framing that is true but gives the controller credit for Emitter State's and Attention's fantasy; "The body that cannot lie," which is true but gives the controller credit for Suit's honesty and risks implying a sanity self-tell, which art bible 5.3 forbids.)
  ```

  ### Notes on choices the brief left open
  
  1. **"The beam shaking in the hand" is kept only as hand-point motion.** The art bible makes the beam a truth anchor. Under the Tell its "ambient, beam radius, beam temperature and direction do *not* change" (2.7). Stage-2 sanity is "the light lies": the cone appears to reach further and shadows appear with no occluder (2.9). So the only allowed shake is the beam's *origin* moving with the body: the run lean and the stagger reaction frames (5.2). Aim angle, cone shape, reach and temperature never change, and there are no screen shake or camera effects (2.8). This keeps body shake clearly separate from sanity distortion: the body moves where the light *starts*, and sanity distorts where the light *seems to reach*. If the team wants aim to wobble in breach, that breaks test 2 and needs a new decision here.
  2. **Stagger can move the beam's origin but not its aim.** The decision rule's "or the simulation caused" clause lets honest body states act on the body. It still stops short of rotating the player's aim, because aim is the eye (3.5) and the player's agency.
  3. **"Never do" list, with sources:**
     - **No acrobatics:** no squash, smear or overshoot (5.2); a splaying crewmate reads as horror grammar (3.2, 5.2); and "NOT a combat game."
     - **No auto-aim:** the beam is the eye and "looking is spending" (3.5, 5.4).
     - **No cinematic takeover:** "no chapters or cutscenes," and CR-16 allows no gameplay verb while time stops.
  
     All three fall under design test 1.
  4. **Running is treated as a Pillar 3 decision, not just a speed.** The controller only emits the step. How much noise that raises belongs to Attention, so the Dependencies section needs a controller→Attention stimulus edge.
  5. **This section doesn't decide who owns character position.** The intent/command split (CR-4) is stated only as a promise: "the body answers the frame; the ship confirms a tick later." Position ownership stays the open C.3 boundary with Ship Body.
  
  ### Validation: we'll know this was right if...
  - In MVP playtests, players describe breaches as "the ship went crazy," not "my character got weird."
  - A code review finds no movement or aim value whose inputs include Attention, rhythm state or sanity.
  - Asked mid-breach where their beam is pointing, a tester can answer straight away.
  
  ### Decision for the user
  1. **Two rhythms, one body (Recommended):** the body stays the same in both rhythms and all panic comes from the world. It belongs fully to this system and is falsifiable, but it gives up any breach-only boost to how the body feels.
  2. **Looking is spending:** leads with the beam as the eye and as exposure. It has the strongest Pillar 3 identity but gives the controller credit for Emitter State's and Attention's fantasy.
  3. **The body that cannot lie:** leads with the honest sprite. It has the strongest Pillar 1 identity but gives the controller credit for Suit's honesty and needs careful wording to avoid implying a sanity self-tell.
