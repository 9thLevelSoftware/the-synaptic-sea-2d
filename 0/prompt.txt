  ### Per-pillar feedback
  
  **P1 — The Ship Is a Body.** Testable and useful. "Hidden stat vs leaking pipe" is a choice the team will actually face many times.
  - *Concern:* "Nothing fails silently" is absolute, and P4 breaks it on purpose. From the hallucinating player's point of view, a lying gauge is a silent failure. As written, P1 and P4 contradict each other, and a design review would have no way to decide between them.
  - *Sharpen:* Separate the physical world from instruments. Suggested rewording: "The physical world never lies. Pipes leak, frost forms, air hisses. Only instruments and perception can be wrong." The leaking pipe becomes the ground truth and the gauge becomes the thing that can lie. This settles the 1-vs-4 tension inside the game world, even with no crewmate present.
  - *Differentiation:* On its own it is close to Barotrauma. It becomes distinctive when combined with the welded-from-dead-ships home-ship.
  
  **P2 — Dread Before Chaos.** The goal is right, but the design test is weak. Nobody on a team argues for jump-scares on boarding. The real arguments will be "how long can a breach build before it fires?" and "can a breach ever happen without warning?"
  - *Sharpen:* Turn it into a rule: "Every breach is preceded by a tell a careful player could have perceived (sound, light, gauge drift, hull stress)." A better test: "Breach triggers at a hidden threshold vs. breach preceded by a 10-30 second escalating tell → the tell."
  - *Overlap risk with P3:* Both are about escalation. Keep them apart. P2 covers tempo and presentation (how the escalation feels). P3 covers causality (what the player did to cause it). Write that split down, or reviewers will treat them as the same pillar.
  
  **P3 — The Web Watches.** This is the strongest pillar for the design itself. It is testable, it gives the player real choices (restraint as a strategy), and it constrains audio and lighting as much as game design.
  - *Concern:* Attention only works as strategy if players can read it, and that makes it a P1 obligation. The web's attention must show up in the world (creaking, light flicker, web movement) and must not be a hidden meter. Otherwise players will experience it as a flat spawn timer, which is the exact thing the pillar rejects.
  - *Tension to note:* Depth (P5) raises attention on its own, regardless of what the player does. Decide whether depth sets the *baseline* attention (my recommendation) or adds to it. If it adds, deep runs will feel like the player's choices don't matter.
  
  **P4 — Sanity Is Information.** This is the hook and the best differentiator in the set. Very few games make perception itself the resource you lose. It is testable, and the test is excellent.
  - *Concern 1 (critical):* Solo play and co-op where everyone is insane. The fix you've written ("visible to a saner crewmate") does nothing with one player, or once all players are low on sanity. You need a fallback that doesn't depend on another player: the physical ground truth from P1 (does the pipe actually leak?), cross-checking two instruments, or a manual check that takes time and risk. Decide now whether solo play is supported at all. If it isn't, say so in the pitch.
  - *Concern 2 (fairness):* If a false reading on a critical system leads to death with no way to detect the lie, players will call it cheap, and that hurts competence. Add a rule: "Every lie is detectable by someone or something. No hallucination is unfalsifiable."
  - *Note for the producer:* Co-op networking is a large scope item for a solo first-time developer. Showing different values to different players is the easy part. The co-op itself is the risk. That is not a pillar flaw, but it should be raised before prototyping.
