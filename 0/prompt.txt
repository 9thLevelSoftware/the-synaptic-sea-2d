  ## 1. What the camera is for
  
  **One-line rule: the frame is a window, not a sensor.** The beam is the player's eye (hero shape #1, 3.5); the camera is the wall the eye looks through. It reveals nothing light has not, and it moves for nobody but the player.
  
  **What it reveals**: the crewmate's body (an anchor), the beam and its rim (where the Tell lives), what the beam lights, and whatever else in the world is *already lit* and happens to fall inside the window (a running loop's lantern, a crewmate's beam, the derelict's residual light). **What it withholds**: everything else — and, because the dark is honest (Principle 1), withholding costs the player nothing they could have had: more frame is more black, not more ship (8.1's own argument for ultrawide).
  
  **Feature or readability problem?** The tight frame is a feature with one named exposure. Feature: a frame that cannot hold a room forces the beam to do the looking (Pillar 3 — every look is a spend), keeps the crewmate large enough that the helmet bands read (the identity anchor the user chose over comfort, 8.2), and makes the cone rim — the Tell's home — fill a large share of the frame. Exposure: Pillar 2's design test is "a frosting elbow seen to worsen from across the room", and a 6-deep machine room puts the far elbow ≈ 0.5–1 tile below the frame when the player stands at the near door; worse, a beam pointed north or south has its tip ≈ 3.25 tiles off-screen, so the rim of the cone — where filaments resolve — is partly off-frame on that axis.
  
  **The reconciling rule — "across the room" is satisfied by four things, none of which is a wider default frame:**
  
  1. **Consequence reaches you.** Pillar 1's physical channel does the work: the frost trail propagates *along the pipe toward the player* and enters the frame even when the elbow itself does not. The player reads "worsening" as a line lengthening into view, then follows it to the source (3.0 continuity). *Dependency*: trail propagation speed (Failure & Trail / Graph) must bring a far-elbow trail across a 6-tile room inside the 10–30 s tell window, or this mitigation is fiction — flag to those GDDs.
  2. **The frame leans where the beam points (bounded look-ahead).** The camera offsets from the crewmate along the beam's aim direction, by a bounded amount, eased so a fast sweep (the core micro-loop verb) never throws the frame. This buys back part of the north/south clipping and puts more of the rim in frame exactly when the player is looking there. It is player-owned state (aim), so it never hints (see §2). **Bound it**: proposed ≤ 1.5 tiles (72 px) along the short axis and ≤ 1 tile (48 px) along the long axis, settling over ≈ 0.4–0.6 s — hypotheses, feel knobs. **Flag to ux-designer**: 7.0's critical-strip placement assumes the crewmate near centre; with a 1.5-tile southward shift (player looking north) the crewmate's feet sit ≈ 2.25 tiles (108 px) above the bottom edge, still clear of the ≤ 18 px strip plus 18 px safe margin, but 7.0 should state the tolerated offset rather than inherit it.
  3. **Room kits favour wide-and-shallow** (already Foundry's squat panel ratio, 3.3; 8.2 names this mitigation) — a Derelict Authoring rule: machine rooms author ≤ 5 tiles deep by default; 6-deep rooms are a deliberate dread choice, not the norm.
  4. **The player's own zoom-out step** — one integer step, player-held, never automatic. Its cost is already quantified: sprites at 50 %, tier-3 surface detail lost, tier-2 marks (band count, fringe, moving medium, trail behaviour) survive. Its *gameplay* cost is the key to why it is not a dominant strategy: the beam's world radius does not change, so at 50 % the lit area is the same ship drawn at a quarter of the screen area and the rest of the frame is honest black. The player trades legibility of the lit for context of the dark. Make it hold-to-step-back (release returns to default) so the default frame remains *the* frame — this is a decision for the user (see Decisions).
  
  **Degenerate-play check (Sirlin)**: if playtest shows players spending > 50 % of boarding time zoomed out, the tight frame has failed as a default and the fix is to revisit the frame (40 px tile fallback in 8.10, or make the wide frame default), not to nerf the zoom.   ## 2. Camera and the Tell
  
  **No. The camera never hints.** Not a drift toward a sound source, not a lean toward the filament, not a tremor on hull stress. Three independent rules in the bible converge on this and should be cited together in the GDD:
  
  - 7.0: "No sanity meter and no Attention indicator — anywhere, including under accessibility settings." A camera that drifts toward a source is an Attention indicator the player did not author; it fails Principle 3's design test exactly as a meter does, just drawn in motion instead of in a bar.
  - 2.7: during the Tell "ambient, beam radius, beam temperature and direction do *not* change." The Tell is defined as a change *at the rim* against an unchanged interior. A camera move changes the whole frame and so dilutes the one signal the state is built on.
  - 9.7: "handheld camera energy" is excluded as a screen effect; 2.8: "the frantic feeling comes from *more things moving*, not from the screen fighting the player."
  
  Why the hidden-meter objection is decisive here: Pillar 2 says a breach is preceded by a tell "a careful player **could have** perceived." Could-have requires that missing it is possible; a camera that turns the player's head removes the perception skill the pillar exists to reward (SDT Competence: the player must know *why* they noticed). It also reads as authorship — "look here" — which 3.5 reserves for round-and-moving hero shapes; the camera would become a hero shape the web has not earned ("the web earns no hero shape at all"). And a camera that reacts to true world state becomes a truth channel the Perception Filter would then be tempted to fake (a drift toward nothing) — keep the camera out of both channels entirely.
  
  **Design rule for the GDD**: *the camera responds to player-owned inputs only — position, beam aim, zoom choice, the vehicle the player is in — and never to world state (Attention level, breach phase, horror position, sound sources, graph failures, sanity).* The Tell is carried by the rim (Lighting + Attention presentation), by audio (system 24), and by real needle drift on real gauges (7.6 item 9). The camera's whole contribution to the Tell is the look-ahead of §1: the player who points the beam at the sound gets more rim in frame — because they looked, not because the game did.
  
  **Screen shake**: none for Tell or Attention under any circumstance (amplitude would be a meter). One bounded exception is *worth a decision* (Decisions, D3): a single integer-pixel impulse on a physical event the player's body is inside — hull section separation, a weld-detach, pod docking contact — never on Attention, never on sanity, never looping, ≤ 2 base px, ≤ 200 ms, decayed. Recommended **off for MVP**; the MVP's one horror behaviour and one weld do not need it, and HR-13 means the HUD would stay still over a moving world, which is itself a readability risk to test before adopting.

  ## 3. Camera and Attention
  
  **Camera behaviour is invariant across calm → tell → breach → subsiding.** No tightening (FOV as meter), no loosening, no change of smoothing ("steadier" would itself be a signal). The breach rhythm is the state in which the camera must be *most* boring: 2.8's hardest test is "frightened and still able to read a pipe," and the beam cone "stays the brightest thing in frame so the player always knows where they are looking" — a camera that moved differently in breach would fight that.
  
  How it stays honest: by having nothing to be honest about. A presentation of Attention that the player "can also read in the world" is still a second channel, and 7.0 closes that door for the screen-space layer; the camera is the one piece of presentation that is neither world nor HUD, and the cleanest position is that it carries no state at all.
  
  The only speed-dependent behaviour permitted is player-owned: if Player Controller has a run/sprint, look-ahead may scale with the crewmate's own velocity (standard follow-camera practice), and that scaling is identical in every Attention state.
  
  **Acceptance criterion (testable)**: record identical player input (position path, aim path, zoom) in calm, in a tell window, and in breach; the camera transform trace is identical frame-for-frame.

  2. **Falsification needs a fixed ruler.** Stage 2 lies by making the cone *appear* to reach further than the geometry it reveals; the player checks by walking into the dark and seeing what the beam actually reveals (the true-radius anchor). A frame that lies about scale (false zoom) or position (drift) lies about *distance* — and distance is time-to-the-hatch in a breach.   ## 5. Transit, re-wake, death — beats the pipeline must support
  
  **Pod transit (2.5)** — the only exterior view; emotional target *smallness*. The camera follows the pod exactly as it follows the crewmate: pod near centre with the same bounded look-ahead along its travel/running-light direction, default zoom, no cuts, no pans. The derelict is never *shown* to the player — it resolves because the pod approaches it, and the first thing to resolve is its **residual human light** (the one visible unknown, 2.5/3.5 #4), then its silhouette in the pod's own light. That ordering is the beat; the pipeline supports it by doing nothing special. **Duration is not given by any source** — it is a gate knob owned by Web Map & Transit (system 16), tuned to the sortie's 5–15 min meso-loop, and this GDD should reference it rather than set it. Not a cinematic because: the camera stays on the player's vessel at game scale and moves only because the vessel moves. Not skippable by default would be my instinct (the unknown must be seen — it is the "one more sortie" pull), but that is system 16's call.
  
  **Crew loss (2.10, VS-A; MVP uses the Save/Load reload stub)** — "the last light going out," seconds long. The camera **holds exactly where it was** at the moment of death: no pull-back to show the ship, no zoom-out, no push-in on the body, no pan to the crewmate who did it. The body stops, so the follow target stops; look-ahead eases back to zero (the hand has dropped); zoom stays wherever the player left it. Sources go out warmest-first with the frame still, then a **hard cut** (not a fade — 2.10 says "a cut") to the cryo-bay lamp guttering on. Any camera motion here is the death-screen 2.10 rejected, in another costume. The MVP stub should play the same lights-out-then-cut into the reloaded save so the beat is built once.
  
  **Wake and re-wake (2.2)** — the camera is player-locked from frame one: no establishing shot, no slow push, no drift (the slowest state in the game; "nothing moves faster than a drip"). Re-wake must satisfy "I know this room" *before* "something has changed," so the camera opens at the **identical** position and default zoom relative to the exit pod as the first wake — snap, not ease — and any zoom step the player held at death is reset. The camera is part of the room's familiarity.

Candidates range from "stay on your own black frame with voice" to "spectate a crewmate"; the latter is a free reveal of a frame the dead player did not pay to light. Name it in Open Questions; do not decide it here.

  ## 6. What this camera is NOT
  
  - **No cinematic takeover**: no cutscene camera, establishing pans, boss/horror intros, or camera cuts in play. The only cuts are the death cut (2.10) and whatever system 16 defines at the pod-hop boundaries.
  - **No revealing pan**: no "look at the objective," no fog-of-war reveal, no camera showing a room the beam has not lit. The Chart Table never commands the world camera — it is screen-space (7.0) and the map is a chart you open, not a view you are taken to.
  - **No minimap, no off-screen indicators**: interaction prompts are drawn only for lit objects (7.0) and are anchored to the object — they never clamp to the frame edge (a clamped prompt is an off-screen arrow, i.e. a minimap in one pixel). Crewmates off-frame get no edge marker; the crew strip (7.5) is HUD's and is not positional.
  - **No auto-aim / aim assist**: the camera never steers the beam, and the beam never steers the camera beyond the bounded, eased look-ahead the player drives.
  - **No camera as a channel**: for Attention or sanity (§2–4). No shake driven by either; no "sanity wobble"; no handheld energy.
  - **No rotation or tilt, ever.** No horizon exists in this projection.
  - **No automatic zoom** (room-fit, event-driven, or state-driven) and **no non-integer zoom or zoom tween** — a lerp between integer steps passes through forbidden scales; the step is a cut. **No sub-pixel camera position** — the whole pipeline exists to protect the grid (owned by Section A/B; stated here so camera feel is designed around integer-snapped motion with no visible jitter at walking speed).
  - **No free-look / peek** beyond look-ahead: a camera the player can push off their body to see a lit region without walking to it is a revealing pan with extra steps (a lit gauge or crewmate across a dark room *is* information). Listed as a decision (D4) because a case can be made for gamepad players; my recommendation is no.
  - **No shared camera in co-op**: each client follows its own crewmate; the human channel (a crewmate's beam in *your* frame) is the only way another player enters your view.
