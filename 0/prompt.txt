  ### 7.0 Governing rule: the HUD is a suit, the ship is an instrument
  
  **Who produced the number decides where it is drawn.** Three layers, one question each.
  
  | Layer | Who produced the information | Drawn where | Lit? | Can a reading lie (class b)? | Anchor? |
  | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
  | **In-world instruments** | the ship, the pod, a derelict | sprites in the world layer: gauges, sight-glasses, dials on panels, the pod dash, the chart-table console | yes — unreadable until lit | yes; this is class (b)'s primary home ("from the gauge to the sprite") | the instrument housing is physical geometry (P1); its reading is not |
  | **Suit layer** | the player's own suit | screen-space `CanvasLayer`, 3.4 frame/reading grammar, 4.6 palette | no — the visor is backlit | readings yes, frame never | the frame is the "HUD frame" anchor of Section 1's Corollary |
  | **Chart table (meta)** | nobody in the fiction: keybinds, menus, settings, the web map | screen-space, 2.11 register | no | **never** — it is not an instrument | whole layer is honest by definition |
  
  **The deciding rule, applied with Section 1's three questions.** (1) *Does it make the player spend light?* Anything the ship reports about itself goes in the world so it must be lit to read — a dead loop's gauge is dark until the beam finds it, and a running loop's gauge is faintly lit by its own emission (Principle 3: a lit gauge is a lantern). (2) *Is it in the world or on top of it?* The suit is the one thing that is always with the player and always powered, so suit telemetry is the only *state* permitted on the screen-space layer. (3) *If the player is lied to here, does the lie leak?* Readings in both the world and the suit layer are drawn in the thin, light, moving "reading" register of 3.4, so every lie-capable mark is visually declared as such, and the stage-1 gleam (4.4) has a glass to sit on. Meta elements are drawn in a register that never carries the gleam because they never carry a lie.
  
  **What the suit layer holds (working proposal, game-designer decides the list).** The suit mirrors the three graph resources as the body feels them — *breath* (suit O₂), *warmth* (suit heat), *charge* (beam and tool power) — plus the hand rail (which tool is bound to which key). Nothing else. In particular:
  
  - **No sanity meter and no attention meter.** Principle 3's design test and 4.6 already forbid them; the only sanity that reaches screen-space is the violet gleam on a *reading* that is lying. Attention lives in the filaments at the cone edge (2.7). A player who wants to know how watched they are must look at the world, which is the game.
  - **No ship state on the suit layer.** "COOLANT LOW" is the banner Principle 2 rejected. The ship tells you its state along its pipes; the suit tells you about you.
  - **No minimap in play.** A minimap is a vignette-free fog-of-war mask drawn small (Principle 1's rejected alternative). The web map (7.4) is a chart you open, not a radar you glance at.
  
  **Interaction prompts — the one apparent exception.** Principle 2 rejected a world-space icon layer, but that rejection was about *state*. A prompt shows a keybind and a verb (`E · Cut`), never the object's condition, so it is meta, not an instrument. It is drawn on the HUD layer in the chart-table register, anchored to the object's screen position, and **is drawn only when the object is inside the lit area** (the technical-artist sets the illumination threshold). Nothing is prompted in the dark, so the prompt teaches the rule rather than breaking it. Whether the *action itself* is possible on an unlit object is the game-designer's call; this is a drawing rule only.
  
  **The inspect view.** In-world gauges carry no numerals at game-camera distance (3.1: readable by needle angle and zone band alone). When the player needs precision they *inspect* the instrument: a screen-space magnification of that same gauge, framed by the ship's own bezel dialect, with numerals in the reading register. Two rules: the inspect view is drawn only for an instrument that is currently lit (whether inspection is *permitted* unlit is the game-designer's; visually there is nothing to magnify), and the inspect view is **the same instrument**, not a second one — it lies exactly when the gauge on the wall lies, gleam included. Pillar 4's "second instrument" check is a *different* gauge elsewhere on the ship, never the zoom.
  
  **Composition.** The beam cone is the brightest shape in frame and the eye's home (3.5). The HUD must therefore stay at the edges, small and cool. Suit cluster bottom-left (the lower corners are where peripheral vision can hold a glanceable reading without pulling the eye off the cone, and keeping the top of the frame clear leaves the wall front-faces — where the pipe grammar lives, 3.3 — unobstructed); hand rail bottom-right; crew strip (7.5) top-left; prompts at their objects. Everything inside a 5 % safe margin. The HUD's non-Ground pixel area at rest is capped at roughly 1 % of the frame (tunable), so no matter how bright Paper White text is per pixel, the beam wins by area.
  
  **Alternatives considered.**
  - *Fully diegetic (Dead Space model): all telemetry on the suit sprite or on wall panels, nothing screen-space.* Rejected. At 3/4 oblique HD pixel scale a suit-mounted readout is two or three art pixels; the player would spend light to read their own breath, which punishes the one thing the human must always be able to know; and the "HUD frame" anchor would have nothing to anchor.
  - *Fully screen-space (survival-genre default): every loop as a bar or icon on the HUD.* Rejected. It violates Principle 2, erases spend-light, and gives class (b) the dullest possible target — an icon flipping — instead of a needle disagreeing with the body of the ship.
  - *Pod HUD as a separate screen-space mode.* Rejected. The pod is a vehicle with a dash; its heading reticle and fuel sight-glass are lit by its own tungsten running lights (2.5) and belong in the world like every other instrument. The suit layer is unchanged in the pod — the suit does not know it is in a pod.
  
  ### 7.1 Typography
  
  **Personality: stamped nameplate and engraved dial.** Industrial, humanist, un-ornamented, slightly condensed. Type on the HUD should look like it was punched into a plate in the Foundry dialect (3.3): capitals for frame labels, numerals that look machined. It is never futuristic-angular (the sci-fi default reads as a different, cleaner civilisation than this salvage hull) and never a terminal/monospace "hacker" face (that says computer; this HUD says instrument).
  
  **Two faces, not one.**
  
  | Face | Used for | Why this face |
  | --- | --- | --- |
  | **Instrument face** — a bitmap pixel font on the art grid | suit readings, frame labels, in-world instrument numerals in the inspect view, keybind glyphs, map labels | the HUD is the ship's dialect (3.4) and must sit on the same pixel grid as the ship; an antialiased vector glyph beside a pixel bezel reads as a browser overlay, and the integer grid makes a needle's one-pixel wander legible (7.3) |
  | **Reading face** — a vector humanist sans, rendered by Godot's font system | lore fragments, recovered logs, settings, long menu copy, tooltips | long-form reading in a pixel font is fatiguing and resists accessibility scaling; the chart table is outside the lit world (2.11) and may use the one face in the game that is not pixel-bound |
  
  Both faces must have **tabular (fixed-width) numerals, a slashed or dotted zero, and distinct 1 / l / I**. Tabular numerals are load-bearing: a reading that changes — or lies — must never shift the frame around it. The frame is an anchor; a proportional "1" to "7" change that nudges a bezel would move the one thing the Corollary says never moves.
  
  **Weight.** The instrument face ships in one weight. Readings are *light and thin*; frame labels are the same face in Hull Grey, which reads as engraved because of colour, not weight. The reading face uses regular and one heavier weight for headings; no light weights (thin vector text on Ground at the sizes below falls under the contrast floor).
  
  **Size hierarchy and floors.** Expressed in screen pixels first, because floors are about the eye, then converted to art pixels at an assumed integer scale. *Assumption, to be confirmed in Section 8:* a 640 × 360 base, scaled 3× at 1080p and 4× at 1440p. The screen-pixel floors are the contract; the art-pixel column is derived and re-derives if the base changes. Two observations for Section 8, which owns the choice: a 640 × 360 base lands on an integer at 1080p, 1440p and 2160p (3×, 4×, 6×), while 960 × 540 gives 2.67× at 1440p, which would soften every pixel in the game, not just the type. And if "HD pixel art" is taken to mean sprites authored at or near 1× native (art px ≈ screen px), the instrument face simply becomes a crisp bitmap font at the floor sizes and the two-face split holds for its other reasons (grid-matched edges, 1-px wander legibility, dialect). Either way the typography does not decide the base.
  
  | Level | Where | 1080p (screen px, cap height) | 1440p | Art px at 3× / 4× |
  | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
  | Reading numerals | suit O₂ value, inspect view, map distance | ≥ 21 | ≥ 28 | 7 |
  | Label | gauge names, hand-rail tool names, crew names | ≥ 15 | ≥ 20 | 5 |
  | Caption floor | the smallest glyph anywhere | 15 | 20 | 5 |
  | Menu body (reading face) | settings, logs | ≥ 20 px font size | ≥ 26 | — |
  | Lore long-form | recovered logs read in full | ≥ 22 | ≥ 29 | — |
  | Section heading | menu groups | ≥ 24 | ≥ 32 | — |
  | Title | screen names | ≥ 32 | ≥ 42 | — |
  
  Nothing on screen falls below the caption floor; if a label does not fit at 5 art px cap height, the frame is too small, not the type. Line length for reading-face body copy: 45–75 characters. Letterspacing on the instrument face: one art pixel; never tracked tighter.
  
  **UI scale.** The settings offer UI scale in **integer steps** of the art scale (the pixel face cannot scale continuously) and a separate continuous reading-face scale. The frame grows with the integer step; the beam does not, so a player who scales the HUD up spends some of the frame area the beam would otherwise own — that is their accessibility trade, made explicit.
  
  **Alternatives considered.**
  - *One pixel font everywhere.* Rejected — lore and settings become hard to read, and every accessibility size step is an integer jump.
  - *One vector font everywhere.* Rejected — a smooth face on pixel bezels breaks "the HUD is the home-ship's dialect" (3.4), and sub-pixel glyph edges make a one-pixel needle wander invisible.
  - *A pixel font with several hand-drawn sizes.* Viable but costly for a solo developer; revisit only if the two-face split fails a readability test.
  
  ### 7.2 Iconography
  
  **Flat silhouettes, one colour, on the art grid.** No outlines, no gradients, no illustration, no drop shadows. A flat stamped silhouette is what a plate on this ship would carry; an outlined icon adds a second line colour and reads as web UI; an illustrated icon is a picture of a thing rather than the thing's grammar.
  
  **Icons are the fitting table.** Per 3.4, loop glyphs are the loop's fitting silhouette plus its flow mark, so shape carries meaning with hue removed (4.7):
  
  | Loop | Glyph | Flow mark inside the glyph |
  | --- | --- | --- |
  | O₂ | thin ring clamp | three close-pitched chevrons |
  | Power | square junction box | a dash-pulse (two dashes) |
  | Coolant | padded collar | one wide chevron |
  | Fuel | bolted flange | a diagonal hazard band |
  | Integrity | rivet line (three rivets) | a hairline |
  
  **Hue follows 4.2, not the icon.** A glyph is drawn in its loop hue only while that loop is *running* (emission is exposure, carried into the UI); a dead or unpowered loop's glyph is Graphite lit; a *failed* loop's glyph stays in hue because the failure is a trail, and the Alarm Red warning mark appears beside it, never over it. Icons never swap to express state. State is a reading — a needle, a fill level, a number — inside or beside the glyph. This keeps the icon set tiny (one glyph per loop, one per tool) and keeps the lie where 3.4 says it lives: in the reading, never in the frame or the glyph.
  
  **Two canonical sizes.** 16 × 16 art px (HUD, inspect view) and 24 × 24 (menus, map legend), each with a 1-art-px stroke and a 1-px inner clearance so the glyph survives on a bezel. Nothing smaller; a 12-px glyph cannot hold a flow mark.
  
  **Tool glyphs** (cutter, scanner, welder, suppressant, flare, torch, decoy) are the tool's own silhouette in profile — the same shape that breaks the crewmate's outline in 3.2 — so the hand rail teaches the in-world read. Tools are Paper White; the bound tool is Tungsten fixture.
  
  **Keybind and gamepad glyphs.** Keys are chamfered-square keycaps, Ground interior, Graphite lit border, Paper White letter in the instrument face. Gamepad face buttons are drawn the **same way** — a chamfered keycap carrying the button's letter (A/B/X/Y) — not as circles. The one grammar exception is a controller whose buttons are named by symbols rather than letters (the PlayStation set): the symbol goes inside the keycap at glyph size, accepted as a platform convention the player already owns, not a shape this UI chose. 3.4 reserves the only UI curves for dial arcs and needles, and a round button glyph would be the one closed circle on screen that is not an instrument; a keycap in the ship's dialect also lets keyboard and gamepad prompts share one component. (The ux-designer owns which prompts exist and when they switch input device; this is the visual grammar only.)
  
  **Alternatives considered.** *Abstract symbols* (a drop for coolant, a flame for fuel) — rejected: they are a second vocabulary to learn and have no shape backup the player already knows. *Outlined icons with hue fill* — rejected: two colours per icon spends hue, and the outline competes with the bezel.
  
  ### 7.3 UI animation: three motions
  
  The HUD is a set of physical instruments drawn by a human hand, so it moves the way instruments move. There are exactly three motion signatures, and the player must be able to tell them apart without colour, because 2.7 already uses one of them as an honest Tell.
  
  | Signature | What it means | Motion | Rests? | Palette |
  | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
  | **Settle** (honest change) | the ship changed; the instrument caught up | damped spring: ease-out over 250–400 ms, overshoot ≤ 5 %, then still | yes | none |
  | **Tremor** (honest stress — the Tell, 2.7) | something physical is loading the hull or the loop | ± 1 art px, irregular, 6–10 Hz, around a *true* value | returns to rest when the stress ends | none — no gleam, because nothing is lying |
  | **Glide** (the lie — class b, stage 1) | the reading has detached from the ship | smooth, uncaused drift at a slow constant rate (0.5–2 ° s⁻¹ on a dial arc), with a slow micro-wander (< 0.5 Hz) that never fully rests | **never** | violet gleam on the glass or needle edge (4.4) |
  
  **The honest/lying distinction is cause, mass and rest.** Honest motion starts with a cause the player could have seen in the world (a valve turned, a seam cracked), has mass (it eases and settles) and then rests. A tremor is honest because it is *fast, small and centred* — the shaking of a real needle on a stressed ship — and it stops when the stress stops. A glide is the one motion no real instrument makes: slow, smooth, directional, with nothing in the ship to explain it, and it never quite comes to rest. A player who learns "needles that wander are lying; needles that shake are warning" has learned Pillar 2 and Pillar 4 from the same gauge.
  
  **Digital readouts.** Honest numerals **tick** — they step once per real change and never tween. A lying readout ticks *continuously*, every few hundred milliseconds, as it tracks the glide, or holds stale while the ship audibly and visibly changes. The mechanism is always honest; only the value lies. (The audio-director decides whether a tick has a sound; the visual tick is defined here.)
  
  **What never moves.** The HUD `CanvasLayer` is **excluded from camera shake and from the hallucination presentation filter** — frame, bezels, labels and glyphs are the Corollary's anchor, and a shaking frame would teach the player that the frame can move. Readings move inside a still frame; that stillness is what makes stage-1 drift readable rather than merely unsettling (3.4).
  
  **Pulse and flash.** The Alarm Red warning mark on a failing instrument pulses **in phase with the beacon sweep** (2–3 s period, ~50 % duty), never faster — one rhythm for alarm everywhere. Nothing on the UI changes luminance faster than 3 Hz; the tremor is positional, not luminous, so it is exempt. No full-frame flashes, ever (2.8's rejected vignette).
  
  **Menus and chart table.** Cuts and short fades (80–120 ms), no slides, no bounces, no parallax. The selection bracket snaps with a 100 ms ease-out. The web map's filaments drift at ≤ 1 art px s⁻¹ in the current ring only (2.11); everything human on the map is static. Sober means almost nothing moves, and what moves is the web.
  
  **Alternatives considered.** *Sci-fi scanline/glitch for the lie* — rejected: it is a screen effect, not an instrument behaviour, and it says "the HUD is broken" when the pillar says "the gauge is wrong". *Red flashing for alarm* — rejected on photosensitivity and on 2.8. *Juicy UI (bounce, squash, scale-pop on change)* — rejected: it puts energy into the HUD, and 2.9 says sanity must never be read off the screen's energy.
  
  ### 7.4 The web map and menus
  
  **Register.** 2.11 defines the mood; this defines the furniture. Ground `#1C2128` full-bleed; one chamfered Graphite-lit border; rivet marks at the four corners only (one per corner — rivet-line detailing is a dialect cue, not a texture). Text Paper White, instrument face for labels and distances, reading face for descriptions.
  
  **Web map components.**
  
  | Element | Drawing | Note |
  | --- | --- | --- |
  | Depth rings | concentric steps of Ground, each ≈ 4 % darker toward centre; the inner ring's centre reaches Void Steel | the only "deeper = darker" rule carried into UI (4.6); composition pulls inward (2.11) |
  | Filaments | Hull Grey, 1 art px, branching, no right angles, density rising inward | the current ring's filaments drift; others are static |
  | Home-ship | a Tungsten fixture point with a 1-px rectilinear Foundry silhouette | the only warm mark on the chart |
  | Known wrecks | Graphite-lit rectilinear silhouettes in their donor dialect (3.3) | a visited wreck gains its dialect; an unvisited one is a plain rectangle |
  | The one visible unknown | a single 1-art-px square tick inside the wreck silhouette in Tungsten or its loop hue (no circle — 3.4), **only once seen from the pod** (2.5) | the chart records what the crew lit; it never reveals |
  | Routes | dashed rectilinear lines, Hull Grey; the planned sortie in Tungsten | human geometry over inhuman geometry (3.4) |
  | Crewmates | their beam-shape glyph (7.5) at their position | identity is beam shape, nowhere a colour |
  | Selection | Tungsten chamfered bracket around the wreck, label and distance beside it | never a filled block |
  
  The map **never** shows iridescence, violet, flesh, attention or sanity. It is a human instrument that cannot lie, because nobody on the ship produced it: it is the crew's own chart.
  
  **Ship schematic (working proposal).** If the game-designer wants a graph view of the composite ship, it is an **in-world chart-table console** opened into the inspect register — lit, instrumented, lie-capable — not a chart-table (meta) screen. The ship reporting on itself is always an instrument.
  
  **Menu components.** Left navigation rail; content on the right; one column of chamfered-rectangle list items with Ground interiors and 1-px Graphite-lit borders. **Selection is a Tungsten bracket** (left bar plus chamfer corners), never a Tungsten fill — a warm plane on screen would be a lantern the UI lit for free. Hover turns the label Tungsten; disabled is Graphite lit (below contrast on purpose; inactive). The gamepad focus ring is the same bracket. Sliders are a bar with a needle-mark; toggles a chamfered switch; **dial arcs appear only on instruments**, never as menu decoration.
  
  **Pause.** A right-third side panel on Ground over the frozen world. **The world is not dimmed, blurred or desaturated** under a menu: dimming is a mask on the world (Principle 1), blur is a lens lie, and desaturation is the sanity convention 4.4 rejected. The world simply stops, lit exactly as it was.
  
  **Loss.** No death screen (2.10). The last light goes out, then the cryo-bay lamp. Any post-loss UI (logs recovered, knowledge kept) is read at the chart table after re-wake, as furniture in the room the player knows.
  
  **Accessibility surface (visual side only; the ux-designer and `design/accessibility-requirements.md` own the requirement).** UI scale (integer), reading-face scale, HUD opacity with a floor (recommend 70 % — below it the suit readings fall under contrast), and a "pattern strength" option that thickens flow marks on glyphs. No colour-remap option is needed because the grammar is already shape-first (4.7).
  
  ### 7.5 Co-op: crewmates on the HUD
  
  *Designed now so the single-player HUD leaves room for it; required at VS-B, not MVP.*
  
  **The crew strip** sits top-left: one tile per crewmate, stacked. Each tile is a chamfered frame (anchor) holding:
  
  | Part | Register | Content |
  | --- | --- | --- |
  | **Identity glyph** | anchor — the human channel (Pillar 1, 3.2) | the crewmate's beam shape (wide cone, narrow cone, split, fan) with their helmet stripe count (1–4), in Tungsten fixture |
  | **Name** | anchor | instrument face, Paper White |
  | **Voice** | anchor — the human channel is never faked | while their voice is active the identity glyph's interior fills (outline → solid) in the same Tungsten fixture; nothing else changes (the audio-director owns voice itself). *Alternative*: stepping the glyph to Tungsten beam core `#FFE2A8` reads more like "their light, brighter", but beam core is not a 4.6 UI colour; adopting it would be a 4.6 addendum (inside the Tungsten family 4.2 already permits) for the user to approve, not a quiet addition |
  | **Location** | reading | a small glyph: Foundry hull (home-ship), pod silhouette, grey wreck rectangle |
  | **Beam state** | reading | glyph lit = beam on, Graphite = beam off (emission is exposure, carried to the strip) |
  | **Vitals** (optional) | reading | their suit breath/warmth as needles, *if* the game-designer relays them |
  
  **What is never on the tile.** Sanity, in any form. 4.6 forbids violet as a UI colour, and Pillar 4 places the class (c) tell *in the world*: the attacker's violet fringe around their outline and beam edge (2.9, 4.4). A player who wants to know whether a crewmate is safe looks at them, under light, and checks the edge — the same lesson as everywhere else. Attention is not on the tile either.
  
  **Why beam shape and not colour.** 3.2 already spent the decision: four player hues would eat the loop palette, and a suit colour is a sprite property a hallucination could plausibly fake, whereas the beam is a light the engine renders honestly. The tile's identity glyph is therefore the same shape the player sees in the world, and under class (c) — when a crewmate renders as a horror — the tile still shows a human beam glyph beside a horror that is carrying that very beam. Silhouette rule, light rule and tile agree, and the tile is on the anchor side.
  
  **Crew loss on the strip.** When a crewmate dies, their identity glyph goes **dark** — Tungsten to Graphite — and the frame stays. The tile is 2.10 in miniature: the last light going out, honestly, with no red and no violet. Visual recommendation: the dark tile stays in the strip for the rest of the sortie rather than vanishing, so the loss is carried as an absence the frame still holds; when it is actually removed is a ux-designer/game-designer condition.
  
  **In-world identity.** No name tags over heads by default — a floating label is a world-space icon (Principle 2) and a lit label in the dark would break Principle 1. In the world a crewmate *is* their beam shape and stripe count (3.2). A hold-to-reveal name is the ux-designer's call; if offered, it is drawn in the chart-table register and only for crewmates inside the viewer's light.
  
  **Off-screen crewmates — a trade-off for the ux-designer and creative-director, not decided here.** A screen-edge arrow toward an off-screen crewmate is a genuine readability aid in co-op and a genuine violation of "nothing is shown that is not lit" (Principle 1) and "no world-space icon layer" (Principle 2). Art-direction's position: the web map and the crew strip's location reading are the honest substitutes, and a sweep of a crewmate's beam across the dark is a better, in-fiction locator than an arrow. If the ux-designer finds in playtest that players lose each other, the least-damaging version is an edge mark in the chart-table register that appears only while that crewmate's **voice is active** — anchoring the aid to the human channel — rather than a persistent arrow. Escalated as a trade-off between Principle 1/2 and co-op readability.
  
  ### 7.6 Art direction vs readability: conflicts and how each was decided
  
  Approximate WCAG contrast ratios of the 4.6 palette on the two surfaces text could sit on (computed from the published hexes; the lighting spike may shift them slightly, so re-check after Section 8):
  
  | Colour | on Ground `#1C2128` | on Graphite lit `#5C6670` (bezel) | Verdict |
  | --- | --- | --- | --- |
  | Paper White `#DDE4EA` | ≈ 12.6 : 1 | ≈ 4.6 : 1 | body text on Ground; marginal on bezel |
  | Hull Grey `#8C949C` | ≈ 5.3 : 1 | ≈ 1.9 : 1 | labels on Ground only |
  | Tungsten fixture `#FFCC66` | ≈ 10.8 : 1 | ≈ 3.9 : 1 | accent text on Ground; large-only on bezel |
  | Alarm Red `#E0102A` | ≈ 3.3 : 1 | ≈ 1.2 : 1 | **fails body text everywhere**; mark only |
  | O₂ Cyan / Power Amber / Coolant Ice | ≈ 9–12 : 1 | — | glyphs and readings fine on Ground |
  | Fuel Ember `#F06A28` | ≈ 5.2 : 1 | — | glyph fine; not small text |
