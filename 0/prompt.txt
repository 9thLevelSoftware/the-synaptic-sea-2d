  ## 1. Camera follow feel
  
  **Recommendation: no dead-zone; continuous first-order (exponential) follow with zero overshoot by construction; aim-lead toward the beam, smoothed slower than the position follow; crewmate bounded to an inner box so 7.0's composition holds.**
  
  **Why no dead-zone.** A dead-zone makes the camera start and stop discontinuously — a motion onset the eye reads as "something happened", which 2.9 forbids as a tempo cue and 2.8 forbids as "the screen fighting the player". Dead-zones serve fast horizontal travel (platformers). At 2 tiles/s a soft follow lags by `v × τ` ≈ 17 px (0.36 tile) and the composition never changes shape. Keep `camera.deadzone_tiles` as a knob with default `0` so a playtest can A/B it.
  
  **Smoothing (frame-rate independent, render-rate `dt`):**
  `cam += (target − cam) × (1 − exp(−dt / τ))` — a first-order lag cannot overshoot, so "no bounce, no overshoot" is structural, not tuned. The art bible's instrument *settle* allows ≤ 5 % overshoot (7.3); the camera gets **0 %** — the frame is the still thing readings move inside (7.3 "readings move inside a still frame").
  
  | Knob | Provisional | Safe range | Affects |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | `camera.follow_tau_s` | 0.18 s | 0.10–0.30 | lag behind the crewmate; at run speed lag = 168 × 0.18 ≈ 30 px (0.63 tile) |
  | `camera.lookahead_tiles` | 2.0 (96 px) | 1.5–2.5 | how far the frame leads into the beam |
  | `camera.lookahead_tau_s` | 0.40 s | 0.25–0.60 | how slowly the lead swings when aim swings |
  | `camera.lookahead_beam_off_scale` | 0.5 | 0–1 | lead when the beam is off (toward facing instead of aim) |
  | `camera.inner_box_frac` | 0.60 × 0.60 of frame | 0.5–0.75 | the crewmate never leaves this box (7.0 "near frame centre") |
  | `camera.deadzone_tiles` | 0 | 0–1 | A/B only |
  
  **Why lead into the beam, and by how much.** The beam is hero #1 and "the eye's home" (3.5). With the crewmate centred, a 7-tile beam aimed north exits the frame 3.25 tiles short (half-height is 3.75 tiles); aimed east it just reaches the edge. Behind the player is the honest dark (Principle 1) — unlit, drawn at the ambient floor only — so pixels spent behind the player show almost nothing. **A 2-tile lead** puts the crewmate 5.75 tiles from the leading edge when aiming north (cone still clipped by ~1.25 tiles, but the lit *floor* under the cone is in view) and leaves 1.75 tiles (84 px) below the sprite's feet — above the 18-base-px critical strip (7.0: 5 % of frame height). Do not go past 2.5 tiles: the crewmate's helmet would sit under the suit panel's reach and 7.0's centre-bottom-strip justification ("near-periphery of the beam origin") weakens.
  
  **Lead source.** Read the *local raw aim* (what the beam renderer uses for same-frame acknowledgement, CR-4), not the sim's coalesced `set_aim` — the quantised intent arrives "a few per second" and would step the lead. Smooth it slower than the follow (0.40 s vs 0.18 s) so a mouse flick does not yank the frame. Mouse-aim feedback loop (lead shifts the world under the cursor → aim angle changes): stable because the lead depends on direction only and the slow τ damps it; still, mark `lookahead_tiles` × direction-only as a playtest check with cursor close to the crewmate.
  
  **Rhythm independence.** Camera constants are identical in boarding and breach. 2.8: "the frantic feeling comes from *more things moving*, not from the screen fighting the player" — and 2.9 says energy must never be read off the screen. The camera is a constant; the sprint speed already grows the lag naturally.
  
  **What must NOT happen, and the one permitted exception:**
  - No bounce, no spring overshoot, no continuous shake, no "handheld" energy (9.6 lists Pandorum's handheld camera as an *Avoid*; 7.3 rejects "juicy" motion). No camera rotation, ever (grid, 8.4).
  - **HUD never shakes** (HR-13, 7.3) — met by construction: HUD on a root-viewport `CanvasLayer`, world in the `SubViewport` (8.4, 8.11 flag 3). The camera lives inside the `SubViewport`; it cannot touch the HUD.
  - **World-layer jolt on hull failure: permitted, bounded.** 7.3 presupposes world shake exists ("the HUD `CanvasLayer` is excluded from camera shake"). Propose a *discrete jolt*, not a shake: triggered only by P1 physical events (hull section failing, pod docking impact, detach), amplitude **≤ 2 base px** (6 screen px at 3×) in **whole base px** steps, decaying to zero in **≤ 250 ms**, no rotation, no repeat while one is active. Knobs: `camera.jolt_px` (0–3), `camera.jolt_decay_s` (0.1–0.4). Reduced motion (HR-8 analogue): `jolt_px = 0`; nothing else changes because the camera has no decorative motion to strip.
  - **Never driven by Attention or Sanity.** A camera that trembles with Attention is a hidden meter made kinetic (Pillar 3's design test); a camera the Perception Filter moves lies about geography. **Recommend adding the camera (position, lead, zoom, jolt) to the HR-6 anchor list.** This matters because `Camera2D` is a `CanvasItem`, and the control-manifest rule of 8.11 ("presentation may write `CanvasItem` properties") would otherwise *permit* the Filter to write it. Route to ux-designer/art-director (HR-6 owners) and lead-programmer (manifest).

  ## 2. Pixel-perfect vs smooth, and `tick_alpha`
  
  **The real trade, stated honestly.** Let `S` = 3 at 1080p.
  
  | Option | World scroll | Hero sprite on screen | Cost |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | **A. Camera on integer base px** | steps of `S` screen px; at `v` = 1.6 px/tick the pattern is 2,1,2,2,1 base px = 6,3,6,6,3 screen px — visible velocity jitter (±30 %); at 144 fps render it is 1,1,0,1,1,0 → zero-scroll frames | rock-steady (same grid as camera) | judder on high-contrast scrolling edges — in 3/4 oblique the worst case is vertical scroll across the 72 px wall faces' horizontal lines |
  | **B. Camera at 1/`S` base px (1 screen px), sprites rounded to integer base px in world space** | 1-screen-px granularity: 4.8 screen px/frame → 5,5,5,4,5 — reads smooth; 2.4 px/frame at 144 fps | wobbles ≤ ±0.5 base px (±1.5 screen px) against the frame, because `round(pos) − cam` varies while `cam` tracks `pos` | hero wobble; one more implementation seam under pass A (below) |
  | C. Fully sub-pixel camera and sprites | smooth | smooth | **forbidden** — sprites off the base grid (8.4: "sub-pixel blur is forbidden everywhere"; grid misalignment for human-built geometry) |
  
  **Recommendation: build B with the quantum as a knob — `camera.quantum_base_px ∈ {1, 1/S}` — and ship MVP on `1` (option A).** One code path, one A/B. Why the integer default is cheaper here than in a bright pixel game: judder is perceived on *static detail scrolling past*, and in this game almost everything beyond the cone is unlit at a 1–3 % floor (Principle 1, 8.11 flag 1). The lit region travels *with* the player. Expect the playtest to tolerate A; switch to `1/S` if testers name the stutter.
  
  **Invariants that hold under either quantum:**
  1. **Every sprite and tile is placed at integer base px in world space by presentation code** (round the interpolated read-view position). This — not the project setting — is the grid guarantee; keep `snap_2d_transforms_to_pixel` (8.4) as a safety net, never as the mechanism.
  2. **The beam's hand point uses the same rounded position as the sprite** plus the per-facing integer offset (5.6), or the cone detaches from the hand by 1 base px at the moment the rounding flips. The light itself rotates freely off-grid (5.6, 8.4 — light is the one thing allowed off the grid).
  3. **The camera quantises after smoothing**: `pose = round_to(cam_smoothed, quantum)`. Smoothing in floats, display in quanta.
  
  **Implementation by render pass (both must work — pass A vs B is undecided until 8.12 step 2).**
  - *Pass A* (`SubViewport` 640×360 scaled by the container): the viewport can only render whole base px, so a 1/`S` camera needs the classic trick — render the `SubViewport` one base px larger per axis (641×361) and offset the container by `−frac × S` screen px in the root canvas (a position of −1/3 base px in the root's 640×360 logical space is exactly −1 screen px at 3×) `[UNV-4.3]`. The root viewport must **not** have `snap_2d_transforms_to_pixel` on (it would snap the fractional container offset away); only the `SubViewport` does. Float: 3 × 0.3333333 = 0.9999999 — no texel straddles a boundary; acceptable, verify in the spike.
  - *Pass B* (`SubViewport` at window size, `size_2d_override = 640×360`): the camera can simply sit at `k/S` base px; lights are already at window resolution. **Spike check (add to 8.12 step 2):** does `snap_2d_transforms_to_pixel` snap in *override* (logical base) px or in *render* px? NOT SOURCEABLE. If render px, sprites can land 1/3 base px off the tile grid — exactly what 8.4 forbids for human-built geometry — and invariant 1 above must be the only snapping in play (turn the project setting off under pass B).
  
  **`tick_alpha` application.**
  - Presentation reads `prev`/`curr` from the position owner's read view and draws the sprite at `lerp(prev, curr, tick_alpha)` **rounded to base px**, every render frame (CR-10, D.3). The **visual sprite is not a child of the physics body**: the body (whoever owns it per Open Question 3) is `visible = false` or a sibling; the sprite is a presentation node fed by the read view. This is how 60 Hz sim renders cleanly at 144 fps and how the kernel's "presentation never causes a tick" (CR-11) stays true for the camera.
  - The camera *target* is the **unrounded** interpolated position plus the lead; smoothing runs at render `dt`; `tick_alpha`'s clamp at 1 means a hitch holds the pose and the camera holds with it (no extrapolation — D.3 calls extrapolation a Pillar 1 hazard).
  - **Turn Godot's own 2D physics interpolation OFF** (`physics/common/physics_interpolation`, `Node2D.physics_interpolation_mode` `[UNV-4.3]`; the reference only records the 3D rearchitecture in 4.7). Two interpolators (engine + `tick_alpha`) on one sprite double-interpolate and fight the rounding.
  - **One-frame camera lag is the classic Godot defect**: if `Camera2D` applies its scroll before the sprite is repositioned in the same frame (tree order / `process_callback` `[UNV-4.3]`), the hero oscillates by one frame of motion. Mitigation: one camera script, all `Camera2D` built-ins off (`position_smoothing_enabled = false`, no drag margins, no limits), `process_priority` after every presentation reader, and `force_update_scroll()` if needed `[UNV-4.3]`; or write the `SubViewport.canvas_transform` directly and skip `Camera2D`. **Spike acceptance test:** walk at constant `v` for 3 s with the camera locked (`lookahead = 0`, `tau = 0`); the sprite's screen position must not change by more than the rounding wobble of the chosen quantum. If `v` is a whole number of base px per tick and quantum is 1, it must not change at all.
  
  **Route to Player Controller:** if MVP ships on quantum 1, pick speeds in **whole base px per tick** (2 px/tick = 2.5 tiles/s walk; 3 px/tick = 3.75 tiles/s run) so straight-line motion is uniform (6 or 9 screen px/frame at 3×, 60 fps — uniform stepping reads as smooth). Diagonals and the smoothed camera break the uniformity anyway, which is why quantum `1/S` stays as the upgrade.

  ## 3. Moving ship
  
  **Recommendation: the interior camera is in the ship-local frame — the ship appears still, everything outside it moves.** Framed as **input to Ship Body & Interior Physics** (kernel Open Question 3, C.3 #6), not a decision I can make.
  
  Arguments, in order of weight:
  1. **The grid.** Ship tiles must sit on integer base px relative to the camera. If the ship drifts at a non-integer world speed, the entire interior judders; if it rotates, human-built geometry rotates — forbidden (8.4: off-grid rotation "tolerated for horrors and web only, forbidden for anything human-built"). Even a camera that counter-rotates risks 1-px seams between tiles from composed-rotation float error. So: **ship rotation is never rendered in the interior view.** Cleanest form: the interior scene lives at origin; the ship's world pose is sim data consumed only by the exterior/transit view and by Ship Body's physics. Then the camera needs no frame conversion at all.
  2. **Ownership already points this way.** C.3 #2 proposes Ship Body owns *ship-local* kinematic position; intents are ship-local (CR-4). A ship-local camera reads one block in one frame.
  3. **Honesty.** Inside a hull there is nothing to see outside; the ship's motion is felt as P1 consequences — props sliding, the crewmate pushed on acceleration (the moving-platform problem C.3 #6 names). A still frame with a shoved body is more honest and more frightening than a drifting frame. Ship Body can apply ship acceleration as a uniform pseudo-force in the ship frame; flag this as a recommendation to that GDD.
  4. **Co-op.** Everyone inside shares one still frame; no per-client wobble from host physics.
  
  **Pod transit (2.5, the only exterior view).** Pod-local frame, same rule: the pod is still, the void and filaments stream past under its running lights; the derelict "resolves out of the dark" because the pod's light reaches it, not because the camera moves. Lead ≈ 1–2 tiles along the heading (the pod's lights point outward — same logic as the beam). HR-3: the suit HUD persists through transit unchanged (7.0: "the suit does not know it is in a pod"). MVP's hop is abstracted (16. Web Map & Transit), so this view may be seconds long or skipped; design it as the same solver with a different follow target and a `transit` param set.
  
  **Exterior views otherwise: none** (2.5 says so). No orbit, no free cam.
  
  **Flag for VS-era detach** (13. Modular Ship Assembly: "detach spawns a new body"): a cut-away section drifting through the interior view would be rotating human geometry. Propose: a detached section translates without rotation in the interior view (whole base px per tick) and is unlit beyond the ambient floor, so the honest dark swallows it within a few tiles. Its rotation, if any, is sim-only until it is seen from a pod.

  ## 4. Integer zoom-out step (8.1)
  
  **Policy recommendation: offer it as hold-to-zoom (release returns), available in every world state including breach, with the cut instant (an integer step cannot tween — a non-integer intermediate is forbidden by 8.1).** Knobs: `camera.zoom_out_enabled`, `camera.zoom_out_hold` (hold vs toggle; default hold), `camera.zoom_out_in_breach` (default true, for the playtest).
  
  Why hold, not toggle: 8.2 chose the tight frame deliberately ("the user chose difficulty over comfort… a tight frame serves dread"); a toggle lets players live zoomed-out and the decision evaporates. Hold keeps zoom-out a *survey posture*. Why allowed in breach: removing a tool at the moment the player wants to see the trail cross the room is "the screen fighting the player" (2.8); and 2.8's rule that the cone stays the brightest thing in frame survives zoom — the cone shrinks with everything else. Chart table only: no — 2.11 is an unlit UI screen, not the world camera; the need 8.2 names (a machine room 8 × 6 tiles that does not fit) is a world-view need.
  
  **What it does to readability.** At 1080p the step is 3× → 2× (sprites at 67 %): 2 px bands become 4 screen px, 1 px separators 2 screen px — inside 5.6 tier 2's guarantee (marks survive 50 %). At 1440p (4× → 2×) and 2160p (6× → 3×) sprites are at 50 %: bands 4 and 6 screen px. Tier 3 surface detail is lost by design; in-world gauges are "readable by needle angle and zone band alone" at 100 % (7.0) and are **not** guaranteed at 50–67 % — so zoom-out is for seeing *where* a trail is going, not for reading a gauge. The HUD does not change (8.1: "the frame grows, the world does not" — HUD scale is independent).
  
  **Implementation constraint — the part that matters most.** Zoom-out must change the viewport's **logical size**, never `Camera2D.zoom`. `Camera2D.zoom = 2/3` inside a 640×360 canvas would render sprites at 0.667 with nearest sampling — dropped pixels, the exact breakage 8.1 exists to prevent.
  - Pass B: `size_2d_override = 960×540` (1080p) or `1280×720` (1440p/2160p). Clean.
  - Pass A: `SubViewport.size = 960×540` and the container drawn at an exact composed 2× (a 2/3 scale in the 640×360 root logical space × the root's 3× stretch) `[UNV-4.3]`; `SubViewportContainer.stretch_shrink` goes the wrong way (it shrinks the viewport). Add to the spike: "a 2 px band measures exactly 4 screen px, with no seam, at zoom-out under pass A".
  
  **Flag — the zoomed field differs by display.** 1080p zoom-out shows 960×540 base = 20 × 11.25 tiles; 1440p and 2160p show 1280×720 = 26.67 × 15 tiles (there is no integer 3× at 1440p: 2560/3 is not an integer). The GDD must choose: *fixed field (960×540) with letterbox on 1440p/2160p*, or *display-dependent field*. I lean display-dependent, on 8.1's own argument ("more *screen* is not more *information* when the dark is honest") and because 8.1 already accepts `aspect = expand` giving ultrawide more field. Note for the spike: the 1080p zoomed field (20 × 11.25 tiles) is exactly the 20 × 11 spike room of 8.12 step 2 and exactly the room 8.2 said does not fit at default.

  ## 5. Cuts: re-wake, pod arrival, death
  
  **2.10 specifies a cut, not a fade, and the dark is the bridge.** "Sources go dark one by one… until ambient collapses to 0 %… then a cut to the cryo-bay lamp guttering on." The last frame before the transition is diegetically black, so a hard cut costs nothing and a fade would add an authored screen effect the bible rejected ("a death screen with text — rejected… the lighting already says it"). **Rule: the frame never fades; light fades.** The only fades anywhere are 7.3's menu fades (80–120 ms), on the chart-table layer, not the world.
  
  **What the kernel's `LOADING` implies for the camera:**
  - `request_restore` → `LOADING`: tick stops, queue flushed with `Dropped(RELOAD)`, blocks are replaced wholesale (CR-18). **Read views are undefined mid-restore, so the world `SubViewport` is hidden (or its last frame is black) from `→ LOADING` until the first `RUNNING` P5 notification.** The HUD persists (it is screen-space; HR-3) but reads nothing new until then.
  - **Camera state is never persisted** (it is presentation; no block). On every `LOADING → RUNNING` transition the camera **teleports**: `cam := target`, smoothing state and lead reset, jolt cleared. Otherwise the camera pans from the death site (or from (0,0)) to the cryo bay — a false statement about geography, and an un-asked-for cinematic.
  - **Flag to kernel / Save-Load:** owners of moving state must set `previous := current` in `post_restore()`, or the first frames after a load interpolate from a stale `previous` and every sprite slides. This is a one-line contract worth adding to CR-10's "owners expose previous and current".
  - Re-wake framing (2.2): identical lighting, only objects differ. The camera is placed deterministically from the spawn pod, so the room frames identically every wake — "I know this room" is partly the camera's doing.
  
  **MVP vs VS-A.** MVP death is the Save/Load death stub (reload last save). Sequence: death event → world goes black via the lighting state (presentation; honest) → `request_restore` → `LOADING` (black) → `RUNNING` → hard cut in with the camera already placed. The 2.10 extinguish sequence ("lasting seconds") is VS-A's Failure Contract & Re-wake; whether the sim is `RUNNING` during it (horrors still moving in the dark) or `PAUSED` is that GDD's call — the camera only needs "teleport on `→ RUNNING`".
  
  **Pod arrival.** C.3 #8: `pod_hop` applies data atomically, Transit goes `ARRIVING`, a `PROJECTOR` builds content and reports ready, the Transit hook completes arrival. **The cut is keyed on the Transit state change (arrival-complete notification), never on a timer**, and the camera teleports to the pod-local or ship-local target with a reset. Transit → interior is a hard cut on dock; the "spike" of 2.5 (the wreck resolving) happens in the light before the cut, not in the cut. If the `PROJECTOR` takes longer than the transit presentation, hold on the transit view (black void, pod lights) — it is already the loading screen.

  ## 6. Risks for a solo first-timer, and the simplest camera that avoids them
  
  **Risks (each with the mitigation already implied above):**
  1. **`Camera2D` built-ins** (position smoothing, drag margins, limits) do not quantise and fight the grid; plus the one-frame camera-vs-sprite ordering lag `[UNV-4.3]`. → All built-ins off; one script owns the pose; constant-velocity test in the spike.
  2. **Double interpolation / double snapping**: engine physics interpolation + `tick_alpha` + `snap_2d_transforms_to_pixel` + a fractional camera → shimmering tiles and 1-px seams. → One mechanism: presentation rounds positions in code; engine interpolation off; project snap is a net. Spike check for pass B's snap semantics.
  3. **Zoom via `Camera2D.zoom`** → non-integer sprites. → Viewport logical size only; "2 px band = exactly 4 screen px" test.
  4. **Camera smoothing across loads and hops** → pans across the map. → Teleport on `LOADING → RUNNING` and Transit arrival; camera never in a save.
  5. **Ship in world frame** → judder and rotated tiles. → Interior at origin (Q3), routed to Ship Body.
  6. **Shake as per-frame random offset** → sub-pixel, continuous, Attention-coupled. → Discrete integer-px jolt on P1 events only; camera on the anchor list.
  7. **Hardcoded 320×180 half-extents** → wrong the day `aspect = expand` (8.1 post-spike ask) or zoom-out lands. → Read viewport extents every frame.
  8. **Camera logic inside a Node** → untestable. → Pure solver.
  
  **The simplest shape I would build:**
  - `CameraSolver` (RefCounted, no Node): `solve(input: {target_pos, aim_dir, beam_on, dt, viewport_half_extents, jolt_events}, state, params: CameraParams) → {pose_base_px, new_state}`. Deterministic, headless-testable in gdUnit4 (6.2.x on 4.7.2 per the kernel GDD's engine note — and Node-free means no orphan-node exit 101). Tests: zero overshoot for a step input; lag = `v × τ` within tolerance; crewmate never exits the inner box for any aim; quantisation to `quantum`; teleport resets; jolt decays to zero within `jolt_decay_s`; identical output at 60 and 144 fps for the same wall time.
  - `CameraParams` (Resource) holding every knob in this report — designer-tunable, no code.
  - `WorldCamera` (one thin Node inside the `SubViewport`): subscribes as `PRESENTATION` (CR-10), reads the position read view + `tick_alpha` + local raw aim, calls the solver, writes the pose. Never submits, never writes a block, never touches the HUD.
  - Zoom = one function that resizes the viewport's logical size by integer step and tells the solver the new extents.
  
  **Defer (and for how long):**
  - *Cinematic camera*: never — the anti-pillar "no cutscenes" (game-concept) means it does not exist; 2.10 is a lighting event, not a camera move.
  - *Multiple cameras / split-screen*: permanently — co-op is online listen-server (VS-B); there is no local multiplayer.
  - *Free-look pan* (Zomboid right-drag): defer indefinitely; the aim-lead is the deliberate replacement and it keeps the frame tied to the light you are spending.
  - *Spectating a dead crewmate* (VS-B): attach the same solver to another crewmate's read view; no free cam. Not MVP.
  - *Camera rotation, smooth zoom, sanity-driven camera effects*: forbidden, not deferred.
