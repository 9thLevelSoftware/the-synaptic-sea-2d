  ## Item detail
  
  **1. Dark-by-default vs frantic-breach readability.** The bible has done most of the work: §4.6 makes the HUD flat, unlit and on a ground darker than world mid-grey so "world emission never competes with UI", and §2.8's readability rule (2–3 s sweep, trails at full saturation, cone brightest) governs the world layer. Three gaps. (a) §3.5 says the beam cone is "always the brightest thing in frame"; Paper White (L*≈89) sits beside the beam core (L*≈92) and HUD text over a lit region is ≈ 1:1, so the HUD layer must be explicitly exempt and never composited over live world without an opaque ground. (b) §3.5 #5 says a trail "must always out-contrast the panel it crosses" with no number; under the red sweep at 1 % ambient that needs a floor. (c) A brightness/gamma slider is the first control a low-vision player reaches for, and it hands out free light, erasing Principle 1's spend-light economy and Pillar 3's exposure. The bible's own structure gives the fix: the web and horrors are drawn only in direct light (§1 P3, §3.2), so raising the *ambient floor* reveals hull geometry (honest and static) and nothing the exposure economy protects.
  
  **2. What must be screen-space.** Test used: *does the player need this reading somewhere no in-world instrument can exist, inside a reaction window shorter than a walk to a panel?* Suit O₂ (lethal, personal, needed in the pod and on derelicts), suit integrity/health, the equipped tool and its state, the Alarm warning mark, and the room-local sensing the suit carries (temperature/pressure where the player stands) pass and must be screen-space. This is reconcilable with the fiction: the HUD *is* the suit's visor instrument in the home-ship dialect (§3.4), so it is diegetic-in-fiction and screen-space-in-render. Ship-level loop totals, graph overview and the web map may be diegetic stations (the Barotrauma pattern); the ship-keeper role wants somewhere to stand. Attention and own sanity: the bible rejects meters for both (§1 P3 design test; §4.6 "sanity is not a meter"); the concept's Pillar 3 rejects only a *hidden* meter. For sighted players the in-world channel is sufficient by design; for players who cannot resolve the cone-rim filaments the concept's "audio-first" tells must become a requirement (HR-9), and for players who can use neither, an off-by-default indicator is an art-direction fork, not a pillar violation (Decision 1). Two flows are not yet covered anywhere: (i) the boarder away from the ship — if a home-ship loop fails while they are on a derelict, no trail is visible to them; whether they receive telemetry, and whether it is an instrument or an anchor, decides whether HR-3 gains a sixth item. (ii) "Heat" is in the concept's MVP graph (O₂/heat/power) but §3.3 has no heat loop (O₂, Power, Coolant, Fuel, Integrity); whether heat is the coolant loop or a room-temperature P1 consequence decides whether it ever has a gauge.
  
  **3. Lying instruments vs accessibility.** The bible's answer to "how do I know it is lying" is a violet gleam on the glass (§4.4 stage 1; §4.6: "the only violet that ever reaches the screen-space layer"). For a deutan/tritan player violet reads blue or grey; for a low-vision player a gleam on a needle edge is sub-perceptual; for an achromat Violet core and the Graphite-lit bezel are the same lightness (§4.5). The bible's §4.0 principle 2 ("hue is never the only channel") is broken by its own stage-1 design. The fix is not to make the lie louder but to give it a motion and sound signature under the same "subtle allowed, absent not" rule, and to recognise that the falsification paths these players actually use are the modality-independent ones: physical consequence (Pillar 1 lists "air hisses", i.e. sound), a second instrument, time, a crewmate. For them, "anchors that never shift" must include things the bible has not listed: HUD frame *geometry, position and scale*; input mapping; UI typography, scale and accessibility settings; menu/pause/settings layers; the speaker indicator and text chat; the caption stream *if* world sound is ground truth (open question); haptics driven by P1 events; and the character sprite's physical animation state (laboured breathing, stumbling) if game-designer confirms animation counts as "sprite" for anchor purposes, which would give solo colour-blind players a free, honest O₂ falsification. The TD's true-value debug overlay must never be exposed as an accessibility feature; it would delete Pillar 4.
  
  **4. Hue-coded loops beyond §4.7.** §4.7 is a static-pair table; three dynamic gaps. (a) Reduced motion: §4.7 names motion as *primary* for Fuel vs Alarm, and loop direction exists only as animated chevrons/dash-pulse, so a reduced-motion toggle must keep direction as static pointing shapes, replace the sweep with a static mark + sound + HUD glyph, and never suppress trail propagation (P1 information). (b) Sound: §4.0 says "most" semantic colours have a cue; for the five loops it must be *all*, with running and failing tones distinct per loop. (c) Filters: a global daltonization/hue-remap shader can push sane colours into the reserved violet/flesh bands or mask the leak; colour-blind support must be shape/pattern emphasis plus per-element alternates entered in §4.7, never a full-frame remap. The Tell (§2.7) is motion and count at the rim, not hue, which is right, but it is fine detail; its audio component must start no later than its visual.
  
  **5. Text and contrast.** Passes on UI ground `#1C2128`: Paper White 12.6:1; Tungsten fixture 10.9:1; O₂ Cyan ≈ 9:1; Hull Grey ≈ 5.3:1. Fails: Alarm Red 3.3:1 as text (fine as a glyph at ≥ 3:1); Violet core 2.9:1 (fringe `#B07BE6` at 5.3:1 is the only violet admissible on screen); Hull Grey on the Graphite-lit bezel `#5C6670` 1.9:1, so readings sit on UI ground, never on the bezel; Paper White vs Tungsten 1.2:1, so focus and selection cannot be colour alone. §3.4 wants readings "thin, light, moving"; thin needs a floor. HD pixel art implies a bitmap font, so UI-scale steps must be integer multiples or the HUD needs a vector face; that is a Section 7 typography decision for art-director, with this UX constraint attached.
  
  **6. Gamepad.** No cursor over diegetic instruments: proximity focus with an interaction volume larger than the sprite, one contextual action, and (because 3/4-oblique gauges are small) a screen-space *mirror card* showing the focused instrument's reading at HUD size. The mirror shows what the instrument shows (same lie, same gleam, same motion tell); it is a magnifier, not a truth channel. Web map: snap-to-node since filaments are off-grid (§3.3). Beam aiming on the right stick; beam shape is identity (§3.2) and must not change with input device. Destructive actions (cut a section) hold-to-confirm. "Partial gamepad" must be enumerated per flow.
  
  **7. Co-op.** The human channel protects voice, beam colour and beam shape. The crewmate sanity mark (§3.2, §4.4) is hue-only and class (c) is lethal by user decision. Beam shape cannot be modulated (anchor), but the fringe lies *outside* sprite and beam pixels, so it can carry motion without touching anchors, and the suit itself can emit a real P1 sound (a medical chirp) that is honest by construction and crosses the human channel whenever they speak. Remote crewmate vitals on your HUD would be a class-(b) instrument and would undercut "crewmates are instruments"; not recommended by default. The speaker indicator (name + beam-shape glyph + stripe count), text chat and any world-space ping are human-channel objects and must be declared anchors; text chat and pings are also the human channel for deaf/HoH and no-mic crews.
  
  ## Hard requirements (testable; each cites what it reconciles)
  
  - **HR-1 HUD exemption.** The HUD renders on its own layer, excluded from world glow, ambient, occlusion and the §3.5 "beam brightest" rule; it never dims, fades or changes opacity in response to world light, Attention or sanity. *Test:* at 0 % ambient and over the beam core, HUD text measures ≥ 4.5:1 against its own ground. (§3.5, §4.6)
  - **HR-2 Opaque ground.** Every HUD reading and all HUD text sits on an opaque UI-ground plate (`#1C2128`); no reading is drawn over live world pixels or on the bezel. *Test:* no Hull Grey glyph ever appears on `#5C6670`. (§4.6; 1.9:1)
  - **HR-3 Screen-space set.** Suit O₂, suit integrity, equipped tool + state, the Alarm warning mark, and suit-sensed local temperature/pressure are screen-space at all times, including pod transit and boarding. *Test:* all five visible with no in-world instrument in frame. (Pillar 1 safety; §3.4)
  - **HR-4 Alarm Red is never text.** On UI it appears only as the warning-mark glyph (≥ 3:1), always paired with the mark's shape and a sound. (§4.6; 3.3:1)
  - **HR-5 Stage-1 lie is tri-modal.** Any class-(b) reading in the screen-space layer or a mirror card carries, in addition to the fringe-violet gleam (`#B07BE6`, never core), one defined motion signature (specified once, e.g. a needle that settles with none of the micro-jitter honest needles show, or a sub-1 Hz glass flicker) and one sound signature. Subtle allowed, absent not. *Test:* with hue removed and with audio removed in turn, a trained tester identifies the lying instrument above chance. (§4.0 p2, §4.4, §4.6)
  - **HR-6 Anchor list extended.** Never shift under hallucination: HUD frame geometry, position and scale; input mapping; UI typography, scale and accessibility settings; menu/pause/settings layers (no violet or flesh, ever); speaker indicator, text chat and pings; haptics driven by P1 events. *Test:* at sanity floor, a diff of these layers against the sane state is empty. (§1 Corollary)
  - **HR-7 Class-(c) victim tell is tri-modal.** The low-sanity crewmate mark carries motion in the fringe (outside sprite and beam pixels) and an audible P1 cue in proximity, in addition to violet. *Test:* under achromatopsia simulation a tester identifies the below-threshold crewmate before contact. (§3.2, §4.4, §4.7; lethal class c)
  - **HR-8 Reduced motion preserves the backbone.** With reduced motion on: flow direction remains as static pointing chevrons/dashes; the Alarm sweep becomes a static mark + sound + HUD glyph; trail propagation is never suppressed; HR-5 motion signatures may slow but not vanish. (§4.7 motion-primary pairs; Pillar 1)
  - **HR-9 Loop audio is complete.** All five loops have a distinct running sound and a distinct failing sound; every Tell has an audio component starting no later than its visual. Routed to audio-director as requirements. (§4.0 "most" → all; concept "audio-first")
  - **HR-10 No global hue remap.** Colour-blind support is shape/pattern emphasis plus per-element alternates recorded in §4.7; no full-frame daltonization shader. (§4.4 reserved bands; §1 Corollary leak)
  - **HR-11 Focus is a shape.** Keyboard/gamepad focus and selection change bezel geometry (thicker chamfer, underline or bracket) in addition to the Tungsten accent. (1.2:1 vs Paper White)
  - **HR-12 Minimums (provisional; 1080p at 100 % scale).** Critical readings (O₂, integrity numerals, alarm label) ≥ 24 px font size; body/secondary ≥ 18 px; needle and glyph stroke ≥ 2 px; text ≥ 4.5:1 and glyphs ≥ 3:1 against immediate ground; UI scale 100–200 % in steps that keep bitmap fonts at integer multiples (or a vector HUD face). Every critical needle has a numeral beside it. Numbers pending accessibility-specialist's tier. (§3.4 "thin, light")
  - **HR-13 HUD flicker ceiling.** No HUD element flashes above 3 Hz; no HUD shake, blur, chromatic aberration or scale change under any state (extends §2.8's rejected red vignette to the UI layer). (§1 Corollary frame anchor)
  - **HR-14 Gamepad completeness.** Section 7 (or `design/ux/interaction-patterns.md`) enumerates which flows are gamepad-complete at MVP and which are keyboard-only, with text chat the only permitted keyboard-only item in the core loop. Diegetic instruments: proximity focus, one contextual action, mirror card at HUD size carrying the same reading and the same HR-5 tells. Web map: snap-to-node. Destructive actions (cut a section, jettison): hold-to-confirm on all input devices. (concept: partial gamepad)
  - **HR-15 Ambient-floor option is bounded.** If a low-vision ambient option exists it raises the floor to a provisional ceiling of ≤ 6 % (below the 10 % stable state; number pending the lighting spike), reveals hull geometry only (web, horrors, filaments remain drawn only in direct light per §1 P3), does not touch the Attention simulation, and is per-client. (Principle 1, Pillar 3)
  
  ## Recommendations
  
  - **R-1** Critical vitals (O₂, alarm mark) in a compact strip near screen-centre-bottom rather than a corner: the character is centred in 3/4 top-down and a corner HUD forces a 20°+ saccade during breach (Fitts / foveal argument). Full panel at the edge. See Decision 3.
  - **R-2** Confirm with game-designer that character *animation state* counts as "sprite" for anchor purposes; if so, suit O₂ depletion gains a physical, honest, colour-free tell (breathing, stumble), the cleanest solo falsification for colour-blind players.
  - **R-3** Hand-tool *readings* (scanner) are not covered by the anchor list; the bible anchors the tool sprite, not its display. Ask game-designer to classify them (likely instrument, per Pillar 4(d) "a second scanner").
  - **R-4** The web map and menus are already "a human instrument" that never lies (§2.11); state it explicitly in Section 7 so the player has one guaranteed-honest screen.
  - **R-5** Design a world-space ping as a human-channel anchor (deaf/HoH and no-mic crews); route to game-designer.
  - **R-6** Captions: if world audio is P1 ground truth, captions are a truth channel and should name sources precisely ("coolant line hissing, north"); if phantom audio can be hallucinated, captions transcribe the client's perception and audio needs its own leak equivalent to violet. Blocking question for deaf/HoH support, below.
  - **R-7** The TD's true-value debug overlay is never exposed as an accessibility option.

  ## Decisions for the user
  
  **1. Attention accessibility indicator** — the bible rejects an on-screen meter; the pillars reject only a hidden one. A deaf low-vision player has no Attention channel without this.
  - *Off-by-default a11y indicator (Recommended)* — a screen-space filament-count glyph available only under accessibility settings; honest; invisible in the default experience, so zero cost to §1 P3 for everyone else.
  - *Audio only* — Attention stays in-world and HR-9's audio-first tells are the accessibility channel; purer, but leaves one audience with nothing.
  
  **2. Crewmate sanity mark channel**
  - *World mark + redundancy (Recommended)* — keep the violet fringe, add fringe motion and a P1 suit sound (HR-7); preserves "sanity is not a meter".
  - *Truthful crew-roster flag* — a screen-space anchor showing "below threshold" per crewmate; clearest for low vision, but it is a sanity meter of someone else and weakens "crewmates are instruments".
  
  **3. Critical-vitals placement**
  - *Centre-bottom strip (Recommended)* — O₂ and alarm within ~15° of the centred character; full panel at the edge.
  - *Corner panel only* — conventional and a cleaner frame; costs a saccade during breach.
  
  **4. Low-vision ambient option**
  - *Bounded floor boost (Recommended)* — HR-15 as written; hull-only reveal, no simulation effect.
  - *No brightness option* — purist; defers low-vision players to HUD scale and audio alone; risks failing the accessibility tier.
