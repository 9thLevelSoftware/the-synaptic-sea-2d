  ## Framing A: "The Ship Cannot Lie" (one truth under every instrument)
  
  > The player never meets this system. What they meet is a promise it keeps: there is only one ship. A gauge in the scrubber bay can read green while frost climbs the elbow beside it, and the player has to decide which to believe. The answer is always the same, and it is always available. The gauge and the frost both read the same state, and only one of them passed through the player's mind on the way to the screen. Nothing the player sees, hears or imagines can reach back and change what the ship is doing.
  >
  > The anchor moment is in the cryo bay or the first sortie. The player has "fixed" a scrubber and the needle has settled. Then their breath fogs, and they realise the room is still getting colder. The horror is personal: *I am the thing that is broken.* The comfort is structural: *the ship is not.* Each time a player checks and the world turns out to be right, they learn to trust the world more and their own eyes less. That trust is the only thing this system gives the player, and one desync between what the ship shows and what the ship does would break it.
  
  **Pillar text it serves:**
  - P1: *"Pipes leak, frost forms, air hisses; the physical world is ground truth. Only instruments and perception can be wrong."* and *"A pipe may look fixed, but the room still gets cold, the air still thins, the pressure still drops."*
  - P4(b): *"Broken reads as fixed, fixed as broken; the lie extends from the gauge to the sprite. The physical consequence (P1) stays honest."*
  - The concept's resolution of the P1/P4 tension: *"the physical ship is ground truth; instruments and perception are what can be wrong. The tension is the game."*
  
  **Strengths:**
  - It is the exact moment the onboarding names: *"watch the gauge agree with the pipe."*
  - It can be tested at MVP.
  - It is precise about what the kernel contributes.
  
  **Weakness:** it says nothing about co-op, which is the second half of this system's reason to exist.

  ## Framing B: "If It Has Your Friend's Light, It Is Your Friend" (identity behind every act)
  
  > Every act in the ship has a name attached to it. When a valve turns, a section is welded or a cutter is raised, the ship knows which crew member did it, and it checks whether they were able to. That is why a sane player can never fake the web's violence. Harm to a crewmate is only accepted from someone whose sanity has actually dropped below the threshold. It is also why the human channel is something you can stake your life on: the thing in the corridor with your friend's beam, the amber cone and the three stripes, is your friend.
  >
  > The anchor moment: a crewmate renders as a horror at the end of a corridor. Its beam is the right shape and colour, and the player has to choose whether to lower the cutter. Later, when the crew tells the story, nobody blames the person who swung. The game does not let them, because the ship recorded who was below the line and the web takes the credit.
  
  **Pillar text it serves:**
  - P1 human channel: *"A monster that speaks in your friend's voice, or carries their torch, is your friend."*
  - P4(c): *"harm to a crewmate is only possible while the attacker's sanity is verifiably below threshold, so a sane player cannot fake it."*
  - Anti-Pillar: *"NOT intentional PvP… attributed to the web. Protects relatedness."*
  
  **Strengths:**
  - It gives real meaning to "peer identity on every command from day one".
  - It speaks to Fellowship (aesthetic #3), the dynamic most likely to go wrong under the lethal-harm decision.
  
  **Weaknesses:**
  - It can't be tested until VS-B.
  - It over-credits the kernel. Voice and beam rendering belong to systems 34 and 20, and the harm gate is enforced by Interaction and Sanity. The kernel only carries the identity and the authority check.
  - As the headline it would push a co-op promise to the front of a foundation that ships single-player first (VS-A is the fallback product).

  ## Framing C: "The Ship Keeps Breathing Without You Watching" (a steady pulse under the dread)
  
  > The ship is alive because the crew keeps it alive, minute by minute, and the beat of each minute is honest. Air thins at the same rate whether you are looking at the scrubber or not. Frost spreads whether your light is on it or not. Attention rises on its own count, not when the plot needs it. Under the dread is a steady, indifferent pulse. Dread comes from that indifference: the ship's state does not wait for the player and does not perform for them.
  >
  > The anchor moment: the player comes back from a sortie to a home-ship they left stable, and finds the coolant loop has drifted exactly as far as the time they spent away would predict. The failure is fair because it is consistent, and that makes it feel like the player's fault rather than the game's.
  
  **Pillar text it serves:**
  - Core Fantasy: *"The ship is alive because we keep it alive, minute by minute."*
  - P2: *"Every breach is preceded by a tell a careful player could have perceived."*
  - P1 design test: *"a hidden stat vs. a visibly leaking pipe → the pipe."*
  
  **Strengths:** it honestly reflects the fixed tick.
  
  **Weaknesses:**
  - Determinism and steady time are mostly the Life-Support Graph's job (fill rates, propagation) and Attention's job (tell timing). The kernel only supplies the beat.
  - It is the weakest link to this system's two stated reasons for existing. Treat it as supporting colour, not the frame.
