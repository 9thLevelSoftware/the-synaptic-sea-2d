  ### Direction A — "Every Pipe Is a Sentence" (serves Pillar 1 directly)
  
  **Visual rule:** If it works, you can see it working; if it fails, you can see exactly where.
  
  **Mood / atmosphere:** A warm, hand-maintained workshop floating in a cold indifferent void. Project Zomboid's lived-in clutter meets Satisfactory's legible flows. The ship is the only honest thing in the universe; the horror is that the honesty erodes (Pillar 4). Interior reads as "ours" the moment power is on.
  
  **Shape language:** Hard separation of two vocabularies. Human/mechanical = rectilinear hull plates, cylindrical pipes, round valves and gauges, rounded fittings, 90- and 45-degree runs that respect the 3/4 grid (vertical runs on walls, horizontal on floor). Web = filamentous, branching, never a straight line, never a right angle. The player's rule: anything organic is not yours. Salvaged modules from other ships keep the mechanical vocabulary but carry a different manufacturer "dialect" (plate rhythm, bolt spacing) so welded additions visibly read as grafts.
  
  **Supporting principles with design tests:**
  1. *State lives in the sprite, not the icon.* Every system has a visible moving part and a visible medium (fluid, spark, airflow). Test: a pump with a green/red status LED vs a pump with a turning rotor and visible fluid in its pipe → rotor and fluid. The LED may exist, but it is never the only tell.
  2. *Failure is a trail, not a flag.* Damage renders as a path the eye can follow back to source: leak puddles, scorch, frost, pressure mist, in the loop's hue. Test: a flashing "COOLANT LOW" warning vs a frosted pipe elbow with a slow drip → the frosted elbow (the warning is the gauge that can lie; the frost is the body that cannot, at least at high sanity).
  3. *Light is emission, and emission is exposure.* With power off, the ship is lit only by the glow of its own loops, so the player reads their ship in the dark by what is running. Test: a flat dark "unpowered" ambient vs loops glowing in their own hue while the hull goes cold blue → the glowing loops. This resolves Pillar 1 vs 3: you can always read the loops, and every loop you run is a lantern the web can see.
  
  **Color philosophy:** Flow-coded. Each resource loop owns a hue and keeps it everywhere it appears: pipe medium, gauge, HUD readout, leak. Working proposal: oxygen cyan, power amber, coolant ice-blue, fuel orange-red, structure/integrity neutral grey, web attention violet-magenta (the one hue no human system uses). Interior under power: warm tungsten pools with soft falloff. Interior unpowered: cold blue-black steel, loops self-lit. Exterior/void: near-black with the web's violet filaments as the only chroma, brightening and reaching toward the player's light sources as attention rises (Pillar 3 made visible). Deeper rings darken and desaturate the ambient toward graphite rather than tinting it; saturated violet-magenta is reserved for filaments and for the sanity bleed below, so saturated violet on a pipe always means sanity, never location (Pillar 5 reads as denser filaments, not a purple room).
  *Sanity shift:* instruments drift first. Gauge needles and HUD readouts show readings that disagree with the body of the ship; at lower sanity the loop hues themselves begin to bleed toward violet at the edges of the pipe medium (independent of ambient), and flow animation stutters on pipes that are actually fine. *Anchor that never shifts:* hull geometry, character sprites, and the HUD frame itself. The player learns to trust their eyes over their instruments, then learns that even that trust has a floor. Accessibility coordination point for ux-designer (not decided here): hue coding must be redundant with shape/pattern (flow chevrons, pipe diameter) for colorblind players.
  
  **Pipeline cost:** Highest tile/prop state count (each system needs idle, running, failing, leaking, dead), lowest lighting complexity. Flow animation can be shader-driven rather than hand-framed (technical-artist call), which recovers most of the frame cost.

  ### Direction B — "The Dark Is the Default"
  
  **Visual rule:** Light is a resource you spend; everything you can see, you paid for.
  
  **Mood / atmosphere:** Barotrauma and Pandorum. Claustrophobic, flashlight-cone, oppressive black broken by hard pools of detail. The ship is a cave you keep partially lit. Dread comes from what the light does not reach and from the moment you must choose to turn it on (Pillars 2 and 3).
  
  **Shape language:** Heavy, angular industrial brutalism. Thick bulkheads, hard shadows, deep recesses that hide things. The web is not rendered as a shape at distance: it is hairline filaments that read as cracks in the black, only resolving into form when caught in the player's light, where it shows a faint oily iridescence. Horror by negative space.
  
  **Supporting principles with design tests:**
  1. *Darkness is honest; light is a decision.* Rooms are not fogged, they are unlit; the geometry is there and will be revealed by any source. Test: a vignette/fog-of-war mask vs true 2D occlusion from light sources with hard falloff → true occlusion.
  2. *Failing systems announce themselves by emitting.* Because the default is dark, any system in trouble must become a light source (sparks, heat glow, warning strobe). Test: a silent dark broken pump vs a dark pump throwing intermittent blue sparks that light the surrounding hull → the sparks. This is B's only route to Pillar 1 and is its structural risk: healthy systems are hard to read in the dark.
  3. *Attention is a reflection.* The web is seen only in the player's own light: the brighter you run, the more filaments resolve, the closer they appear. Test: an attention meter vs more filaments becoming visible in your light cone as attention rises → the filaments.
  
  **Color philosophy:** Near-monochrome cold base (blue-black steel, graphite) with exactly two warm accents: human light (tungsten amber) and alarm (red). The web has no hue of its own; it is absence, with an oily iridescence under direct light. Deeper rings do not change hue; they reduce ambient further and raise the iridescence (Pillar 5 as a darker, glossier dark).
  *Sanity shift:* the light lies. Your cone appears to reach further than it does, shadows render where no occluder exists, light temperature cools or warms without cause. Readouts flicker between two values. *Anchor that never shifts:* character sprites, hand tools, and the physical radius of what your light actually reveals when you walk into it.
  
  **Pipeline cost:** Lowest tile/prop variant count (dark hides repetition), highest lighting and normal-map load for high-res pixel art; feasible in Godot 4 2D lighting but it becomes the technical-artist's main workload, and co-op readability of a partner's dark ship section is a known cost.

  ### Direction C — "The Ship Grows a Nervous System"
  
  **Visual rule:** The deeper you go, the less your ship looks like a machine.
  
  **Mood / atmosphere:** Beyond the Aquila Rift and Stellaris's slow-reveal horror. Bodily uncanny, horror by accretion. The game begins clean-industrial and ends with a ship that has veins. Salvage from the inner web is better and wronger, so the player's own home becomes the escalating tell (Pillars 2 and 5).
  
  **Shape language:** A gradient indexed to depth. Outer-ring salvage is rectilinear, symmetrical, clean. Inner-ring salvage carries sinew-like cabling, bulbous asymmetric fittings, housings that look grown around their mechanism. Web filaments are drawn as neurons: nodes, dendritic branching, synaptic gaps that flash when attention fires, making the title literal. Pipes already read as veins, so Pillar 1's "body" metaphor is drawn, not implied.
  
  **Supporting principles with design tests:**
  1. *Salvage carries its depth with it.* Any module can be read at a glance for where in the web it came from by how organic its silhouette is. Test: a depth-tier number on the module tooltip vs the module visibly more organic → the shape.
  2. *Two axes, never one.* Depth drifts shape; sanity drifts color and motion. Kept on separate axes so the player can disentangle "this module is from deep" from "I am seeing things." Test: deep salvage that also pulses and hue-shifts at full sanity vs deep salvage that is organic in silhouette but painted in the same stable steel/loop palette until sanity drops → color-stable. This principle is what keeps C viable against Pillar 4.
  3. *The web fires, it does not swarm.* Attention is shown as synaptic pulses travelling along filaments toward the player, brightening at the nodes. Test: more enemies spawning vs the filament pulse frequency rising → the pulse.
  
  **Color philosophy:** Depth is carried by the web and the void, never by the ship. Outer ring: near-black void, sparse filaments. Inner rings: the void saturates with the web's own bioluminescence until the ambient chroma belongs to the web rather than to humans. Salvage modules and loop hues stay color-stable at every depth; only silhouette carries depth. The web hue must sit outside every loop hue: with a cyan oxygen line, the web takes acid chartreuse-green rather than cyan-green. Flesh tones (ochre, sick pink, bruise purple) appear nowhere in the world at full sanity, at any depth.
  *Sanity shift:* flesh is the sanity signal and only the sanity signal. Your own ship appears more organic than it is: pulse animation on static pipes, loop hues bleeding toward ochre and pink, fittings that seem to breathe. Because no depth palette ever uses flesh tones, a flesh-toned pipe always means sanity, never location. *Anchor that never shifts:* character sprites, HUD frame, and the geometric silhouette of modules you already own (sanity alters color and motion, never shape).
  
  **Pipeline cost:** Highest overall: two full prop/tile vocabularies (geometric and organic) plus blends between them, on top of system-state variants.
  
  ---
  
  ### Recommendation: Direction A — "Every Pipe Is a Sentence"
  
  Why: Pillar 1 is primary and A is built on it. Pillar 4 needs a baseline trustworthy enough that a lie is detectable, and A has the most stable baseline of the three. Co-op depends on shared readability ("the cyan line in the aft bay is leaking" has to work for a partner who is not looking at your HUD). For a solo first-time developer, A puts the cost into sprite states, which scale linearly and can be shader-assisted, rather than into a lighting pipeline that must work on every screen. A also absorbs the best of the others as sub-rules without becoming them: B's "emission is exposure" is already A's principle 3, and C's depth-indexed organic silhouette can be adopted later as a secondary shape rule for inner-ring salvage once the legible base exists.
  
  Pick B if the team decides Pillar 2/3 dread should outrank Pillar 1 legibility and is willing to carry a lighting-heavy pipeline. Pick C if the title's neural imagery and Pillar 5's escalation are meant to be the game's signature image and the budget for a second prop vocabulary is accepted.
  
  Trade-off flagged for creative-director: A's legibility and B's darkness are in direct tension on how readable a healthy, unlit system should be. That is a pillar-weighting decision (1 vs 2/3), not an art-bible one.
