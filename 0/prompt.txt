  This system can only promise that **the image is faithful**. It owns:

  ## Candidate A: "Nothing moves without a cause" (Recommended)
  
  *Indirect: the player never meets this system; they meet a stillness it keeps.*
  
  **When the ship is still, the screen is still.** The game teaches the player to read motion as evidence. A needle that shakes ±1 art pixel is a stressed hull warning them. A needle that glides slowly with nothing in the ship to explain it is lying. The four trim bands on a crewmate's helmet are who that is. All of this lives at the scale of one or two art pixels, so it only works if no pixel ever moves for a reason that isn't in the world. That means no shimmer from a non-integer scale, no camera crawl from rounding, and no actor jittering one pixel against the floor as the camera follows. Every pixel that changes was changed by something in the world.
  
  The anchor moment is on the first sortie. The player stands in a dead scrubber bay with their beam on a wall gauge. They stop walking and the frame stops with them. Then the needle moves. Because nothing else on screen moved, the player knows the motion belongs to the ship (or to their own mind), not to the screen. They lean in to see whether it shakes or glides, and that judgement is the game. The comfort is structural: *if I saw it move, something moved it.*
  
  **Pillars served.**
  - *Dread Before Chaos*: "Every breach is preceded by a tell a careful player could have perceived (sound, light, gauge drift, hull stress)." Gauge drift is only perceivable if render noise can't fake it or hide it.
  - *Sanity Rewrites Reality*: "Broken reads as fixed, fixed as broken; the lie extends from the gauge to the sprite." The art bible's tremor/glide grammar (7.3) is how a player tells the lie from the warning. A pipeline that adds its own motion corrupts both.
  - *The World Never Lies*: "the physical world is ground truth. Only instruments and perception can be wrong." The rendering stack is neither instrument nor perception, so it may not be wrong either.
  - *The Web Watches*: "Attention is shown in the world (creaking, flicker, filament movement), never as a hidden meter." Filament movement at ≤ 1 art px s⁻¹ (2.11) is readable only on a grid that does not crawl.
  
  **Design tests.**
  - If we are debating a presentation effect that moves world pixels with no cause in the world (sub-pixel smoothing, eased camera lag that overshoots, non-integer zoom, a softening filter, screen shake) against holding the grid: hold the grid, or move the effect into the world (a needle, a sprite, a light).
  - The fantasy has failed if a player can see a one-art-pixel movement that no system put there. Two cases count: a static scene, and a followed actor that shifts relative to the floor while the camera tracks it. That is a Pillar 2 and Pillar 4 violation (a counterfeit tremor or a counterfeit glide), not a cosmetic bug. It is testable on day one with a frame-diff. Hold the sim with no committed change and the camera target still, and the only world pixels allowed to differ between frames are those owned by a declared animating source (flicker, scrolling medium, web drift). Then walk an actor across a static room, and the actor-to-floor offset may change only by whole art pixels in step with committed position.
  
  *What this section does not claim.* The pipeline does not make the world honest; the kernel does. It does not make motion meaningful; the art bible's settle/tremor/glide grammar does. It does not make a reading true; the HUD frame stays put and undimmed, but the needle inside it may lie under class (b). The image is up to one tick behind committed state and never ahead of it (kernel D.3). Under lighting pass A the cone edge is a 3-px stair-step at 1080p, and this section does not promise a smooth one. (Alternatives considered: "the edge belongs to the light", true but mostly Lighting's and Principle 1's fantasy, so it over-credits the pipeline; "a porthole, not a lens", true but describes a camera policy rather than a moment the player feels.)

  ## Candidate B: "The edge belongs to the light"
  
  **Where the picture goes black, the light stopped.** The art bible requires that "Rooms are not fogged, they are unlit; geometry is there and any source reveals it." This system's part in that is narrow but absolute. Nothing in the stack can draw a boundary of its own: no vignette, no fog-of-war mask, no post-process darkening at the frame edge, and no HUD dimming that could pass for the world getting darker. Anchor moment: in a dark corridor, the player swings their beam and a doorway appears exactly where the cone reaches it, at the same distance every time. At low sanity the cone "seems to reach further than it does", and they can walk forward and find the real edge, because the edge is the light's and the screen has no edge of its own.
  
  Pillars: *The World Never Lies* ("the physical world is ground truth"), the anchor list ("the physical radius your light actually reveals when you walk into it"), and *The Web Watches* (light is the resource being spent). Visual Principle 1's test, "a vignette / fog-of-war mask vs. true 2D occlusion from light sources with hard falloff → true occlusion", becomes a pipeline rule.
  
  *Why not recommended:* the radius, occlusion and hard falloff all belong to Lighting. The pipeline's only contribution is something it doesn't do (insert a mask), which is a constraint, not a fantasy. I'd move it into Candidate A's "does not claim" paragraph and into Core Rules as a prohibition.

  ## Candidate C: "A porthole, not a lens"
  
  **The frame is a window bolted to the ship, not a camera that performs.** Zoom changes only in whole steps. The view stops at the ship's real bounds and never cuts. On ultrawide or Steam Deck, more screen shows more dark, not more information (8.1: "more *screen* is not more *information* when the dark is honest"). Anchor moment: in a machine room too deep for the 48 px tile's 5.5-tile floor view, the player steps back to the integer zoom-out and the room fits, with every helmet band still countable at 50 %.
  
  Pillars: *The World Never Lies* and Principle 1.
  
  *Why not recommended:* it is mostly camera policy (bounds, zoom steps, aspect) that belongs in Core Rules and Tuning Knobs. The moment it describes is a convenience, not something the player feels. It also brushes against the 8.1 `keep`/`expand` decision, which is still open until after the spike.

  ## Downstream implications (for the user and game-designer; not decided here)
  
  1. **Camera shake.** Under the 7.3 motion grammar, any whole-frame shake counterfeits a Tremor, the honest Tell of 2.7. If Candidate A is adopted, Game Feel should send hull stress into real needles, dust direction and sprites, not into the frame. Whether any shake survives (for example on breach impact, after the tell has already landed) is the game-designer's call, but it should be decided explicitly against design test 1.
  2. **Camera follow with snapping.** The most common way this fantasy fails in practice is the followed actor and the world rounding to different integers in the same frame. QA's criteria must test motion during follow, not only a still scene. Design test 2 is written that way on purpose.
  3. **HUD exemption is anchoring, not truth.** Core Rules should say the root-viewport HUD keeps the frame steady and undimmed (HR-1). It must not imply readings are protected; Perception Filter class (b) needs that channel.
