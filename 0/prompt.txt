  ## 1. Readability at 2× (Steam Deck 1280×800 / 720p)
  
  **Verdict: needs a rule.**
  
  **Analysis.** 7.1 makes the *screen-pixel* floor the contract (21 / 15 px cap height) and the art-px column a derivation; the table has 1080p and 1440p rows only. At a 2× world the 7 / 5 art-px faces render at **14 / 10 screen px** — 67 % of the floor — and the derivation for a 2× HUD (10.5 / 7.5 art px) is not an integer, so no bitmap face at "100 %" can meet it. Angular check on the Deck (7-inch, ≈ 216 PPI, held ≈ 38 cm): 14 px cap ≈ 15 arcmin, 10 px ≈ 11 arcmin — the label floor falls below the commonly cited 16 arcmin minimum. At a **3× HUD** the same screen gives 21 / 15 px ≈ 22 / 16 arcmin, meeting the floor exactly. 8.1 already states the HUD's integer steps are independent of camera zoom; the rule is to make them independent of the *world scale* too.
  
  **Bands.** 8.2 sized the 2 px bands and 1 px separators to survive the single integer zoom-out step from a 3× world (sprites at 67 %). At a 2× world the only step out is 1×: separators become 1 screen px (≈ 1 arcmin on the Deck), below the acuity needed to count four bands. Whether bands read at the *default* 2× on a 7-inch screen (4 px band, 2 px separator ≈ 2 arcmin) is an on-device check for the accessibility-specialist, not a desk verdict. Identity is redundant by design — beam shape (3.2) and the crew-strip glyph (7.5) carry it — so a band failure degrades, it does not break.
  
  **Conflicts to surface, not swallow.**
  - 7.0 caps the critical strip at 12 % × 5 % of frame "at 100 % UI scale" and calls exceeding it "the player's explicit accessibility trade". On a 1280×800 screen with the 3× HUD the plate (230×54 px) is ≈ 18 % × 6.8 % **by default**, not by choice. Routed to art-director: re-express the cap relative to a 1080p reference frame, or accept the Deck overage as a platform default.
  - HR-12 labels HUD scale "100–200 %" relative to the world scale, so "100 %" is 3× at 1080p and 2× on the Deck — different physical sizes under the same label.
  
  **Rules to add.**
  - **C-R1 (hard).** HUD integer multiplier `H` is chosen independently of world scale `W`: `H_default = max(W, 3)` — never below 3 on any display — and the player may raise it in integer steps up to `2 × H_default`. *QA:* at 1280×800, 1280×720, 1366×768, 1920×1080, 2560×1440, 3840×2160 the critical-strip numeral cap height is ≥ 21 screen px and the smallest label ≥ 15 at the default HUD scale.
  - **C-R2 (hard).** The HUD layout is authored anchor-relative to the visible viewport edges with the 5 % safe margin measured in HUD pixels, never against an assumed 640×360 HUD canvas; it must survive a fractional logical canvas (1280×800 ÷ 3 = 426.7×266.7) and both `keep` and `expand` aspects. *QA:* no HUD element clipped or overlapping at the six resolutions above in both aspect modes.
  - **C-R3 (hard).** Camera zoom-out is unavailable when the resulting world scale would be below 2×. *QA:* at 1280×800 and 1280×720 the zoom-out input is a no-op.
  - **C-R4 (recommendation).** Label the HUD scale setting by resulting cap height ("Numerals 21 px / 28 px / 35 px") or absolute multiplier, not a world-relative percentage.
  - **C-R5 (recommendation, routed to accessibility-specialist).** Express the 7.1 floor additionally in arcminutes with a per-device-class table (desktop ≥ 60 cm, handheld ≈ 38 cm) so the Deck does not inherit a desk assumption; run the band-count check on a real Deck LCD and OLED.
  
  ## 2. Motion comfort
  
  **Verdict: needs a rule** (the existing architecture supports HUD immunity; two conflicts and several gaps remain).
  
  **Analysis.**
  - *Smoothing and look-ahead.* 8.4 snaps transforms to the base grid (both passes — under pass B sprites must still land on base-px positions or "sprites on grid" fails). Smoothing plus rounding produces the classic 0/1 px alternation at low pan speed; the hazard is oscillation, not smoothing. Look-ahead shifts the frame on a facing change — motion not proportional to the player's own displacement, the standard vestibular trigger.
  - *World shake on hull failure.* Positional, so a vestibular question, not photosensitivity. HR-13 already implies it exists (HUD excluded from camera shake) and 8.4's architecture satisfies HUD immunity **only** if the shake is applied inside the `SubViewport`. **Conflict:** 7.0 anchors interaction prompts (HUD layer) to object screen positions, so under shake they would shake with the object — violating HR-13.
  - *4× catch-up.* The kernel's CD-GDD-ALIGN adoption drops lag **beyond** `LAG_FORGIVE_TICKS` (30 ticks); lag ≤ 30 ticks is still repaid at up to 4× (D.5: "repaid at 3/frame"), so a residual of up to 0.5 s of sim time replayed over ≈ 8–10 frames survives the forgiveness. With `tick_alpha` clamped at 1 the presented player position advances 3–4 ticks' worth per frame — a few base px per frame, not a tile — so the camera follow runs at 3–4× normal pan speed for those frames. The forgiveness fixes fairness; the camera still needs a speed rule, and the GDD should not describe catch-up as fully "forgiven".
  - *Low-sanity drift.* 2.9 holds energy unchanged and 4.4's drift order (instruments → light → world hue) never names the camera. Nothing currently forbids the Perception Filter from touching the camera transform. A moved or drifting camera would be a lie about the player's own position with no instrument to check it against — the concept's "no lie is unfalsifiable" fails.
  - *Already forbidden elsewhere, inherited here:* non-integer zoom (8.1), runtime rotation of human-built geometry (8.4), dim/blur/desaturate on the world (7.4), HUD shake/blur/CA/scale change (HR-13), reduced motion never alters the tick (kernel CR-11).
  
  **Rules to add.**
  - **C-R6 (hard).** The camera moves in whole base pixels in the frame it is attached to (see item 7); per-frame displacement is 0 or an integer, and the camera never reverses direction within 100 ms unless the player's own velocity reversed. *QA:* frame-stamp capture of a slow diagonal walk shows no 1-px back-and-forth.
  - **C-R7 (hard).** The smooth follow has a **pan-speed ceiling** `CAMERA_MAX_PAN` (provisional: 1.25 × the crewmate's walk speed in base px per frame). If the follow target exceeds that ceiling for more than `N` consecutive frames (provisional 6) or the accumulated follow lag exceeds `K` base px (provisional 24, half a tile), the camera **cuts** to the target — never a fast pan. A single-frame target displacement ≥ 48 base px (teleport, body transfer, pod embark) cuts immediately. Catch-up repayment inside the forgiveness window is covered by the ceiling branch. *QA:* inject a 24-tick hitch while walking; no frame shows camera pan speed above `CAMERA_MAX_PAN`, and the capture shows at most one discontinuity.
  - **C-R8 (hard).** World shake is a `Camera2D` offset inside the world `SubViewport`, amplitude ≤ 2 base px, duration bounded per event (provisional ≤ 600 ms), never a post-process offset of the composed frame and never an offset of the `SubViewportContainer`. *QA:* automated diff of the HUD layer across a hull-failure shake is empty.
  - **C-R9 (hard).** HUD elements anchored to world positions (interaction prompts) compute screen position from the **unshaken** camera transform. *QA:* a prompt stays still while the world under it shakes.
  - **C-R10 (hard).** The Perception Filter may not write camera position, zoom, rotation, offset or any viewport/stretch property; the camera is an anchor alongside the true light radius. *QA:* control-manifest check — the Filter's write set excludes `Camera2D` and `Viewport` properties.
  - **C-R11 (hard).** Reduced motion disables look-ahead (camera centres on the player) and sets world-shake amplitude to 0. The information shake carried must land somewhere named: the gauge tremor (7.3), hull-stress hairlines (Failure & Trail), the audio Tell (HR-9) — HR-8's backbone — none of which this setting may suppress. *QA:* with reduced motion on, a hull-failure event still produces the tremor on at least one visible instrument and its audio cue within the same tick window.
  - **C-R12 (hard).** Never allowed on the world camera under any state: rotation, non-integer zoom, zoom pulses, FOV punch, blur, chromatic aberration, vignette, or drift not caused by player displacement or an explicit bounded shake event. *QA:* no code path writes `rotation` or a fractional `zoom` on the camera node.
  - **C-R13 (recommendation).** Expose look-ahead strength (off / short / default) and shake strength (off / half / full) as separate controls feeding the two parameters reduced motion zeroes.
  
  ## 3. Integer scale on odd window sizes
  
  **Verdict: needs a rule.**
  
  **Analysis.** 8.4 fixes `stretch/mode = canvas_items`, `scale_mode = integer`, `aspect = keep` for the spike; 8.10 records `expand` as a post-spike ask. Common odd sizes: 1366×768 (2× with 86×48 px bars under `keep`), 1600×900 (2× with 320×180 px bars — a quarter of the screen), 1680×1050, 3440×1440 (4× with 880 px of side bars). Under `expand` there are no bars and the viewport shows more world at the same integer scale. Comfort: bars are not disruptive on a dark game, but there is a **Principle 1 hazard** — unlit hull sits only 1–2 8-bit steps above true black (8.11 flag 1), so a true-black letterbox bar is nearly indistinguishable from unlit room and may be read as geometry. Readability is unaffected by bars (C-R1 governs the HUD). Under `expand` on ultrawide the bottom-left suit panel sits ≈ 1,700 px from the critical strip — the corner trip 7.0 avoided for the critical strip reappears for the panel.
  
  **Rules to add.**
  - **C-R14 (hard).** Settings expose: display mode (exclusive fullscreen / borderless / windowed); resolution (fullscreen); world scale (Auto = largest integer that fits, plus an explicit integer list); HUD scale (integer, floored per C-R1); reading-face scale (continuous); aspect (`keep` / `expand`) once `expand` ships; frame cap and VSync. No non-integer world scale is offered anywhere. *QA:* every control exists, persists across restart, and the world is pixel-exact (uniform N×N blocks in a screenshot) at every option.
  - **C-R15 (hard).** Minimum window size is 1280×720 (2× world, 3× HUD fits). Live resize in windowed mode re-derives world and HUD scale and re-anchors the HUD without a reload. *QA:* drag-resize from 1920×1080 to 1366×768; world steps 3→2, HUD stays 3×, nothing clipped.
  - **C-R16 (hard).** Letterbox bars render true black and are never part of the world `SubViewport`; the Perception Filter and world shake never touch them. *QA:* bar pixels unchanged across a shake and at sanity stage 3.
  - **C-R17 (recommendation, routed to art-director).** Where bars exist, mark the viewport edge with a 1-HUD-px Graphite-lit hairline in the chart-table register so the frame edge is never mistaken for unlit hull — or ship `expand` as the default once the spike confirms it, which removes the bars and the hazard.
  - **C-R18 (recommendation, routed to game-designer).** `expand` shows more lit floor on ultrawide — a real information advantage inside a lit room even though the dark is honest. Decide whether that is acceptable or whether the visible field is clamped at 16:9, with an optional "HUD width" setting clamping the HUD to a 16:9 inner frame.
  
  ## 4. Dark-by-default screens on bright rooms / monitors
  
  **Verdict: supports** (with one rule to lock it and one flag).
  
  **Analysis.** 8.11 flag 1 condition (i) is what saves Principle 1: web, filaments and horrors are **not drawn** unlit, so no gamma, brightness or monitor setting can reveal them — only direct light does. A full-frame gamma lift therefore cannot break "looking is spending". What it does break: the mood ladder (2.1's 0–10 % ordering compresses), and HUD contrast — a lift on the composed frame lightens Ground `#1C2128` under Paper White and erodes the 4.5 : 1 that HR-1/HR-2 meet by construction. Flag 1 (iv) already says HR-15 and the hull floor are one mechanism, so a bounded step control costs no second path. Steam Deck LCD vs OLED differ in black level by more than the 1–2-step gap, which argues for calibrating rather than guessing.

  - **C-R19 (hard).** No full-frame gamma, brightness or contrast post-process exists on the composed frame or the root viewport. The only brightness control the game exposes is the HR-15 ambient-floor step (0 to the bounded ceiling, provisional +3), implemented as the flag-1 hull material raise, so it (a) can never render web-grammar content, (b) never touches the HUD layer, (c) never exceeds the 10 % stable state. *QA:* at the maximum step, a horror and a filament outside direct light are absent from the frame buffer; HUD Ground pixel value is identical at step 0 and step max.
  - **C-R20 (hard).** The pipeline guarantees the measured floor is actually output: `hdr_2d` and debanding on (8.4), and the 8.12 step 7 histogram values are the ones the HR-15 control steps from. *QA:* step 7 histogram shows unlit hull 1–2 steps above black at step 0 and exactly N additional steps at step N.
  - **C-R21 (recommendation — reframes a user decision; flag to creative-director / user).** A first-boot calibration screen built on the real floor swatches (Void Steel at ring floor, Graphite at +1, +2) that sets the HR-15 step for everyone ("adjust until the right-hand mark is just visible").   ## 6. Perception Filter full-screen pass
  
  **Verdict: conflicts** — a rule is needed before the material is built.
  
  **Analysis.** 8.10's Materials row places the "full-screen sanity filter" as a `canvas_item` shader on the `SubViewportContainer` (or `BackBufferCopy`). Two structural facts collide with it: (1) the truth-channel anchors — character sprites, hand tools, crewmate beams, trails, the true light radius (Corollary; systems-index 20's tag contract) — live **inside** the `SubViewport`, so an unmasked container shader tints them; (2) the container sits in the root viewport beside the HUD `CanvasLayer` — safe only if the HUD is drawn above and never sampled; a `BackBufferCopy` placed in the root would capture the HUD. Accessibility risks of a per-client pass: deutan players see violet as blue — already tri-modal under HR-5/HR-7, so acceptable *only if the pass cannot also remove the shape/pattern backups*; low-vision players lose contrast if the pass lowers hull below the floor or softens edges; any displacement or blur is both a lens lie (7.4) and a motion-sickness vector; HR-10 forbids global hue remaps, and a stage-3 "surfaces warm toward flesh" tint is one unless bounded to *adding* the reserved hues at the places 4.4 names.
  
  **Rules to add.**
  - **C-R28 (hard).** The Filter's screen-wide pass never samples or writes the HUD `CanvasLayer`, the inspect card, menus, text chat, pings, letterbox bars or the debug overlay; it is bound to world `SubViewport` content only. *QA:* HUD-layer pixel diff at sanity stage 0 vs stage 3 is empty.
  - **C-R29 (hard).** The pass is multiplied by a **truth-channel mask** rendered with the world (sprites, tools, beams, trails, the true-radius cone edge, the Failure & Trail layer) so anchor pixels are untouched — or the screen-wide effects are decomposed into per-element materials on corruptible nodes only. *QA:* with the pass at maximum, anchor pixels are bit-identical to the pass-disabled frame.
  - **C-R30 (hard).** The pass performs no displacement, warp, blur, chromatic aberration, pixelation or resolution change; it writes colour only. Geometry is an anchor — the light-radius edge may not move by one pixel. *QA:* edge-position diff of the beam cone at stage 0 vs stage 3 is zero.
  - **C-R31 (hard).** The pass may only **add** violet (edges: cone rim, pipe silhouettes, gauge glass, sprite fringe) and flesh (surfaces and phantom content) per 4.4; it never shifts, desaturates or re-maps sane hues (HR-10), never reduces the luminance contrast between a pipe and its flow marks or between a trail and what it crosses (the trail-contrast number of 7.6), and never lowers hull below the HR-15/flag-1 floor. *QA:* 4.7 pairs re-measured at stage 3 keep their shape backups' contrast within 10 % of stage 0.
  - **C-R32 (hard).** The pass obeys C-R22/C-R23 — the Corollary's "a flicker" leak is bounded by the same clamp. *QA:* stage-2/3 scenes pass the C-R23 scan.
  - **C-R33 (recommendation).** Pattern-strength (7.4) is applied before the Filter in material order so thickened flow marks are what the Filter tints, never what it erases.
  
  ## 7. Camera-induced disorientation in a moving ship
  
  **Verdict: needs a rule** — ship-local frame.
  
  **Analysis.** Systems-index 12 makes the composite ship one physics body with a ship-local frame and characters moving inside a moving body. A **world-frame** camera would sweep the whole interior across the screen while the player stands still — full-field optic flow with no proprioceptive match, the textbook simulator-sickness trigger — and if the ship rotates it rotates the tilemap off-grid, which 8.4 forbids for anything human-built. A **ship-local** camera keeps the interior static on screen; the only exterior view is pod transit (2.5), where the sparse filament field at constant velocity is low-density flow and the state's energy is already "low". Snapping matters too: if the player snaps to the world grid and the floor to the ship grid, they shimmer against each other as the ship moves. Under a ship-local camera, hull shake becomes an explicit bounded effect (C-R8) rather than emergent physics jitter — better for comfort and for HR-13.
  
  **Rules to add.**
  - **C-R34 (hard).** During interior play the camera is rigidly attached to the frame of the body the player stands on; the body's world motion is never visible as interior optic flow. *QA:* with the ship under thrust and the player idle, the interior frame is pixel-static.
  - **C-R35 (hard).** All pixel snapping (player, props, actors, floor) is performed in the camera's body frame, never in world space. *QA:* no relative 1-px jitter between the crewmate and the floor tile under them while the ship moves.
  - **C-R36 (hard).** Transfer of the camera between bodies (detach into a new body, docking, boarding across the seam, pod embark/disembark) is a cut through C-R7's immediate branch, never a pan or blend. *QA:* frame capture of a detach event shows exactly one discontinuity and no interpolated frames.
  - **C-R37 (hard).** Pod transit is the only state with exterior optic flow; in it the camera is pod-local with zero lag, flow velocity is constant (no acceleration curves on the camera), and the flow field is the sparse 2.5 filament set. Reduced motion lowers filament density or parallax speed, never the pod's own motion. *QA:* transit capture shows constant-velocity drift and a stationary pod sprite.
  - **C-R38 (flag, routed to game-designer and technical-director — ties to kernel open question #3, who owns character position).** The weld-on-moving-ship spike puts two bodies with relative motion in view (home ship and derelict across the seam). The camera follows the body underfoot; the other body moves on screen. Comfort needs that relative motion bounded and, for the grid, relative orientation quantised (dock aligned at 0°/90°) — a design and physics decision, not a pipeline one.
