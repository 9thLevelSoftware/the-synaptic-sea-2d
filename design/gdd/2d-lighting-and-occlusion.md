## Summary

2D Lighting & Occlusion draws every light in the game: the player's chest-mounted beam, ship lamps, the glow of running machinery, the alarm beacon, glowsticks and fault glows, with real shadows from walls and a hull that is never fully black. It decides nothing about the world. It draws the facts Emitter State publishes, so the edge the player sees is the edge the game acts on (a prompt appears exactly where the light visibly ends), and it is the single place a light-shaped hallucination may be drawn, always as a violet-leaking overlay that never changes the real light beneath it. Skip it for what a light means (Emitter State) or where walls are (Layout).

**Terms.** `reach_px` is the simulation's fade-out distance. `r_lit` is the readable radius a soft pool is drawn to. A slot is one of six shadow-casting lights. A layer is one step of the modifier chain. A stand-in is the glow that replaces an over-budget light. A decoy is an unlit hallucination overlay, never a light. The tell tier is Attention's published level, drawn as a gutter on lamps.

> **Quick reference** — Layer: Feature · Priority: MVP · Key deps: Pixel-Perfect Render Pipeline & Camera (designed), Emitter State & Light-Exposure Query (designed in this run), Ship Section & Layout Data Model (designed); Attention, Horror Entities and Perception Filter are designed submitters

## Overview

**What it is, in one sentence.** 2D Lighting & Occlusion is the presentation-class renderer that turns the simulation's emitter records into the picture: every frame it reads the per-emitter view and the shape table that Emitter State publishes, and draws `Light2D` cones and pools, true occluder shadows, the ring and ship ambient, the material-level hull floor, the emission glow of running machinery and the light textures behind all of them, while owning no simulation fact of its own (no reach, no output, no `lit`, no block, no hook, nothing persisted).

**Framing: a data/infrastructure layer with a very large player impact.** The player never meets "a lighting system". They meet a beam that ends hard at the exact place a prompt stops appearing, a wall that throws a shadow shaped like the wall, a lamp that sags when the ship is short of power, a machine glowing in its own colour in an otherwise black hull, and (late) a glow that runs past where their light really ends and leaks the wrong colour at its rim. The system is the *picture of the truth*: the simulation decides what is lit (Emitter State, `lit(ref)`, `illumination`); this GDD decides how that fact is drawn, and the two must agree to the pixel (hard cones at `reach_px` ±1 px, soft pools at the readable radius `r_lit` ±1 px). The system exists for two reasons that pull in the same direction. First, **Pillar 1**: the physical radius your light actually reveals is one of the few anchors that never shift, and an anchor the player cannot see is not an anchor. Second, **Pillar 4**: a hallucination is frightening only against a picture that has been reliable for hours, and the single place where a light-shaped lie may be drawn is owned here, so that the lie can be bounded, made to leak, and never allowed to touch the real light beneath it. Without this system the game has no honest dark, no readable edge, no lanterns, no tell with a light channel, and no stage for the lie; and the art bible's first spike (the frame budget of six shadow-casting lights, many emissive loops and true occlusion on D3D12) stays unanswered, which the index names as the single decision on which the whole visual direction depends.

**What it draws and what it owns.** Lighting is a `PRESENTATION` subscriber (kernel CR-10, KERN:108-113) with one `PROJECTOR`-class sub-duty (the occluder builder; flagged for the technical-director ruling the kernel already requests). It reads last-committed `SIM` views and, for decoys and module-body glows only, `PERCEPTION_OUT` lists; it writes only `CanvasItem`, `Material`, `Light2D`, `LightOccluder2D` and `CanvasModulate` properties in layer 0 of the world viewport that the Pixel-Perfect Render Pipeline & Camera GDD owns. It owns, by name: the light textures; per-shape light configuration conforming to the shape table; the six shadow-casting slots (`SHADOW_SLOTS_MAX`) and their assignment; the non-shadow light budget (`NONSHADOW_LIGHTS_MAX`, of which at most `LANTERN_LIGHTS_MAX` are node lanterns) and its deterministic demotion; occluder attachment over Layout's footprint rectangles (`OCCLUDER_POLYS_VIEW_MAX`, `OCCLUDER_VERTS_MAX`); ambient and the single shared HullSurface material with its floor; emission glow (the unshaded additive emissive layer and `GLOW_HDR_THRESHOLD`); the warm-up, gutter and flicker recipes; the layered **modifier contract** through which Attention's tell, Horror's disturbance and the Perception Filter's decoys reach the picture without ever changing the real reveal radius; the per-light, regional and frame-mean flash governor (`flash_governor`, `FLASH_MAX_PER_S`); behaviour at every rung of the zoom ladder `k_z` in [1, `k_auto` + 2]; and the HR-15 low-vision raise and the reduce-flashing toggle as renderer-side consumers.

**Index wording, reconciled.** The systems index (IDX:23, bullet 6, enumeration row 6, Recommended Design Order 9) says Lighting "owns the final Light2D values through a modifier contract: Attention flicker, horror light disturbance and Perception Filter decoys submit layered modifiers; the Filter may add decoy visuals but never changes the real reveal radius". This GDD keeps every clause and sharpens three words. "Final `Light2D` values" means every *value* member of every light (`enabled`, `energy`, `color`, `texture`, `texture_scale`, `offset`, shadow and range members); the local seat's beam *pose* members (`position`, `rotation`) are the Avatar Presenter's (Player Controller PC-5), so the two writers own disjoint member sets (LO-2). "Submit" means *publish state that Lighting reads* (KERN:117: "state that Lighting reads, not calls into Lighting"), never a call. "Attention flicker" is rendered as a **gutter** on fixtures (never the cryo-bay EMERGENCY lamp) and node lanterns only (the verb *flicker* is reserved for deployables, LO-30), an art-bible amendment this GDD requests rather than assumes. "Decoy visuals" are unlit overlays on a Lighting-owned layer and never lights (LO-21), which is what makes "never changes the real reveal radius" structural instead of a policy. The soft-cycle wording at IDX:266 ("Lighting never reads Attention") is superseded accordingly: Lighting reads Attention's published tell tier for the gutter recipe only and never derives reach, output, colour or edge from it.

**What it is not.** It is not where light is *decided*: reach, output, `lit`, hysteresis, priority and the caps on emitters are Emitter State's; the Graph's `served_pm` is the only source of a lamp's brightness. It is not a builder: lights come with authored sections and welded modules, and the glowstick is the only player-placed light. It is not a combat system: a beam only reveals. It adds no meter, vignette, fog mask, gamma control or darkness penalty (LO-31). It does not own the viewport, the camera, `RENDER_MODE` or the z-bands (Pipeline), occluder geometry (Layout), hue tokens and hexes (art-director), or which decoy appears and when (Sanity and the Perception Filter).

**MVP scope.** Single-player: one seat beam (slots, replication hints and the shape table are sized for four); the home ship and the two handmade derelicts (A with four compartments, B with five); the cryo-bay emergency lamp, which takes the sixth shadow slot; one alarm beacon; glowsticks; fault glows for the eight MVP faults that have a glow row; both render passes specified in full with pass A (lights at the 640x360 base resolution) the fallback and default until the lighting spike decides; the modifier contract with stubs for Attention (one tell tier) and Horror; and decoys as a contract plus a stub, because the MVP's single hallucination class (false system state) reaches light only through a lamp housing's own sprite (LO-14, LO-21). Flares, torches and decoy-deployables with their flicker, `GLOW_EXTENSION` and `PHANTOM_SHADOW` decoys, cross-body illumination and the three-ring ambient table are Vertical Slice A; four simultaneous beams and remote beam poses are Vertical Slice B.

**Engine posture.** Godot 4.7.2, Forward+ pinned, D3D12 the Windows default since 4.6 (verified from `docs/engine-reference/godot/`, but the installed binary reports `vulkan` in the art bible's class dump, so the driver is written explicitly and printed at spike step 1). The reference folder has no `Light2D`, `LightOccluder2D`, `CanvasModulate`, 2D-glow or `SubViewport` 2D-resolution content, and no `scripting.md` or `core.md` exists; every engine statement in this GDD therefore carries a tag, `[REF]` (verified from the reference), `[BIN]` (existence, type or default only, read from the installed 4.7.2 class dump recorded in the art bible 8.4 and 8.10) or `[UNV]` (unverified, settled by a named spike step), and costs the engine reference cannot source are marked NOT SOURCEABLE. The behaviour in this document is written so that a spike outcome selects between stated alternatives and never invalidates a rule.

## Player Fantasy

**Framing: indirect.** The player experiences this system as the picture itself (what is lit, what is dark, what changed) and never as "lighting". *(creative-director: Framing A "The Honest Dark" adopted as the headline with Framing B "Every Loop a Lantern" as its second beat; Framing C "Cinematic Dread Lighting", lights that flicker, swing and fail for mood and scripted blackouts, rejected because a light that changes without a cause the world can name breaks P1, a scare without a tell breaks P2, scripted events break the "no authored story" anti-pillar, and a liar outside the sanity system destroys the baseline Pillar 4 needs. Logged.)*

> The dark here is not a fog. It is a ship with the lights off. The deck plates are there, a step above black, and anything you light shows exactly what it is. Where your beam ends, it ends hard, and a wall throws a shadow that is the shape of the wall.
>
> Every light you see has a reason. A lamp that sags is a ship short of power. A lamp that dips and comes back is something else, not the power. A machine glowing in its own colour is alive, and you are not the only one who can see it. A red sweep is the ship telling you it is losing. Nothing in the light moves for nothing.
>
> So you learn to read the ship by its lights, and the dark by its shadows. And when your mind starts to go, the picture is where the lie has to live: a glow that runs past where your light really ends, a shadow with nothing to cast it, a lamp that looks lit over a floor that is not. Every one of them is wrong at the edges, in a colour the sane ship never uses. Trust the floor, not the lamp.

**The emotion named: the honest dark.** Trust in what you see, inside a place that frightens you. The horror of this game lives in instruments, sanity and the web; the light is the one thing the player learns to believe, and that trust is what makes Pillar 4 land (art bible Principle 1 rationale: "A hallucinated shadow with no occluder is then frightening *because* occlusion is normally trustworthy"). The power fantasy is **literacy, not dominance**: the player cannot push the dark back, but after an hour they can read a ship from its lights the way a sailor reads a harbour at night: which lamps are sagging (power), which one dipped (something noticed), which machines are lanterns (alive and seen), and whose shadow that is (a real wall, or not). In MDA terms the system serves Discovery (the picture is how a derelict is read) and Challenge (lamp sag and fault glow are diagnostic), and is the main carrier of Sensation (CON:87, "HD pixel art under a light-as-resource rendering model; flow-coded loops glowing in the dark"); it must never buy Sensation at the cost of the first two.

**Division of labour with Emitter State's fantasy.** Emitter State owns "the bargain of sight": where the edge is, that the edge equals the prompt, light as a spend, exposure, the reach-for-it falsifier and the per-kind meaning table (EM Player Fantasy). This section does not repeat it. Lighting's fantasy is the **picture**: that the dark is *drawn* honestly (geometry present, hull above black), that every change in a light's look has a cause in the world, the grammar of a dimming lamp, the ship as lanterns, shadow as testimony, how the picture holds at every zoom rung, and where a lie is allowed to live in the picture and how it leaks.

**Anchor moment: the cryo-bay wake (the first minute of the game).** You wake. One emergency lamp hangs low and dim in Tungsten (ART 2.2; fixture hex `#FFCC66`), and it **gutters**: a dip of 0.3-0.5 s, then another 10-20 s later, never on a beat. It holds the sixth shadow slot (LO-9), so the pod row throws short, hard shadows down-screen: the room has walls you can trust before you have touched anything. The far wall is suggestion only, an ambient near 3 % over a hull floor that never drops to true black (`HULL_FLOOR_TINT` `#0B1018`, `HULL_FLOOR_GAP_STEPS`). If the pod you came out of carries a light, it is a real FIXTURE placement whose `output_pm` falls in the simulation, never a Lighting-only effect (a Lighting-only glow would be a light that exposes nothing, a P1/P3 lie); whether it does is Derelict Authoring's and Emitter State's call (Ask E-H). You switch on the chest lamp: the light lands on the committed tick and warms over at most `LAMP_WARM_FRAMES` without its edge ever moving. A frost trail crosses the floor in Coolant Ice and catches in your cone. The lesson of minute one: **the lamp you did not choose is a fact; the light you did choose is a decision; neither one lies.** The cryo lamp is also the one fixture the Attention tell never touches (LO-20), so nothing the player sees in this room can be the web leaning in: the gutter here is the room's baseline and the first tell arrives later, on a home-ship lamp, read against it. *What the player must not be able to confuse here:* the gutter (a dip, the lamp's readable radius keeps its size) with a real light going out (simulation state, for example a pod light if the placement carries one).

**Second beat: the home ship, conserved (Framing B).** Before a sortie you kill the corridor lamps. Home-ship ambient falls from about 10 % (stable) toward about 2 % (dark) (ART 4.3, `AMBIENT_HOME_STABLE_PM` / `AMBIENT_HOME_DARK_PM`), not because a menu said so but because the fixtures that set it went out. What is left is the scrubber, glowing O2 Cyan `#3FD6E8` along its pipe (an emissive overlay) and casting light at its node (a node lantern), and the reactor cell in Power Amber `#F2C230`: "isolated lanterns in a black hull" (ART 2.4). You can count them. So can the web. The image is the creative core of this system: **the ship drawn only by what you keep alive.**

**Third beat: the tell (P2 and P3 in one lamp).** Back on the home ship with lamps on, the scrubber pulls power and the galley lamp **sags**: steadily, and its pool *pulls in* (a fixture served at 600 permille is drawn at 60 % energy with its readable pool ending at 108 px instead of 122 px, `r_lit = floor(144 × (600 − 150) / 600) = 108`, `EM D.7`, reused by name). The junction box at the old edge loses its prompt. A minute later the same lamp **dips**: 0.3-0.5 s at no less than `MOD_FLOOR_PM` of its served brightness, and its readable pool does **not** shrink (no modifier touches the texture radius, LO-18; on a dark floor the soft rim may look a little closer for the length of the dip, which is why the dependable cue is that it returns, Named rule 1). Then again, sooner. Your beam does not change at all (the beam is "a truth anchor", ART 2.7). That is Attention's tell tier, the web leaning in, and the only light-side tell in the MVP's one Attention tier. The player who has learned the grammar knows the second event is not the power.

### The pillars this section answers (verbatim intent)

- **P1 The World Never Lies (primary).** Three channels are inviolable: the physical consequence, **the sane palette** ("Flesh tones and violet never appear in the sane world, and every hallucination leaks them somewhere: a fringe, a flicker, a tint at the edge of the light. Subtle is allowed; absent is not") and **the human channel** (a crewmate's own light beam, colour and shape, is never faked). CON Visual Identity Anchor, principle 1: "Rooms are not fogged, they are unlit; geometry is there and any source reveals it." Lighting *draws* the anchor "the physical radius your light actually reveals when you walk into it".
- **P3 The Web Watches (primary).** "Attention is shown in the world (creaking, flicker, filament movement), never as a hidden meter ... Restraint and stealth are real strategies." Art bible Principle 3: every loop you run is a lantern the web can see. The lantern image is drawn here; the gutter is Attention's state drawn here.
- **P4 Sanity Rewrites Reality (channel, not content).** "No lie is unfalsifiable": the truth is always available through the physical world, a second instrument, a saner crewmate, the human channel, or time. Lighting owns the place where light-shaped lies are allowed to be drawn and the requirement that they leak.
- **P2 Dread Before Chaos (supporting).** Every breach is preceded by a tell a careful player could have perceived (sound, **light**, gauge drift, hull stress). The gutter, the beacon sweep (`BEACON_SWEEP_TICKS`, 2.13 s) and fault glows appearing on the Graph tick of their descriptor are the light channel of that tell.
- **P5 Deeper Is Richer (weak).** Depth changes only the ambient floor and iridescence, never hue or reach (ART 4.3; EM Open Question 3), stated so that nobody reads the ring ordering (outer ~3 % > middle ~1-2 % > inner 0-1 %) as a difficulty dial.

### The light grammar the player learns

The player's literacy is a small, closed set of looks, each with exactly one cause and one channel. This table is the creative contract the Core Rules must make true; each row is testable.

| What the player sees | What it means | Cause (owner) | Channel | Never confused with |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A lamp's pool **pulls in** and stays dimmer | The ship is short of power | `output_pm` from `served_pm` (Graph D.6 via Emitter State) | Consequence (truth; LSG:168) | The dip: a dip never shrinks the pool |
| A lamp **dips** 0.3-0.5 s and recovers, readable radius unchanged (the soft rim may look closer on a dark floor during the dip), irregular, spacing shortening | Something noticed you (MVP: one tier) | Attention tell tier (L2) | Presentation of true SIM state | A sag (steady, shrinking); a flicker (deployables only) |
| A lamp **dips** with no tell rhythm, bounded the same way | Something is near the light (VS-A) | Horror disturbance (L3) | Presentation of true SIM state | Never applied to beam, beacon, fault glow, glowstick (LO-18) |
| The cryo lamp **gutters** | Nothing: the room's baseline | Lighting recipe (L1) | Presentation | It is the reference grammar the dip borrows; that is why the cryo bay teaches it first. The cryo lamp itself never carries the Attention tell (LO-20), so its gutter is always the baseline |
| A machine **glows** in its loop hue at its node, the pipe carries emission | It is running, and it is seen | Node-lantern emitter plus emissive overlay (LO-14) | Light = truth; overlay and sprite = module-body (may lie) | A lamp housing: see "trust the floor" |
| A light **warms** over `LAMP_WARM_FRAMES` after a switch | A lamp turning on, not lag | Warm-up recipe (LO-19) | Presentation; edge fixed from the first frame | Never drawn before commit (PC :950) |
| A green pool **wanes** in steps | A glowstick running out; its reach really shrinks (96 -> 24 px) | `GLOWSTICK_*` (EM-15) | Simulation truth | Never flickers |
| A red cone **sweeps** every 2.13 s | The ship is losing (Attention's breach machine) | BEACON (EM-16) | Truth; static mark + sound under reduce-flashing | Never a full-frame flash (ART 2.8) |
| Ember, arc, cyan mist **pop / glint / glow** as points | A failure, lighting its own repair | FAULT_GLOW (EM-17) from the Failure & Trail descriptor | Truth; never demoted while younger than `ticks_for_seconds(W_MIN_WARNING_S)` | The trail stamp (an albedo mark; casts no light) |
| A **hard shadow** shaped like a wall | A wall | Shadow casters over Layout's footprints (LO-9, LO-11) | Truth | A shadow with no occluder (stage-2 lie, violet-black) |
| Unlit hull **one or two steps above black** | Geometry is there; the dark is honest | The hull floor (LO-13) | Cosmetic; never actionable | Ambient revealing anything: it reveals nothing actionable |

**Named rule 1: "A dip dims; a sag shrinks."** Power changes a lamp's *reach of readability*; nothing else does. Attention and Horror change only brightness, bounded at `MOD_FLOOR_PM`, and only on fixtures (not the cryo-bay lamp) and node lanterns. The two most important lamp events are therefore distinguishable by two cues, and the GDD is honest about which is reliable where (CD-GDD-ALIGN revision). The **readable radius** (`r_lit`, the edge where the prompt stops) is unchanged by a dip and shrinks in a sag; that is the cue the HUD prompt, the Debug Overlay and AC-LO-30 measure. The **perceived** edge is a different thing: a 30 % dip lowers the pool's apex but not its radius, so on a dark Graphite floor the soft rim can look about 11 px closer for the 0.3-0.5 s of the dip (ESTIMATE, LO-7), and on those floors a still frame cannot be trusted to tell a dip from a sag by pool size. Pool size in a still frame is reliable on bright plates only. The redundant cue that holds everywhere, and that colour-blindness and a single captured frame cannot break, is the **temporal shape**: a dip is transient and returns to full, a sag is steady and stays (the same redundancy ART 2.4 demands for conserved versus lost). Open Question 19 (the playtest) is the arbiter of whether the temporal cue alone is enough.

**Named rule 2: "Every change in light has a cause in the world."** No real light changes its look for mood. Each change traces to exactly one of: a committed switch, `output_pm`, a Lighting-owned recipe (warm-up, gutter, deployable flicker, cut-fade), the Attention tier, Horror disturbance, the governor's delay, the beacon clock, a deployable's burn. A lighting effect that cannot name its cause is cut.

**Named rule 3: "Trust the floor, not the lamp."** The light on the floor is consequence channel (LSG:168) and cannot lie; a lamp *housing* is a module's own sprite (module-body channel, LSG:213) and may. In the MVP this is the whole of the light-lie: a housing drawn lit over a dark floor, or dead over a lit pool.

### The zoom ladder (user decision: Zomboid-style in and out)

Zooming out (`k_z` down to 1) is **stepping back to count the lanterns**: you see more of the ship and more of the dark, never more of what is in it. The rules of the fantasy: (1) zoom reveals nothing: ambient is cosmetic and the simulation is identical at every rung (`state_hash` unchanged; an off-screen lit object stays lit); (2) the hard edge, the six shadow casters and soft pools at `r_lit` hold at every rung (the cost lever is a quality tier, never truth, LO-17); (3) the edge stays visibly *hard*: 1 base px is `k_z` screen px under pass A (1 px at `k_z` 1, 5 px at `k_z` 5 on 1080p), 1-2 screen px under pass B (ART 8.3); (4) zooming in is leaning in to read a gauge, and the narrow cone's rim may soften slightly at high magnification under pass B, accepted by ART 8.3 because softening is not moving. The pixel-art feel must not depend on the rung; if the spike shows the edge reads as a smear at `k_z` 1 under pass A, that is a CONFLICT for the art-director and never a reason to drop a rung.

### Design tests (one sentence each)

1. **The honest dark (system test).** *When a lighting choice makes the scene more atmospheric vs. keeps every drawn change in a real light traceable to a cause in the world: the caused change.* Testable: for each real light, every drawn change in energy over a scripted run is attributable to exactly one row of the grammar table (Debug Overlay modifier stack plus governor log).
2. **A dip dims; a sag shrinks.** *When an Attention or Horror effect could change how far a lamp's light is readable vs. only how bright it is: only how bright.* Testable: during a dip the soft pool's readable radius equals `r_lit` from `output_pm` ±1 px.
3. **Trust the floor, not the lamp.** *When a hallucination could alter a lamp's light vs. only its housing: only its housing.* Testable: with the Filter stub forcing a housing state, the pool's energy and radius are bit-identical to the no-Filter run.
4. **The lie is drawn on top, never through.** *When a decoy could light real geometry vs. only overlay it: only overlay.* (Art bible Principle 1: the Filter "may add things but may not move that boundary".)
5. **Dark is not a penalty.** *When the dark could cost the player something by itself vs. only withhold agency: only withhold.* (EM Open Question 4: darkness is never a sanity cost; go-dark is a valid, unpenalised strategy.)

### Under hallucination the fantasy deepens rather than breaks

Lighting gives Pillar 4 its best stage precisely because the picture has been reliable. The lie is permitted only on top of the true picture, and every lie is falsifiable by a cheap physical check.

| Cannot lie (Lighting guarantees, structurally) | May lie (drawn from the Filter's `PERCEPTION_OUT` list, or by the module-body channel) | The leak (required) | The cheapest falsifier |
|---|---|---|---|
| A real light's brightness, reach, edge, colour and shadow (consequence channel) | `GLOW_EXTENSION`: a glow past the true edge (VS-A) | Violet fringe `#B07BE6` at its rim (ART 4.4) | Reach for it: no prompt past the true edge; walk to the edge |
| Every beam's colour and shape, local and remote (human channel) | `PHANTOM_SHADOW`: a shadow with no occluder (VS-A) | Violet-black, not steel-black (ART 2.9) | Walk around it; drop a glowstick and see whether a second light agrees |
| Fault glow and beacon light (consequence witnesses) | A lamp **housing** drawn lit or dead (module-body; the MVP's false-system-state class reaches light only here) | The missing or unexplained pool | **Look at the floor**: a "lit" housing over a dark floor, or a "dead" housing over a lit pool |
| The hull floor and the ring ambient | (VS-A) phantom entities lit on the decoy mask bit | Violet or flesh (ART 4.4) | Horrors never occlude: a figure in your beam casts no shadow, real or not, so shadow is never the test for bodies; the human channel and the Filter's own leaks are |

The drift order (art bible Corollary: instruments first, *then the light lies*) means the player meets the light-lie only after hours of a truthful picture. The lie's power is borrowed from that trust, and its leak is placed exactly where the player has learned to look: the edge of the light (ART 2.9: "the player learns to look at edges: cone rim, pipe silhouette, gauge glass, which is where the truth test lives").

### Tone and language

**Use:** lamp, chest lamp, beam, the light, the edge, pool, the dark, go dark, lantern; the lamp warms, the pool pulls in (sag), the lamp dips (tell), gutters (cryo lamp), sweeps (beacon), wanes (glowstick), pops (arcs), glints (fuel edges), glows (pipes); shadow, hull. **The verb vocabulary is closed (LO-30):** arcs *pop*; deployables *flicker*; the cryo lamp *gutters* and, at the tell, fixtures and node lanterns *gutter* too; "dip" is only the noun for one gutter event, never a separate verb; pipes *glow*; fuel edges *glint*; glowsticks *wane*; the beacon *sweeps*. "Flicker" never describes a fixture, lantern, beacon, beam or glowstick. **Avoid:** fog, fog of war, vision, visibility, light level, darkness meter, stealth light, exposure (player-facing), lux, flashlight, torch (for the beam), strobe, blackout (as a scripted event), mood lighting. **Tone:** quiet, physical, maritime; lighting is described as a ship's lamps are described by the crew who keep them, never as a renderer or a horror effect.

### Reference techniques (adopt / reject)

| Reference | Adopt | Reject |
|---|---|---|
| *Pandorum* | Emergency lighting in a derelict as the first thing you see on waking; the chest lamp swinging with the body (user decision) | Strobing, chaotic emergency lights in action scenes (PIPE PR-13; P2) |
| *Beyond the Aquila Rift* | The kind-looking lie whose wrongness is at the edges: the decoy's violet fringe | A lie with no tell (P4 corollary) |

### Anti-pillar guard rails this GDD states

1. **No meter of light**: no light gem, exposure ring, brightness readout, light-level text or world-space light marker (P3).
2. **No vignette, no fog mask, no full-frame darkening, no gamma or brightness slider.** HR-15 (hull-only, 0..`HR15_STEP_MAX`) and reduce-flashing are the only player controls (PIPE PR-13 (d)).
3. **No light that does nothing physical and no light that changes for mood**; no scripted blackouts or ambient flicker (anti-pillar "no authored story").
4. **The beam reveals only**: no light repels, burns, stuns or damages; VS-A flares and torches repel by Horror's rules, never by Lighting's (anti-pillar "not a combat game"; EM Open Question 2).
5. **Darkness costs nothing by itself**: no dark debuff effect, no sanity drain rendered as darkness (EM Open Question 4).
6. **No light progression**: no brighter-beam unlocks, no shadow-quality "upgrades"; LOD tiers are performance, never reward.
7. **Depth never changes hue or reach**, only the ambient floor and iridescence (ART 4.3).
8. **Lighting is not a builder**: lights come with welded modules and authored sections; the glowstick is the only player-placed light and it is consumed (anti-pillar "not a freeform builder").
9. **Shining a beam on a crewmate does nothing mechanical** (no intentional PvP, VS-B), and no modifier, decoy or LOD ever rewrites a beam's colour or shape.
10. **Horrors cast no shadow and emit nothing**, so "a shadow with no occluder" stays unambiguous as a sanity tell (ART 3.2; EM-20).

## Detailed Design

### Core Rules

**Conventions.** Rules are `LO-n` (the prefix is free in every sibling GDD). Formulas are referred to by their registry names (`soft_pool_scale`, `hard_cone_scale`, `modifier_stack`, `flash_governor`, `ambient_value`, `hull_floor`, `shadow_slot_assign`, `cut_order`, `lod_tier`, `per_rung_lit_pixels`, `texture_magnification`, `warmup_ramp`, `glow_threshold_bound`, `light_energy_from_pm`, `gutter_recipe`, `flare_flicker`, `decoy_budget`, `edge_width_screen_px`, `beam_offset`); the Formulas section assigns their `D.n`, and every formula that is not this GDD's is qualified (`EM D.7`, `Graph D.6`, `PIPE D.5`, `PC D.12`, `kernel D.10`). Every engine statement carries `[REF]` (verified from `docs/engine-reference/godot/`), `[BIN]` (existence, type or default only, from the installed 4.7.2 class dump in ART 8.4/8.10) or `[UNV]` (unverified; the named spike step settles it, ART 8.12). Every number this GDD introduces is a placeholder, owner technical-artist, "confirmed by the Lighting spike step n"; every borrowed number is used by its registry name and value and is never restated differently (a needed different value is a CONFLICT surfaced in Open Questions, never a silent change). Rule text is behaviour; where an implementation choice would be needed it is a "-> ADR candidate" note. Zoom-dependent statements hold at **every** rung `k_z` in [1, `k_auto` + 2] and under **both** render passes (PIPE PR-7), on 1080p (`k_auto` 3), 1440p (`k_auto` 4), the Deck (1280x800, `expand`, `k_auto` 2) and 2160p (`k_auto` 6), not only at the default zoom.

**LO-1. Rendering only; Lighting reads, the simulation never reads Lighting.** Lighting is a `PRESENTATION` subscriber (KERN:108-113). It reads the last-committed `SIM` views (`emitter.state` and its `shapes()` table, Layout's `OccluderSource`) and, for decoys and module-body glows only, `PERCEPTION_OUT` lists; its notification handlers only mark dirty (kernel CR-10: handler = dirty mark only, writes disabled in handlers) and all drawing happens in the render callback. It writes only `CanvasItem`, `Material`, `Light2D`, `LightOccluder2D` and `CanvasModulate` properties, all inside layer 0 of the world viewport that the Pipeline owns. It owns no simulation fact, no reach, no output, no `lit`; it never writes an emitter, never reports a cull to the simulation (a sim-lit but unrendered light would be a P1 lie, so the answer to over-budget is demotion, LO-23, not silence), and registers **no state block, no step hook and no `after:` entry**. Hook ids appear in this document only as things Lighting does *not* order against (`emitter.place`, `body.integrate`) and never in an example. The occluder builder (LO-11) is the one derived-object duty and is classed `PROJECTOR` (KERN:112); the kernel marks that reading provisional pending a technical-director ruling (KERN:117, Open Question 1) and this GDD does not pre-empt it (-> ADR candidate: occluder projector class). The test for ownership (mirroring Emitter State guard G1): if a sentence needs a `Light2D` to be true it belongs here, otherwise it belongs to the owner and is cited by name.

**LO-2. The member split, and the one ordering dependency it creates (orchestrator ruling R3, option B).** Lighting writes every *value* member of every light: `enabled`, `energy`, `color`, `texture`, `texture_scale`, `offset`, `shadow_enabled`, `shadow_filter`, `shadow_color`, `shadow_item_cull_mask`, `range_*`, `blend_mode`, `height` (all `[BIN]` existence). Lighting also writes the *pose* members (`position`, `rotation`) of every light **except the local seat's beam**, whose `position` and `rotation` the Avatar Presenter writes from raw aim in the same frame (PC-5: the local beam's `Light2D` rotation, continuous, in the same frame). The Presenter's every other `Light2D` write is forbidden: `enabled`, `energy`, `texture` and `color` are read from `emitter.state` by Lighting, so PC :951 is satisfied vacuously and PC :1062 (the Presenter's `Light2D` writes are exactly `position` and `rotation`) holds as written. The Perception Filter writes **no** `Light2D` member of any kind. The tool layer carries no light, and no `Light2D` is a child of, or positioned by, the tool layer (hands-free chest lamp, LO-28). The beam node's parent is a non-rotating body-frame node, so that a rotated `offset` is never composed with an avatar rotation (lint). Under shadow-origin outcome B (LO-10) the Presenter's `position` write is the **floored foot** (the `body.state` pose, the same floor as the sprite) and Lighting writes `offset` so the texture apex lands on the chest point; under outcome A the Presenter's `position` is the **floored chest point** and `offset` carries only the texture's own apex term, which is non-zero by default because the apex sits at a texture edge (LO-15), so under both outcomes Lighting writes `offset` every render frame. **The ordering dependency, stated:** under outcome B Lighting's `offset` is a function of the rotation the Presenter wrote that same frame (`beam_offset`), so within one render frame the Presenter's pose write precedes Lighting's apply step, always; this is an ordering inside the render callback (no hook, no block, no `after:` entry) and is one-directional (the Presenter never reads Lighting). The earlier claim that the two writers "have nothing to race over" remains true, but the dependency is real; if the Presenter's rotation changed after Lighting's `offset` write the apex would be off by up to `|chest − foot| × sin(Δθ)` (1.7 px for PC-5's accepted 2.8 degrees), which the apex-equals-chest lint (≤ 0.01 px every render frame) catches. -> ADR candidate: presentation frame order (a single presentation driver calling Presenter, Lighting, Filter, diagnostics in a fixed order, rather than per-system `process_priority`).

**LO-3. Read model, cadence and determinism.** Lighting wakes on the dirty keys `E/<emitter id>` (`(KIND << 32) | id`; fired on create, remove, `switch_on` flip, `shape_id` change, an `output_pm` bucket crossing of `OUTPUT_DIRTY_QUANTUM_PM`, an integer `reach_px` step; pose never dirties) and polls pose at render rate. **The `OUTPUT_DIRTY_QUANTUM_PM` bucket is a wake-up granularity, never a value granularity:** every geometry-bearing quantity (`r_lit`, `texture_scale`, drawn energy) is computed from the *exact* `output_pm` and `reach_px` of the last committed tick, re-sampled each render frame for every SOFT light and for any light not in STEADY (reason: `r_lit` is steep at low output, `dr/d(output_pm) = R × LIT_ON_PM / output_pm²`, about 0.24 px per permille for a 144 px fixture at 300 permille, so a 49-permille lag would be roughly 12 px of edge error, ESTIMATE). Work runs at three cadences: **per committed tick** (a change in the read view's `tick`, observed at the render callback): the active set, slot assignment, demotion order, LOD tier, ambient value, occluder sync, using committed inputs only; **per render frame**: pose of moving lights, the modifier chain value, the governor, envelopes, the apply step; **on event**: camera cut, a rung change, a window or pass change, a restore, `LOADING`/`TRANSIT_HOLD` exit, a settings change, the `ALL` dirty collapse (more than `MAX_DIRTY_KEYS` keys). Dirty keys are an optimisation hint only: a full reconcile of every record runs on every event and every `FULL_RECONCILE_TICKS` (placeholder 60), and the incremental result must equal the full result (debug assertion); correctness never depends on a notification arriving. **Determinism:** every Lighting decision (which emitters are active, slot assignment, demotion order, the modifier chain, the LOD tier, the governor outcome, every member value) is a pure function of the read-view snapshot, the committed camera field, the present clock, the per-client settings and the previous decision, so a replay and a live run produce identical bindings and identical demotion order (-> ADR candidate: a plan step computed headlessly and a separate apply step that alone names `Light2D`; this is also the testability seam the coding standard asks for). **Fixed pool:** Lighting instantiates a fixed pool of lights, stand-ins, decoy sprites and occluder objects at boot; nothing is created or freed during play; a bound emitter keeps its node while eligible (sticky binding, so reordering the demotion list does not rebind lights); every member write is skipped when its value is unchanged (write-on-change). **First observation is never a transition (EP-15):** the first time a light is observed after boot, a restore, a hold exit, a camera cut, a body or pool re-bind, it enters STEADY at its L0 value with no warm-up, no fade and no governor history; warm-up fires only on an observed `output_pm` change from 0 to non-zero between two consecutive committed ticks. **Active set:** an emitter is active iff its reach circle intersects the committed field rectangle expanded by `VIEW_MARGIN_PX` (`VIEW_MARGIN_PX`, placeholder 24 px: the minimum margin is `V_RUN` 3 px per tick × `MAX_TICKS_PER_FRAME` 4 = 12 px of camera travel between committed evaluations, and the placeholder keeps a 2× allowance for camera look-ahead, confirmed at spike step 3, so a light scrolling into view already exists); the test is integer-only so the set is reproducible. A read-view path that does not allocate per poll (struct-of-arrays columns or a caller-provided buffer, plus a monotone `tick` stamp on the view) is asked of Emitter State as Ask E-F (-> ADR candidate), and "newest light" is defined as the **higher emitter id** (ids are monotone, EM-28; Ask E-G confirms), which is reproducible after a restore.

**LO-4. Clocks, and what a "frame" means in a knob.** Three clocks, each with one job. The **kernel clock** (`tick` and `tick_alpha`) drives everything the simulation also computes and that `lit` depends on: the beacon's aim, beam and deployable poses, a glowstick's wane. `present_time` (Pipeline PR-9; frozen while `PAUSED`) drives purely cosmetic phase and duration: gutter dips, flare micro-flicker, fringe breathing, warm-up and fade envelopes, the governor's rolling window. The tick counter is never used for anything animated (PIPE PR-8 rule 6) and wall-clock is never used. **The `*_FRAMES` knobs have two units, stated once here, because LO-3 runs two cadences.** The per-render-frame envelopes (`LAMP_WARM_FRAMES`, `SHADOW_FADE_FRAMES`, `CUT_FADE_FRAMES`, `AMBIENT_SLEW_FRAMES`) count **1/`TICK_RATE` of a second of `present_time`** (one frame = 16.67 ms at `TICK_RATE` 60), independent of the render rate, so `LAMP_WARM_FRAMES` 5 is 83.3 ms at 30, 60 or 144 fps. The per-committed-tick decisions (`SLOT_DWELL_FRAMES`, which LO-9 counts in consecutive ticks, `CUT_HYSTERESIS_FRAMES`, `FULL_RECONCILE_TICKS`) count **committed ticks**, observed at the per-tick cadence. The two units are equal whenever the kernel keeps up and diverge only under simulation lag (kernel CR-17), when a decision is slower in wall time but never reordered. Every other time quantity in this GDD is written in seconds or in the unit of its own clock (the Tuning Knobs table carries a Unit column); the names are those of the ruling. While the kernel is `PAUSED`, envelopes, gutters, the governor window and flare phase freeze and the beacon's drawn aim holds because the kernel clock holds. **The beacon's drawn aim is the kernel clock's `beacon_aim(T)` interpolated with `tick_alpha`, not `present_time`**: the drawn cone must equal the cone `lit` uses (P1), and `present_time` and the kernel clock diverge under simulation lag (kernel CR-17) and after a restore. PIPE PR-9 lists "the beacon's sweep phase" among `present_time`'s consumers; that is a refresh owed (Cross-References), and only the beacon's non-geometric cosmetic phase (if any) stays on `present_time`.

**LO-6. Hard cones are drawn to `reach_px` and nothing else (`hard_cone_scale`).** For every HARD row (1-4, 8) the texture's hard outer edge sits at the table's `reach_px` ±1 px measured along the aim axis in base px (the rendered-reach lint, EM-26), at every rung and under both passes. `texture_scale` is `reach_px` divided by the texture's *measured* edge radius (a sidecar produced by a build tool that scans the imported image, LO-15), so a re-import at another resolution cannot move the drawn edge. The **lateral** edge of the cone is a hard step of the same edge width at the table half-angle; shape 3 is one two-lobe texture whose lobe axes sit at aim ±206 units (each lobe 114 units half-angle, the wedge between them 184 units = 16.17 degrees that never closes), so it is one light and one shadow slot. The texture's rim level is `EDGE_FLOOR_PM / 1000` of the apex and its interior follows the simulation's HARD profile `f = EDGE_FLOOR_PM + (1000 − EDGE_FLOOR_PM) × F / 1000` with `F` the LINEAR fall-off (`EM D.7`); both are **generated from the registry values by a tool** (-> ADR candidate: light textures are generated from the shape table and never hand-painted), so a confirmed change to `EDGE_FLOOR_PM` regenerates them and the lint stays honest. Divergence between the drawn cone (raw aim) and the simulated cone (committed aim) of at most 2.8 degrees and one tick is accepted and agreement is promised only at rest (PC-5; at the narrow cone's rim 2.8 degrees is 18.8 px lateral, arithmetic); the identity channel is shape and colour from the replicated loadout, never aim. At `output_pm` below 600 the hard rim is below `LIT_ON_PM` (`EM D.7`) and the rendered rim step shrinks accordingly; the edge stays at `reach_px` ±1 px. That is not an MVP case (Suit rules out charge dimming), but the mechanism is generic.

**LO-7. Soft pools are drawn to the readable radius `r_lit`, never to `reach_px` (`soft_pool_scale`; orchestrator ruling R8).** Two radii are defined once. `reach_px` is the simulation's **fade-out** radius (Emitter State). `r_lit = floor(R × (output_pm − LIT_ON_PM) / output_pm)` for `output_pm > LIT_ON_PM`, else 0 (`EM D.7`, reused by name) is the **readable** radius: the distance at which a registered subject stops being actionable. For every SOFT row (5-7, 9, 10) the pool's visible boundary is drawn at `r_draw = max(r_lit, POOL_RESIDUAL_PX)` (`POOL_RESIDUAL_PX` placeholder 8 px, range 0-16), and `texture_scale = r_draw / R_tex`, so that the pool visibly ends where the HUD prompt ends (the sim/render agreement of EM AC-55/AC-56 and the load-bearing P1 test of the lighting stack). This answers EM Open Question 12's "3 tiles / 2 tiles" question: the art bible and Item DB radii are the fade-out `reach_px`; the readable pool is `r_lit`, pending spike step 2. **Interior profile (orchestrator ruling, logged): the simulation's own curve truncated at `r_lit`**, not renormalised to zero there. The simulation's illumination at the readable edge is `LIT_ON_PM` (a non-zero rim of `LIT_ON_PM / output_pm` of the apex), so a profile renormalised to zero at `r_lit` would make the pool visibly end about 21 px *inside* `r_lit` on mid-Graphite (ESTIMATE); truncation meets AC-56 within about 3 px on mid-Graphite and exactly on bright plates, and keeps the interior soft. **The truncated profile ends in a low step of `LIT_ON_PM / output_pm` of the apex at `r_lit`** (15 % at full output, `EM D.7`'s own rim): on a pool ART 8.3 :1085 calls "soft, no hard edge" that step is a low hard edge, and it is the readable edge (P1: the prompt stops there), so it is stated here and not hidden. The interior shape remains data (`LINEAR` today; replacement `TABLE17`, 17 knots, monotone) so the spike may reshape it without an API change; the rim height can only come from the texture (a `Light2D` has no per-light shader `[UNV]`), so one baked rim ratio (profile mode `FIXED`, default, baked for full output) is compared at spike step 2 with a profile family of one texture per `OUTPUT_DIRTY_QUANTUM_PM` bucket (`FAMILY17`) and with a raised-rim hard disc; the geometric edge, and therefore the prompt, is unmoved in all three. **A tell dip lowers apex energy but not `r_lit`**, so on a mid plane the *perceived* edge moves inward while the actionable edge does not (ESTIMATE: about 11 px for a 30 % dip); this is an honest dimming and AC-56 is evaluated at full modulation. **Gap closed: `output_pm` at or below `LIT_ON_PM` (`r_lit` = 0).** The pool is drawn at `POOL_RESIDUAL_PX` with energy proportional to `output_pm`, visibly dim and visibly tiny, never a readable floor pool, so a starved lamp still shows its brightness (consequence channel) without an actionable-looking area; the radius is never smaller than the actionable radius, so a prompt can never appear outside the visible pool; the Debug Overlay reports `r_lit` = 0. The sub-threshold tail between `r_lit` and `reach_px` is not drawn; the consequence, **two soft skirts that sum to `lit` in the simulation where the render shows near-black**, is bounded and rare, and an optional refresh (Emitter State clamping SOFT contributions beyond `r_lit`) is routed only if spike step 2 shows it. A pool drawn at `r_lit` costs about 28 % less shaded area than one drawn to `reach_px` for a full fixture (arithmetic, 122² against 144²), a free reduction.

**LO-8. Energy and hue come from the read view and nothing else (`light_energy_from_pm`).** A real light's drawn energy is `LIGHT_ENERGY_PER_PM × KIND_GAIN[kind] × output_pm`, then the modifier chain (LO-18), then the governor (LO-22); it never exceeds the first term, so **a lamp never renders brighter than the power it is served** (`EM D.1`, Graph D.6). `LIGHT_ENERGY_PER_PM` (placeholder 0.001, so `E_1000` = 1.0 at full output) is bounded below by rim legibility on mid planes and above by bloom (`glow_threshold_bound`, LO-14); `KIND_GAIN[kind]` (placeholder 1.0 each, range 0.6-1.4) trims fixtures against beams without touching the shared constant. A lamp's brightness is on the Graph's closed **consequence channel** (LSG:44, :168), so Lighting reads it from `emitter.state` (SIM), never from `PERCEPTION_OUT`. **LO-11. Occluders: one source for collision, light and shadow (ruled R17; the `PROJECTOR` duty).** The occluder builder reads Layout's `OccluderSource` (designed: Layout LY-12): `occluder_source(body_id) -> OccluderSource` per body, `rects_in(aabb)` answering in that body's frame, `version(section_id)` a monotone u32 counter not persisted and reset to 1 on restore (LY-12 d: consumers treat a restore as an unconditional rebuild, because a post-restore `version` can numerically equal a pre-restore cached value), and a flags byte `SOLID | OCCLUDES_LIGHT | DOOR_LEAF | PROP | MACHINE | HULL` (LY-7). **LO-13. **LO-14. Emission glow, and which channel it reads (ruled R18, with the shader finding on summed light).** Lighting owns the `_msk` medium-fill term, the unshaded additive emissive layer and `GLOW_HDR_THRESHOLD` (default 1.0 `[BIN]`, placeholder 1.3 pending step 8; glow processes **before** tonemapping with screen blending since 4.6 `[REF]`, so no older tuning transfers). `_msk` has two consumers, so there are two shaders: HullSurface (mix blend, lit) uses `_msk` only as a lit hue tint of *marks*; the **Emissive** shader (add blend, unshaded; blend mode is fixed at compile time `[UNV]`) carries the flow scroll of loop media, running trails and arcs and exists only for loops with `served_pm` above 0, so a dead loop has no emission (ART 8.3, 4.2). Pipes are emissive overlays and expose nothing (FT-27, EM-17); only NODE_LANTERN emitters cast light at machinery nodes. **Threshold bound (`glow_threshold_bound`):** `GLOW_HDR_THRESHOLD` must sit above the brightest lit trail albedo under the *summed* per-channel light at a pixel (the "colour-neutral energy 1.1" figure of F&T TA-F13 understates it: a beam apex over a full fixture apex sums to 2.0 in the red channel, which blooms a Coolant Ice stamp at the default threshold), below the weakest *blooming* loop token's emission, with the fringe, every decoy and every stand-in below 0.9 of it by construction; if the bounds cross, the response is to lower the beam's `LIGHT_ENERGY_PER_PM` ceiling, never to raise the threshold past its upper bound. The Integrity loop (Hull Grey, max channel 0.332) cannot bloom at any reasonable gain and is declared a **non-blooming token**: its running state reads as a pale sheen, a statement the art-director must ratify because Principle 3 says every running loop is visible as emission. **LO-15. Light textures: formats, generation and the layout choice (registry: `light_texture_layout`).** Per ART 8.3: `light_` textures are greyscale 8-bit PNG with soft alpha allowed and linear filter; 2048 for the beam shapes and the beacon, 512 for pools (deployable, glowstick, lantern and fault glow share one), 1024 for the fixture; the importer `size_limit` is 1024 under pass A and 2048 under pass B, with mipmaps off under A and on under B (ART 8.4 preset). Hard-edged textures carry an authored edge band of `EDGE_TEXELS` texels (placeholder 2) so the edge is one base px under pass A and 1-2 screen px under pass B. Hard-edged beam and beacon textures import **lossless** (block compression moves the edge by sub-block amounts and would break the ±1 px lint); pool gradients may compress if the spike shows no banding. Every texture carries a **measured sidecar** (`edge_radius_texels`, `apex_offset_texels`) produced from the imported image, the runtime scale is `reach_px` or `r_draw` over the sidecar radius, and the reach lint becomes a headless image test: `|reach_px − edge_radius_texels × texture_scale| ≤ 1` at every `reach_px` the shape can take; a sidecar that disagrees with its image fails the build. Texture layout is a spike-decided choice behind that contract: **the default is the art bible's 2048² square with the apex at the midpoint of one edge (the arithmetic of ART 8.3 :1070, where a 7-tile beam of 336 base px stays sharp at 6x because 336 × 6 = 2016 ≤ 2048, and of :1075, where 2048 / 6 / 48 = 7.1 tiles, only holds when the reach spans the full 2048 side), and a tight non-square box with the apex at the long edge (long side at most 2048, sector bounding box plus `LIGHT_TEX_MARGIN_PX` 8) is offered as an option for spike steps 2 and 3**, because it saves lateral fill (the narrow cone's half-width at reach is 384 × sin 12° = 80 px, 426 of the 1024 lateral texels of the square), cuts shaded fill 4x-19x and removes the pass-B memory overrun (the square set is about 16 MiB, 21 MiB with mipmaps, against the art bible's 20 MB estimate); it requires non-square textures `[BIN]` and changes the ART 8.3/8.4 wording (Cross-References). **Under the default the apex is at a texture edge, so `apex_offset_texels` in the sidecar is non-zero and `a_tex` in `beam_offset` (LO-10) is non-zero under both shadow-origin outcomes** (for the wide beam at pass B, `a_tex` = (−144, 0) base px). Whether the importer expands greyscale to RGBA8 (memory x4), whether textures are sampled from an engine atlas (no mipmaps), and which channel carries the profile (a squared profile would shorten the apparent reach) are `[UNV]` with probes at step 2 (2a, 2b). Textures are loaded once at boot behind the hold quad; nothing is loaded in play.

**LO-16. Zoom changes no light value; what a rung does change (the per-rung thread).** The ladder is implemented through the world viewport's logical size (PIPE C6, D.5), never `Camera2D.zoom`. With the art bible's layout (a 2048² square whose apex is the midpoint of one edge, so the reach spans the full 2048 side, LO-15) the density is `2048 / reach_px` texels per base px: wide 7.11, split 6.10, narrow and beacon 5.33, fan 12.19. Under pass B a texture is minified at every rung below that figure and magnified only above it: the narrow cone and the beacon above `k_z` 5.33 (the art bible's "~6x", ART 8.3 :1070-1075), which is reachable only at `k_z` 6 on 1440p (1.13x, the one 1440p case) and at `k_z` 6-8 on 2160p (1.13x, 1.31x, 1.50x), never on 1080p (ladder maximum 5) or the Deck (maximum 4); the split above `k_z` 6.10 (2160p `k_z` 7-8, 1.15x and 1.31x); the wide beam at `k_z` 8 only (1.13x); the fan never. The rim softens slightly where a texture magnifies, accepted by ART 8.3, with the tight non-square layout of LO-15 as a remedy. **Shadow *positions* are `k_z`-independent** (occluders are in base px); shadow-edge stepping is a whole base px (= `k_z` screen px) under pass A and window resolution under pass B; the shadow edge error at maximum reach (about `2πR / N_ang` base px for an `N_ang`-sample shadow map: 1.18 px at 384 px reach for 2048 samples, ESTIMATE on an `[UNV]` model) is measured at spike step 3 for both passes. **LO-18. Rejected: additive offsets (they can exceed `output_pm`, breaking "a lamp never renders brighter than the power it is served"); last-writer-wins (non-deterministic with two sources).

**LO-19. **LO-22. The flash governor (`flash_governor`; ruled R26 with the engine findings F9 and the counting rule).** The pipeline hosts the frame-level invariants and Lighting owns the per-light clamp (PIPE PR-13 (a), (b)). A *flash* is a pair of opposing luminance changes of at least 10 % relative luminance, red included (locked, REG:616); `FLASH_MAX_PER_S` (3, locked, REG:1362) flashes per rolling 1 s are allowed **per light, per region and on the frame mean**. *Counting rule (conservative):* a *transition* is a monotone run of drawn energy between two local extrema and it *counts* when its magnitude is at least 10 % of the energy at its start; a flash is two opposing counted transitions completing within the window; the 8 % micro-flicker never counts because each reversal is below 10 %, and its generator reverses direction at least every run so runs cannot accumulate. The governor works on **modelled** luminance steps from the decision (drawn-energy changes, a proxy that over-delays and never under-delays: a request counts whenever the dominant light's apex energy changes by at least 10 %, ignoring dilution by other lights), never on GPU read-back; the capture-based flash scan (PIPE PR-13 (f)) is the offline arbiter and part of the spike, not of the shipping path. *Window:* a flash completing at time `t` is allowed iff fewer than `FLASH_MAX_PER_S` flashes completed in `(t − 1 s, t]` of `present_time`; otherwise it is delayed to the earliest `t'` at which the oldest leaves the window, rounded up to the next render frame. *Region:* one cell of a 3x3 grid over the **displayed picture at the current field** (a ninth of the picture); at the default field this is about 213x120 base px, as PIPE D.15 states, and it scales with the field at every other rung so a screen-ninth can never exceed 3 flashes (PIPE D.15's "213x120 base px, fixed" wording is a refresh owed); a light contributes its flashes to every cell its quad overlaps (an overcount, the safe side). The governor **delays** a requested transition and never brightens or darkens beyond the requested pair; two individually compliant lights may sum past 3 in a region, which the regional pass catches; the transitions of the **newer light** (the higher emitter id) are delayed first. It governs only Lighting-originated and recipe transitions (L1-L3, fades, stand-in crossfades, decoy alpha ramps, ambient slew on the frame mean). **It never delays a simulation-committed on/off or aim of a BEAM, the BEACON or a FAULT_GLOW onset, nor a committed `lit`-bearing change (orchestrator ruling: the picture must never disagree with `lit`, and R26's "requested transition" is read as a Lighting-originated one).** The compliant toggle rate of a beam is therefore a simulation-side bound: Ask E-E to Player Controller and Emitter State, a command-level cooldown of at least `ticks_for_seconds(1 / (2 × FLASH_MAX_PER_S))` = 10 ticks between flips so a player-caused on/off pair cannot complete more than `FLASH_MAX_PER_S` times per second (EM D.2's 0-5-tick switch latency bounds the delay, not the rate); if the governor ever finds it would have to delay such a transition, that is a defect, logged as a DIAGNOSTIC and failed by the rapid-toggle stub. F&T's `arc_flash_share` (REG:978) is a consumer-side rate the governor backstops, never a second bound; the emissive layer's luminance values (FT AC-140) are counted on the post-gain value. Diagnostics log `(light_id, F, window_start_tick, clamped_transitions)`. Reduce-flashing semantics are LO-26's. A rolling counter is four timestamps per light, region and the frame mean (no allocation).

**LO-23. The authoring lint adds a per-chunk count (lights whose quad can overlap any one chunk at once at most `LIGHTS_PER_ITEM_MAX`, placeholder 12), a runtime defect report fires at 14, the chunk size is an ADR value (placeholder 4 tiles = 192 px per side, the smallest pool that matters; smaller is safer for lights and costlier for batching, spike step 5), and a light dropped by the engine is a render bug reported to diagnostics and caught by a criterion that renders more than 16 overlapping lights on one chunk (-> ADR candidate: tilemap chunk size and the light-overlap lint). The authoring lint inputs (the per-view counts) come from Derelict Authoring. **The reverse lie is not a budget matter (CD-GDD-ALIGN revision).** This rule closes sim-lit and render-unlit; it does not cover render-lit and sim-dark, a bound non-shadow light drawing its pool across a wall into a compartment the simulation calls dark. That case is closed by the containment rule LO-33, and a demoted light's stand-in is bound by it too (its radius is clamped), because an additive overlay blob crosses a wall as freely as a pool does.

No real light includes bit 2 or bit 3, which is the structural reason a decoy can never light geometry. Occluder masks follow `shadow_item_cull_mask`. **Cross-body isolation (orchestrator ruling):** all bodies draw into one canvas, and a `Light2D` lights every item whose mask intersects its own regardless of body; EM-30 says that in the MVP a light lights only its own body, so each live body gets partition bits and every light and occluder carries **only its own partition bit(s)**, and a foreign partition bit, or bit 1, on a light is a lint failure while partitioning is on. **Intersection finding (CD-GDD-ALIGN revision, logged as C-MASK):** a `Light2D` lights an item whose `light_mask` shares any bit with the light's `range_item_cull_mask` `[UNV]` (the engine reference carries nothing on cull masks, so this is the pre-cutoff API behaviour, confirmed at spike step 4). A light that also carried bit 1 would therefore light every bit-1 item of every body and the body bits would do nothing; the earlier text `BIT(1) | BIT(body)` is superseded. Items carry bit 1 and their partition bits, lights carry the partition bits and not bit 1. Lights of other bodies that are in view are still bound and still count against the caps (their presence is not hidden: a sim-lit but unrendered light would be a P1 lie); they simply light only their own body's items. If more bodies are live than `BODIES_LIVE_MAX`, the surplus bodies' lights are drawn as stand-ins (reason OTHER_BODY, outside the budget). The gameplay surfaces of this system are the lit picture and what it gates by proxy: prompts are the HUD's, gated by `lit(ref)` from Emitter State, never by the renderer (PIPE :104 (d)); the HUD, hold quad, menus and debug are other viewports or CanvasLayers and are unlit by construction. Debug Overlay surfaces (`DIAGNOSTIC`, dev builds only, absent from release): occluder outlines (`draw_line` width at least 1 base px, no AA feather `[REF]`), slot assignment, the modifier stack per light, the governor log, `r_lit` circles, the LOD tier and the `diag.*` counters.

**LO-32. Spike-gated decisions: which step decides what.** The ten-step plan is ART 8.12 (PIPE proposes steps 11-13: stretch and window, motion integrity, pacing). Lighting does not own `RENDER_MODE`; it participates in step 2 and must work under either result. "Confirmed by the spike" means confirmed or **revised with a logged conflict**, never restated silently. Step 4 (the mask and per-item probe) also decides the containment mechanism of LO-33.

**LO-33. Containment: a non-shadow light draws only where the simulation's segment can reach (CD-GDD-ALIGN revision; named P1 divergence PD-1 until spike step 4 closes it; owners technical-artist and engine-programmer).**

*The gap this rule closes.* Emitter State occludes every emitter in the simulation (EM-20: the closed, conservative segment test over Layout's `OCCLUDES_LIGHT` occupied spans, covering its static layer of FIXTURE, NODE_LANTERN, FAULT_GLOW and resting DEPLOYABLE as well as the dynamic BEAM and BEACON; it fails safe to dark). In the render only the six shadow-slot lights (LO-9) are checked against `LightOccluder2D`. Every other real light is a non-shadow `PointLight2D` that lights every item whose mask intersects its own inside its quad, so in the MVP only the beam, the beacon and the cryo lamp stop at a bulkhead on screen. A full fixture draws a pool of `r_draw` up to 122 px (2.5 tiles); behind a one-cell wall (24 px) that pool can reach up to 122 − 24 = 98 px into the next compartment (arithmetic, worst case with the fixture touching its own wall face). Without this rule the sane picture lights a room the simulation calls dark, with no violet leak: a P1 lie, free information about neighbouring rooms, and a break of "go dark is legible as light" (P3) when a neighbour's pool spills into a conserved room. LO-7 (sim-lit skirts the render shows dark) and LO-23 (sim-lit lights left unrendered) cover the opposite direction, and the radius lints cover radius only.

*The invariant (I-CONTAIN).* For every real light `L` and every lit item or registered subject `s` in view: if the simulation's segment test (EM-20) says the segment from `L`'s origin to `s` is blocked, then `L`'s drawn light does not contribute to `s`'s pixels. Shadow-slot lights meet it through their occluders (LO-11). Every other real light (FIXTURE not EMERGENCY, NODE_LANTERN, FAULT_GLOW, a glowstick not holding slot 6, and demoted stand-ins) meets it through the mechanism below. **Containment errs toward the dark** wherever it cannot be exact: a missing sliver of pool is bounded and still shown by the housing and the consequence channel, while a pool through a wall leaks information the player has no way to falsify (P4: no lie unfalsifiable).

*Candidate mechanisms, in preference order; spike step 4 decides, and a result that moves a value is a logged conflict (LO-32).*

- **C1 (preferred): compartment-colour mask bits.** Extends the pattern of LO-24 (partition bits, any-shared-bit semantics `[UNV]`). `bit(body_index, colour)` is defined in LO-24; `CONTAIN_COLOURS` is a placeholder of 4 (range 1-6; 1 means containment off, one bit per body). The authoring lint colours each body's compartment adjacency graph greedily so that **no two compartments whose floor regions lie within `R_DRAW_MAX_PX + WALL_FOOTPRINT_DEPTH` of each other share a colour**, where `R_DRAW_MAX_PX` is the largest `r_lit` at 1000 permille among non-shadow kinds in the shape table (122 px, FIXTURE R 144, for the MVP) and `WALL_FOOTPRINT_DEPTH` is 24 px (EM-20). The MVP bodies hold 5, 4 and 5 compartments, so at most 5 colours are ever needed, and the lint reports the number it needed (Derelict Authoring's authored layouts need 3, within `CONTAIN_COLOURS` 4: DA C-9); a body that needs more than `CONTAIN_COLOURS` fails authoring. **Items:** floor and prop chunks are compartment-aligned (a chunk never spans two compartments, so the chunk of LO-23 splits at a wall line; the ADR value `ITEM_CHUNK_TILES` stays a maximum, not a fixed square); a wall cell, a door-aperture cell and a character footprint carry the colour of every compartment they overlap. **Lights:** a non-shadow light carries exactly one bit, its own compartment's colour for its body; a shadow-slot light carries every colour bit of its body, because its containment is its occluder set and an open doorway must pass its light exactly as the simulation's segment does. A slot-6 swap switches the light's bit set in the same apply step as the slot state, and spike step 3b verifies that no frame shows either a pop or a leak. **Bit budget:** `BODIES_LIVE_MAX` × `CONTAIN_COLOURS` = 4 × 4 = 16 bits at indices 4 to 19, which fits a mask of 20 bits `[UNV]` with bit 20 spare; if step 4 finds fewer than 20, `CONTAIN_COLOURS` falls first (its range floor is 1) and the lint reports the colours the MVP needs, so the cost is known before a bit is spent.
- **C2: per-compartment canvas layers** with `range_layer_min` / `range_layer_max` `[UNV]`, LO-24's existing second choice; it fights the Pipeline's single layer 0 and is taken only if C1's bits do not fit.
- **C3: authoring lint only, defined per wall face by room depth (Derelict Authoring C-9 / OQ-DA-28).** For each non-shadow light and each `OCCLUDES_LIGHT` wall run whose far side is lit floor of **another compartment** (a hull wall onto void or exterior is exempt), the spill depth is `s = max(0, r_draw − d_face − WALL_FOOTPRINT_DEPTH)`, where `d_face` is the distance from the light's origin to that wall's near face along the wall normal and `r_draw` is taken at 1000 permille (the largest `r_lit`), or at the placement's actual `r_lit` for a lamp that is never at full output. A placement passes when `s` is 0 on every such face, i.e. when its origin sits at least `r_draw` − 24 px (98 px for a full 144 px FIXTURE) from each interior face that backs onto another compartment; the lint never demands a room of any width. **The earlier reading is withdrawn:** a 244 px clear span in both axes (2 × 122 px) treated every wall as backing onto a neighbour and, read literally, failed 12 of the 14 authored rooms (DA C-9, PD-1), against ART 6.4 and PIPE:874. A room whose depth between two interior faces is under 2 × (`r_draw` − 24) cannot pass on both faces with a full fixture; there the lamp is moved toward its hull side or kept and logged as a PD-1 exception with its spill depth `s` (interim (i) below), and the room is never resized for the lint. C3 is **a spike input and a cross-check that keeps C1 honest, not a shipping mitigation on its own.**
- **Not available:** raising `SHADOW_SLOTS_MAX` (6, locked; raising it is a CONFLICT to surface and is not made here).

*Open doors.* While a door is open (`leaf_w` < `width_px`, LY-9) the simulation's segment passes through the gap. Under C1 the door cells themselves (the cells the leaf occupies in the wall line) carry both neighbours' colours like any wall cell, and the one-cell **far-side strip** is its own item whose `light_mask` gains the near side's colour **only while `leaf_w` < `width_px`**, applied in the same apply step as the occluder swap of LO-11, so it follows the committed leaf with at most one tick of lag (the same acceptance as LO-11's door text); while the door is closed the strip carries its own colour only and a closed door leaks nothing. The rest of the through-gap light on the far side is drawn dark. **Named step-4 sub-case, wall-face directionality:** a wall cell lit from a north room shows its lit face to a viewer standing south of it, so the bulkhead can read as lit from the south side; the spike measures whether the wall cell must carry directional (north-face and south-face) items rather than both colours on one item, and until it does the wall-cell lit face is accepted as a bounded sub-case counted by `diag.contain_leak`. That is the sim-lit, render-dark class of LO-7 (a prompt may be actionable in a corridor that looks unlit past the strip), accepted as the lesser lie by the dark-erring rule above, bounded by `diag.contain_dark` (count of in-view subjects that are sim-lit and outside every drawn pool) and measured at step 4. A wider strip (`DOOR_SPILL_CELLS`, proposed 1, range 1-3) re-admits render-lit and sim-dark where the through-gap geometry bends behind a corner, and is taken only if a capture shows the dark sliver is unreadable.

*Stand-ins.* A demoted light's emissive stand-in (LO-23) has its radius clamped to the distance from the light's origin to the nearest `OCCLUDES_LIGHT` edge (never above `r_lit` / 2) and is not drawn below 4 px; the same lint derives the clamp.

*Interim, if C1 or C2 cannot be closed before step 4 reports.* PD-1 stays open and is listed in Cross-References E and Open Question 21, with this mitigation in force: **(i) static lights.** Every authored FIXTURE, NODE_LANTERN and FAULT_GLOW placement in the three MVP bodies passes the C3 lint (at the largest `r_lit`, or at the placement's actual `r_lit` for a lamp that is never at full output) or is moved, or the compartment wall is moved; a placement kept with a crossing is logged as a PD-1 exception with its spill depth. **(ii) glowsticks, which the player places at run time.** A resting glowstick's spill beyond a one-cell wall is at most `r_draw` − `d` − 24, which is at most 57 px at the steady 81 px and zero once the stick has waned to `r_draw` 24 px or less (the last waning steps, EM D.9: 20 px at the last step); this bounded spill is the accepted interim divergence, counted by `diag.contain_leak` (in-view non-shadow lights whose `r_draw` disc crosses an `OCCLUDES_LIGHT` rectangle onto lit floor of another compartment) and never reported to the simulation. The interim never changes `lit`, a registry value or the read view. No criterion passes on an interim: AC-LO-68 fails until a mechanism closes PD-1.

*What the rule does not do.* It adds no simulation state, persists nothing, reads only Layout's rectangles and compartment partition (a compartment-of-cell answer from Layout, provisional, Ask E-J), writes no `Light2D` member beyond the existing `range_item_cull_mask` and the stand-in's radius, and is not a lighting mode the player can see or choose. The decoys' structural isolation (bits 2 and 3, LO-24) is unchanged.

### States and Transitions

Lighting persists no state (LO-29), so every table below is *derived*: it is a function of the committed views and the present clock and is rebuilt from scratch after a restore. "Who may cause" names the only party whose input can move the state; Lighting itself moves a state only as the pure function of those inputs (LO-3), and **no state transition is ever reported to the simulation**.

#### S.1 Light lifecycle (one record per pooled light node)

| State | Entry condition | Exit condition | Transitions | Who may cause them |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| ABSENT | no read-view row for the emitter, or the row is not active (not in view, LO-3) | the row becomes active | -> WARMING if `output_pm` goes 0 -> non-zero on an observed committed tick; -> STEADY if first observed already emitting (first observation is never a transition, LO-3) | Emitter State (create, remove, `switch_on`), the camera (view), Layout (section load) |
| WARMING | `output_pm` observed 0 -> non-zero between two committed ticks | the warm-up ramp completes (`LAMP_WARM_FRAMES`), or `output_pm` returns to 0 | -> STEADY on completion; -> ABSENT/FADING on switch-off (instant for a BEAM); the texture radius is already `r_lit` / `reach_px` on the first frame | Emitter State (`output_pm`), the present clock |
| STEADY | warm-up done, or first observed emitting; no steady-modulation dip active | a dip layer starts, the light is ranked out of its cap, the emitter is removed, or `output_pm` falls to 0 | -> MODIFIED on a gutter / flicker / L2 / L3 dip; -> DEMOTED on `cut_order`; -> FADING on removal; SOFT radius re-sampled every frame, HARD radius re-sampled on each integer `reach_px` step | Lighting recipes (gutter, flicker), Attention tier (L2), Horror disturbance (L3), the LOD tier, Emitter State |
| MODIFIED | a steady-modulation layer (gutter, flicker, L2, L3) is active on a FIXTURE / NODE_LANTERN / flare | the dip ends | -> STEADY when the dip ends (the governor may stretch the start of a dip, never deepen it or end it early); energy is at least `MOD_FLOOR_PM` of L0; the radius is unchanged throughout | the submitters' published state; the governor only retimes |
| DEMOTED | `cut_order` ranks it out of the tier cap, or its body has no mask bit | re-promoted by hysteresis (`CUT_HYSTERESIS_FRAMES`), or removal | -> STEADY by crossfade over `CUT_FADE_FRAMES` (no warm-up if it was STEADY before demotion); the node is disabled, an emissive stand-in in the light's own hue is drawn at about `r_lit / 2` | the LOD tier, the in-view count, priority and distance (all deterministic) |
| FADING | removal (glowstick end or evict, section discard, body unload) or a crossfade to a stand-in | energy reaches 0 | -> ABSENT; never a BEAM (a beam switch-off is instant on the commit) | Emitter State, Layout, the LOD tier |

#### What each dependent must repeat

- **Emitter State:** it publishes facts and Lighting conforms; Lighting never writes an emitter and never tells the simulation a light was culled; the rendered reach of every HARD row equals the table reach ±1 px and a soft pool's readable boundary is `r_lit` ±1 px (EM :358).
- **Pipeline:** Lighting renders into the viewport the Pipeline owns; the Pipeline owns no light value; the clamp is Lighting's through the modifier contract.
- **Controller / Presenter:** the Presenter's `Light2D` writes are `position` and `rotation` only, and the Presenter's pose write precedes Lighting's apply in the render callback.
- **Attention, Horror, Perception Filter:** submission is published state Lighting reads and never a call; no submitter touches a beam, the beacon, a fault glow or a glowstick; no submitter changes a real light's radius, position or colour.
- **Layout:** the same rectangles serve collision, light and shadow; a door leaf is one committed rectangle per tick; `version(section_id)` is O(1) and `rects_in` does not allocate per call in the hot path.

## Formulas

Nineteen formulas, numbered `D.1`-`D.19` in the order of the list that closes Core Rules (LO-32); Core Rules keeps citing them by registry name. Every formula that is not this GDD's is qualified (`EM D.7`, `PIPE D.5`, `kernel D.10`) and is reused by name, never restated with another value. **Integer discipline:** Lighting persists and replicates nothing (LO-29), so floats appear only where a value is handed to the engine in the apply step (`energy`, `texture_scale`, `offset`); every *decision* quantity (counts, ranks, the permille chain, the governor window, hashes, tier boundaries) is an integer, and every decision is a pure function of the committed read view, the committed camera field, the present clock, the per-client settings and the previous decision (LO-3). Every number introduced here is a placeholder, owner technical-artist, "confirmed by the Lighting spike step n"; every `ESTIMATE` is arithmetic on a stated model, not a measurement. Pixel and texel counts are in base px unless a column says screen px.

### D.1 Light energy from output

The `light_energy_from_pm` formula is defined as:

`E_L0 = LIGHT_ENERGY_PER_PM × KIND_GAIN[kind] × output_pm`

**Variables:**

| Variable | Type | Range | Source | Description |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `output_pm` | int | 0..1000 | `emitter.state` read view (SIM) | served brightness; the only source of a real light's brightness (LO-8) |
| `LIGHT_ENERGY_PER_PM` | float | 0.0005..0.0015; placeholder 0.001 | ★ owner technical-artist; spike steps 2, 7, 8 | engine energy per permille; `E_1000` = `LIGHT_ENERGY_PER_PM` × 1000 |
| `KIND_GAIN[kind]` | float | 0.6..1.4; placeholder 1.0 each | Tuning Knobs | per-kind trim so fixtures can sit under beams without touching the shared constant |
| `E_L0` | float | 0..1.4 | output | layer-0 energy, the ceiling of every later layer |

**Output Range:** 0 to `LIGHT_ENERGY_PER_PM` × `KIND_GAIN` × 1000 (1.000 at the defaults); non-decreasing in `output_pm`, so **a lamp never renders brighter than the power it is served** (`EM D.1`, Graph D.6). Two bounds on `E_1000`: below, the rim must stay legible on mid Graphite, `E_1000` ≥ lin(floor code + `RIM_VIS_CODES`) / (`EDGE_FLOOR_PM` / 1000 × A_G × c_G) with floor code 1, `RIM_VIS_CODES` 8, A_G 0.0296 (Graphite mid green, linear) and c_G 0.761 (beam green) = 0.00273 / 0.00562 = 0.49 (ESTIMATE); above, the bloom bound of D.8. **Example:** a fixture at 1000 permille draws 0.001 × 1.0 × 1000 = 1.000; starved to 600 it draws 0.600; at 150 (`LIT_ON_PM`) 0.150, a dim light with a tiny pool (D.3).

### D.2 Hard cone scale

The `hard_cone_scale` formula is defined as:

`texture_scale = reach_px / edge_radius_texels`, with the lint `|reach_px − edge_radius_texels × texture_scale| ≤ 1` base px

**Variables:**

| Variable | Type | Range | Source | Description |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `reach_px` | int | 168 / 288 / 336 / 384 (rows 4 / 1 / 3 / 2 and 8) | `light_shape` REG:121 via the read view (`EM-8`) | the simulation's fade-out radius; for HARD rows it is also the drawn edge; never restated |
| `edge_radius_texels` | float | pass B nominal 2048, pass A nominal 1024 | the texture's measured sidecar (LO-15), produced by a build tool from the imported image | texel distance from the apex to the hard edge along the axis (apex at the midpoint of one edge of the 2048² square, so the reach spans the full side) |
| `texture_scale` | float | 0.0820..0.375 | output | written to the light |

**Output Range:** pass B 0.0820 (fan) to 0.1875 (narrow, beacon); pass A 0.1640 to 0.375. The sidecar is authoritative; the nominal figures are the layout's arithmetic. **Example:** narrow beam, `reach_px` 384: pass B 384 / 2048 = 0.1875, so the quad is 2048 × 0.1875 = 384 base px square with the apex at its left-middle; pass A (`size_limit` 1024) 384 / 1024 = 0.375. Both draw the edge at 384. Wide beam 288: 0.140625 (B), 0.28125 (A). The cone's half-angle and rim level are baked into the texture from the shape table (LO-6), so narrow (12°) and beacon (20°) share a scale and differ in texture.

### D.3 Soft pool scale

The `soft_pool_scale` formula is defined as:

`r_lit = ⌊R × (output_pm − LIT_ON_PM) / output_pm⌋` for `output_pm > LIT_ON_PM`, else 0 (`EM D.7`); `r_draw = max(r_lit, POOL_RESIDUAL_PX)`; `texture_scale = r_draw / edge_radius_texels`

**Variables:**

| Variable | Type | Range | Source | Description |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `R` | int | 72 (fault glow default) / 96 / 120 / 144 | `light_shape` rows 10 / 6, 9 / 5 / 7; the glowstick's `reach_px` follows `EM D.9` | the simulation's fade-out radius |
| `output_pm`, `LIT_ON_PM` | int | 0..1000, 150 (locked) | read view; REG:2244 | served brightness and the lit threshold |
| `POOL_RESIDUAL_PX` | int | 0..16; placeholder 8 | ★ Tuning Knobs | drawn size of a starved light's pool |
| `edge_radius_texels` | float | `light_fixture` 1024²: 512; `light_pool` 512²: 256 (nominal) | sidecar | texel radius of the pool disc |

**Output Range:** `r_draw` ≥ `r_lit` always, so a prompt can never appear outside the visible pool; fixture 8..122 px. The profile is the simulation's own curve truncated at `r_lit` (LO-7), so a step of `LIT_ON_PM / output_pm` of the apex remains at the edge. **Example:** fixture (R 144), `output_pm` 1000 / 600 / 300 / 200 / 160 / 150 gives `r_lit` 122 / 108 / 72 / 36 / 9 / 0, `r_draw` 122 / 108 / 72 / 36 / 9 / 8, `texture_scale` 0.2383 / 0.2109 / 0.1406 / 0.0703 / 0.0176 / 0.0156 (the last drawn at energy 0.150 by D.1). Glowstick at its last wane step: `reach_px` 24, `r_lit` ⌊24 × 850 / 1000⌋ = 20, scale 20 / 256 = 0.0781. Baked rim: `FIXED` mode bakes 0.15 of the apex; at 600 permille the simulation's rim is 150 permille absolute and the drawn one 90 (0.15 × 600), at the correct radius (ESTIMATE; spike step 2 compares `FAMILY17`).

### D.4 Shadow slot assignment

The `shadow_slot_assign` formula is defined as:

`holder6 = argmin over C of (rank(c), d2(c), id(c))` (lexicographic), `d2(c) = (ox_c − cx)² + (oy_c − cy)²`; a challenger replaces the incumbent iff it has out-ranked it on `SLOT_DWELL_FRAMES` consecutive committed ticks; `n_shadow = n_beam_seats_bound + 1 (beacon) + [holder6 ≠ ∅] ≤ SHADOW_SLOTS_MAX`

**Variables:**

| Variable | Type | Range | Source | Description |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `C` | set | in-view candidates | read view `kind`, `subkind` | rank 0: DEPLOYABLE other than a glowstick; rank 1: FIXTURE with `subkind` EMERGENCY; rank 2: glowstick, only when no rank 0 or 1 is in view |
| `d2` | int | 0..(field diagonal)² | `origin_px` and the local seat's chest point `(cx, cy)` | integer squared distance; no square root |
| `id` | int | monotone (EM-28) | read view | tie-break, lower wins |
| `SLOT_DWELL_FRAMES` | int, committed ticks | 15..90; placeholder 30 | ★ Tuning Knobs | challenger dwell |
| `SHADOW_FADE_FRAMES` | int, `present_time` frames | 6..30; placeholder 18 | ★ Tuning Knobs | shadow-colour fade on a swap |
| `SHADOW_SLOTS_MAX` | int | 6 | ★ owner technical-artist, spike step 3 | slots 1-4 beam seats (MVP: three vacant), 5 beacon, 6 above |

**Output Range:** one emitter id or vacant for slot 6; slots 1-5 are never re-evaluated. **Example:** the cryo lamp (rank 1, 200 px away, `d2` 40,000) against a glowstick (rank 2, `d2` 900): the lamp holds slot 6 because rank decides before distance. Two flares (ids 14 and 17) both at `d2` 2,500: id 14. A strictly better challenger for 29 ticks that then loses rank resets to 0 and no swap occurs; at tick 30 the swap starts and the shadow colour crossfades over 18 frames.

### D.5 Beam offset

The `beam_offset` formula is defined as:

`o = Rot(−θ)·v − a_tex` (`OFFSET_FRAME` = LOCAL); `o = v − Rot(θ)·a_tex` (WORLD); under shadow-origin outcome A the node sits at the chest and `o = −a_tex`

**Variables:**

| Variable | Type | Range | Source | Description |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `θ` | float | 0..2π | the rotation the Presenter wrote this frame (raw aim; PC-5) | node rotation, +x toward +y on screen |
| `v` | int pair | `|x| ≤ 2`, y −45..−25 | `chest_point` per drawn sheet (5) and animation (REG:521), minus the floored foot | foot-to-chest vector in base px |
| `a_tex` | float pair | (−`w_s`/2, 0), `w_s` = side × `texture_scale` ≈ `reach_px` | sidecar `apex_offset_texels` × `texture_scale` | apex displacement from the texture centre; **non-zero by default** (apex at an edge, LO-15) |
| `o` | float pair | magnitude ≤ |`v`| + `w_s`/2 ≈ 237 | output | value written to `offset` every render frame |

**Output Range:** continuous; the realised apex minus the floored chest point stays ≤ 0.01 px every render frame (lint). **Example:** wide beam, pass B (`texture_scale` 0.140625, `w_s` 288, `a_tex` = (−144, 0)), facing south, `v` = (1, −35), `θ` = π/2: `Rot(−π/2)·v` = (−35, −1), so LOCAL `o` = (−35, −1) − (−144, 0) = (109, −1); check `Rot(π/2)·((109, −1) + (−144, 0))` = `Rot(π/2)·(−35, −1)` = (1, −35) = `v`. WORLD `o` = (1, −35) − `Rot(π/2)`(−144, 0) = (1, 109). Aim east (`θ` 0), LOCAL: (145, −35). Outcome A: `o` = (144, 0).

### D.6 Ambient value

The `ambient_value` formula is defined as:

`ambient_raw = AMBIENT_RING_PM[ring]` (DERELICT); `ambient_raw = AMBIENT_HOME_DARK_PM + ⌊(AMBIENT_HOME_STABLE_PM − AMBIENT_HOME_DARK_PM) × mean_fixture_pm / 1000⌋` (HOME); `ambient_eff' = ambient_eff + clamp(ambient_raw − ambient_eff, −s_amb, +s_amb)` per frame, `s_amb = (AMBIENT_HOME_STABLE_PM − AMBIENT_HOME_DARK_PM) / AMBIENT_SLEW_FRAMES`

**Variables:**

| Variable | Type | Range | Source | Description |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `AMBIENT_RING_PM[1..3]` | int (permille of white) | placeholder 30 / 15 / 5; ordering only | ★ ART 4.3 (outer ~3 % > middle ~1-2 % > inner 0-1 %) | cosmetic floor per ring; ring id is Web Map's (MVP: both handmade derelicts ring 1) |
| `AMBIENT_HOME_STABLE_PM`, `_DARK_PM` | int | placeholder 100 / 20 | ★ ART 4.3 (10 % / 2 %) | HOME bounds, independent of ring |
| `mean_fixture_pm` | int | 0..1000 | mean `output_pm` of the section's FIXTURE records (read view; switched-off fixtures count as 0); no Graph read | how lit the home section is |
| `AMBIENT_SLEW_FRAMES` | int, `present_time` frames | 5..120; placeholder 30 | Tuning Knobs | slew limit so the frame mean sees a ramp |

**Output Range:** 0..100 permille (10 % of white); it never changes `lit` (`AMBIENT_PM` is 0 in the simulation) and is delivered through one setter to carrier B (a material value) or carrier A (`CanvasModulate`, LO-12). **Example:** HOME, all fixtures lit then switched off: raw 100 to 20, `s_amb` = 80 / 30 = 2.67 permille per frame, so the ambient reaches its dark value in 30 frames (0.5 s); at `mean_fixture_pm` 500 raw is 20 + 40 = 60, reached from 100 in 15 frames. ### D.7 Hull floor

The `hull_floor` formula is defined as:

`out_c = max(albedo_c × ambient_eff / 1000, floor_c)` (linear light), `floor_c = lin(enc(floor_scale × VOID_c) + hr15_step)`, `lin(c) = c / 12.92` for `c` ≤ 0.04045 else `((c + 0.055) / 1.055)^2.4`, `c` = code / 255

**Variables:**

| Variable | Type | Range | Source | Description |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `albedo_c` | float | 0..1 | sheet, linear | hull item albedo; Graphite mid `#2A303A` = (0.0231, 0.0296, 0.0423) |
| `VOID_c` | float | fixed | `HULL_FLOOR_TINT` `#0B1018` (codes 11, 16, 24) = (0.00335, 0.00518, 0.00913) | Void Steel |
| `floor_scale` | float | 0.03..1.0; placeholder 0.066 ("reading S"); 1.0 is the literal hex ("reading U") | derived from spike 7b; the art-director picks the reading (CP-5) | scales the floor so the final frame meets `HULL_FLOOR_GAP_STEPS` |
| `hr15_step` | int codes | 0..`HR15_STEP_MAX` (3) | per-client setting | low-vision raise, hull geometry only; proposal one 8-bit code per step |
| `HULL_FLOOR_GAP_STEPS` | int | 1..2 | ★ ART 8.11 flag 1 | the contract: the unlit hull's brightest channel is this many 8-bit codes above black, as a mean over ≥ 32×32 px |

**Output Range:** the contract is on the final 8-bit frame, not the percentage; `k_unlit` (the engine's unlit factor) is probed at step 7a and the material writes `floor_c / k_unlit`. **Example:** reading S, `hr15_step` 0: floor codes (0.73, 1.13, 1.99) = (1, 1, 2). Graphite mid at the outer ring (ambient 30): (2.29, 2.92, 4.18) codes, the floor does not bind; at the inner ring (ambient 5): (0.38, 0.49, 0.70), the floor binds, so the planes collapse to one value and the two-value read exists only under light. `hr15_step` 3: (3.73, 4.13, 4.99) = (4, 4, 5). ### D.8 Glow threshold bound

### D.18 Flash governor

The `flash_governor` formula is defined as:

`allowed(t) = |{ f : t − 1 s < t_f ≤ t }| < FLASH_MAX_PER_S` for the light, for each of the 9 regions and for the frame mean; if not allowed, `t_resume = ⌈(oldest t_f + 1 s) × render_rate⌉ / render_rate`; a transition counts iff `|ΔE| × 10 ≥ E_start`, and a flash is two opposing counted transitions completing inside the window

**Variables:**

| Variable | Type | Range | Source | Description |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `FLASH_MAX_PER_S` | int | 3 (locked) | REG:1362, PIPE PR-13 | flashes per rolling second |
| flash definition | pair of opposing changes of at least 10 % relative luminance | locked | REG:616 (`flash_count`) | reused by name; no new definition |
| `t_f` | `present_time` | rolling 1 s | the governor's rings of `FLASH_MAX_PER_S` + 1 = 4 timestamps per light, per region and for the frame mean | completion times of counted flashes |
| region | cell of a 3×3 grid | a ninth of the displayed picture at the current field | PIPE D.15 (refresh owed, C row 4) | 213×120 base px at the default field; 640×360 at 1080p `k_z` 1 |
| "newer light" | emitter id | higher id | Ask E-G | whose transitions are delayed first |

**Output Range:** a retimed start time, never a brighter or darker pair than requested; only Lighting-originated transitions are governed (L1-L3, fades, stand-in crossfades, decoy alpha ramps, ambient slew on the frame mean); a simulation-committed on/off, the beacon's aim and a FAULT_GLOW onset pass untouched, and a delay that would be needed for one is a DIAGNOSTIC defect (LO-22). The counter is a proxy that over-delays and never under-delays; F&T's `arc_flash_share` (REG:978) is a consumer rate the governor backstops, never a second bound. **Example:** flashes complete at 0.0, 0.4 and 0.8 s; a request completing at 0.9 s sees 3 inside (−0.1, 0.9]: blocked; at 1.0 s the 0.0 flash has left the half-open window: allowed, so the delay is 0.1 s = 6 frames. Two individually compliant lights at 3 flashes per second overlapping one region: each passes its own ring, the region ring sees 6, and the higher-id light is delayed until the count is 3. A gutter dip (`M1` 700, 30 %) is one counted pair.

### D.19 Cut order

The `cut_order` formula is defined as:

`demote_next = argmin over U of (priority, −d2, −id)` (lowest first, over the unprotected non-shadow set `U`), applied in two passes: until `lanterns ≤ LANTERN_LIGHTS_MAX_eff`, then until `non-shadow ≤ NONSHADOW_LIGHTS_MAX_eff`; protected lights are never demoted, and `CUT_HYSTERESIS_FRAMES` keeps a returning bound light unbound

**Variables:**

| Variable | Type | Range | Source | Description |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `priority` | int | BEAM 255, BEACON 200, FIXTURE 120, DEPLOYABLE 100, FAULT_GLOW 80, NODE_LANTERN 60 (`priority_default`, REG `emitter_kind`) | read view | lower is demoted first |
| `d2`, `id` | int | as D.4 | read view and the local seat's chest point | the farther, then the higher id, is demoted first |
| protected set | — | — | BEAM, BEACON, any shadow-slot holder, a FAULT_GLOW younger than `ticks_for_seconds(W_MIN_WARNING_S)` = 600 ticks | may exceed a cap only up to `MAX_FAULT_GLOW` 10 |
| `CUT_HYSTERESIS_FRAMES` | int, committed ticks | 10..60; placeholder 30 | ★ Tuning Knobs | anti ping-pong at the cut line |
| `CUT_FADE_FRAMES` | int, `present_time` frames | 6..30; placeholder 12 | ★ Tuning Knobs | stand-in crossfade |

**Output Range:** every emitter is bound (a light node) or demoted (a stand-in at radius about `r_lit / 2` in its own hue, none if `r_lit` is 0; the radius is clamped to the distance to the nearest `OCCLUDES_LIGHT` edge and not drawn below 4 px, LO-33); none is dropped; the simulation is never told (EM:813). **Example (T2, caps 16 / 6):** in view 10 FIXTURE (120), 1 glowstick (100), 8 NODE_LANTERN (60) and 2 FAULT_GLOW younger than 600 ticks (protected); no EMERGENCY or other rank 0 or 1 light is in view, so the glowstick holds shadow slot 6 (protected, outside the non-shadow count). Pass 1: 8 lanterns > 6, so the 2 farthest lanterns are demoted. Pass 2: non-shadow is 10 + 6 + 2 = 18 > 16, so 2 more are demoted, lowest priority first, which are the 2 farthest remaining lanterns (60 is below 80 and 120). Result: 10 fixtures, 4 lanterns, 2 fault glows bound as non-shadow lights (16), plus the glowstick in slot 6; 4 lanterns drawn as stand-ins.

## Edge Cases

Each case states the exact outcome and the rule it protects; `LO-n` and `D.n` point at the owner of the behaviour and are not restated. Every case holds at every rung `k_z` in [1, `k_auto` + 2] and under both render passes unless it says otherwise.

### Zoom ladder, render passes and the active set

- **If the view is 1080p pass A at `k_z` 1 (ρ exactly 9.00)**: the tier is T2, 16 non-shadow lights and 6 lanterns, 2,073,600 lit px; the inclusive boundary is T2, not T3 (D.12). The caps bind only if more than 16 non-shadow lights are in view; truth rules are unchanged.
- **If a ladder cut changes `k_z`**: the active set, LOD tier, occluder reach set and slot dwell timers are re-evaluated in one callback; a bound light rebinds only if it left the active set; no light value changes (all lengths are in base px, LO-16) and `state_hash` is identical at every rung (EM-30, AC-86).
- **If the target is 2160p at `k_z` 1 (field scale `s` 6.0, outside `PIPE D.5`'s printed 0.6-4.0)**: the tier is T3 (ρ 36.00, caps 12 and 4); the printed range is a stale Pipeline wording (C row 5), not a limit on the ladder.
- **If a light's reach disc crosses the field edge**: it is active iff `dx² + dy² ≤ reach_px²`, with `dx = max(0, field_x0 − M − ox, ox − (field_x1 + M))` (likewise `dy`) and `M = VIEW_MARGIN_PX` 24; a 144 px fixture 36 px beyond the margin is active (1,296 ≤ 20,736), one 236 px beyond is not. Integer-only, so the set is reproducible.
- **If the narrow cone or beacon magnifies under pass B (1440p `k_z` 6, 2160p `k_z` 6-8)**: the rim softens to 2.25-3.0 screen px but the edge position is the band's half-step and does not move, so the ±1 px lint holds; accepted by ART 8.3 (D.9, D.11).
- **If the engine samples light textures from an atlas without mipmaps `[UNV]`**: pass B minification (5.33x narrow, 7.11x wide at `k_z` 1) shimmers as a beam rotates; fallbacks in order: a second lower-density texture swapped for `k_z` ≤ 2 on the existing cut, then accept pass A (spike step 2b).
- **If the window, pass or driver changes at run time**: the tier is re-evaluated on that frame as a one-frame cut, a full reconcile runs and every light is a first observation (no warm-up, no governor history); a hidden or minimised window skips the apply step and resumes the same way.

### Hard cones and soft pools

- **If a beam's `output_pm` is below 600**: its hard rim falls below `LIT_ON_PM` and the rendered rim step shrinks (`EM D.7`); the edge stays at `reach_px` ±1 px. Supported generically, not an MVP case (Suit rules out charge dimming, SE-8).
- **If a fixture's `output_pm` is at or below `LIT_ON_PM` (`r_lit` 0)**: it is drawn at `POOL_RESIDUAL_PX` 8 with energy 0.001 × `output_pm`; the Debug Overlay reports `r_lit` 0; at the inner ring the 8 px pool may sit within 1-2 codes of the floor and vanish, which is acceptable because it is not actionable and the housing still shows the lamp (LO-7).
- **If a lamp is warming up**: the pool is at its full `r_lit` from the first committed frame while energy ramps over `LAMP_WARM_FRAMES`; `lit` may already be true while the picture is at 40 % on frame 2, bounded by 5 frames (D.14).
- **If two soft skirts sum to `lit` in the simulation where the render shows near-black**: accepted as bounded and rare; Emitter State clamping SOFT contributions beyond `r_lit` is an optional refresh, routed only if spike step 2 shows it (LO-7).
- **If a tell dip (`M2` 700) is active on a mid plane**: the perceived pool edge moves inward (about 11 px for a 30 % dip, ESTIMATE) while `r_lit` and the prompt do not; AC-56 is evaluated at `m_mod` 1000 and the shrink is an honest dimming. On dark Graphite floors that still-frame difference between a dip and a sag is therefore not reliable, and the cue that holds is the temporal shape, transient versus steady (Named rule 1; Open Question 19).
- **If a glowstick wanes**: each integer `reach_px` step redraws the pool at the new `r_draw` on the committed tick, with no tween and no flicker (96 → 24 px reach, `r_lit` 81 → 20); waning is simulation state, not a presentation effect (LO-27).
- **If the beacon sweeps past a fixed observer**: the governor counts about 0.47 flashes per second (ESTIMATE) and never delays its rotation; the drawn cone leads the committed cone by at most 32 units (2.8°) inside a tick and `lit` uses the committed cone only (LO-27).

### Shadow slots, occluders and the shadow origin

- **If two non-glowstick deployables are in view (VS-A)**: the nearer by integer `d2` (ties by lower id) holds slot 6 after `SLOT_DWELL_FRAMES`; the other is a non-shadow pool (D.4).
- **If a glowstick is the only candidate and the cryo lamp is also in view**: the lamp (rank 1) holds slot 6 and the glowstick is a non-shadow pool; a glowstick holds the slot only when no rank 0 or 1 candidate is in view.
- **If two equidistant candidates swap rank every tick**: no swap occurs, because the dwell counter resets each time the challenger loses rank; the incumbent keeps the slot.
- **If a slot swap would pop on screen and no pop-free mechanism exists `[UNV]`**: the swap defers up to four dwell periods (120 ticks), then the challenger is demoted to a non-shadow pool and the holder stays (S.2, spike step 3b).
- **If the player stands 10-34 px south of a wall**: outcome B shadows start at the foot like the simulation's segment; outcome A puts the node at the chest, which can lie inside the wall footprint, where the engine's behaviour for a light inside a closed occluder is `[UNV]` (an all-around shadow or none); AC-57 records the band (LO-10).
- **If Layout bumps `version(section_id)` for a section a shadow light's reach disc intersects**: the polygons are rebuilt in the same render callback and swapped in one apply step; the old set stays bound until then, so no frame is missing a wall and none lets light through (LO-11).
- **If the bump is for a section wholly outside every reach disc**: it is rebuilt within `OCCLUDER_SYNC_BUDGET_US`, nearest first; its pooled objects are hidden, not freed.
- **If a door leaf is half open (`area` between 0 and `area_max`)**: the occluder is the committed leaf rectangle of that tick, `leaf_w = width_px − ⌊width_px × area / area_max⌋` (LY-9), one rectangle with no threshold; the shadow can lead or lag the sprite by at most one tick of leaf travel, accepted because `lit` uses the same rectangle.
- **If the shadow-reach set holds more than `OCCLUDER_POLYS_VIEW_MAX` polygons**: every wall is still built, because a missing wall is a P1 lie, and `diag.occluder_overflow` fires as an authoring and budget defect; the cap is a lint and spike target, not a runtime drop.
- **If a horror or a crewmate stands in a beam**: it casts no shadow and occludes nothing; actors never occlude (ART 3.2, EM-20).
- **If a non-shadow pool's `r_draw` disc crosses an `OCCLUDES_LIGHT` rectangle into another compartment (CD-GDD-ALIGN revision, LO-33)**: with the containment mechanism in force the pool lights only items carrying its own compartment colour, so a subject that is sim-dark behind the closed wall is never inside a drawn pool; the wall cell itself carries both colours and may show the lit face. Under the interim (PD-1 open) the pool is drawn through the wall, an authored placement is flagged by the C3 lint and moved or logged with its spill depth, a glowstick's spill is at most 57 px beyond a one-cell wall at its steady 81 px and zero from `r_draw` 24 px down, `diag.contain_leak` counts it, and the simulation's `lit` is unchanged (the prompt follows the segment test, never the picture).
- **If a closed door separates a lit compartment from a dark one**: the door leaf is an `OCCLUDES_LIGHT` rectangle for the simulation (LY-9), but a non-shadow light ignores occluders, so under C1 it is the mask bits that stop the pool: the far-side strip carries only its own colour while the door is closed, and the pool ends at the door cells (which may show the lit face). When the committed leaf opens (`leaf_w` < `width_px`) the strip gains the near colour in the same apply step as the occluder swap, and the sim-lit corridor beyond the strip may be drawn dark, a sim-lit, render-dark sliver counted by `diag.contain_dark`. Under the interim (PD-1 open) a non-shadow pool is drawn through the closed door as through any wall.
- **If a character stands in a door aperture or beside a bulkhead**: its footprint carries the colour of every compartment it overlaps, so it is lit by both sides' lights while the simulation's `lit` for that subject is evaluated per anchor; the sim-dark anchor behind the wall is not drawn inside the far side's pool.
- **If a demoted light's stand-in would cross a wall**: its radius is clamped to the nearest `OCCLUDES_LIGHT` edge (not drawn below 4 px), so a stand-in never paints the far side of a bulkhead (LO-33).

### Budget, demotion and cross-body light

- **If more than the tier cap of non-shadow lights are in view**: `cut_order` demotes the lowest to stand-ins with `CUT_FADE_FRAMES` fades (D.19); none is dropped and the simulation is never told; a demoted light whose `r_lit` is 0 draws no stand-in (`r_lit / 2` is 0).
- **If more than the lantern cap of lanterns are in view**: lanterns are a subset of the non-shadow lights and the lowest-priority ones (60) are demoted first, even when the non-shadow total is under its cap.
- **If protected lights fill most of a tier cap (T3 cap 12 with the maximum 10 young fault glows in view)**: the two remaining places go to the highest-priority unprotected lights and every other one is demoted; the floor rule `MAX_FAULT_GLOW` + 2 = 12 is what keeps protected lights from exceeding the cap, and `diag.cap_exceeded_by_protected` counts any excess a later cap change would allow (LO-17, LO-23).
- **If a fifth glowing fault shares a view under `TRAIL_LIGHT_REQUESTS_MAX` 4**: it is still rendered and is demoted only once older than 600 ticks; the authoring lint flags any compartment where more than 4 can share a view; dropping a trail light never drops a stamp (LO-23).
- **If a bound light drops below the cut line and returns within `CUT_HYSTERESIS_FRAMES` (30 committed ticks)**: it is not rebound, so a pool node cannot ping-pong at the boundary.
- **If more than `LIGHTS_PER_ITEM_MAX` (12) lights overlap one tile chunk**: the authoring lint fails; at run time a defect report fires at 14 (`diag.max_item_overlap`); at the engine's 16 `[UNV]` a sim-lit pool would silently vanish over part of the floor, which the 17-light criterion exists to catch.
- **If a demotion stand-in sits beside a decoy**: the stand-in is in its own hue with no violet; the decoy keeps its violet rim on top; both are below 0.9 `GLOW_HDR_THRESHOLD`, and they are told apart by rim colour.
- **If a light belongs to another live body in view**: it is bound, counted against the caps and lights only its own body's items by its mask bit; bodies beyond `BODIES_LIVE_MAX` 4 draw their lights as OTHER_BODY stand-ins outside the budget; a foreign body bit on a light is a lint failure (LO-24).
- **If `MAX_EMITTERS` (96) are all in view**: the stand-in pool is sized `MAX_EMITTERS` − `SHADOW_SLOTS_MAX` = 90 so that every tier, including T3's cap of 12, can demote the rest (orchestrator ruling: the specialist's 66 would run out at T3).

### Modifiers, decoys, channels and hallucination

- **If a tell dip and a gutter or warm-up overlap on one fixture**: the factors multiply (`m_mod × env_warm`), the floor excludes the envelope, and the governor counts both transitions (D.13).
- **If an Attention tell (L2) and a Horror disturbance (L3) are both active on one fixture**: L3 replaces L2 for that light and a single bounded dip is drawn, then governed (LO-18 iv).
- **If an Attention tell tier is active while the cryo-bay lamp (`subkind` EMERGENCY) is in view**: the lamp ignores it (`M2` = `M3` = 1000) and keeps only its baseline gutter at 600-1200 frames, so a dip seen there is always the baseline; the tell shows on every other fixture and lantern, and Attention's non-light world channels carry any tell in the cryo bay (LO-20).
- **If a lamp housing's emissive sprite is drawn off while its light is on (false system state, the MVP class)**: the housing is dark and the pool is present; the lie leaks as a pool over an unlit housing; the sprite and the light share no sampler or value path (LO-14).
- **If a housing is drawn on while the lamp is dead (`served_pm` 0)**: there is no pool, and no decoy or housing ever adds one; the lie leaks as a missing pool.
- **If a `GLOW_EXTENSION` decoy (VS-A) is drawn at the true cone edge**: it is an overlay past the edge with a violet rim; the real hard edge stays at `reach_px` ±1 px and no real `Light2D` member changes (LO-21).
- **If the Filter's list holds more than `DECOY_OVERLAYS_MAX` entries**: the earliest 4 are drawn and the rest counted in `diag.decoy_overflow` (D.17).
- **If the Perception Filter or the tool layer writes a `Light2D` member**: the source lint fails; there is no run-time guard because a guard would add a check to every write.
- **If the Filter's container pass drifts warm over beam pixels (CD-2)**: that is a Filter-side defect; Lighting never tints a real light and the beam stays `#FFE2A8`; the Filter must exclude beam pixels (C row 22).
- **If a crewmate is hallucinated as a horror (class 3)**: the Filter may substitute the body but never the beam; the beam's shape and colour are the loadout's and no modifier, decoy or LOD touches them (PC :216, LO-28).
- **If Suit's breath-vapour, fog or rime overlays cover a lit area**: they are unlit-layer sprites; no `Light2D` member and no drawn edge changes, so the revealed radius is unchanged (Suit :467).
- **If an unknown `hue_class` token arrives**: the light draws Tungsten `#FFCC66` and raises a DIAGNOSTIC; never a sanity-palette hex.
- **If the player kills every fixture to go dark**: the HOME ambient slews from 100 to 20 permille over 30 frames, the lit area falls to what runs, and the running loops stay visible as lanterns; there is no penalty and no screen effect (LO-31).
- **If the player stacks glowsticks (`GLOWSTICK_STACK_MAX` 4, `MAX_DEPLOYABLES_LIVE` 8) over a Coolant Ice mark**: the summed light can exceed the D.8 bound and bloom the stamp; recorded as a residual risk, bounded by the stack cap and by demotion; no rule is changed to hide it.

### Governor and accessibility

- **If the governor would have to delay a simulation-committed switch, the beacon's aim or a FAULT_GLOW onset**: that is a defect logged as a DIAGNOSTIC and failed by the rapid-toggle stub; the 10-tick cooldown of Ask E-E is what keeps it from arising.
- **If the governor delays a gutter dip to keep a region at or under 3**: the dip starts later, never deeper and never shorter than its 0.3-0.5 s envelope (D.18).
- **If the beacon, power arcs and a flare share one region**: the region ring counts the sum, the newer light (higher id) is delayed first, and the beacon rotation and arc onsets pass untouched, so only the flare's dips are retimed.
- **If `reduce_flashing` is on**: deployables are steady, arcs are a held glow at their mean luminance, the beacon is a static mark (sound is Audio's concern; no HUD glyph, ART:741), the cryo gutter and the tell gutter are kept; toggling mid-dip snaps `M1` to 1000 on the next frame as one counted transition.
- **If HR-15 is at step 3**: the hull floor rises by 3 codes (D.7) and nothing else changes; web, horrors, filaments, lights and the HUD are untouched; it applies at the next apply step.
- **If `CanvasModulate` is found to multiply unshaded items**: carrier B (the default) already avoids it; carrier A stays valid only while one section's ambient applies to the whole view, and is rejected whenever the home ship and a docked derelict can share a view (3 % against 10 %).

### Time, restore and holds

- **If a light is first observed after boot, a restore, a hold exit, a camera cut or a body re-bind**: it enters STEADY at its L0 value with no warm-up, no fade and no governor history (LO-3); warm-up fires only on an observed 0 to non-zero `output_pm` between two committed ticks.
- **If a restore completes (death handling is restart from the last autosave)**: nothing was persisted; every light and occluder is re-derived after Layout's `post_restore` (door leaves are valid only after it, LY-26 c), `version` has reset to 1 so the rebuild is unconditional, and governor windows, dwell timers, gutter epochs, caches and bindings are discarded; the beacon aim jumps to the restored `T` under the hold quad.
- **If `LOADING` or `TRANSIT_HOLD` is active**: the world is under the hold quad and lights are not updated; each shader is drawn once with its worst-case light combination so none compiles on first use; the exit is a first observation.
- **If the kernel is `PAUSED`**: `present_time` and the kernel clock hold, so envelopes, gutters, the governor window and flare phase freeze and the beacon's drawn aim holds (`tick_alpha` clamped at 1). At 30 fps, if two ticks commit within one render frame, only the final committed state is observed, a 0 → 1000 → 0 pulse inside the burst is never drawn, and the warm-up still takes 83.3 ms because it advances by `present_time`.
- **If the read view's `tick` has not advanced**: per-tick work is skipped and per-frame work still runs; **if** more than `MAX_DIRTY_KEYS` (256) keys arrive, the `ALL` collapse triggers a full reconcile whose result must equal the incremental one (debug assertion).
- **If a body unloads (a derelict undocks, a restore)**: every pooled light, occluder and stand-in bound to it is unbound in one pass and the node count returns to baseline (leak audit).
- **If the Presenter's rotation write lands after Lighting's `offset` write within a frame (an ordering defect)**: the apex is off by `|v| × sin(Δθ)`, 12 px for a 20°-per-frame stub, and the apex-equals-chest lint (0.01 px) fails; the ordering is tested with that stub (LO-2).
- **If two clients show the same lamp (co-op, later)**: gutter phase may differ because the hash is presentation-only, accepted since the tell tier and not the phase is the information; a remote beam's shape and colour come from the replicated loadout and are never rewritten, and a stale remote record pair holds the last pose without extrapolation (provisional, replication undesigned).

## Game Feel

The player feels three things of this system, each a measurement before a mood. **The edge is a place you can walk to.** A beam ends where a prompt stops appearing, to ±1 px at every zoom rung; this is the key feel property and it is pass/fail (AC-LO-6 to AC-LO-8). **A lamp catches; it does not lag.** The pool is at its final size from the first committed frame and only its brightness climbs over `LAMP_WARM_FRAMES` (83.3 ms), so a switch reads as a ship's lamp warming, never as input delay (LO-19). **A dip dims; a sag shrinks.** A tell gutter returns to full with the readable radius unchanged and reads as presence; a starvation sag is steady and the pool gets smaller and reads as power (LO-20). The honest qualification (CD-GDD-ALIGN revision): the readable radius is unchanged in a dip, but the perceived edge may move inward on dark floors (about 11 px at a 30 % dip, ESTIMATE), so a still frame is not a reliable way to tell the two apart there, and the redundant cue is the temporal shape, transient versus steady. **The pool ends at the wall.** A non-shadow pool never lights a room the simulation calls dark (LO-33), so what the player sees lit is what the game acts on; while PD-1 is open this property holds for the shadow-casting lights only. Shadows never pop: a slot swap crossfades over `SHADOW_FADE_FRAMES` and waits `SLOT_DWELL_FRAMES`.

**Owners.** Beam-follow and aim feel belong to Player Controller & Input, zoom-step feel to the Render Pipeline; Lighting owns the lamp, edge, shadow and gutter feel above. Feel is checked by capture and playtest (AC-LO-30, AC-LO-58; CD-R1), not automated.
