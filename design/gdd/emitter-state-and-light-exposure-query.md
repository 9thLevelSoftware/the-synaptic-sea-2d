## Summary

Emitter State & Light-Exposure Query is the headless, integer-only simulation record of every light in the world (the chest beam, ship fixtures, machinery lanterns, thrown glowsticks, alarm beacons and fault glows) plus the one question it answers: how much of that light reaches a registered point, and is it enough to work by. Read this GDD if you design or build anything that must ask "is this lit" (Interaction's lit-only verbs, Attention's light stimulus, Horror Entities, Suit's beam charge, 2D Lighting's drawn edge, Derelict Authoring's lamps): it fixes the emitter records and their commands and mutators, the shape table, the lamp, glowstick, beacon and fault-glow formulas, the cone and occlusion tests, the hysteresis, the caps and the acceptance criteria, and it never reads a `Light2D`, a physics space or a `PERCEPTION_OUT` view.

**Two `lit`s, kept verbatim (EM-2):** the *record field* `emitter.state.lit` means an emitter is emitting (`output_pm > 0`, true for any kind and read by the Presenter on the seat's beam); the *query* `lit(ref)` means a registered subject's exposure is above the technical-artist's threshold with hysteresis (`LIT_ON_PM` 150 / `LIT_OFF_PM` 120). Neither is ever renamed.

## Overview

Emitter State & Light-Exposure Query is the simulation's record of every light in the world and the one question it answers about them: *how much of that light reaches this point, and is it enough to work by?* In one sentence: it is a scene-tree-free, integer-only `SIM` block, `emitter.state`, that holds an **emitter record** for each chest beam, ship fixture, machinery lantern, thrown glowstick, alarm beacon and fault glow, written only through commands and published mutators, plus a pure **exposure query** that combines those records with the Layout's opaque footprints into a per-subject `lux_pm` (0..1000) and a `lit` bit. It is the data layer under three things the player feels directly: the edge of their own lamp (what the lamp reaches is exactly what they can read and touch), the dimming of a ship lamp when the scrubber draws, and the way the web answers to light (Attention, Horror Entities and the act-only-on-lit rule all read the same answers). It never reads a `Light2D`, never touches a physics space, never obtains a `PERCEPTION_OUT` view, and runs identically headless, on the host and under test.

The player never operates an emitter table. They meet it as a **pool of light with an edge**. The chest lamp (hands-free, Pandorum-style, origin at the per-facing chest point, `|x| <= 2` px) lays a wedge across the deck; a valve wheel half inside it prompts exactly when the wheel's anchor crosses the drawn edge and not a pixel sooner; the corridor lamps sag when the tier browns out and the junction box at the rim of their pool loses its prompt; a glowstick thrown into the next room blooms Chemlight green, holds steady, and then visibly shrinks; a lamp switched on takes a handful of ticks to warm because the ship has to serve it first. Nothing is ever shown as an exposure readout of any shape, or a "visibility" number (P3: shown in the world, never as a hidden meter): the only gameplay surface of the whole system is a prompt that is present or absent, drawn by the HUD, and the world itself.

The block exists because three decisions already made elsewhere need one honest place to live. The index (TD-SYSTEM-BOUNDARY) split *emitter state* from *light rendering* so that light is a simulation input no hallucination can touch (kernel CR-12) and so that Attention can run headless and on the host for remote players. The Controller made the beam an emitter "like any other for Attention" and made every verb act only on a lit object (PC-5, PC-6), which demands one query that the prompt, the `validate` step and the channel re-check all call. The Graph made lamps ordinary LOADs whose brightness is `base * served_pm / 1000` (registry formula `power_allocation`), which demands a place that turns that served power into a light a subject can be inside of. The result is a bottleneck with six dependents (2D Lighting & Occlusion, Interaction, Suit & Equipment, Derelict Authoring, Attention, Horror Entities).

**What the game loses without it.** There is no honest "go dark" strategy (nothing for Attention to read), no lit-only rule (PC-6 has no query to call), no way for a lamp to be dimmer because the ship is starved (P1: "a lamp never renders brighter than the power it is served"), no stable edge for the Tell's filaments to resolve at (P2), and no sim-side truth for Lighting to conform to, so the first hallucination that bends a cone would be indistinguishable from a bug. The sim/render agreement that this block guarantees by construction (the drawn hard edge *is* the queried edge for a full-output emitter) is the load-bearing P1 test of the whole lighting stack.

**Index wording, reconciled.** The index (rationale bullet 5) describes a geometric light-exposure query made of a cone test plus occluder tests on physics shapes. This GDD honours the substance, the Layout's collision footprint rectangles as the occluding shapes, and changes the means: occlusion is an integer segment-versus-rectangle test against those same rectangles behind an `OccluderSource` seam, with no physics backend in the MVP (EM-20). The reason is the index's own intent — the block "runs headless" and lets Attention run headless and on the host (kernel H10; CR-13 G3: the 2D physics engine makes no determinism claim, so a physics-derived `lit` flowing into a persisted block would have to be recorded for replay). The index and kernel wording refresh this implies is routed in Cross-References. Index numbering, once and for all: this system is **enumeration row 5**, rationale bullet 5, **Recommended Design Order 7**.

*Implementation questions raised while designing and deferred to ADRs (→ `/architecture-decision`): the `OccluderSource` seam and the integer segment test (ADR candidate 1); the subject-registration model with static and dynamic layers and counted work units (ADR candidate 2); the fixed-point direction table and Q28 cone test with zero floats on the sim path (ADR candidate 3); the broadphase (uniform grid versus room-gated) and whether the spatial index lives in Layout or here (ADR candidate 4); the occlusion-origin rule, contingent on the Lighting spike (ADR candidate 5). No ADR exists yet (`docs/architecture/` holds only `tr-registry.yaml`).*

## Player Fantasy

**Framing: indirect, with a cost layer.** The player experiences this system as the edge of their own light and the prompts that live inside it; they never see "exposure", an "emitter" or a "query". *(creative-director consulted; Framing A "The Honest Edge" adopted as the headline, with Framing C "Light as a Spend" as its price. Framing B "Lamp Management" — brightness dials, shape switching, lamp banks as a resource panel — was rejected: it breeds an exposure readout, which P3 forbids, and a shape-change input, which PC-5 forbids. Logged.)*

> The lamp is on your chest. It is the only light that follows you, and it is the only way to touch anything: what is not in the light cannot be worked (the three exceptions PC-6 names aside). Every fixture on the home-ship, every running machine's lantern, every glowstick you leave behind is the same bargain. Light lets you read the ship, and light lets the web read you.
>
> Competence is choosing light, not having more of it. A veteran crew does not carry a brighter lamp; they know which corridor lamp to kill before a sortie, where a glowstick buys the most room for the least time, and when a second of beam on a dark corner is worth what it shows.
>
> And the edge never lies. Eyes can be wrong about what stands in the light, but not about where the light ends. When the sanity goes, the dark may fill with shapes and the cone may seem to reach further than it does; walk to the edge and it is exactly where it always was, and nothing past it will answer your hand.

**The emotion named: the bargain of sight** — agency purchased with exposure, under a guarantee that the light itself is never the liar. The power is not dominance over the dark; it is the small, honest sovereignty of your own light: you choose where the edge of the known world is, and the edge is where you left it.

**Tone and language.** Use: *lamp, chest lamp, beam, in the light, the edge, go dark, the lamp warms, the pool shrinks, spend light.* Avoid meter words (*visibility, detection, stealth, hidden, light level, lux*; `lux_pm` is an internal name and is never player-facing) and avoid *flashlight* (it implies a hand-held tool; the lamp is chest-mounted) and *torch* for the beam (the anti-pillars use "torches" for the VS-A deployables; concept Pillar 1's "carries their torch" means the beam, a wording refresh owed to the concept, listed in Cross-References). The ludonarrative anchor is the user's decision of 2026-10-26: the lamp is on the player's chest, hands-free, like Pandorum. The player *is* the lantern, so going dark is not putting a tool down; it is hiding your own body.

**Anchor moment (solo; the MVP is single-player).** Derelict A, power dead. The hatch opens on black. You thumb the chest lamp on: the click comes first, the light a breath later (PC-5: the sound pre-plays, the light follows committed state). A wedge of your wide beam — 30° half-angle, 288 px / 6 tiles, placeholder rows of the art bible 8.3 table — lays a hard edge across the deck plates. At the far end of it, half in, half out, a valve wheel. You turn a hair to the left and the wheel's prompt appears at the same moment the wheel does; a hair back and both are gone. The edge is a fact. The next compartment is past a bulkhead your beam cannot bend around. You crack a glowstick and throw it (Interaction's throw, `THROW_RANGE_TILES` 3). Chemlight green (#86B896) blooms in a room you have not entered yet, a 2-tile pool (96 px, an Item DB placeholder) that will not flicker but will shrink. Now there are two lights in this derelict and only one of them follows you. Something creaks overhead; filaments are resolving at the rim of your cone (Attention's presentation). You switch the lamp off. The dark is honest: the valve still exists, you just cannot touch it now. Behind you the glowstick you left keeps burning. You chose to leave it. It is still lit, and it is still yours to answer for.

**Second beat (home-ship, Graph-coupled).** Back aboard, the scrubber spins up and the corridor lamps sag as the power tier browns out (`lamp_output_pm` = `base * served_pm / 1000`). The pool under the galley lamp pulls in; the junction box at the edge of it, which you could reach a minute ago, is now outside the light and has no prompt. Nobody told you the power is short. The light told you. *(The crewmate beats — a downed crewmate's beam as a locator in the dark, a saner crewmate's beam as the truth check — are Vertical Slice B (VS-B); the MVP fantasy rests on the honesty of your own edge.)*

**Pillar alignment.** *Pillar 1 (primary), verbatim:* "Three channels are inviolable ... The human channel. Voice/proximity chat and a crewmate's own light beam (colour and shape) are never faked." And the art bible's Principle 1 anchor: "The physical radius your light actually reveals is a fixed anchor and never lies. Even at low sanity, the hallucination layer may add things but may not move that boundary." This block is that anchor in the simulation: the edge the player sees is the edge the query answers. *Pillar 3 (primary), verbatim:* "The players' light, noise, power use and restraint raise or lower its attention ... shown in the world, never as a hidden meter ... Restraint and stealth are real strategies." Every emitter is exposure; off-screen emitters still expose; going dark is a real, measured choice. *Pillar 4:* "No lie is unfalsifiable: the truth is always available through the physical world, a second instrument, a saner crewmate, the human channel, or time — and checking costs time, risk or trust." The true edge is a falsifier that costs a step and the exposure of standing in your own beam. *Pillar 2 (supporting):* the Tell's signature image, filaments resolving at the cone edge, needs a stable edge to resolve at. *Pillar 5 is not served* (glowstick scarcity is Suit's and the Item DB's). The "spend light" cost layer leans on glowstick scarcity, which is P5-adjacent (scarcity pushes inward), so the spend has a named economic owner and is not an unanchored promise: **the scarcity-to-depth link is routed to the Item & Module Database and Suit & Equipment** (the Item DB's `GLOWSTICK_STACK_MAX` and its salvage-only faucet, Suit's per-seat carry and start counts, and Derelict Authoring's loose-stick supply by depth); this GDD prices the light (a stick is a real, shrinking reveal radius and a real exposure) and owns none of the supply.

**Design tests.**
1. **The honest edge (system test).** *When the place you can act differs from the place you can see light vs. they coincide → they coincide.* At rest (aim committed, not sweeping), an object whose anchor is one base pixel inside the drawn hard edge of a single full-output hard-edged emitter is in the light and prompts; one pixel outside is not and does not. This is the sim/render agreement test (a `(spike)` until Lighting exists). It holds for a *full-output* emitter; a dimmed emitter's actionable area honestly shrinks (see Formulas, `EDGE_FLOOR_PM`).
2. **Light is the price of agency.** *When a choice lets the player act without light vs. requires light to act → require light.* (PC-6's exceptions: `drop`, `toggle_beam`, the inspect card of an already-lit instrument.) Otherwise working in the dark becomes a free Attention dodge and "go dark" degenerates into the dominant strategy.
3. **Light is never free.** *When a choice makes a light safe (unseen by the web, no cost) vs. keeps every emitter an exposure → exposure.* Corollary: off-screen emitters still expose; a glowstick you walked away from is still yours to answer for.
4. **The lie draws, it does not reach.** *When a hallucination could change what is actionable vs. only what is drawn → only what is drawn.* No Filter output changes `emitter.state` or any query answer.

**Under hallucination the fantasy deepens rather than breaks.** Light gets the same split the Graph drew for the physical world. The left column is what this GDD guarantees by construction (a `SIM`-only block, kernel CR-12, EM-29); the right is what the Perception Filter and Lighting may do, as presentation only.

| Cannot lie (truth channel — this GDD guarantees) | Can lie (presentation — a decoy overlay drawn by Lighting on mask bit 3 from the Filter's per-viewer decoy list, LO-21) |
|---|---|
| The true reveal edge of every emitter: reach, cone, occlusion | Decoy lights that are not emitters (not in `emitter.state`, expose nothing) |
| Beam colour and shape, including a crewmate's (VS-B) | Shadows with no occluder (stage-2 drift, art bible 4.4) |
| Lamp and fixture brightness as served (`lamp_output_pm`) | A decoy glow appears past the true edge (an overlay, leaking violet) |
| Whether an object is in the light (`lit(subject)`), and therefore whether it prompts and accepts verbs | A decoy overlay that may itself flicker, on the decoy mask bit (Lighting; flash-clamped) |
| Glowstick waning: a real, monotone shrinking of the reveal radius | What a lit object looks like (the module-body channel: sprite, gauge) |
| Exposure to the web | — |

**The falsifier this hands the player.** A phantom extension of the cone carries no prompts and accepts no verbs, because the query never answered "in the light" there. So the cheapest truth check in the game needs no instrument: *reach for it.* If it is real light, the prompt is there. Cost: a step forward, a second, and the exposure of standing in your own beam. The ladder, cheapest first: (1) reach for it; (2) walk to the edge; (3) drop a glowstick (a second light, a second witness); (4) a saner crewmate's beam (VS-B, the human channel); (5) time. **Required leak:** any drawn extension past the true edge must carry the sane-palette leak (violet or flesh fringe at the false region — art bible 4.4, "subtle is allowed; absent is not"). That is a requirement this GDD states on Lighting and the Perception Filter, not something it implements. **Dependency of the falsifier (flag, routed):** "reach for it" holds only if no system ever draws a prompt where the query says not in the light — phantom salvage targets (kernel Open Question 14, Interaction's) and HUD prompt drawing must route through `lit(subject)` and nothing else. If a phantom target could prompt inside a decoy region the cheapest falsifier dies. An unregistered ref therefore answers exactly like an unlit one (EM-21); Interaction owns the ruling on phantom targets. *Art-bible wording tension (routed, not a value conflict):* art bible 4.4 lists "the cone seems to reach further than it does" as stage-2 drift, while Principle 1 names the true light radius an anchor that never shifts. They reconcile exactly as above — the *drawn* cone may be extended by a decoy; the *revealing* radius (what is actionable, what the query answers) does not move — and the art bible should say "a decoy glow appears past the true edge" so nobody reads it as licence to move the boundary.

**Reference techniques, taken precisely.** *Pandorum* — a chest-mounted, hands-free lamp that swings with the body (*adopt*, user decision); the hand-held torch traded against tools (*reject*: the hand point is a lightless grip, PC-5). *Project Zomboid* — lit houses seen from outside draw the horde (*adopt*: fixtures expose even when nobody is near; off-screen emitters count); the flashlight as a battery item (*adopt*, but the charge is Suit's). *Alien: Isolation* — your own light gives you away and turning it off is a tactic (*adopt*); the motion tracker as a threat readout (*reject*: no readout of any shape). *Darkwood* — the vision cone as the boundary of the known (*adopt*); the cone as a mask that hides drawn geometry (*reject*: art bible Principle 1, unlit not fogged, real occlusion). *Monaco* — line of sight as the honest rule of what you know (*adopt*: real occluders make "a shadow with no occluder" a readable lie). *Lethal Company* — a crewmate's flashlight as their locator (*adopt for VS-B*). *Amnesia / Don't Starve* — darkness drains sanity or kills (*reject*: darkness here is honest and is the *safe* choice; light is the cost; routed to Sanity so go-dark stays a valid strategy). *Doom 3* — flashlight or weapon (*reject*: no weapon exists to trade against). *Thief / Splinter Cell* — a light gem or any visibility readout (*reject*).

**What this GDD does not promise (anti-pillar guard rails).**
1. **No exposure readout of any shape.** No gauge, gem, icon, overlay, light-level number or "visibility" text in gameplay. The only gameplay surface is a prompt that is present or absent. The Debug Overlay (`DIAGNOSTIC`) may draw cones, segments and subject exposure and is absent from release.
2. **The beam reveals; it never repels or harms.** No beam stuns, burns, repels or damages a horror or a crewmate (rejected: the Alan Wake light-as-weapon pattern; anti-pillar "not a combat game"). Horrors may read `lit_by(point)` and exposure to decide behaviour — that is Horror Entities' — and being revealed is the beam's whole effect. VS-A flares, torches and decoys may repel or distract (anti-pillar "tools may repel"); that behaviour is Horror's and is never a damage channel. *(provisional — Horror Entities undesigned, sibling stage running; whichever GDD lands second adopts the other's text.)*
3. **No light progression.** No beam upgrades, brightness levels or unlocks. Shape is a Suit loadout fact; better light comes from salvage, if at all.
4. **No mid-run shape switching and no brightness dial.** On and off is the only beam choice.
5. **Shining a beam on a crewmate has no mechanical effect** (anti-PvP): it does not blind, mark or harm; in VS-B it is only a truth check.
6. **Fixtures are placed by welding sections, never freeform** (anti-builder). No player-placed lamps in the MVP; glowsticks are the only player-placed light, and they are consumed.
7. **No stealth score or "hidden" status.** Going dark lowers a stimulus Attention reads; nothing tells the player they are hidden.
8. **Darkness is never punished by this system.** It neither drains, damages nor penalises; the dark only withholds agency (you cannot act on what is unlit). Any sanity cost of darkness is a **hard constraint on the Sanity & Hallucination Rules GDD, not a warning** (creative-director, CD-GDD-ALIGN: darkness never costs sanity, because go-dark collapses as a strategy, and P3's restraint with it, if darkness costs more than the exposure it saves; Open Question 4).
9. **No light for free.** No ambient reveal radius around the player; the avatar is a light source only through its beam.

## Detailed Design

### Core Rules

*Every rule below is a sim rule of the Emitter State owner module (`src/sim/emitter/`), stated so that an implementer can write it and a tester can falsify it. Registry constants are cited by their registered name (`TICK_RATE` 60, `TILE_PX` 48, `AIM_UNITS` 4096, `REACH_PX` 72, `WALL_FOOTPRINT_DEPTH` 24, `GRAPH_STEP_TICKS` 6, `MAX_DIRTY_KEYS` 256, `STEP_BUDGET_MS` 4, `rng_derive`, `state_hash`, `hook_runs_this_tick`, `chest_point`, `power_allocation`, `served_pm`, and the Graph's `INRUSH_STEPS` 5 / `INRUSH_PM` 1500) and are never restated with another value; owner-GDD tuning rows that are not registry entries (the Controller's `AIM_SEND_THRESHOLD`, `AIM_SETTLE_TICKS`, `FOCUS_STICKY_MARGIN`) are cited by name the same way. Every number this GDD introduces has its own name and the tag "placeholder, owner X, confirmed by Y" (Tuning Knobs); a number another GDD owns is cited by that GDD's name for it. Where a rule records an alternative that was considered and rejected, the rejection is part of the rule: a later change must answer it. Markers: "(orchestrator ruling: ...)" records a point where the specialists disagreed and the rulings, or a pick, decided; "(provisional — X undesigned/in flight)" marks an assumption about a system that is not yet a GDD.*

**EM-1 — One block, one hook, one truth.** Emitter State is exactly one persisted `SIM` state block, `emitter.state` (owner Emitter State; `class` SIM; `persist` true; `persist_scope` WORLD; `scope` SHARED; `repl_hint` SHARED_SUMMARY; `schema_version` 1; `restore_after: [layout.state, graph.state, body.state, controller.state]` (`layout.state` and `body.state` provisional — Layout and Ship Body undesigned; the Graph's `restore_after: [layout.state]` and the Controller's PC-14 are the precedents) — fixture anchors and occluding rectangles need the Layout, lamp nodes need the Graph, the beam's foot origin and frame need Body, and the chest point and aim need the Controller; the three-block list in the context brief omitted `body.state` and is superseded), registered by the module's composition-root call during BOOTING (kernel CR-6, CR-8). Exactly one step hook, `emitter.place` (divisor 1, offset 0, `after: [body.integrate, graph.propagate.failure]`), does all per-tick work: beam pose, output, lifecycle, the subject table. Its position after `body.integrate` is fixed by PC-15 and the registry comment on `hook_runs_this_tick` ("Controller/Body/Emitter 1"); the second entry, `graph.propagate.failure`, is this GDD's (the wording refresh owed to PC-15 is in Cross-References); no second Emitter hook exists in the MVP, because placement, lamp reads, fault glow and the subject table all need the same ordered reads. Hook ids are written exactly: `graph.propagate.power`, `graph.propagate.atmo`, `suit.vitals`, `controller.step`, `body.integrate`, `emitter.place`; the kernel has no alias concept and an unknown id or a cycle fails boot (CR-3), so the three Graph hook ids are always written with their phase suffix. The direct entry `after: graph.propagate.failure` puts `emitter.place`, on every Graph tick, after all three Graph hooks (`graph.propagate.power` → `graph.propagate.atmo` → `graph.propagate.failure`, Graph LS-8), so the hook reads the state the Graph run just produced — served power, node state after the failure phase and the descriptor list — and a snapshot taken at that tick and restored gives `validate` at the next tick the same subject table (kernel G4). The second chain, `graph.propagate.atmo` → `suit.vitals` (divisor 6) → `controller.step` → `body.integrate` → `emitter.place`, fixes where Suit's `force_off` lands (EM-10): `suit.vitals` is `after: graph.propagate.atmo` (Graph LS-8) and `controller.step` is `after: suit.vitals` (PC-15). That chain is **assumption A2, locked by PC-15 and LS-8; Suit must keep the id** `suit.vitals` (Suit & Equipment, designed, keeps it with `after: graph.propagate.atmo`: SE-8); the lamp latency of D.2 no longer depends on it. In `MIRROR` mode the hook never runs; the replica reads the replicated `SHARED_SUMMARY`, which in VS-B must carry the beam's origin, aim, shape and switch (derived values the host publishes) so a remote client can draw a crewmate's beam (an ask to Save/Load and Co-op). The module is scene-tree free, headless-testable and deterministic (same seed and command stream → identical `state_hash`).

**EM-2 — Vocabulary: the two `lit`s, kept verbatim.** The Controller locks `emitter.state.lit` as a *record field* (PC acceptance criteria test it on the seat's beam) and `lit(o)` as the *exposure query* (PC-6, PC D.9). Both names stay exactly as the Controller wrote them; renaming either breaks the Controller's tests. (a) **`emitter.state.lit`** — the record field: true iff the emitter's `output_pm > 0` (any kind). It means "this emitter is emitting". Suit's `suit.vitals` reads it on the seat's beam to drain charge; the Presenter draws a beam only when it is true. (b) **`lit(subject)`** — the query: the hysteresis-filtered comparison of a subject's action-gating light, `act_lux_pm`, against the threshold `LIT_ON_PM` / `LIT_OFF_PM` (EM-22). It means "this thing is lit enough to read and touch". Related terms: **`output_pm`** — an emitter's own steady brightness, integer 0..1000 permille; **`reach_px`** — an emitter's current outer radius in base px; **`lux_pm`** — a subject's total light, the saturating sum of every contribution at its anchor, integer 0..1000, read by Horror Entities (Attention reads the `emitters()` list, never `lux_pm`; Attention AT-4); **`act_lux_pm`** — the same sum restricted to emitters that gate actions (the BEACON does not); **`top_emitter_id`** — the emitter with the largest contribution to a subject (ties by ascending id). These two `lit`s are also defined in the Summary glossary line.

**EM-3 — Six emitter kinds, a closed list.** An emitter is one of BEAM, FIXTURE (subkind EMERGENCY marks the declared self-powered cryo-bay lamp, authored by Derelict Authoring DA-23 as `fixture_subkind_emergency`; every other fixture carries no subkind), NODE_LANTERN, DEPLOYABLE (subkind GLOWSTICK in the MVP; FLARE, TORCH, DECOY in VS-A), BEACON or FAULT_GLOW. A seventh kind is a schema bump (registry: `emitter_kind`). No record carries a `phantom`, `perceived`, `decoy` or `hallucinated` field of any kind (EM-29).

**EM-5 — Nobody writes emitters directly.** Records are created, changed and destroyed only through the published mutators below, each callable inside a kernel `WriteScope` (CR-9) by its declared caller, and by `emitter.place` itself for the two derived kinds. **EM-6 — The beam is a chest emitter.** For the seat's BEAM the *apex* and the *reach* are measured from `controller.state.chest_sim` (= `pos_curr + CHEST_POINT[sheet][anim] (.) mirror`, `|x| <= 2` px, registry `chest_point`; PC D.12), and the *axis* is the committed `aim_angle` (1/4096 turn, `AIM_UNITS` 4096; 0 = E, counter-clockwise, spawn aim 3072 = S). Both are **reads** of `controller.state` inside `emitter.place`, never commands: aim is not an Emitter command (the kernel row's "set/toggle/aim commands" wording is refreshed in Cross-References). The tool-grip anchor `HAND_POINT` is lightless and is never read by this block, and no rule anywhere names it an origin. The beam follows *aim*, not facing: facing diverges from aim while running (PC-4 rule 3). The drawn cone and the simulated cone may diverge by up to the PC-5 accepted tolerance (≤ 2.8°, ≤ 1 tick; the Controller's `AIM_SEND_THRESHOLD` / `AIM_SETTLE_TICKS` knobs widen it to 3 ticks), which is why the agreement test (design test 1) is stated at rest. **Occlusion starts at the foot, not the chest** *(orchestrator ruling: the foot-origin rule of the technical-director over the systems-designer's sanitised walk-back, because `pos_curr` is outside every footprint by construction — `LocomotionStep` keeps the avatar's box out of them — and is free of the 2 px mirror jitter; the walk-back remains the documented alternative, ADR candidate 5)*: `CHEST_POINT.y` is -45..-25 px, so with a 24 px wall footprint the chest point stands *inside* the footprint whenever the feet are 10-34 px south of a north wall. The cone's apex, reach and distance are therefore measured from the chest, while the occluder segment to a subject starts at the foot origin (`pos_curr`, `beam_pose`). Other emitter kinds use their floor-plane anchor for both. **Near-field is aim-dependent and that is accepted**: an object between the lamp and the feet in screen-y is behind the apex when the player faces up-screen; the cone is honest about it (nothing in the drawn cone reaches it either) and the sim adds no hidden compensation — a skill expression, not a bug. A small omnidirectional spill disc (`BEAM_SPILL_RADIUS_PX`, default 0) is the documented reserve if playtests reject this (Open Questions).

**EM-7 — The beam changes only on committed state.** `toggle_beam` is owned by Emitter State and fixed by the Controller (PC-5, PC-2): `actor` = the seat's beam emitter id, `actor_binding OWNED_BY_ISSUER`, `coalesce NONE`, `max_age_ticks` 120, origin `PLAYER`. The payload carries no state (it is a toggle: the `beam_toggle` press flips `switch_on`), so a rejected or duplicated command can never desynchronise the intent. Its apply flips `switch_on` in P2 of tick T; `emitter.place` in P3 of the same tick computes `output_pm`; the committed light therefore changes in the tick the toggle applies, with zero internal latency. The click and the hand's switch animation pre-play immediately (Presenter); **the light changes only on committed state and the Presenter never predicts beam state** (PC-5), because the beam is the human-channel anchor and may never lie, not even for one flicker. The seat's beam id is allocated at boot by E9; the session owner enters it in the kernel binding table (`PlayerId -> {controlled entity IDs}`) through the host-only API (CR-7: "a boot step in MVP"), which is what lets `toggle_beam` carry `OWNED_BY_ISSUER`. The binding table is not saved (CR-7: sessions rebuild it), so after a restore the session owner re-enters `beam_of(player_id)` (EM-28). *Alternative considered:* the Controller allocates the beam id — rejected (CR-8). **Strobe protection** *(orchestrator ruling: the systems-designer's `TOGGLE_LOCKOUT_TICKS` is not adopted as a new rejection reason on a Controller-locked command; it is routed to the Controller as an Adapter-side input debounce of `beam_toggle`, because a strobe is an input-rate concern, not a world rule: a sim-side `COOLDOWN` would make the one light that must never lie depend on a wall-clock-shaped state. `NOT_ACTIONABLE` and `NO_SUCH_EMITTER` are this GDD's own domain codes under the kernel's `VALIDATION_FAILED(domain_code)` namespace, which the Controller's registration (PC-2) leaves to the command owner)*: a player who strobes the one light that must never lie does so outside Lighting's `flash_count` clamp, an accessibility concern (WCAG 2.3.1, three flashes per second) the Controller owns the answer to.

**EM-9 — The beam in every actionable state; DOWNED keeps it, DEAD ends it.** The beam is available while the actor is IDLE, WALK, RUN, CARRY_WALK or CHANNELLING (PC-8; the user's chest-lamp decision: nothing the hands do touches the lamp). While `loco_state == DOWNED` the beam keeps its committed state — the aim is frozen at the last committed value and the origin is the `chest_sim` of the down animation row; a downed crewmate is a locator (P1) — which is the handoff PC left to this GDD ("Emitter State decides what a downed emitter does"). While STAGGERED the state is likewise unchanged. At the tick `emitter.place` reads `loco_state == DEAD`, it forces `switch_on` false (the Controller's `DEAD` row: "beam off (Emitter State on actor death)") as an `emitter.place`-internal `force_off`, a read of `controller.state` with no command. Because Save/Load's death stub restarts from the last autosave through `request_restore` at that tick's P6, the DEAD-tick off-state is unobservable in the MVP (say so in tests). A beam that stays on over a corpse until Suit's charge runs out — a corpse as a lantern the web can see — is *not* a rule here; it is a VS-B question for Attention and Sanity (Open Questions). In IN_POD the beam follows its actor into the pod's body frame (EM-30; provisional — Ship Body and Web Map undesigned).

**EM-10 — Beam charge is Suit's.** Suit & Equipment owns charge, drain, `CHARGE_EMPTY` and the HUD "beam and tool charge" reading (art bible HUD set). `toggle_beam.validate` reads Suit's charge view and rejects a turn-ON at zero charge with `CHARGE_EMPTY`; a turn-OFF is always accepted. Suit's divisor-6 `suit.vitals` reads `emitter.state.lit` on the seat's beam to drain charge and calls `force_off(beam_id)` (E5) at zero; because `suit.vitals` runs earlier in the tick than `emitter.place`, a `force_off` takes effect in the same tick's `emitter.place`, and the owner's six-tick re-check (EM-25) then finds a channelled target unlit and aborts, so at most five ticks of channel run on a dark target. The beam is a *suit load, not a ship LOAD*: Emitter State never calls `set_load_request` for it and the Graph is not involved. (Suit & Equipment, designed: SE-8; Suit keeps the hook name `suit.vitals` and its `after: graph.propagate.atmo`.)

**EM-11 — Fixtures are switchable Graph LOADs; the lamp formula gates on the switch.** A FIXTURE carries `node_ref` (a Graph `13 LOAD` node of the LOAD subtype `LAMP` — the Item DB assigns the subtype id, currently `2 LAMP`; the Graph names only `HEATER`; provisional), `base_pm` (from the def's `emits`) and `switch_on`. Its brightness is `lamp_output_pm` (Formulas D.1): `output_pm = switch_on and armed and live ? ⌊base_pm * served_pm / 1000⌋ : 0`. The lit branch is the registry formula `power_allocation`'s output "lamp brightness = base * served_pm / 1000", reused verbatim. **`served_pm` is the tier's satisfaction `sat_k`, independent of the node's own request** (Graph D.6), so a switched-off lamp can still read `served_pm` 1000: the Emitter's own `switch_on` is the gate, not the Graph, or an off lamp would shine. Power draw is a Graph LOAD: inside the `set_switch` apply the Emitter calls M14 `set_load_request(node_ref, req_w)` with `req_w` the def's rated draw (Graph MVP: 20 W) when on and 0 when off (Class B: it clamps to `0..req_max`, queues to the next Graph phase 0, and is a no-op on an unknown, tombstoned, dormant or SALVAGED node). A lamp never renders brighter than the power it is served (Graph P1); under re-energise inrush the bank draws `req_w * INRUSH_PM / 1000` for `INRUSH_STEPS` runs, which can dim the tier-3 bank for half a second — the Graph's business, visible as an honest dip, changing no Emitter rule. The Emitter never recomputes watts and never calls M14 for node lanterns or the beam. A FIXTURE with `node_ref` 0 is *self-powered*: permitted only for the cryo-bay emergency lamp when Derelict Authoring declares it so; it has no M14 call, no ARMING and a fixed `base_pm` (provisional — Derelict Authoring undesigned).

**EM-12 — One latency story for switching.** A `set_switch` applies at P2 of tick `T_a`; its M14 call is queued to the next Graph phase 0; the lamp is **ARMING with `output_pm` 0** until `arm_tick`, the first `graph.propagate.power` run that consumed the request, and is lit on the first `emitter.place` at or after it (Formulas D.2 `switch_latency`: `arm_tick = T_a + ((GRAPH_STEP_TICKS - (T_a mod GRAPH_STEP_TICKS)) mod GRAPH_STEP_TICKS)`). Under the stated assumptions the lamp is lit between 0 and `GRAPH_STEP_TICKS - 1` ticks after the apply — at most 83 ms — and no single number from this paragraph is a design fact: D.2 derives the range with a worked example at both alignments (switch on a Graph tick; switch on the tick after one). **Assumption A1 (derived, not assumed; the Graph's confirmation is requested):** kernel CR-2 ("P3 sees all of P2") and Graph LS-8 phase 0 ("apply queued unconditional inputs: `set_load_request` changes") together mean that an M14 call made in P2 of a Graph tick is consumed by that same tick's phase 0, so the range is 0..`GRAPH_STEP_TICKS - 1` ticks; the negative case, a Graph that applied only strictly later requests, would give 1..`GRAPH_STEP_TICKS` and is kept in D.2 as the labelled alternative. The direct `after: graph.propagate.failure` entry (EM-1) guarantees that the hook reads that run's output on the same tick. Switching **off** is immediate (`output_pm` 0 from the apply tick; M14 `(node_ref, 0)`). The same "light changes only on committed state" rule PC-5 states for the beam holds for fixtures; the asymmetry (ON waits for the Graph pulse, OFF does not) is deliberate and physical: the ship has to serve the lamp, and the lamp cannot go on ahead of the ship. Fixtures never pre-play light; the wall-switch click and animation pre-play.

**EM-13 — Liveness: a lamp is dark when its node is dead.** A lamp or lantern is *live* iff its `node_ref` exists and `node(id).state` is neither `3 DESTROYED`, `2 FAILED`, `4 SALVAGED` nor `5 ABSENT` (Graph LS-10) and the node is not dormant or tombstoned (read each tick from the Graph read view). A not-live lamp has `output_pm` 0 while its `switch_on` stays as committed — when the node is repaired the lamp lights again without a new command. `served_pm` and node `state` are read live each tick; both change only on Graph ticks, and because `emitter.place` is `after: graph.propagate.failure` (EM-1) a Graph-tick read sees the state the run just produced, never a pre-failure-phase state. Which Graph states count as "running" for a *lantern* is provisional (the Item DB set decides, EM-14). *(orchestrator ruling: R22 as amended at the checkpoint (CP-3) — `emitter.place` declares the direct `after:` entry, so no read is ever lagged; the technical-director's proposal to sample slow inputs once per Graph run into canonical state is not adopted, because the direct ordering needs no sampled state and a restore at a Graph tick rebuilds the identical table.)*

**EM-14 — Node lanterns are derived and switchless.** A NODE_LANTERN stands for the glow of a running machine (a pump, a reactor). It has no switch and makes no `set_load_request` call: `output_pm` is `⌊base_pm * term / 1000⌋` when the node is live and *running* (Formulas D.3 `node_lantern_output`), where `term` is the node's `run_pm` reading. A lantern gated on `served_pm` alone would glow from a switched-off machine (the same trap as EM-11), so the lantern reads a *running* quantity `run_pm` from the Graph read view — **an ask to the Graph** (the Graph's `node()` view has `served_pm`, `flow` and `state` but no explicit "running at duty d" read; the ask is `run_pm` = 0 when the node's requested load is 0 (machine off: LOAD `req_w` 0, PUMP `throttle_pm` 0, REACTOR `out_w` 0), else the served fraction (`served_pm` for a consumer, `⌊1000 × out_w / out_w_rated⌋` for a REACTOR); it is not the Graph's D.9 `duty_pm`, which is `served_pm` for consumers and carries the same trap as EM-11, because a machine switched off by M14 on a met tier still reads `served_pm` 1000; provisional). Lanterns are created and destroyed with their section (EM-5), counted against Lighting's lantern budget by authoring lint (EM-26), and killing the machine is the only way to dark one — "every loop you run is a lantern the web can see" (art bible Principle 3).

**EM-15 — Deployables and glowsticks: spawn on landing, steady, then waning, then gone.** `spawn_deployable(kind, pos_px, owner, life_ticks)` (E3) is called by Interaction's landing-tick hook (PC D.16: "the light exists only on landing"; the thrown light is never pre-played). The glowstick numbers are the Item DB's placeholders and are cited by name: `GLOWSTICK_LIFE_S` 150 (60–300), `GLOWSTICK_RADIUS_TILES` 2 (= 96 base px, 1.5–3), `GLOWSTICK_STACK_MAX` 4, `GLOWSTICK_WANE_FROM_PM` 400, `GLOWSTICK_WANE_FLOOR_PM` 250 (Item DB IDB-12). This GDD owns the curve's *semantics*, id `WANE_LINEAR_25`: while the remaining life fraction is at or above `GLOWSTICK_WANE_FROM_PM / 1000` the emitter holds its full reach (steady); below it, `reach_px` falls linearly (in the permille factor `f`, then floored to whole px) to `GLOWSTICK_WANE_FLOOR_PM / 1000` of full reach at the last tick (24 px at `life_remaining_ticks` 1); this is the Item DB's F-7 `reach_px(rem)` verbatim, so the two GDDs agree tick for tick; at `life_remaining_ticks` 0 the record is removed and its id retired (Formulas D.9). *(orchestrator ruling: the Item DB's steady-then-waning parameterisation, `GLOWSTICK_WANE_FROM_PM` 400, is adopted over the routed ruling's linear-from-spawn curve — they are the same curve at `GLOWSTICK_WANE_FROM_PM` 1000 — because the art bible specifies "steady at full value, then dimming monotonically", and the Item DB owns these numbers. A different number there is a conflict to surface, not a silent change.)* The output stays constant (`GLOWSTICK_BASE_PM`), the reveal radius shrinks, and that waning is **sim state**, not presentation, because it changes the real reveal radius (P1). It never flickers (art bible 3.2); the flicker of the VS-A flare, torch and decoy family is Lighting's and invisible to the query, which uses the steady mean. The stick lights the floor plane in ship-local px, counts as exposure like any pool (Attention may weight by area rather than radius — a pool at the floor radius has one sixteenth of the full area; the read view carries `reach_px` and the shape so either is derivable), and a stick landing in a closed room lights only that room (occluders). At most `MAX_DEPLOYABLES_LIVE` non-evicting deployables exist. **Cap eviction is a visible wane, never an instant removal** *(creative-director CD-GDD-ALIGN concern 2: a light dying with no physical cause would break the promise that a stick's waning is its only clock, and would be a P1 lie)*: a spawn over the cap (Class B never rejects; the item was already spent, and Item DB rule (c): a landed stick is spent without refund) is applied in full, and the lowest-id deployable of the same kind that is not already evicting enters an **evict-wane** — `evict_tick` and `reach_at_evict` are written, its output stays `GLOWSTICK_BASE_PM`, and its reach falls linearly from its current reach to the 24 px floor over `ticks_for_seconds(GLOWSTICK_EVICT_WANE_S)` ticks (placeholder 10 s = 600 ticks, owner game-designer, Tuning Knobs), then the record is removed and its id retired (Formulas D.9b). It reads in the world as a stick guttering fast, the same visible monotone shrink as the ordinary wane, only quicker; the 24 px floor is the ordinary wane's own last value, so the final removal is the same event the ordinary end of life already is. An evicting stick still exposes, still gates actions and still sums at its current reach until it is gone; it does not count against `MAX_DEPLOYABLES_LIVE`, but the evicting tail is itself bounded by `MAX_DEPLOYABLES_EVICTING` (placeholder 4); an eviction that would exceed it completes the oldest evicting stick at once, increments `emitter_deployable_evict_overflow` and fires a debug assertion — a budget defect, never a gameplay state. **Eviction is meant to be unreachable by supply in the MVP, and that supply bound belongs to other owners** *(cross-GDD invariant I-SUPPLY, routed, Cross-References; the total glowsticks one seat carries across all stacks is at most `MAX_DEPLOYABLES_LIVE`, so no seat can have more sticks in flight than the cap)*: Suit & Equipment owns the per-seat carry count and start count, the Item DB owns `GLOWSTICK_STACK_MAX`, and Derelict Authoring owns the loose-stick supply by depth, which must not allow a throw-pickup-throw cycle to exceed the cap inside one `GLOWSTICK_LIFE_S` (a landed stick is never picked up again, Item DB rule (c), so a cycle needs a loose stick on the floor). The evict-wane exists because the bound can be violated by content and by VS-B (four seats × a stack of 4 is 16 against a cap of 8); every eviction increments the release telemetry counter `emitter_deployable_evictions`.

**EM-16 — The beacon is a pure function of time.** A BEACON is a 20° cone (shape row 8) whose axis is `beacon_aim = (beacon_phase + beacon_dir * ⌊T * AIM_UNITS / BEACON_SWEEP_TICKS⌋) mod 4096` with `T` the kernel clock tick (non-negative modulus), `BEACON_SWEEP_TICKS` 128 (2.13 s, inside the art bible's 2–3 s; placeholder, owner art-director / audio, confirmed by the Lighting spike), so the step is exactly 32 units per tick. Direction and phase are authored data; **no per-tick angle state exists**, which is what makes save-equivalence automatic (the kernel clock is persisted by the kernel). *(orchestrator ruling: the rational form of the systems-designer over the routed ruling's `T * BEACON_UNITS_PER_TICK`, because it is identical at 128 and lets any integer sweep length be valid — the ruled range 120–180 contained exactly one divisor of 4096.)* The beacon's brightness is the lamp formula when it carries a `node_ref` (MVP: it does — a dead ship's beacon is dark, P1), switched by `set_beacon`; turned on, it starts from the angle the pure function gives at that tick (no restart at phase 0). It is a dynamic-layer emitter (it rotates) and `gates_actions` is **false** *(orchestrator ruling: the systems-designer's F9 adopted provisionally — a cone sweeping over a point lights it for 14 or 15 of every 128 ticks by phase (the cone is 456 units wide and the sweep advances 32 units per tick, so ⌊456/32⌋ = 14 or ⌈456/32⌉ = 15; about 11 %), and a prompt that strobes at the sweep rate, or a channel that aborts every 128 ticks, would be the system lying about what can be worked; the beacon exposes and is seen, it does not enable verbs. This narrows the Controller's PC-6 for one kind; routed to the technical-director and game-designer)*.

**EM-17 — Fault glow is a derived emitter kind.** FAULT_GLOW emitters stand for a venting ember, a power arc and cyan O₂ mist as point sources (art bible 2.8 / Failure & Trail). They are created and removed by `emitter.place` from the Graph's failure descriptors (`failures(filter)`), each keyed by `FailureDescriptor.mode` (a failure mode 1..10, or a descriptor-only id 20..26) plus `medium`, matching the Failure & Trail glow-table keys; the MVP rows with a glow are `1/FUEL`, `1/GAS`, `2`, `3`, `7`, `9`. They use Failure & Trail's descriptor-to-glow data table (rows owned by F&T; a descriptor with no glow row creates no emitter), by comparing the Graph's list, read on Graph ticks, against the FAULT_GLOW records that already exist — no subscription (kernel CR-10) and no stored previous list. They have no switch, no Graph LOAD and no command; `output_pm` comes from the descriptor's `sev` 0..4 and the row's `sev_min` gate (D.11); they are non-shadow and count as exposure. They are capped at `MAX_FAULT_GLOW` (placeholder 10); **a fault's light never goes dark with no physical cause** *(creative-director CD-GDD-ALIGN concern 1: a real failure whose light vanishes weakens P1, the physical consequence becomes invisible, and P2, a fault glow is one of the tells a careful player reads)*, so the cap is a content bound that is held by a boot invariant (I-CAPS (c), D.15: `MAX_FAULT_GLOW` is at least the most simultaneous glowing authored faults on one body plus a 25 % margin; in the MVP slice a welded body carries at most 6 simultaneous glow rows, so the floor is ⌈1.25 × 6⌉ = 8 ≤ 10, the 8 live faults being spread world-wide across bodies; registry: `mvp_fault_allocation`), and eviction is a last resort with three rules. (1) **Lowest severity first**: when reconciliation would hold more than `MAX_FAULT_GLOW` records, the records evicted are those with the lowest `sev`, ties broken by the higher descriptor id first (the kept set is the same as "highest `sev` wins, ties by ascending descriptor id"). (2) **A protected record is never evicted**: a record whose age `T − placed_tick` is below `ticks_for_seconds(W_MIN_WARNING_S)` (the Graph's registered fairness constant, 10 s = 600 ticks; cited, not restated) is protected, so the tell of a new fault is always shown for at least the Graph's warning window. (3) **Protection beats the cap**: if every candidate for eviction is protected, no record is evicted and the table stays over `MAX_FAULT_GLOW` (bounded only by `MAX_EMITTERS`), increments the release telemetry counter `emitter_fault_glow_overcap` and fires a debug assertion; it is trimmed back by rules 1 and 2 on the first Graph tick on which a candidate is no longer protected. Overcap is a content or budget defect and never a gameplay state. Every eviction increments `emitter_fault_glow_evictions`. An evicted glow removes only the descriptor's light and its exposure: the Failure & Trail emissive overlay on the sprite (an unshaded overlay, not an emitter) still draws the fault, so the fault stays visible in the world. **Timing (R22 as amended, CP-3):** `emitter.place` is `after: graph.propagate.failure`, so on a Graph tick it reads the descriptor list that run just produced and a glow appears on the Graph tick of its descriptor, never later; between Graph ticks the list cannot change. A FAULT_GLOW record is a normal record (it has an id from the canonical counter), so it persists as the set of descriptors it stood for. The emissive `_msk` trail glow on a sprite is an *unshaded overlay, not an emitter*, and exposes nothing: a lit-but-unregistered source would light a room Attention cannot see (Failure & Trail's brief). Whether a glow gates actions: yes (an arc lights the scrubber beside it). (Failure & Trail's designed FT-27 agrees: it supplies "a descriptor-to-glow data table (palette hex, radius in base px, flicker class bounded by FT-29)" and "Emitter State registers the emitter from the same descriptor". **Failure & Trail owns the glow-table rows including the ledger values `GLOW_BASE_PM[key]` and `SEV_GLOW_PM[0..4]` (placeholders it carries in V/A.6); this GDD owns D.11, the FAULT_GLOW record, `MAX_FAULT_GLOW` and `FAULT_GLOW_REACH_PX`, and multiplies the ledger values.** This GDD takes each row's `reach_px` from the F&T `radius_px` at the sev 4 column (the sev 3 column where sev 4 is n/a), defaulting to `FAULT_GLOW_REACH_PX` when a row carries none. F&T's emissive layer "casts no light", which is the `_msk` sentence above.)

**EM-18 — The illumination model: contribution, curve classes, saturating sum.** For one emitter and one subject, the contribution `c` is **0** unless all of: the subject's `body_id` equals the emitter's (EM-30), the subject is inside the emitter's radius (`d2 <= reach_px²`, a closed edge), inside its cone for cone shapes (EM-19), not blocked (EM-20). The one exception is the owner's own beam on the owner's ACTOR subject, which contributes its apex value, facing- and occlusion-independent (EM-21). For every other pair `c = ⌊output_pm * f(d, reach_px) / 1000⌋` (Formulas D.7), `d` the exact integer floor of the distance from the emitter's origin (the chest, for the beam). Two **curve classes** are carried as a data table so the Lighting spike can replace a curve without changing the API: **SOFT** (pools, fixtures, glowsticks, lanterns, fault glow) `f = 1000 * (R - d) / R`, linear to zero at the fade-out radius; **HARD** (the cones and the beacon) `f = EDGE_FLOOR_PM + (1000 - EDGE_FLOOR_PM) * (R - d) / R`, so the contribution at the rim of a full-output hard-edged emitter is `EDGE_FLOOR_PM` (placeholder 250 permille, range 160–400, owner technical-artist, confirmed by the Lighting spike) and the lit boundary **is** the drawn hard edge. *(orchestrator ruling: this is a deliberate deviation from the routed ruling's single default curve `c = output_pm * (R - d) / R`, which contradicts its own "hard outer edge exactly at `reach_px`" clause: with `LIT_ON_PM` 150 a full-output wide beam of reach 288 px would be actionable only to `d <= 244` px — 44 px, almost a full tile, inside the hard edge the player sees, the light visibly falling on a valve the hand cannot reach, which is the game lying (P1) and contradicts art bible hero #1. Three specialists converged on the edge-floor remedy; rejected: lowering `LIT_ON_PM` toward 0 (a near-black pixel becomes actionable — a different lie, and hysteresis dies) and redefining the "drawn edge" as an iso-line at 150 permille (contradicts the hard edge). Surfaced as a deviation from the ruling's default in the return value, not a registry conflict.)* The consequence is intended: a *dimmed* hard-edged emitter (output below `⌈LIT_ON_PM * 1000 / EDGE_FLOOR_PM⌉` = 600) has an honestly smaller actionable area; a full-output emitter is actionable to its last pixel (invariant I-EDGE: `BEAM_BASE_PM * EDGE_FLOOR_PM >= 1000 * LIT_ON_PM`). For SOFT classes the actionable radius is `r_lit = ⌊R * (output_pm - LIT_ON_PM) / output_pm⌋`, inside the fade-out radius `reach_px` by design (a full fixture of reach 144 px is actionable to 122 px); Lighting must draw a soft pool's readable boundary at `r_lit`, which the Lighting spike confirms (the art bible and Item DB "radius" numbers — 3 tiles, 2 tiles — are provisional, and the spike must decide whether they mean fade-out or readable radius). **Aggregation is a saturating sum**, `lux_pm = min(1000, Σ c)`: integer addition is commutative, so no iteration-order dependence; several lamps in one room read brighter and expose more until fully lit. There is **no ambient term**: `AMBIENT_PM` is fixed at 0 in the sim, and Lighting's ambient is cosmetic and can never make an object actionable (P1; art bible Principle 1: unlit, not fogged). `lux_pm` sums every emitter (Horror reads it; Attention reads `emitters()` and never `lux_pm`, AT-4); `act_lux_pm` sums only the emitters whose `gates_actions` is true (EM-3) and feeds `lit` (EM-22).

**EM-19 — Cone membership is fixed-point and mirror-symmetric.** A cone test uses no trigonometry, square root or float at run time (Graph LS-1(b) precedent). Directions come from a table `DIR` of Q14 unit vectors — **the table is generated at build time by a data tool outside `src/sim/emitter/` and checked in as an integer data file, never computed in the sim module** (the boot step only loads it and asserts I-MIRROR and I-OVERFLOW, so the AC-2 lint holds) — indexed by an `AIM_UNITS` angle (0 = E, counter-clockwise, screen y down, so aim 3072 = S is `(0, +16384)`), generated from one quadrant by reflection so that `DIR[2048 - a] = (-ux, uy)` **exactly**. A subject at offset `r = anchor - origin` is in the cone iff `d2 <= R²`, `D > 0` and `D² >= d2 * COS2_Q28`, with `D = rx*ux + ry*uy` (Formulas D.5); the `D > 0` guard is required (the squared test alone accepts the opposite half-plane) and `d2 = 0` is inside every cone of that emitter, the apex included. The split beam is two tests at axes `aim ± 206` units; the wedge between them never closes (I-WEDGE), so a point is in at most one cone and its contribution is never doubled. Every product has a documented bound under 2^62 and the boot assertion refuses a build that violates it (Formulas D.15). The half-angles and the 18° offset are not integral in `AIM_UNITS`, so the shape table (EM-8) carries the encoded integers; the quantisation misplaces a rim by under 0.1° (under 0.7 px at 384 px), far inside the accepted committed-versus-drawn divergence. **The symmetry property is a test, not a hope**: the exposure of a mirrored scene (x → -x, aim `a` → `2048 - a`, `flip_h` toggled) equals the mirror image of the original exactly, for the five drawn and three mirrored facings, every `CHEST_POINT` animation row and all four shapes (Acceptance Criteria).

**EM-20 — Occlusion is integer geometry over the Layout's opaque footprints.** *(orchestrator ruling R3 — the one deliberate deviation from a routed thread's wording.)* The routed thread and the index describe occlusion as tests on "physics shapes". This GDD honours that in substance and changes the test method: the occluding shapes are the Layout's collision-footprint rectangles — walls (`WALL_FOOTPRINT_DEPTH` 24 base px: face 72, cap 24, tile 48), closed door leaves, and props flagged `occludes_light` — which are the very rectangles the Layout `PROJECTOR` hands to physics and `LocomotionStep` already sweeps the avatar's box against (PC-14, PC-18). The query tests a segment against those rectangles in exact integer arithmetic (Formulas D.6) behind an **`OccluderSource` seam** (`rects_in(aabb)`, ascending rect id; `version(section_id)`, a monotone counter bumped when any opaque rectangle in the section changes); there is **no physics backend in the MVP**, and one may exist only as a test cross-check (the hybrid option). The reasons that decide it: kernel H10 (systems stay scene-tree free, headless) and CR-13 G3 (the 2D physics engine makes no determinism claim, so a physics-derived `lit` flowing into a persisted block would be a physics observation that must be recorded for replay); a direct-space-state query is valid only during the physics step, while the kernel allows this query from presentation prompts at render rate, exactly the unsafe site; and the 2D ray API is not sourceable in the pinned engine reference (its `physics.md` carries only a 3D example). *Rules.* (a) **Rectangles are closed and the rule is conservative**: a segment that touches a rectangle (a corner graze, an edge-on pass) is blocked; light does not leak around a corner. (b) **Anchors must be at least `ANCHOR_CLEARANCE_PX` (1 base px) outside every occluding rectangle** (Chebyshev distance on integer anchors, which is the same as "not inside or on the boundary of the closed rectangle": a wall stored [408,432), whose occupied closed span is x 408..431, admits anchors at x = 407 and x = 432 and rejects 408 through 431; registry: `rect_algebra_closed_span`, the Layout's half-open convention), checked by authoring lint at registration; an anchor found strictly inside a footprint at run time is treated as blocked from every emitter (fails safe to dark). (c) **Actors and horrors never occlude.** A shadow with no occluder is a stage-2 sanity symptom (art bible 3.2 open item: it should not cast an occluding shadow) and must stay an unambiguous tell; so a horror standing in a beam leaves the beam unchanged and the subject beyond it lit. (d) **Door state is read from the Layout projection of the Graph's `door_state`** (Graph LS-6: the Graph owns door state and opening `area`) — the same door-leaf rectangle `collide` uses (PC-3, PC-18 test 7: same-tick door response) — so there is one source for collision and light, derived from the Graph in the same tick, and Emitter State holds **no door flag**. The door-leaf rectangle is the Layout's `door_leaf_gap` projection (Layout D.5, LY-9): `leaf_w` is continuous in the Graph's opening area and `leaf_w` 0 yields no rectangle, so no open-fraction threshold exists; `DOOR_LIGHT_OPEN_PM` is deprecated and unused. (e) A door interactable registers **two** face subjects, one per face, each `ANCHOR_CLEARANCE_PX` outside the leaf, so a closed leaf blocks the other side's emitters from each face subject (Interaction's registration; EM-21). (f) No glass or translucent occluders in the MVP. (g) The occlusion segment starts at the occlusion origin of EM-6 (the foot, for the beam). The wording this implies for the index and the kernel is routed in Cross-References. *Alternatives considered:* a physics-space query against a dedicated occluder layer — rejected (above); reusing a rendering occluder object as the sim's occluder — rejected outright, it is Lighting's rendering object and the sim never reads it.

**EM-21 — Subjects register; each has one anchor; an unregistered ref answers like an unlit one.** Anything that asks "am I lit" registers a **subject** with `register_subject(ref, kind, anchor_px, section_id)` (E6) and withdraws it with `unregister_subject(ref)` (E7). Subject kinds: OBJECT (an interaction anchor), ACTOR (the foot origin, *read* from `body.state` each tick, not supplied), HORROR, ITEM (loose items). The subject's single `anchor_px` is the only sampled point: a large object half in a beam is lit iff its anchor is (the same one-point model as `REACH_PX` and focus scoring, PC-6, and the cheapest symmetric one). Sections are never point-sampled; they aggregate by emitter list (`section_emission`, EM-27). `moving` is a per-registration flag: ACTOR and HORROR subjects are always moving; an ITEM registers static (a carried item is unregistered at pickup and re-registered at drop); only moving registrations count against `MAX_MOVING_SUBJECTS`. **The owner stands in their own light** *(orchestrator ruling: the systems-designer's SD-7 adopted with the checkpoint fix CP-9 — as first proposed it excluded the owner's beam from the owner's lux, so a player alone with the lamp on would have read `lux` 0 to Horror, the opposite of P3)*: the owner's own beam contributes its **full `output_pm`** — the d = 0 apex value, which EM-19 already places inside every cone — to the owner's ACTOR `lux_pm`, independent of facing and of occlusion, so the owner's self-lux never flips with the way they face; it is not added to the owner's `act_lux_pm` (the player is never the target of their own verb). Attention reads the beam as *emission* (`emitters()`, `section_emission`) and does not add the same beam again to the owner's lux (EM-27). Every other beam lights the owner, and the owner's beam lights everyone else, by the ordinary cone test. **An unregistered ref answers exactly like an unlit registered one** *(orchestrator ruling: SD-16 adopted — a P4 requirement)*: `lux(ref)` is 0 and `lit(ref)` is false in release builds, so the Perception Filter's phantom targets (kernel Open Question 14) cost Interaction the same check as real unlit ones and the query cannot become an existence oracle that undermines the phantom-loot hallucination. A debug-only `registered(ref)` exists for tests and the Debug Overlay. Registration over `MAX_EXPOSURE_SUBJECTS` returns false, increments `emitter_subjects_overflow` and the subject reads dark (fails safe); a duplicate registration is a no-op returning false. **Runtime overflow is a content or budget defect, never a gameplay state** *(creative-director CD-GDD-ALIGN concern 3)*: failing safe to dark is the right behaviour for a subject the table cannot hold, but a real object sitting visibly inside a beam that refuses to prompt breaks design test 1 (the honest edge) and the P4 "reach for it" falsifier, because the player reads a real lit object as a hallucination. **EM-22 — `lit(subject)`: threshold, hysteresis and the beacon exception.** `lit(ref)` is a hysteresis filter over `act_lux_pm` (Formulas D.8): it becomes true when `act_lux_pm >= LIT_ON_PM`, becomes false when `act_lux_pm < LIT_OFF_PM`, and holds between. Placeholders `LIT_ON_PM` 150 (range 100–300) and `LIT_OFF_PM` 120 (60–140), the `LIT_THRESHOLD_PM` family **owned by the technical-artist** (art bible 7.0: "the illumination threshold that enables prompt and inspect-card drawing"), confirmed against the rendered edge in the Lighting spike (8.12 step 2). A newly registered subject starts unlit and is evaluated at the next `emitter.place`. A hard-edged rim is a *step* (a full-output rim contributes `EDGE_FLOOR_PM` then 0 across one pixel), which the 150/120 hysteresis never sees; rim chatter while a beam sweeps is held by the Controller's committed-aim quantisation (`AIM_SEND_THRESHOLD` 32 units) and `FOCUS_STICKY_MARGIN` (PC-6), and the hysteresis bites only for dimmed or soft light (brownout, waning glowsticks, fixture pools). There is **no dwell** (`LIT_OFF_DWELL_TICKS` fixed at 0): keeping `lit` true after the light went would be a window where the sim says lit and the world is dark, a P1 lie. `lit(ref)` and `lux(ref)` are separate answers because the beacon's sweep must not strobe prompts (EM-16). **The hysteresis bit is canonical** (checkpoint ruling CP-2): `lit(ref)` depends on last tick's answer, and a subject whose `act_lux_pm` sits between `LIT_OFF_PM` and `LIT_ON_PM` has no other source for its bit, so by EM-4's own definition the bit is a discrete fact — one bit per registered subject, at most `MAX_EXPOSURE_SUBJECTS` bits, snapshotted and hashed. `post_restore` rebuilds `lux_pm`, `act_lux_pm` and `top_emitter_id` from the restored emitters and **keeps the bit**; the next `emitter.place` applies hysteresis normally. A subject saved at 130 permille and lit is therefore lit after the restore, and Interaction's `validate` at T+1 reads the same answer in the live run and the restored run (kernel G4). *(orchestrator ruling: the systems-designer's canonical bitset is the rule. Rejected alternative: rebuild every bit against `LIT_ON_PM` in `post_restore` (the earlier ruling R6) — a subject between the thresholds would flip from lit to unlit across a restore, `validate` at T+1 would accept in one run and reject `NOT_LIT` in the other, and canonical state downstream would diverge.)* Interaction's `validate` and the HUD prompt call this same `lit(ref)`, so the prompt never lies by omission (PC-6).

**EM-23 — The order of work inside `emitter.place`; static and dynamic layers; no subscriptions.** The hook does, in this order and nothing else: **W0** read upstream (`controller.state` and `body.state` for bound seats; the Layout `OccluderSource.version(section_id)`; the Graph's `served_pm` and `node(id)`; on Graph ticks the Graph's `failures(filter)`); **W1** lifecycle (DEAD force-off, deployable aging, evict-wane (D.9b) and removal, `arm_tick` arming, fault-glow reconciliation); **W2** derive every emitter in ascending id (origin, aim, reach, `output_pm`, record `lit`); **W3** invalidate static-layer entries whose inputs changed; **W4** rebuild the static layer for invalidated subjects in ascending ref; **W5** the dynamic layer for every subject (with the owner-beam special case of EM-21: the owner's own beam adds its apex value to the owner's ACTOR `lux_pm` with no cone or occlusion test); **W6** saturating sums, hysteresis, write the subject table, record the tick's counted work; **W7** write dirty keys (EM-26). The **static layer** (FIXTURE, NODE_LANTERN, FAULT_GLOW, resting DEPLOYABLE) is cached per subject as a saturated partial sum (exact: `min(1000, min(1000, a) + b) = min(1000, a + b)`); the **dynamic layer** (BEAM, BEACON, which rotate or translate every tick) is recomputed per tick with the cheapest rejects first — radius, then cone, then the occlusion test. A subject's static cache is rebuilt when `emitter.place` *observes* a change in what it read last tick: the section's `OccluderSource.version(...)` changed, an emitter's `output_pm` or integer `reach_px` changed, an emitter was created or removed, or the subject moved. **Never by subscription**: kernel CR-10 ("simulation systems never subscribe; sim-to-sim is ordered step reads or commands"); dirty keys are the notification channel for PRESENTATION and friends, not an input to this block. A pose-unchanged skip (a dynamic emitter whose origin, aim, output, shape and occlusion origin equal last tick's skips re-evaluation) is a pure optimisation and changes no answer. A static emitter's cost is paid when something changes, not every tick; a glowstick's reach steps by one px every 50 ticks while it wanes (3600 ticks over 72 px, D.9), so the caches of subjects in its range rebuild that often.

**EM-24 — Counted work, not wall-clock; the caps.** The per-tick exposure cost is bounded by **counted work units**, never by timing — a time-based governor would be nondeterministic. The caps (all *placeholder, owner technical-director, confirmed by the exposure spike and the Interaction / Derelict Authoring content counts*; never inherited from a default of 64): `MAX_EXPOSURE_SUBJECTS` 192 (derived from the MVP slice's 14 compartments — home-ship 5, derelict A 4, derelict B 5 — their verb-bearing nodes, doors counted twice for their two face subjects, loose items, actors and horror subjects; Formulas D.14; *orchestrator pick: the systems-designer's 192 over the technical-director's 128, because doors register two subjects (EM-20 e) and the MVP Graph's node count supports a larger interactable set*), `MAX_MOVING_SUBJECTS` 16 (the 17th moving registration returns false, increments `emitter_moving_overflow` and the subject reads dark, the same fail-safe as `MAX_EXPOSURE_SUBJECTS`), `MAX_EMITTERS` 96, `MAX_DEPLOYABLES_LIVE` 8, `MAX_DEPLOYABLES_EVICTING` 4, `MAX_FAULT_GLOW` 10. **The caps are content budgets, held at data load, not gameplay states** *(CD-GDD-ALIGN concerns 1 and 3)*: the MVP slice has exactly 8 live faults world-wide, of which a welded body carries at most 6 simultaneous glow rows (registry `mvp_fault_allocation`); I-CAPS therefore requires `MAX_FAULT_GLOW` to be at least the most simultaneous glowing authored faults on one body plus 25 % (⌈1.25 × 6⌉ = 8; the cap of 10 keeps 2 of headroom), and requires the **dynamic registrants** that arrive after boot to be held to per-category budgets owned by their authors: Derelict Authoring owns the budget for authored OBJECT and ITEM subjects and for dropped loot and spawned salvage per section, Horror Entities owns the horror-subject budget (`HORROR_SUBJECTS_MAX` 2 (Horror Entities, registry), all moving; A runtime overflow of any cap is a defect of one of those budgets, is counted by a release-build telemetry counter (`emitter_subjects_overflow`, `emitter_moving_overflow`, `emitter_probe_overflow`, `emitter_fault_glow_overcap`, `emitter_fault_glow_evictions`, `emitter_deployable_evictions`, `emitter_deployable_evict_overflow`) as well as asserted in debug, and is never something a design relies on. `EXPOSURE_BUDGET_MS` is **0.4 ms P99** for `emitter.place` plus all exposure evaluation (placeholder, spike on the Release export template; the kernel's `STEP_BUDGET_MS` 4 is a logging threshold and P3's target is ≤ 2.0 ms P99 with the Graph at 0.5 ms and Controller + Body about 0.3 ms). Mandatory work (the dynamic layer, static rebuilds, the subject table) is never skipped. One-off points — Horror's pathing probes, `lit_by(point)` — are served by `probe(point)` from the units left over after the previous tick's mandatory work **and** a hard `MAX_PROBES_PER_TICK` (4; ruled as a ceiling of 16 and lowered by the cost model of D.14, which prices 16 probes at about 0.35 ms, 87 % of `EXPOSURE_BUDGET_MS`, and affords 4 at the design point — the systems-designer's 8 does not fit either; CP-11); a probe over either bound returns `UNKNOWN`, **never a stale `true`**, and increments `emitter_probe_overflow`. Probes are sim-side only (callable from `validate` and P3 hooks); presentation prompts use `lit(ref)` lookups. The GDScript op cost is not sourceable from the pinned engine reference (no scripting module), so every cost number is a model parameter for the spike; the fallback if the dynamic layer is too slow is a baked per-room illumination grid for the static layer (the brief's option c), then reducing `MAX_MOVING_SUBJECTS`, then room-gated broadphase.

**EM-25 — Query semantics, the read view and the staleness bound.** Public reads are table lookups against the subject table and the emitter records; they are pure, side-effect free and callable from `validate`, from P3 and from presentation prompts, **never from notification handlers that decide state** (kernel C.3 row 7). Source ages stack on top and are named, not hidden: the beam's committed aim is up to the Controller's tolerance behind the drawn aim; a lamp's brightness follows `served_pm`, which changes only on Graph ticks. **EM-26 — Dirty keys, the Lighting contract and the budget agreement.** Dirty keys are emitted for **emitters**, never for individual subjects (publish rule): a key `E/<emitter id>` — an int `(KIND << 32) | id` as in Graph LS-20 — fires on create, remove, `switch_on` flip, `shape_id` change, an `output_pm` change that crosses a bucket (`OUTPUT_DIRTY_QUANTUM_PM`, placeholder 50) and an integer `reach_px` step; continuous pose (origin, aim) never dirties — Lighting polls the read view at render rate, and keys wake only discrete presentation (a light switching on or off, a record spawning). Subject `lux` and `lit` are poll reads: sim consumers read inside their hook, presentation prompts poll at render rate, so a sweeping beam over 192 subjects cannot collapse notifications to `ALL` against `MAX_DIRTY_KEYS` 256 (worst case here is every emitter, at most `MAX_EMITTERS` 96, in one tick). **The Lighting contract** (2D Lighting & Occlusion reads and never writes): per emitter `{id, kind, subkind, shape_id, origin_px, aim_units, half_angle_units, reach_px, curve_class, output_pm, hue_class, lit, section_id, body_id, priority}` plus the shape table. This block publishes facts; Lighting conforms to them. Lighting owns textures, ambient, the canvas modulate, shadow-slot assignment (the sixth slot), the flash clamp (`flash_count` ≤ 3 per rolling second per light), flicker, gutter and decoy visuals; flicker is invisible to the query, which uses the steady mean. **Budget agreement (R17):** the sim never culls by view (an off-screen emitter still exposes — correct for P3); instead authoring lint keeps in-view emitter counts under Lighting's hypotheses (shadow-casting ≤ 6, non-shadow ≤ 24, node lanterns ≤ 8; art bible 8.10 budget shape and 8.11 flags 2 and 8), a debug assertion reports overflow, and every record carries a deterministic `priority` (default by kind: BEAM 255, BEACON 200, FIXTURE 120, DEPLOYABLE 100, FAULT_GLOW 80, NODE_LANTERN 60 — placeholder, owner technical-artist, confirmed by the Lighting spike) so Lighting's "emitters are cut before occlusion" is reproducible; a sim-lit but unrendered light would be a P1 lie, and the residual risk stays in Open Questions. A build lint asserts the rendered reach of every hard-edged row equals the table reach ±1 px.

**EM-27 — The light stimulus for Attention.** The primary read is the per-emitter list (`emitters()`: id, kind, `section_id`, `body_id`, origin, `output_pm`, `reach_px`, shape); the convenience read is `section_emission(section_id)` (Formulas D.12). **EM-28 — Persistence, restore and the binding table.** *Canonical* (snapshotted, hashed): the id counter; per record, the canonical fields of EM-4 (including the FAULT_GLOW records, which are the descriptors they stand for, with their `placed_tick`, the age that protects them from eviction; `arm_tick`; and a deployable's `evict_tick` and `reach_at_evict`, so an evict-wane in progress survives a save); and the subject registrations (EM-21 table, canonical columns, including each subject's `lit` hysteresis bit, EM-22). *Derived, rebuilt in `post_restore`, never hashed:* beam origin and aim, `origin_px` / `aim_units` / `reach_px` / `output_pm` / record `lit`, the static-layer caches, and the subject `lux_pm` / `act_lux_pm` / `top_emitter_id` table (the subject's `lit` bit is not rebuilt: it is kept). Rejected: `persist false` (`switch_on`, `life_remaining_ticks`, `arm_tick` and the beacon's data have no other source, so save-equivalence would break). `post_restore` runs after all blocks restore (CR-8) and rebuilds the derived set in ascending id; the block does not run its hook while RESTORING. After a restore the kernel's binding table is empty (CR-7: sessions rebuild it), so the host session owner re-enters `beam_of(player_id)` before the first command is accepted. Save-equivalence (kernel G4) holds with no accepted exception: the `lit` bit is canonical (EM-22), and the hook is ordered after the Graph's last hook on a Graph tick (EM-1). The G4 acceptance criterion fails if the requirement is unmet. `schema_version` 1; the restore migration policy is the owner's (kernel CR-8: an unsupported version is a reported failure, never a silent default). The block has a round-trip test (kernel CR-8) and a `state_hash` that covers the canonical set only.

**EM-29 — Isolation: the truth channel.** `emitter.state` is a `SIM` block. The module **never obtains a `PERCEPTION_OUT` view** (kernel CR-12; a lint mirroring PC's acceptance criterion on the Adapter's requested views), never references `Light2D`, `LightOccluder2D`, `Node` or `PhysicsServer2D` (the H10 lint over `src/sim/emitter/`), and runs under a headless test with zero scene nodes. The beam, the real reveal radius and lamp / fixture brightness are on the closed **consequence channel** (the Graph's closed consequence-channel list names "lamp brightness"; the Controller's Perception Filter row: the Filter "may never move the chest point ... or touch the beam"). **A Perception Filter decoy light is a Lighting visual**: it is not an emitter, is absent from `emitter.state`, and exposes nothing; rejected: decoys as `phantom = true` emitters (a perception fact written into a `SIM` block breaks CR-12 and H5). No sentence in this GDD says the Filter may dim, move or add a lamp. The honest cross-check — walk to the real edge and it is where it always was — depends on this. Isolation is by construction, not by policy: no field named `phantom`, `perceived` or `decoy` exists, and the query never takes a Filter-wrapped value (the Graph's `true_value` is the only wrapped path and Emitter State never calls it).

**EM-30 — Frames, zoom, mirror and the determinism contract.** All geometry is integer base px in the **body frame** (ship-local); an emitter and a subject are compared only if their `body_id` matches. A subject on another body (a detached section, a pod) is not lit by the home frame's emitters in the MVP (Open Question for Ship Body, VS-A). Zoom, camera, render scale and the integer ladder change **nothing** in the sim; an off-screen lit object stays lit and exposes, and no prompt is drawn off-screen (the HUD's rule). Light positions are integer base px at 60 Hz (`TICK_RATE`); every duration is in ticks via `ticks_for_seconds`. The determinism contract mirrors the Graph's LS-1: integer-only SIM state; **zero floats** stored, hashed, replicated or used on the query path (a designer float in a data file is converted once by `int(round(x * scale))` in the build-time data tool outside `src/sim/emitter/`, which emits the immutable integer tables — `DIR`, the EM-8 `COS2_Q28` column and the like — that the boot step loads and asserts; they are not block state, and no float or trig call exists in the sim module, AC-2); the Q14 direction table, the Q28 cone test, an exact integer floor square root and integer segment-versus-rectangle tests; no global RNG (the block draws none); iteration in ascending id; budgets in counted units; `state_hash` over the canonical set only. Physics-derived observations never enter this block, so the kernel's G3 recording obligation does not apply.

## Formulas

*Conventions. Every quantity is an integer (permille `_pm` 0..1000, base px, ticks, Q14 / Q28 fixed point). Every `⌊a / b⌋` divides non-negative operands (GDScript integer division truncates toward zero, so no formula divides a negative). Names are used verbatim — registry (locked): `TICK_RATE` 60, `TILE_PX` 48, `AIM_UNITS` 4096, `GRAPH_STEP_TICKS` 6, and the Graph's `INRUSH_STEPS` 5 and `INRUSH_PM` 1500. Foreign formula ids are qualified (PC D.12, Graph D.6, kernel D.12); a bare D.n is this section's. Every number this section introduces is a placeholder with an owner (Tuning Knobs).*

### D.1 Lamp output

The `lamp_output_pm` formula is defined as:

`output_pm = switch_on × armed × live × ⌊ base_pm × served_pm / 1000 ⌋` — the lit branch is the registry formula `power_allocation`'s output "lamp brightness = base * served_pm / 1000", reused verbatim

**Variables:**

| Variable | Type | Range | Source | Description |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `switch_on` | bool | 0/1 | `emitter.state` | the committed switch (fixture, beacon) |
| `armed` | bool | 0/1 | D.2 | `T >= arm_tick` (always 1 for a self-powered FIXTURE, `node_ref` 0) |
| `live` | bool | 0/1 | Graph `node(node_ref).state` | node exists, is not dormant or tombstoned, and its state is not `2 FAILED`, `3 DESTROYED`, `4 SALVAGED`, `5 ABSENT` (EM-13) |
| `base_pm` | int | 0..1000 | Item DB `emits.base_pm` (`mod_lamp` 1000; placeholder) | rated brightness |
| `served_pm` | int | 0..1000 | Graph `served_pm(node_ref)` | tier satisfaction `sat_k`, independent of this lamp's own request; a self-powered lamp uses 1000 |

**Output Range:** 0..1000, never above `base_pm` and never above the `served_pm`-scaled value (Graph P1: a lamp never renders brighter than the power it is served). **Example:** `base_pm` 1000, `served_pm` 1000, on, armed, live → 1000; `served_pm` 450 → ⌊1000 × 450 / 1000⌋ = 450 (the actionable radius of a 144 px fixture falls from 122 to ⌊144 × 300 / 450⌋ = 96 px, D.7); switched off with `served_pm` 1000 → 0; SALVAGED node → 0.

### D.2 Switch latency

The `switch_latency` formula is defined as:

`arm_tick = T_a + ((GRAPH_STEP_TICKS − (T_a mod GRAPH_STEP_TICKS)) mod GRAPH_STEP_TICKS)`, `L = arm_tick − T_a`; the negative case (A1 false, a Graph that consumes only strictly later requests) is `arm_tick′ = T_a + GRAPH_STEP_TICKS − (T_a mod GRAPH_STEP_TICKS)`

**Variables:**

| Variable | Type | Range | Source | Description |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `T_a` | int | ≥ 1 | kernel clock | the tick whose P2 applies `set_switch` / `set_beacon` |
| `GRAPH_STEP_TICKS` | int | 6 | registry (locked) | Graph hooks run at divisor 6, offset 0, so a Graph tick is `T mod 6 = 0` |
| `arm_tick` | int | `T_a`..`T_a + 5` | calculated | the first Graph tick whose phase 0 consumes the queued M14 request; the lamp is ARMING (`output_pm` 0) before it |
| `L` | int | 0..5 | calculated | ticks from apply to lit; switching off is `L = 0` |

**Output Range:** `L` in 0..5 ticks (0..83 ms); the negative case 1..6. The hook is `after: graph.propagate.failure` (EM-1), so the lamp is lit by the `emitter.place` of the same tick as `arm_tick`. **Example:** `T_a` 1200 (a Graph tick) → `arm_tick` 1200, `L` 0 (negative case 1206, `L` 6); `T_a` 1201 → 1206, `L` 5; `T_a` 1205 → 1206, `L` 1. Inrush (the Graph's business, visible as an honest dip): eight lamps switched together on a tier-3 island with 700 W free request 8 × ⌊20 × `INRUSH_PM` / 1000⌋ = 240 W plus a neighbouring 500 W heater, so `served_pm` = ⌊1000 × 700 / 740⌋ = 945 for `INRUSH_STEPS` 5 runs (0.5 s), then 1000.

### D.3 Node lantern output

The `node_lantern_output` formula is defined as:

`output_pm = lantern_on × ⌊ base_pm × run_pm / 1000 ⌋`

**Variables:**

| Variable | Type | Range | Source | Description |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `lantern_on` | bool | 0/1 | Item DB `lantern_on_states` (default `{0 OK, 1 DEGRADED}`) on `node(node_ref).state` | the machine's state counts as running; a dead module is dark (P1) |
| `base_pm` | int | 0..1000 | Item DB `emits.base_pm` (`mod_reactor_cell` 300, `mod_coolant_pump` 150; placeholder) | rated glow |
| `run_pm` | int | 0..1000 | Graph (ask: a `run_pm` read in `node(id)`) | how hard the machine runs: 0 when the node's requested load is 0 (machine off: LOAD `req_w` 0, PUMP `throttle_pm` 0, REACTOR `out_w` 0), else the served fraction (`served_pm` for a consumer, `⌊1000 × out_w / out_w_rated⌋` for a REACTOR); not `served_pm`, which is 1000 for a machine whose request is 0 and would glow from a switched-off machine, and not the Graph's D.9 `duty_pm`, which is `served_pm` for consumers and carries the same trap as EM-11 |

**Output Range:** 0..`base_pm`. Until the `run_pm` read exists the fallback is `run_pm` = 1000 only when `lantern_on` AND the node's requested load is nonzero (LOAD `req_w` > 0, PUMP `throttle_pm` > 0, REACTOR `out_w` > 0 — persisted LS-3 fields read from the Graph; the `node()` view lists none of them today, so the ask adds the request field with `run_pm`), else 0 (the weaker form: a switched-off machine is dark, but a browned-out or starved machine that still requests load glows at full `base_pm`; flagged to the Graph). **Example:** reactor cell at `run_pm` 1000 → 300, lit radius ⌊96 × (300 − `LIT_ON_PM`) / 300⌋ = 48 px; pump at `run_pm` 1000 → 150, which lights nothing beyond its own anchor (`r_lit` 0); pump at `run_pm` 500 → 75; a pump switched off (`throttle_pm` 0) on a met tier → 0 although its `served_pm` is 1000; FAILED → 0. The designed Item DB keys lantern defs (`mod_reactor_cell`, `mod_coolant_pump`) to `LANTERN` (its lines 169 and 397; its Open Question 21 marks the key resolved), so shape row 9 and the Item DB agree.

### D.4 Beam pose

The `beam_pose` formula is defined as:

`origin = chest_sim`, `occ_origin = pos_curr`, `axis_k = DIR[(aim_angle + off_k) mod 4096]`, `off_k ∈ {0}` for single-cone shapes and `{−SPLIT_AXIS_UNITS, +SPLIT_AXIS_UNITS}` for BEAM_SPLIT

**Variables:**

| Variable | Type | Range | Source | Description |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `chest_sim` | int2, ship-local px | x within ±2 of the foot, `y` −45..−25 | `controller.state` (= `pos_curr + CHEST_POINT[sheet][anim] ⊙ mirror`, PC D.12) | the apex, reach and distance origin; read, never recomputed |
| `pos_curr` | int2, px | ship-local | `body.state` | the foot origin: the start of every occluder segment (EM-6); `LocomotionStep` keeps it outside every footprint |

### D.5 Cone membership

The `cone_membership` formula is defined as:

`in_cone = (d2 = 0) ∨ ( d2 ≤ R² ∧ D > 0 ∧ D² ≥ d2 × COS2_Q28 )` with `r = anchor − origin`, `d2 = rx² + ry²`, `D = rx × ux + ry × uy`; a 360° shape is `d2 ≤ R²`; BEAM_SPLIT is the OR of two such tests, of which at most one is true (the dark wedge never closes)

**Variables:**

| Variable | Type | Range | Source | Description |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `r` | int2 | each component within ±400 | calculated | subject anchor minus emitter origin (EM-8: the single `anchor_px`) |
| `d2` | int | 0..147,456 | calculated | squared distance; 0 is inside every cone (the apex) |
| `R` | int px | 96..384 | `reach_px` | the hard reach; the edge is closed (`d2 = R²` is inside) |
| `(ux, uy)` | Q14 int pair | ±16384 | D.4 | the axis |
| `D` | int | within ±6,291,456 | calculated | Q14 dot product; the `D > 0` guard is required (the squared test alone accepts the opposite half-plane) |
| `COS2_Q28` | int | per shape row | EM-8 table (square of the Q14 cosine of `half_angle_units`) | 201,441,249 wide; 256,768,576 narrow; 260,305,956 split; 66,994,225 fan; 236,913,664 beacon |

**Output Range:** bool. Every product is below 2^62 (`D²` and `d2 × COS2_Q28` ≤ 3.96 × 10^13; I-OVERFLOW). The quantised edge misplaces a rim by under 0.1° (under 0.7 px at 384 px). **Example (wide cone, origin (300, 166), aim 0):** subject (540, 200) → `r` (240, 34), `d2` 58,756 ≤ 82,944, `D²` 15,461,882,265,600 ≥ 58,756 × 201,441,249 = 11,835,882,026,244 → inside; subject (540, 28) → `r` (240, −138), `d2` 76,644, product 15,439,263,088,356 ≤ `D²` → inside (29.90°); subject (540, 27) → `r` (240, −139), product 15,495,062,314,329 > `D²` → outside (30.08°; the encoded edge is 29.97°). One pixel flips the answer, which is the sim/render agreement test.

### D.6 Occluder segment test

The `occluder_segment_test` formula is defined as:

`blocked = ∃ K ∈ OccluderSource.rects_in(aabb(S, P)) : t_lo ≤ t_hi`, `t_lo = max(0, tx_lo, ty_lo)`, `t_hi = min(1, tx_hi, ty_hi)`; per axis `a` with `d_a = P_a − S_a`: if `d_a = 0` the interval is all of ℝ when `min_a ≤ S_a ≤ max_a` and empty otherwise, else `t = (min_a − S_a) / d_a` and `(max_a − S_a) / d_a` ordered by the sign of `d_a`; fractions are compared by cross-multiplication with positive denominators (no division)

**Variables:**

| Variable | Type | Range | Source | Description |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `S` | int2, px | ship-local | D.4 `occ_origin` (the foot for the beam, the anchor for other kinds) | segment start |
| `P` | int2, px | ship-local | subject `anchor_px` | segment end; at least `ANCHOR_CLEARANCE_PX` outside every rectangle (authoring lint) |
| `K` | int4, px | the closed span `[x0, x1−1] × [y0, y1−1]` of a stored half-open rectangle `[x0,x1) × [y0,y1)` (registry `rect_algebra_closed_span`); `min_a`, `max_a` are that span's bounds | Layout via `OccluderSource` | wall (`WALL_FOOTPRINT_DEPTH` 24), closed door leaf, `occludes_light` prop; actors and horrors never occlude |

**Output Range:** bool. Closed rectangles and a conservative rule: a touch (corner graze, edge-on pass, endpoint on a face) is blocked (EM-20 a). An anchor strictly inside a rectangle is blocked from every emitter. All cross-products are below 2^40 (offsets within ±2^10). **Example:** wall stored [408,432) × [96,288) (occupied span x 408..431, y 96..287; registry `rect_algebra_closed_span`); `S` (302, 200): `P` (540, 200) → `dx` 238, x-slab [106/238, 129/238], `dy` 0 with 96 ≤ 200 ≤ 287 → `t_lo` 0.445 ≤ `t_hi` 0.542 → blocked; `P` (407, 200) → x-slab [106/105, 129/105], `t_lo` 1.010 > `t_hi` 1 → not blocked; `P` (408, 200), on the face → `t_lo` = `t_hi` = 1 → blocked, which is why the clearance lint exists.

### D.7 Illumination

The `illumination` formula is defined as:

`c = ⌊ output_pm × f / 1000 ⌋`, SOFT: `f = F(d, R)`; HARD: `f = EDGE_FLOOR_PM + ⌊(1000 − EDGE_FLOOR_PM) × F(d, R) / 1000⌋`; `F(d, R) = ⌊1000 × (R − d) / R⌋` (curve id LINEAR, the MVP default); `d = max{k : SQ[k] ≤ d2}`; `lux_pm = min(1000, Σ c)`; `act_lux_pm = min(1000, Σ c over gates_actions emitters)`; SOFT actionable radius `r_lit = ⌊ R × (output_pm − LIT_ON_PM) / output_pm ⌋` for `output_pm > LIT_ON_PM`

**Variables:**

| Variable | Type | Range | Source | Description |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `output_pm` | int | 0..1000 | D.1, D.3, D.9 (constant), D.11, `BEAM_BASE_PM` | the emitter's steady output |
| `d` | int px | 0..R | `SQ` table: 385 squares, binary search, 9 probes | exact integer floor of the distance from the origin (the chest for the beam); no square root, no float |
| `R` | int px | 24..384 | `reach_px` | current reach (D.9 for a waning stick) |
| `F` | int | 0..1000 | curve id (data) | 1000 at the apex, 0 at `R`; a replacement curve (a 17-knot table, linear interpolation on `u = ⌊d × 1024 / R⌋`) keeps the signature and must stay monotone, integer, with non-negative operands (R15) |
| `EDGE_FLOOR_PM` | int | 250 (160..400) | placeholder, owner technical-artist, confirmed by the Lighting spike | the HARD-class contribution kept at the rim; jointly bound with `LIT_ON_PM` by I-EDGE |
| `LIT_ON_PM` | int | 150 (100..300) | D.8 | the turn-on threshold |

**Output Range:** `c` 0..1000, `lux_pm` and `act_lux_pm` 0..1000 (saturating; addition commutes, so there is no order dependence). There is no ambient term (`AMBIENT_PM` 0). The owner's own beam adds its full `output_pm` (the d = 0 apex value) to the owner's ACTOR `lux_pm` and nothing to its `act_lux_pm` (EM-21). **Example:** wide beam `output_pm` 1000, `d2` 43,264 → `d` 208 (208² ≤ 43,264 < 209²), `R` 288: `F` ⌊1000 × 80 / 288⌋ = 277, `f` 250 + ⌊750 × 277 / 1000⌋ = 457, `c` 457; at the rim `c` = `EDGE_FLOOR_PM` 250 (lit), and a beam at `output_pm` 600 has rim `c` 150 (lit) while 599 gives 149 (unlit), the ⌈`LIT_ON_PM` × 1000 / `EDGE_FLOOR_PM`⌉ = 600 consequence. SOFT fixture `R` 144, `output_pm` 1000: `d` 122 → `F` 152 (lit), `d` 123 → 145 (unlit), `r_lit` ⌊144 × 850 / 1000⌋ = 122. Sum: beam 457 + a lamp of `output_pm` 848 at `d` 100 (`F` 305, `c` ⌊848 × 305 / 1000⌋ = 258) → `lux_pm` 715; adding a fixture at `d` 60 (`c` 583) gives 1298 → 1000. Alone with the lamp on, the owner reads `lux_pm` 1000.

### D.15 Boot invariants

The `boot_invariants` formula is defined as:

`I-EDGE ∧ I-HYST ∧ I-WEDGE ∧ I-OVERFLOW ∧ I-MIRROR ∧ I-CAPS ∧ I-WANE` — every one is checked at data load; a violated invariant refuses the build or the boot (no run-time fallback), and each has an acceptance criterion

**Variables:**

## Edge Cases

**Switching, power and lamps**

- **If `set_switch` applies on a Graph tick (`T mod 6 = 0`)**: the lamp is lit by that tick's `emitter.place`; on any other tick it is lit at the next multiple of 6, at most 5 ticks later (D.2). One latency story, no hidden term.
- **If a switched-on lamp's `served_pm` is 0 (brownout to zero, SCRAM, shed)**: the lamp is STARVED, `output_pm` 0, `switch_on` unchanged and its request still queued; it lights again with no new command when power returns.
- **If a lamp's `output_pm` is at or below `LIT_ON_PM` (a deep brownout, or the 150 permille pump lantern)**: it lights nothing but its own anchor, still adds to sums, and Lighting draws it dim; an honest dim, never a prompt.
- **If a lamp's node is SALVAGED, dormant or tombstoned**: M14 is a no-op, `live` is false, `output_pm` is 0, `set_switch` is rejected `NOT_LIVE`, and the record stays until its section is discarded.
- **If a lamp's node goes FAILED or DESTROYED**: `output_pm` is 0 on that Graph tick (the hook is after the failure phase); `switch_on` is retained, so a repair re-lights the lamp without a command.
- **If eight lamps are switched on in one tick on a marginal island**: all read the same inrush dip of `served_pm` for `INRUSH_STEPS` runs (945 in the D.2 example); ascending-id application makes the result order-free.
- **If a lamp is switched off while ARMING**: `switch_on` is false at once, M14 `(node_ref, 0)` is queued and the lamp is never lit; the pending `arm_tick` is discarded.
- **If `set_switch` targets a non-FIXTURE (a lantern, the beam)**: rejected `NOT_SWITCHABLE`; no state change and no M14 call.
- **If a FIXTURE has `node_ref` 0 and is not the declared cryo-bay emergency lamp**: the authoring lint and boot refuse it; the declared lamp is armed always, fixed `base_pm`, no M14 (provisional — Derelict Authoring undesigned).
- **If a lantern's machine is switched off (requested load 0), starved (breaker open) or the Graph's `run_pm` read is not yet provided**: with the read, output is 0 for a switched-off or starved machine; without it the fallback is 0 for a switched-off machine and full `base_pm` for an OK machine that still requests load while starved, a flagged weaker form (D.3).

**Beam and pose**

- **If `toggle_beam` applies**: `switch_on` flips and the beam is lit on the apply tick; a turn-ON at zero charge is rejected `CHARGE_EMPTY` and the light does not change (the pre-played click is the Presenter's).
- **If charge reaches zero mid-channel**: Suit's `force_off` lands before the same tick's `emitter.place`; the owner's 6-tick re-check then finds the target unlit and aborts, so at most five ticks of channel run on a dark target.
- **If the actor is DOWNED**: the beam keeps its committed state, aim frozen at the last committed value, origin from the down row's `chest_sim`; a downed crewmate is a locator. **If DEAD**: forced off at that tick, unobservable in the MVP because the death stub restarts from the last autosave at that tick's P6.
- **If the actor is STAGGERED**: the beam is unchanged and `toggle_beam` is rejected `NOT_ACTIONABLE` (a defensive double of the Controller's input-ignore).
- **If the player runs with aim different from facing**: the beam follows the committed aim; the split axes are ±206 units off aim, not facing.
- **If an interactable lies between the chest and the feet in screen-y while the player faces up-screen**: it is behind the apex, unlit and unprompted; the sim adds no hidden compensation (EM-6), and `BEAM_SPILL_RADIUS_PX` 0 is the reserve if playtests reject it.
- **If the feet stand 10–34 px south of a north wall**: the chest point is inside the footprint, the apex stays at the chest, and the occluder segment starts at the foot, so a north-aimed beam is blocked at once and a south-aimed beam is unaffected.
- **If the avatar is mirrored (`flip_h`)**: `chest_sim.x` moves by up to 4 px and the cones are recomputed from the new apex in the same tick; a mirrored scene yields the exact mirror image of every `lux_pm` and `lit` (I-MIRROR).
- **If any animation row changes the chest point (run lean, carry, work, stagger, down)**: the apex moves by the data delta in the same tick with no smoothing.
- **If the mouse flicks the aim**: the committed aim lags the drawn aim by up to 2.8° and 3 ticks; a subject in that sliver may disagree with the drawn edge for up to 3 ticks, tolerated by the 6-tick re-check.
- **If a subject lies in the split beam's dark wedge**: it is in neither cone and contributes 0 at every aim (the wedge is at least 16° and never closes); dead ahead of a split beam is dark, by design.
- **If a subject is exactly on the hard edge (`d2 = R²`) or exactly on a cone boundary ray**: it is inside (the edge is closed and the cone test is `≥`); one pixel farther it is outside. **If a subject is at the apex (`d2 = 0`)**: it is inside every cone of that emitter.
- **If the owner is alone with the lamp on**: the owner's ACTOR `lux_pm` is the beam's full `output_pm` (1000), facing- and occlusion-independent, so Horror sees a lit player and Attention counts the beam once from the emitter list; the owner's `act_lux_pm` gains nothing and Attention must not add the beam again.
- **If `bind_beam` changes the shape**: it takes effect at the next `emitter.place`; there is no input for it, and a shape change is a loadout event.

**Occlusion, queries and caps**

- **If a segment only touches a rectangle (corner graze)**: it is blocked; an anchor within `ANCHOR_CLEARANCE_PX` of a footprint fails the registration lint, and an anchor found strictly inside a footprint at run time is blocked from every emitter and reads dark.
- **If a door closes mid-channel**: the next `emitter.place` observes the `OccluderSource.version(section_id)` change, rebuilds the static layer for the section, the target reads dark and the owner's re-check aborts the channel within 6 ticks.
- **If a door is partly open**: the door-leaf rectangle is the Layout's `door_leaf_gap` projection (`leaf_w` continuous in the Graph's opening area; `leaf_w` 0 yields no rectangle), so light passes exactly the gap the leaf leaves; `DOOR_LIGHT_OPEN_PM` is deprecated and unused.
- **If a horror or an actor stands between an emitter and a subject**: nothing is occluded and the subject beyond stays lit; a shadow with no occluder is a stage-2 symptom and must stay an unambiguous tell.
- **If a carried item is dropped**: it re-registers as a static ITEM subject at the drop anchor and is evaluated at the next `emitter.place` (it was unregistered at pickup, EM-21).
- **If the subject is an unregistered ref (including a Perception Filter phantom target)**: `lux` 0 and `lit` false, indistinguishable from a registered unlit subject in release, so the query is no existence oracle; `registered(ref)` exists in debug builds only.
- **If registrations exceed `MAX_EXPOSURE_SUBJECTS`, or moving registrations exceed `MAX_MOVING_SUBJECTS`**: `register_subject` returns false, `emitter_subjects_overflow` (or `emitter_moving_overflow`) increments in release builds as telemetry, the subject reads dark (fails safe) and a debug assertion fires. **This is a content or budget bug and never a gameplay state**: a real object inside a beam that refuses to prompt would break the honest edge (design test 1) and read as a hallucination (the P4 "reach for it" falsifier), so the per-category budgets (Derelict Authoring: authored objects, items, dropped loot and spawned salvage; Horror Entities: horror subjects; Interaction: dropped items) are held by I-CAPS (d), a breach seen in telemetry is fixed in the owning budget, and no design may rely on the refusal. **If `probe()` is called beyond `probes_allowed` (D.14)**: the call returns `UNKNOWN`, never a stale `true`, and `emitter_probe_overflow` increments.
- **If a subject's `body_id` differs from an emitter's (a detached section)**: that emitter contributes 0 (EM-30); cross-body light is a Ship Body question for VS-A.
- **If in-view emitters exceed Lighting's budget (24 non-shadow + 6 shadow, 8 lanterns)**: the sim never culls by view, a cut light still exposes (P3), a debug assertion reports it and `priority` makes Lighting's cut reproducible; the authoring lint should have refused the content.

**Deployables, beacon and fault glow**

- **If a glowstick lands on a dormant or unknown section**: `spawn_deployable` is a no-op returning false, Interaction has already spent the item and no light ever existed. **If it lands inside a closed room**: it lights that room only (the walls occlude).
- **If two glowsticks overlap**: contributions saturate at 1000 and each wanes on its own clock. **If a spawn exceeds `MAX_DEPLOYABLES_LIVE`**: the new stick spawns in full (Class B never rejects) and the lowest-id non-evicting deployable of the same kind enters a visible evict-wane (D.9b): its reach falls from where it is to 24 px over `GLOWSTICK_EVICT_WANE_S` (10 s) and only then is it removed, so no light dies with no physical cause; `emitter_deployable_evictions` increments. **If an eviction would exceed `MAX_DEPLOYABLES_EVICTING`**: the oldest evicting stick completes at once, `emitter_deployable_evict_overflow` increments and a debug assertion fires (a budget defect, I-SUPPLY). **If a section is discarded with live deployables**: they are removed with it.
- **If `life_remaining_ticks` reaches 0**: the record is removed that tick and its id retired, the subjects it lit re-read next tick, and there is no fade (the waning already took the reach to 24 px).
- **If `set_beacon` turns the beacon on**: it starts from the angle the pure function gives at that tick, not phase 0; off, it contributes 0 from the next tick. **If its bus is dead**: `output_pm` is 0 (D.1) while the aim stays a pure function of `T`.
- **If the beacon sweeps a subject in an otherwise dark room**: `lux_pm` is above 0 for 14 or 15 of every 128 ticks (by phase; the same count in two runs and across a restore) and `lit` stays false, so no prompt strobes and no channel aborts at the sweep rate.
- **If more than `MAX_FAULT_GLOW` descriptors have glow rows**: the unprotected records with the lowest `sev` (ties: the higher descriptor id first) are evicted and `emitter_fault_glow_evictions` increments; a record younger than `ticks_for_seconds(W_MIN_WARNING_S)` (600 ticks) is never evicted, and if every candidate is protected nothing is evicted, the table stays over the cap, `emitter_fault_glow_overcap` increments and a debug assertion fires, with the table trimmed on the first Graph tick that has an unprotected candidate. The MVP slice does not reach the cap: a welded body carries at most 6 simultaneous glow rows of the 8 live faults (registry `mvp_fault_allocation`), and 10 ≥ ⌈1.25 × 6⌉ = 8 (I-CAPS (c)). An evicted glow removes only the light and exposure; the Failure & Trail overlay on the sprite still shows the fault. **If a descriptor's `sev` falls to 0 or its `intensity_pm` ends**: the record is removed on that Graph tick.
- **If a long-aged fault (a SCRAM held for minutes) keeps its descriptor while Failure & Trail's overlay decays** (Derelict Authoring OQ-V3): the FAULT_GLOW `output_pm` follows the descriptor's `sev` alone (D.11) and never the overlay's age; overlay decay is Failure & Trail's presentation and changes neither `output_pm` nor exposure, so a glow that should fade with age must be expressed by F&T as a falling `sev` or `intensity_pm`.
- **If a `mod_lamp` sits on the lowest `power_tier`** (Derelict Authoring OQ-V4): its output is `base_pm × served_pm / 1000` (D.1); the lowest tier is the first shed under a shortfall, so it reads dimmer only while its tier is under-served (`served_pm` < 1000) and identical to any other lamp at full supply; whether a given `served_pm` reads *visibly* dimmer is the Lighting spike's to confirm.
- **If a sprite carries an emissive `_msk` trail glow**: it is an unshaded overlay, not an emitter, and exposes nothing.

**Save, restore, boot and co-op**

- **If a game is restored with a subject between `LIT_OFF_PM` and `LIT_ON_PM`**: its `lit` bit is canonical and kept, so `validate` at T+1 reads the same answer as in the uninterrupted run (kernel G4).
- **If a save is taken at a Graph tick and restored**: the Graph's `post_restore` rebuilds `served_pm` and the descriptor list, so the subject table and every lamp `output_pm` match uninterrupted play at the next five ticks; if the Graph does not, lamps read dark for up to 5 ticks and the G4 criterion fails (the requirement is unmet, not accepted).
- **If the kernel binding table is empty after a restore**: the session owner re-enters `beam_of(player_id)` before the first command is accepted; a `toggle_beam` before that is refused by the kernel's binding check.
- **If a registrant does not re-register in its own `post_restore`**: nothing is lost (registrations are canonical); a repeat registration is a no-op returning false.
- **If a hook id is unknown or creates a cycle at boot** (for example someone registers `after: [emitter.update]`): boot fails loudly (CR-3); only `emitter.place` exists. **If the kernel is FAULTED during a mutator apply**: standard CR-9 handling; every mutator here is Class B or validated, so a fault is a defect.
- **If the kernel is `MIRROR`**: it never ticks `emitter.place`; the replica reads `SHARED_SUMMARY`, which in VS-B must carry each beam's origin, aim, shape and switch so a remote crewmate's beam can be drawn.
- **If zoom changes**: nothing in the sim changes; an off-screen lit object stays lit and exposes, and the HUD draws no off-screen prompt.

**Hallucination and degenerate strategies**

- **If the Perception Filter shows a lamp as broken or a dead lamp as working, or adds a decoy light**: `emitter.state` still holds the real `output_pm`, so the room is as lit as it was and the prompt follows the real light; the false lamp is a Lighting visual that exposes nothing, and walking to the real edge is the falsifier.
- **If the player strobes the beam**: every toggle is committed state and each change is visible to Attention; the sim adds no cooldown (the WCAG debounce is routed to the Controller's adapter).
- **If the player goes dark (beam off, no fixtures)**: `lux_pm` is 0 everywhere, nothing is lit and no lit-only verb is possible; darkness is never free (only `drop`, `toggle_beam`, `throw_glowstick` and the inspect card of an already-lit instrument remain, registry `act_gate`).
- **If the player lays glowsticks everywhere or abandons a lit fixture**: each still adds to `section_emission` and Attention's cost, the live cap is 8 and each wanes; light is a spend, never a safe state.
- **If Horror spams probes**: only `probes_allowed` (4 at the design point) are served per tick and the rest are `UNKNOWN`, so pathing probes cannot starve the mandatory work.

## Visual/Audio Requirements

*This block draws and plays nothing. It publishes facts (the emitter read view, the shape table, dirty keys) that the renderer and the audio mixer conform to; every item below is owned by the GDD named in its row and is listed so that the owner's brief is complete. **What is visible.** Light is the only thing this system shows. A beam is a wedge with a **hard outer edge exactly at its reach** (3 screen px of edge, then a soft interior falloff) in the Tungsten hue every human light uses (art bible 4.2); the four shapes, the fan, the deployable pool, the glowstick, the fixture and the beacon are the art bible 8.3 rows (lines 1079–1086) carried verbatim into the shape table, with two rows this GDD adds (lantern, fault glow) and the HARD / SOFT curve classes owed back to 8.3 (Cross-References). ## Game Feel

*A real note, not a template fill: this system has no animation, no input of its own and no hit; its feel is the **honesty and timing of light**. The feel of the toggle's input belongs to the Player Controller (PC-5 and its debounce), the feel of how light looks to 2D Lighting & Occlusion, the feel of every sound to Audio; this GDD owns the numbers underneath them, which are below.*

**Feel reference.** The chest lamp should feel like Pandorum's helmet light and the Alien: Isolation torch in one respect only: it is *yours*, hands-free, always where you are facing, and it is the one thing in the game that never lies. The ship's lamps should feel like a machine that has to be served: they come up with the ship, not ahead of it.

**Input responsiveness (beam).** The `toggle_beam` press applies at P2 and the light is committed in the same tick's `emitter.place`: zero internal latency, at most one tick (16.7 ms) from apply to the lit state the renderer sees, inside the Controller's ≤ 17 ms click-to-commit budget (PC-5). The click and the switch animation pre-play; the **light never does** (the Presenter never predicts beam state: the lamp is the human-channel anchor and may not lie for one frame). Turn-off is immediate. Feel target: decisive, mechanical, slightly heavy; never a lag the player reads as the game being slow, never a flash the player reads as the game being wrong.

**Ship lamps warm; the chest lamp snaps.** A fixture switched on is ARMING until the Graph serves it: 0 to `GRAPH_STEP_TICKS` − 1 ticks (0–83 ms), below perception on its own, and Audio's relay clunk and hum rise fill it so it reads as a ship lamp, not as lag. Under a weak bus a bank of lamps switched together draws `INRUSH_PM` 1500 for `INRUSH_STEPS` 5 Graph runs (0.5 s): the neighbouring lamps dip (the Graph example is 945 permille) and recover. That dip is an honest, audible, **player-caused** flicker and the best single lesson the ship teaches about load; it must never be smoothed away.

**The rim.** At full output the edge of a HARD emitter is a step (about 252 permille inside, 0 outside; `EDGE_FLOOR_PM` 250 with `LIT_ON_PM` 150), so an object pops into reach at an exact pixel. The 150 / 120 hysteresis never sees that step; it bites only on **dimmed** emitters (brownout, waning glowsticks) whose interior crosses the threshold band, so a prompt does not strobe in a fade. While the aim is sweeping, committed-versus-drawn divergence of up to 2.8° and ≤ 3 ticks (PC-5) moves the rim by about 14 px at 288 px (WIDE) and 19 px at 384 px (NARROW); a rim object may prompt a few ticks late and rim chatter is held by the Controller's `FOCUS_STICKY_MARGIN`, not by this block. The edge-agreement test (design test 1) holds **at rest and for full-output emitters**; a dimmed light's actionable area shrinks honestly (a fixture at 500 permille loses prompts for objects within 22 px of its full-output readable edge (`r_lit` falls from 122 to 100 px): "the junction box at the edge went out of reach when the scrubber drew").

**Glowstick wane and beacon.** A glowstick is steady for its first 60 % of life, then shrinks over the rest in visible integer steps; the shrinking pool is the only clock and no number is ever shown. A stick put out by the live cap is never snuffed: it takes the same visible shrink, faster (10 s, D.9b), so the pool still only ever dies by shrinking. The beacon lights a point for 14 or 15 of every 128 ticks, so it is *seen* and never gates a prompt (EM-16): nothing strobes at the sweep rate.

**The split beam's dark wedge is its identity.** Two cones at ±18° off the aim with a wedge of at least 16° that never closes: the one shape that cannot see straight ahead. It is a loadout personality, not a bug; nobody should "fix" it.

**Impact moments.** (1) A brownout takes the corridor lamps down and, when power returns, the corridor beyond is exactly where it was (the world never lied). (2) A glowstick thrown into the next room: the pool blooms Chemlight green on landing, not in flight. (3) A lamp bank clicking on and the whole deck sagging for half a second. (4) The tell: filaments resolving at the cone edge, which needs this stable edge to resolve at.

**Feel acceptance.** AC-FEEL (manual, retained screenshots, technical-artist and game-designer sign-off) covers the toggle, the inrush dip, the rim at rest at three zoom rungs, and the glowstick wane. Weight and responsiveness profile: instant for the beam and turn-off, 0–5 ticks for a fixture, 0.5 s for a bank dip, 150 s for a glowstick.
