## Summary

The Item & Module Database is the game's read-only catalogue of the 24 physical things that can be cut free, carried, installed, held, spent or thrown: thirteen ship modules, four hand tools, a glowstick, five repair parts and a data core, each with a true mass, the pipes and wires it fits, and the one part that fully mends it. It exists because salvage is the game's only source of anything: nothing is bought or crafted, a salvaged module keeps the damage and the patch cap it died with, and a patch can only hold a module at 60 percent until a finite part cut from another wreck makes the repair last. Read it if you design anything that places, carries, installs, repairs or saves an item, or if you need a stable id or a weight for one.

> **Quick reference** — Layer: `Feature` · Priority: `MVP` · Key deps: `Life-Support Graph (designed; hard: node kinds, port-type ids, M5/M6/M7)`, `Command & Simulation Layer (designed; soft: BOOTING order, no block)`, `Player Controller & Input (designed; soft: PC-8, V_CARRY)`, `Pixel-Perfect Pipeline and art bible (designed; soft: TILE_PX, sprite cells)`; downstream and provisional: Suit & Equipment, Ship Section & Layout, Modular Ship Assembly, Derelict Authoring, Boarding & Salvage (undesigned/in flight), Interaction (undesigned); designed and soft: Emitter State and Save/Load (designed; soft)

## Overview

The Item & Module Database is the game's **static, read-only catalogue of every physical thing that can be cut free, carried, installed, held, spent or thrown** — twenty-four definitions ("defs") in the MVP across six classes (module, tool, consumable, repair part, curio, and a reserved junk class) — and each def fixes only what its thing *is* (a true mass, the ports it exposes, its ratings, the part that mends it) and what it *fits* (a Graph node kind and port types, a carry class). Nothing on a def says what the thing is *worth*: there is no price, rarity, recipe or loot weight anywhere in the catalogue, because salvage is the only faucet in the game and a thing's value is whatever the ship will not do without it. The catalogue is loaded once in BOOTING as an immutable `ItemCatalog`; it registers **no kernel state block, no step hook, no command and no mutator** (the kernel's Interactions table has no Item DB row), so it can be called from `validate`, from P3 and from presentation as a set of pure queries. Two keys identify a def: the string `def_id` — the **only** item key that ever reaches a save, a snapshot, a `SalvageRecord` or a hash — and an append-only numeric `type_id` that exists for in-memory tables and later replication summaries and never reaches persisted data, so renumbering can never break a save. The Graph owns the loop ids, node kinds and port-type ids; the Item DB consumes them by number and never defines one. The Item DB also defines the single `ItemInstance` value schema that every holder (the Suit's per-player block, a loose-world placement, a Graph node about to be salvaged) embeds in its own block, because Suit, Assembly, Boarding and Save/Load must serialise the same shape; it stores, allocates and moves no instance itself.

The player never meets "the database"; they meet its consequences as things with a body. A Foundry scrubber cut from derelict A's dead bus arrives at the home-ship carrying the condition it died with — still dark, still FAILED — because a def never refreshes what the Graph recorded. It is hauled at the locked flat carry speed with both hands full and the chest lamp still lit, installed into a hardpoint it physically fits, and patched, which the Graph caps at `PATCH_CAP_BP` 6000; only a **repair part**, itself cut from another wreck and spent once, restores the full ceiling and ends the patch's decay clock; cutting a patched module out and putting it back lifts nothing. A glowstick is one finite pool of Chemlight (`#86B896`) that counts as exposure like any other light. A carried module's mass is reported to the player by their own body (breath, strain — the Suit's consequence-channel cue), never by a number, a bar or a pack screen. And because every def carries a true mass and never a believed one, a phantom crate that looks like a data core can be caught by how it weighs (hallucination sub-class d, vertical slice) — the catalogue is the thing being lied about, never the liar. The two anchor numbers the rest of the game reads from it are small and exact: an item's `def_id` (what it is) and its `mass_g` (how much of it there is).

Without this system, salvage and welding have nothing to act on, and each neighbour (Assembly, Interaction, Suit, Derelict Authoring, Boarding, Layout) would invent its own item vocabulary until the save format broke on every module change — the concept's named technical risk ("save format not versioned from v1"). With it, the home-ship stays "a body assembled from the corpses of other ships" (concept, Core Fantasy) in the strictest sense: every object in it has a provenance, a dialect, a condition and a true weight, and none was bought, crafted or minted. The index flags this system as a **quiet bottleneck** — nine dependents (five in the MVP: Suit & Equipment, Ship Section & Layout Data Model, Modular Ship Assembly, Derelict Authoring, Boarding & Salvage) — so the schema is frozen early, versioned from v1 (`catalogue_version`), and kept data-contract shaped even though `modes.workflow: full` requires the whole template.

**Index wording, reconciled.** The index rationale reads: "Definitions of salvageable modules (scrubber, reactor cell, hull plate, coolant unit, data core), tools, consumables and their dialect and weights; references graph port-type IDs that the Life-Support Graph GDD defines." Four of the five named modules do not map one-to-one onto a Graph node kind, and this GDD resolves them (IDB-10): the **scrubber** is `mod_scrubber` (node kind 20); the **reactor cell** is `mod_reactor_cell` (kind 10) and is *not* the battery cell, which is its own def `mod_battery_cell` (kind 11); the **coolant unit** is a roster family name over three defs (`mod_coolant_pump`, `mod_radiator`, `mod_coolant_reservoir`) because one def is exactly one Graph node; the **hull plate** is a *repair part* (`part_hull_plate`), not a module, because a HULL_SEGMENT has no `def_id` and `salvage_node` refuses it — a conflict with the index wording that is routed to the Graph owner (Open Questions); the **data core** is a *curio* with no Graph presence (`curio_data_core`, node kind 0), because the Graph has no data-core node kind and the concept forbids an authored story. "Dialect" is a sprite-and-port-shape field with no gameplay stat in the MVP; "weights" are true integer grams that change neither speed nor noise.

## Player Fantasy

**Framing: an Economy system has two fantasies, and this one leads with the direct one.** *(creative-director consulted; three framings weighed — "Organs From the Dead" leads, "The Weight Is the Truth" is the hallucination beat, "Every Part Reads Like Its Pipe" is the presentation beat.)* The direct fantasy is the one the player feels while handling a thing: **salvage-surgeon**. The indirect fantasy is what the catalogue makes possible inside other systems' moments: the home-ship becomes legible history.

> **Everything we own, we cut out of a dead ship and carried home — and it still remembers what happened to it.**
>
> The concept's Core Fantasy says the home-ship is "a body they assembled from the corpses of other ships" [CON:46-48]. The Item DB is the anatomy of that sentence: a scrubber is not loot, it is a lung taken from a corpse, and it arrives with the corpse's injuries. Nothing in this world is minted, bought or crafted; every object has a provenance (the wreck it came from, its dialect) and a condition (the `hp` the Graph recorded, carried across the cut). The player's power is not owning things. It is **knowing what a thing really is** — what it fits, what it weighs, what state it is truly in — when the world, the scanner and their own eyes are under pressure to lie.

*Tone guard on the metaphor.* The body-and-organ language belongs to the design text and the player's own head only. It must never become visual or textual flesh: flesh tones and violet "appear nowhere in the sane world" [CON:333-334], no bare skin is ever shown [ART:508], and materials are cold metals only [ART:635-636]. Display names stay technician-plain (guard rail AP-6 below).

**Anchor moment — "the scrubber from derelict A".** It uses only locked MVP fixtures and roster ids; placeholder numbers are tagged.

1. **Want.** In derelict A the beam finds a Foundry scrubber, FAILED, authored at `hp` 2000 [LSG:228]. It sits dark — no rotor turning, no medium moving. The home-ship's own scrubber is wearing.
2. **Cost before the cut.** Cutting it free (V8; the cutter lists the verb, Interaction owns the command) leaves its fluid ports `open_end` until they are capped [LSG:772]. The player hears the hiss they caused.
3. **Carry.** `mod_scrubber` is the one carried module (illustrative; in the MVP slice the `scr_a` carry has no destination and the reference carry is the heater `htr_a`, Boarding & Salvage C-BS-3) (`carry_ref` [PCI:131]): gait forced to `V_CARRY` 1 px/tick, **no run**, every `interact` mode except `drop` rejected `BUSY`; the chest lamp still lights the way [ART:512]. A 30-tile corridor (1,440 base px at `TILE_PX` 48) takes 1,440 ticks = 24 s carrying against 720 ticks = 12 s walking at `V_WALK` 2, and emits 1,440 `NOISE_PER_TICK` units against 720 (carry 1 per tick, walk 1 per tick, over twice the ticks [REG:5442-5457]). The module's mass (`mass_g` 40000, [PH]) is felt only as the Suit's body cue, never a number. This is the stretch where tells are heard (P2) and Attention is earned (P3).
4. **Install, then disappointment.** At home it is installed with the `hp` of the record, 2000 [LSG:184, 185]: still FAILED, still dark. The catalogue did not refresh it. The world never lies.
5. **Patch, hope on a clock.** A patch lifts `hp` toward, never past, `PATCH_CAP_BP` 6000. At 6000 the Graph's `eff` (cited by name, D.4) is 750 permille and the scrubber runs DEGRADED at an output of 300 mGU/step — the figure the Graph's own tuning note describes as "equals one crew — any degradation loses the room" [LSG:927]. It runs, but with zero margin and a decay clock (`PATCH_DECAY_S` 240) ticking behind it. A bolted patch plate on its casing says "we did this in a hurry" [ART:617].
6. **The part, earned.** Only a `part_machine_kit` — a finite thing cut from another wreck — lets a full repair restore the ceiling to `HP_FULL` and end the decay clock [LSG:222]. The part, not the timer, is what makes the next sortie necessary. **Scarcity pushed inward.**

The emotional arc is want, exposure (the cut's hiss), dread (slow, loud, hands full), relief (home), disappointment (still dead), hope with a deadline (patch), earned competence (the part). It is the Project Zomboid "the base becomes self-sufficient" feeling [CON:52-55] with "survivable never quite certain" built into the item itself.

**Pillar alignment.** *Pillar 5 (primary), verbatim:* "The outer ring is kept unsafe by depletion and wear (scrubbers degrade, salvage runs out), so there is no safe place to farm forever." The catalogue is the vocabulary of the only faucet: a part is finite, a patch is capped, and the faucet is bounded by the authored node count. The other half of the pillar, "Deeper Is *Richer*", is delivered by placement and never by stat: no def carries a worth, dialect is a skin and `grade` has no behaviour, so richness deeper in the web is expressed by Derelict Authoring and Depth Rings & Scarcity through placement density, part supply (`part_cover_pm` by ring, F-11) and authored `hp` (better-condition modules deeper), never through def stats (binding, IDB-29 rule 5). *Pillar 1 (primary), verbatim:* "Pipes leak, frost forms, air hisses; the physical world is ground truth. Only instruments and perception can be wrong." Every def holds only true values; a module's condition is its output in the room and the scuffs and patch plates on its body, not a field on a card. *Pillar 4(d), verbatim:* "A crate of treasure is garbage once it is home. At least one cheaper field check exists (carried weight, a second scanner, the palette leak); the trip home is the fallback, not the only reveal." The Item DB supplies the true mass that check reads. *Pillar 3* is served by the carry gait and the glowstick pool; *Pillar 2* only indirectly, because a part or module is what surviving a tell costs and the Item DB fires nothing.

**MDA alignment** [CON:80-89]. The Item DB's felt contribution is *Challenge* (the repair economy: the part as the scarce input), *Fellowship* (the concept's rank-3 aesthetic, "crew roles (boarder vs. ship-keeper) ... a shared home-ship" [CON:84]: the haul is a division of labour. Each player has one `carry_ref` (IDB-2), so the hauler is slow, loud and hands-full while a crewmate lights the way with glowsticks and guards the corridor; parts are spent per player (IDB-20 (7)), so who mends what is a crew decision; and F-14's two-crew margin makes the second scrubber a shared crew goal), *Fantasy* ("a ship that is ours because we welded it") and *Discovery* (a module's real condition is learned by installing it and watching it, not by reading it). It must deliver *Competence* (reading what a salvaged thing is really worth from the world) and *Autonomy* ("which module", and "weigh a module's value against the noise of taking it" [CON:98]). It must never deliver Submission, which the concept deliberately omits: there is no comfortable stockpile.

**Design test (Item DB-specific, falsifiable).** *A module whose condition and worth are numbers on a card vs. a module whose condition is its own output in the room and the scuffs and patch plates on its body, and whose only cure is a finite part cut from another wreck → the module with the body and the part.* It resolves two live decisions. Should a def carry a `quality`, `rarity` or `value` field shown on pickup? No: `grade` is a reserved integer that is 0 everywhere in the MVP and has no behaviour. Should a free, infinitely repeatable action restore a module to full? No: a patch caps at `PATCH_CAP_BP`, a full repair takes a part, and cutting a patched module out and putting it back does not lift the cap or stop the decay clock either (IDB-18). *Secondary test (mirrors Pillar 4's):* **a weight that slows you vs. a weight your body reports truthfully → the truthful report.** Speed and noise never scale with mass.

**Under hallucination the fantasy deepens rather than breaks.** The catalogue is the thing being lied about, never the liar. "We know what our things really are" becomes harder and more valuable as sanity falls; it does not collapse, because at least one channel of truth always remains [CON:261-265]. The split is the Graph's: the **consequence channel** is a closed list (compartment temperature, O2 and pressure as the body feels them; frost, mist, scorch and drip trails; the acoustic classes at a descriptor anchor; Suit body cues — breath, visor fog, rime, shiver and the rest of Suit's SE-27 set (no vignette, SE-27); lamp brightness from `served_pm`; draught) and is never corruptible; the **module-body channel** (sprites, gauges, inspect cards, a module's own wear state) may lie [LSG:44, LSG:864].

Three rules follow, restating decisions rather than adding any. No def field is a believed, displayed or perceived value (`appears_as`, `displayed_as`, `fake_mass`, `lure_sprite` and any `hallucination_*` key are forbidden); a `junk` def holds only its own true data. There is no "unidentified item" mechanic: a def's identity is never hidden behind an identify step, and the player who learned what `part_pipe_fitting` looks like keeps that knowledge across a reload or a death, because Pillar 5's failure contract costs ship and body, never knowledge. Identification uncertainty comes only from Pillar 4 perception, which is falsifiable. **Risk carried to Suit & Equipment:** the carried-mass body cue must ride on a cue already in the closed consequence-channel list (breath, visor fog, rime, shiver and the rest of Suit's SE-27 set; no vignette, SE-27); if Suit proposes a new cue type (a shoulder-strain frame), its owner must either add it to the closed list or accept that it is module-body, corruptible, and so cannot serve as the sub-class d field check. Suit must say which.

## Detailed Design

### Core Rules

*Every rule below is a data-contract rule of the Item DB module (`src/data/items/` in the code root — location is an ADR candidate), stated so an implementer can write it and a tester can falsify it. Rules are `IDB-n`, numbered and named, as the Graph's LS-n and the kernel's CR-n are. Graph- and registry-sourced numbers are written by their locked name with a `[LSG:n]`/`[REG:n]` tag and appear once; every number this GDD introduces is tagged **[PH]** and is a registry candidate (Section G). Where a rule records an alternative that was considered and rejected, the rejection is part of the rule: a later change must answer it. Examples use only roster `def_id`s (IDB-31) or are marked "illustrative, not in the catalogue", because an example in a GDD is read as a contract.*

**IDB-1 — The Item DB is a static, immutable catalogue.** It defines what a thing *is* (mass, ports, ratings, repair part, yield) and what it *fits* (port types, node kind, carry class); nothing on a def says what it is *worth*. It registers **no kernel state block, no step hook, no command and no mutator** — consistent with the kernel's Interactions table (KRN:190-206) having no Item DB row and with CR-6 freezing the block registry at the end of BOOTING [KRN:98]. It is loaded once in BOOTING into an `ItemCatalog`, scene-tree free (kernel H10), read-only afterwards, and callable from `validate`, from P3 and from presentation, because every query in IDB-28 is pure. It **rejects nothing**: every rejection reason that concerns an item (`BUSY`, `OUT_OF_CHARGES`, `PORT_INCOMPAT`, `NOT_REPAIRABLE`) belongs to Interaction, Suit or the Graph. *Alternative considered:* one WORLD block `items.state` owning every instance with its own id counter — rejected: it duplicates Suit's carried-module state (the index gives Suit "carried modules" [IDX:27]), adds a write path every neighbour must call, and contradicts the producer's advice to keep this system thin and data-shaped [IDX:143]. *Alternative considered:* a hybrid catalogue plus a small `items.loose` block — rejected: it invents ownership the neighbours may not want; the loose-world holder is an Open Question, not a decision made here (IDB-16).

**IDB-2 — Six def classes, one prefix and one numeric range each.** Every def belongs to exactly one class, named by its `def_id` prefix and bounded by its `type_id` range; the class fixes the default carry class.

| Class | `def_id` prefix | `type_id` range | Default `carry_class` | MVP count | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Module | `mod_` | 1000-1999 | `CARRIED` | 13 | One Graph node (IDB-6); installed, salvaged, patched, repaired |
| Tool | `tool_` | 2000-2999 | `HAND` | 4 | A hand-rail tool; never consumed (IDB-11) |
| Consumable | `cons_` | 3000-3999 | `STOWED` | 1 | A counted item spent on use (IDB-12) |
| Part | `part_` | 4000-4999 | `STOWED` | 5 | Consumed by a full repair (IDB-13) |
| Curio | `curio_` | 5000-5999 | `CARRIED` | 1 | Mass with no Graph presence (IDB-14) |
| Junk | `junk_` | 9000-9999 | `CARRIED` | 0 (reserved, vertical slice) | A true-mass decoy for phantom salvage (IDB-22) |

`CARRIED` means the instance occupies the single `controller.state.carry_ref` [PCI:131, PC-8]; `HAND` means it occupies the hand rail; `STOWED` means it is a counted entry in the Suit's block. The Item DB defines the class; Suit and the Controller own the slots (IDB-16). Capacity is 999 `type_id`s per class (a boot assertion): the class base (1000, 2000, ... 9000) is reserved and never assigned, so the usable range is base+1 to base+999 (orchestrator ruling, QA B8; F-8 and AC-73 pin it). The classes are fixed in the MVP; a seventh class would need a new prefix, a new range and a `catalogue_version` bump (IDB-27).

**IDB-3 — Two keys: `def_id` persists, `type_id` stays in memory.** `def_id` is a lowercase ASCII snake_case string `<class>_<family>[_<variant>]`, at most `ID_MAX_LEN` 40 characters [PH], matching `^(mod|tool|cons|part|curio|junk)_[a-z][a-z0-9]*(_[a-z0-9]+)*$` (digits are legal after the first letter: `mod_o2_tank` is a roster id). `type_id` is an unsigned 16-bit integer, append-only, never reused. **Only `def_id` ever reaches a save, a snapshot, a `SalvageRecord`, a hash or a replicated record**; `type_id` exists for in-memory tables and the vertical slice's replication summaries, so renumbering can never break a save. This mirrors the Graph's own contract: "`def_id` is a string and type ids are numbers" [LSG:832], with stable content type IDs under ADR-LSG-08 [LSG:26]. A dialect is a separate field, never an id token (IDB-23). A rename after release is an alias, not a new id (IDB-27). *Alternative considered:* a string key only (no compact in-memory key, nothing to bound a typo) and a numeric key only (contradicts the Graph's string-in-saves rule) — both rejected; the pair is kept in step by the bijection assertion of IDB-4.

**IDB-4 — The id ledger and the boot assertions.** A committed ledger `assets/data/items/ids.lock.json` lists every `(def_id, type_id)` pair ever shipped and the `retired_ids[]`. Boot refuses the build with `ITEM_BOOT_TYPE_ID(class, id)`, by analogy to `GRAPH_BOOT_TYPE_ID(table, id)` [LSG:1037], when any of these holds: two defs share a `type_id`; a `type_id` lies outside its class range; a `def_id` prefix disagrees with its class; a `type_id` is in `retired_ids`; a shipped `def_id` appears in the ledger with a different `type_id`; or a ledger pair is missing from the catalogue without a `retired_ids` entry. Formula F-8 is the assertion. Aliases (`renamed_from[]`, IDB-27) are resolved transitively to a depth of at most `ALIAS_DEPTH_MAX` 8 [PH]; a cycle, a self-alias, or an alias equal to a live `def_id` refuses to boot (`ITEM_BOOT_ALIAS(def_id)`), because an alias never creates a def and a retired string reused for a different def would let an old save silently load the wrong thing. The sim never iterates a Dictionary [LSG:64, LS-1 (i)]: the catalogue exposes arrays sorted ascending by `type_id` (`defs_of_class`), and a `def_id`-keyed map is for lookup only.

*Rules for caps.* (a) A port `cap` is in the loop's unit per step [LSG:68]: mGU/step for loop 1, W for loop 2, **mL/step** for loops 3 and 4 [LSG:64, 72]. (b) **A fluid-port cap (loops 1, 3, 4) is binding**: it is one of the two inputs to the D.14 seam capacity at a weld [LSG:640, 644, 654] and a SEVERED stub leaks at `OPEN_END_RATE_PM` of it per step until `cap_port` [LSG:772, 784, 960]. Fluid caps are therefore declared as the def's **rated flow**, and the severed-leak column above is the audible price of cutting that port out of a line. **POWER-port caps (201-204) are declared at or above the Graph bound** (inrush-safe) because power has no inventory and a power stub leaks nothing [LSG:172]; whether the island balance reads a power-port cap at all is an Open Question. (c) The non-loop couplings `901`-`903` carry `cap` **0** (routed): LS-4 says they never carry an edge [LSG:70], so no seam capacity or stub leak is computed from them, and the Graph owner confirms it reads cap 0 on them as not applicable, not blocked. `301 CLT_HEAT_IN` and `302 CLT_HEAT_OUT` are **loop-3 edge endpoints** — LS-4 says they "attach only to `304`" [LSG:70] — so they are fluid ports under rule (b): their cap is binding for D.14 at a weld and for the severed-stub leak, and they are declared at the coolant loop's rated flow (40 mL/step [PH], the pump's 304 cap). (d) `local_index` is the declaration order, and it is what a hardpoint matches (F-6). (e) A catalogue lint runs `connectable` over every declared port against every Layout hardpoint declaration and reports a hardpoint with no compatible def [LSG:774], printing the distinct reason (`STORE-STORE`, `SRC-SRC`, `SNK-SNK`) so the author knows whether to insert a junction or choose another def. (f) F-12 is a **blocking** lint over every fluid port: it prints the severed leak, evaluates the seam capacity by calling the Graph's D.14 (never a copy of its formula) and fails a cap below the def's rated flow or a severed leak that floors to zero. *Decided by:* LS-3, LS-4, [LSG:774]; Pillar 1 — a slot either fits or it does not, and a cut port is heard.

**IDB-9 — The `hp_start` contract.** The def's `hp_start` (10000) is the `hp` of a **fresh** module: one an author places in a template or a catalogue spawns. The Item DB never edits `hp`. Wear is the Graph's and appears on the module itself (Pillar 1): its output falls through D.4, its WORN descriptor is module-body channel [LSG:44, LS-14], and its weight is a Suit body cue. A def with `hp_start` 0 or above `HP_FULL` refuses to boot (`ITEM_BOOT_FIELD`), because a node installed at 0 is a DESTROYED node leaking at its maximum [LSG:772].

Invariants (lint at serialise, assert at restore): `1 <= condition_bp <= cap_bp` for a module, where `cap_bp` is `HP_FULL` (10000) or at most `PATCH_CAP_BP`; `patched_at_step` is 0 whenever `cap_bp` is `HP_FULL`; and all four (`condition_bp`, `cap_bp`, `patched_at_step`, `charge`) are 0 for a non-module; a module instance at `condition_bp` 0 is refused at restore (a DESTROYED module yields a part, never a module, F-5); `count <= stack_max` (a lint at serialise only: after a catalogue edit lowers a `stack_max`, a restore keeps the held count and the holder refuses further pickups, see Edge Cases); `stack_max` is 1 for every CARRIED def; no float, no `res://` path, no perceived field. Canonical zeros are mandatory because holder blocks feed `state_hash`: two encodings of one state would desync a replica. Persisted instances hold `def_id` plus these fields and **never `mass_g`, `weight_class`, `carry_class` or any other def number**, so a balance edit to a mass or class re-derives on load with no migration. Two instances merge iff `def_id`, `condition_bp` and `charge` are equal (and, for a module, `cap_bp` and `patched_at_step`; moot, because modules never stack). `life_ticks` is **not** an instance field in the MVP (a stowed glowstick is always at full life; life exists only on the Emitter's record after the throw), so the field named `charges_or_life_ticks` in the working draft is one field with one meaning here, `charge`, and the second meaning is reserved. (orchestrator ruling: canonical zeros adopted from the systems proposal so one state has one byte form; the game-designer proposal's 10000 for non-modules is dropped.) An instance serialises to roughly 40-60 bytes, so a holder carrying twelve is far below `MAX_PAYLOAD` 4 KiB.

**IDB-17 — Salvage yield is deterministic in the MVP.** `salvage_yield(rec)` (F-5) maps the `SalvageRecord{def_id, hp, state}` that an applied M6 returns [LSG:185] (plus `hp_cap` and `patched_at_step`, provisional, IDB-18) to the instance that appears. A module node in OK, DEGRADED or FAILED yields exactly **one instance of its own def** with `condition_bp = rec.hp`, `cap_bp = rec.hp_cap` and `patched_at_step = rec.patched_at_step`; a DESTROYED node (`hp` 0) yields `scrap_count` of its `repair_part_id` and **no module**; SALVAGED and ABSENT are rejected by the Graph before this runs. There is no randomness. The Graph's phrase "the loot roll is the Item DB's" [LSG:185, 850] is satisfied by this function. The yield is computed from the return value of the *applied* M6, never from a pre-read of the node, so two players cutting one node in the same tick cannot duplicate it: the second M6 sees SALVAGED and is rejected under validate-then-apply [KRN:CR-9]. **Salvage-once**: the node becomes SALVAGED [LSG:244], so the faucet is bounded by the authored node count (plus at most one part per DESTROYED node). A keyed draw is *reserved by name only* for Procedural Derelict Generation (vertical slice): `rng_derive(world_seed, "item.yield", node_id)`, never a stored counter — the Item DB has no block, so a registry-style counter "stored in the owning block" [REG:2742] cannot exist and the Graph's pattern of an owner-defined key [LSG:123] applies. Node ids are never reused [LSG:68] and a node is salvaged once [LSG:244], so node identity alone is a sufficient key and the draw is a pure function of the node: there is no step number in the key and so no re-roll by choosing when to cut. It is not a loot table and has no MVP effect. *Alternatives considered:* a per-def seeded bonus roll (reserved, not used) and full container loot tables (rejected: both MVP derelicts are handmade with authored salvage slots, and the faucet must be legible — Pillar 5).

**IDB-18 — What survives salvage then reinstall (provisional rule, routed question).** The Graph's `SalvageRecord` carries only `{def_id, hp, state}`, and M5 takes `hp_start` and no cap, clock or stock [LSG:184, 185]; both are locked and not restated or edited here. Until the Graph owner rules, the Item DB states the following as its **provisional default** (OQ-10's recommendation, adopted after the creative-director review CD-GDD-ALIGN, 2026-10-26): (i) **stored quantities** — BATTERY `stock`, O2_TANK `stock`, RESERVOIR `stock_ml` and FUEL_TANK `stock_ml` — are **discarded into the section's `discarded_*` counters on salvage**, conserved the way `discard_section` (M4 [LSG:183]) conserves [LSG:799], and a reinstalled store starts at stock 0 (the Graph's own conservation note lists M5 among the only mutators that create a Graph quantity [LSG:799]; stock 0 creates none, so the invariant stays trivial); (ii) **patch state persists through salvage.** A module cut out and put back is the same body with the same history: a reinstalled module gets `hp = SalvageRecord.hp`; a module whose `hp_cap` was `PATCH_CAP_BP` reinstalls **capped** at it and **carries its decay clock** (`patched_at_step` unchanged); an un-patched module reinstalls at `hp_cap = HP_FULL` with no clock (the fresh-node default [LSG:68]). Only a FULL repair, which spends a part (IDB-20 (6)), lifts a cap; no cut and reinstall does. **The launder is closed by rule, not by a tripwire:** a free, repeatable action must not stand in for the finite part (the Player Fantasy design test), and the bolted patch plate keeps its meaning after the module has been cut out and put back (Pillar 1, physical history; Pillar 5). **Routed CONFLICT:** the rule needs the record to carry `hp_cap` and `patched_at_step` and the reinstall to restore them, which locked M5 cannot do as written. Either the Graph owner extends `SalvageRecord` and M5 (recommended), or Assembly's `install_module` issues a Graph-owned restore of cap and clock in the same step as M5; the Item DB extends neither, restates no M5 signature and edits no Graph value, and until a ruling the rule holds as a contract between Assembly and the Graph (AC-32 is PROVISIONAL on it). **The clock is absolute and time in transit counts:** `patched_at_step` is the Graph's `step_no`, copied unchanged, and the Graph's wear terms apply only to subjects in the graph [LSG:505, 769], so transit never costs `hp`; but a module carried or shelved longer than `PATCH_DECAY_S` 240 s reinstalls already past its hold and starts to decay at the next wear period. A shelf is not a pause button. `hp` is the damage and carries unchanged. The cut still costs real time, noise and air, so a salvage is never a free action: the SEVERED stubs leak at `OPEN_END_RATE_PM` until `cap_port` [LSG:772], the island re-solves with the module gone [LSG:773], the carry runs at `V_CARRY` 1 and the module stays DEGRADED at `eff` 750 until a part is spent. The break-even in F-15 is retained as a **regression tripwire**, not as the control: a measured cycle cheaper than `T_CYCLE_MIN`, or any build in which a reinstall resets a cap or a clock, reopens this rule. **Stock is in situ, not hauled:** O2 is regenerated by the scrubber, charge by the reactor at `CH_CAP`, and coolant circulates rather than being consumed; **fuel is the exception** — it is finite and non-renewable ("nothing the Graph does refills the fuel tank or the O2 tank" [LSG:793]) — so fuel reaches the home-ship by welding a derelict section whose tank joins the loop, not by hauling a tank, and the MVP roster has no fuel-canister def. The patch-state rule (ii) and the stored-quantity rule (i) are routed to the Graph owner as one Open Question (OQ-10); `SalvageRecord` is **not** extended here. A reinstalled empty store must read as empty in the world (a dark store, a silent gauge on the sane channel), or it reads as the game cheating — a note for the presentation systems.

**IDB-20 — Part consumption economics (Interaction designed: IA-18 discharges these statements).** The Item DB owns `parts_per_full`, so it states the economic rule; Interaction owns the command, the channel timing and the state that implements it. (1) **One part-count per job.** A full repair job is one accepted `begin_patch(FULL)`; its cost is `parts_per_full` of `repair_part_for(subject)`, consumed at the **first productive channel step** — the first step in which `hp_cap` or `hp` rises (registry: `full_job_charge`, Interaction's: the part is RESERVED at the Applied FULL begin tick, so the actor's visible stock falls then, and is returned at `until` = Tb + `FULL_CHANNEL_TICKS` if no step was productive; it is PAID, i.e. spent for good, at the first productive step). A full repair on a subject with `hp_cap = HP_FULL` and `hp = hp_cap` consumes nothing and resolves `PRECONDITION(NOTHING_TO_REPAIR)` (Interaction IA-18 statement 7, an Applied-and-did-nothing outcome). (2) **A full job issues only `kind = FULL` steps**: a PATCH step inside a paid job would re-cap `hp_cap` to `PATCH_CAP_BP` and waste the part [LSG:186]. (3) **No refund on interruption**: the part is spent, and the `hp` already applied stays (steps are real). (4) **Recommendation to Interaction (not an Item DB rule or knob)**: a resume allowance for a re-issued `begin_patch(FULL)` on the same subject shortly after an interruption (adopted: Interaction's `FULL_JOB_GRACE_S` 60 s [PLACEHOLDER], IA-18 statement 6, for the same seat on the same subject; other seats are charged), so a stagger or a pod hop does not double-charge a part. (5) **A PATCH never consumes a part** [REG:6033]; **no patch-kit def exists** in the roster, and a PATCH consumes no item at all [REG:6033; LSG:222] — the phrase "patch kits are the Item DB's finite consumable" at LSG:795 contradicts LS-23, M7 and the registry and is routed to the Graph owner as a wording fix (Open Questions); behaviour of a PATCH on a subject whose `hp` already exceeds `PATCH_CAP_BP` (does `hp` clamp down?) is the Graph's to answer (Open Questions), and the recommended Interaction rule is to offer only FULL there. (6) **Wear is a full repair**: a duty-worn machine at 6900 `hp` with `hp_cap` 10000 can only be raised above `PATCH_CAP_BP` by a full repair, which is the engine of Pillar 5 — the part is what wear costs. (7) In co-op, each player's part is consumed only if that player's first step is applied; a second seat beginning on a subject already under a job is `BUSY` at `validate` and makes no reservation (Interaction IA-34). (8) A missing part produces no `BLOCKED` and no reject: FULL is simply not offered without `parts_per_full` of the part (Interaction IA-18 statement 1); the Item DB rejects nothing. (9) **`NONE` means Interaction offers no FULL job on that subject** (PATCH only); a FULL repair never costs zero parts, so a subject whose `repair_part_for` is `NONE` cannot have its `hp_cap` restored for free. The SEAM record (subject_kind 4) is held at `NONE`; whether M7 is FULL-capable on a SEAM subject in the MVP is routed to the Graph owner. The weld that makes a seam is V9 (MVP); V11 SEAL_SEAM, which sets a seam's `leak_bp` to 0, is vertical slice A [LSG:228, 648].

**IDB-21 — Weight: true integer grams, a class, and a body cue — never speed or noise.** `mass_g` is an integer (the Graph's pattern is scaled integers: mGU, mC, dkPa, µmL [LSG:64]). `weight_class` (F-1) is LIGHT, MEDIUM or HEAVY by two thresholds `WEIGHT_MEDIUM_G` 5000 and `WEIGHT_HEAVY_G` 25000 [PH]. The Item DB supplies the true number; **Suit turns carried (and stowed) mass into a consequence-channel body cue** — breath, strain; never a number on screen — and the class is the resolution that cue must at least have. Mass does **not** change `V_CARRY` (1 base px/tick, one constant [REG:5338-5356]) or the carry `NOISE_PER_TICK` (1 [REG:5442-5457]); the Controller already makes the carry visibly slow ("a module at chest height halves the walk" [PCI:98]), and the haul cost of a module is its distance and the single `carry_ref`, not its mass (F-13 is the worked comparison: a 90 kg reactor cell and a 6 kg data core cost the same per tile). **There is no pack capacity and no weight readout in the MVP**: weight matters for the single carried module, the hull plate and the field check. **BINDING on Suit & Equipment (a requirement, not a courtesy): stowed mass counts, not only the carried module.** When `stowed_mass_g` (F-2: the sum of `instance_mass_g` over the holder's STOWED instances) is at or above `WEIGHT_HEAVY_G`, the Suit must produce a closed-list consequence-channel body cue (breath, visor fog, rime or shiver, from Suit's SE-27 set, no vignette; the Suit chooses which, it may not choose none and may not choose a module-body cue). The MVP case is the 30 kg `part_hull_plate`, the heaviest object a player can hold: a design in which it has no world footprint, or costs the hauler nothing the body can report, makes the exposure cost of hauling it avoidable and weakens "the physical world is ground truth" (Pillars 1 and 3). **It is also seen, derived and not authored:** a STOWED def at `weight_class` HEAVY draws a back-frame sprite (a plate on the back rig) that never covers the chest point, the per-facing beam origin [ART:512]; there is no new def field, the rule is the class (and `stack_max` 1, above). The sprite keys on a stowed def's own `weight_class` and the body cue on the summed `stowed_mass_g`; they are two readings of one rule and in the MVP roster only the hull plate crosses `WEIGHT_HEAVY_G` either way. The sprite is module-body channel and may lie, the body cue is the truthful report, so the sprite cannot serve as the sub-class d field check. Whether a stowed haul adds carry-rate noise is Interaction's open choice (OQ-4); any such term is flat per tile and keyed to the class, never a function of grams, so the locked `NOISE_PER_TICK` carry value and the rule above stay true. Channel split: the body cue is consequence-channel and never corruptible; the sprite and the inspect card are module-body and may lie [LSG:44]. Boundary hazard: a def sitting exactly on a threshold flips class on a 1 g edit, so the lint `WEIGHT_CLASS_MARGIN_PM` (default 100 permille [PH]) keeps every def at least that far from both thresholds (the forbidden bands are 4500-5500 g and 22500-27500 g at the defaults) and is **blocking in CI**; a boundary value is a defect, not a class-population argument. Every roster mass clears both bands (`mod_battery_cell` is 22000 g, MEDIUM; the nearest others are `tool_welder` 4000 g and `mod_vent` 6000 g). *Alternatives considered:* weight shown only on the inspect card (corruptible, fails the field check); a second instrument such as a pod scale (reserved for the vertical slice); weight scaling noise (conflicts with the locked carry noise for heavy modules) — rejected or deferred.

**IDB-22 — Phantom-salvage support (hallucination sub-class d): a true mass on every def, and off-band junk.** The Item DB supplies exactly two things to sub-class d: a **true `mass_g` on every def**, and the existence of `junk_` defs. A junk def's `mass_g` differs from the mass of the lure it will be dressed as by at least `WEIGHT_TELL_MIN_PM` 300 permille [PH] (F-10), **and** the two fall in different `weight_class` values — because the Suit's cue resolves classes, not grams, a 30 percent gap inside one class gives the same cue and the check could not fail the lie (provisional — Suit's cue ladder in flight; if the ladder is finer than three steps the class clause is replaced by "differs by at least one cue step"). The lure sprite is the Perception Filter's to draw; the lure-band data and the lie belong to Sanity (SN-21); a def never carries a believed, displayed or fake value. The junk-tell lint (F-10) reads the Sanity-owned band file (registry: `lure_bands.json`; path `assets/data/sanity/lure_bands.json`, SN-21; `{band_id, mid_g, class}`, lint-only, never loaded at runtime or persisted). **No lure data lives under `assets/data/items/`**: the table of what a junk def is dressed as is the lie's data, so neither a junk def nor any file in the Item DB folder says what a thing will look like. The MVP uses the field for `weight_class` and the body cue only; junk defs ship in the vertical slice (reserved ids 9000-9999), and a junk def that fails F-10 is not releasable.

**IDB-23 — Dialect is a skin, not a stat.** Defs carry `family` and `dialect`. A variant row may override stats, but **no MVP def does** (lint: every MVP def has `dialect: foundry` and no override), so no dominant dialect can emerge. `lattice` and `civil` are reserved enum values. The MVP derelicts are Foundry [IDX:31] and the home-ship's origin is Foundry [ART:298-330, 3.3]. Port *shape* (round, octagonal, rectangular) is sprite grammar and never a port-type id. A grafted section keeps its donor dialect, so a foreign module reads foreign and plays identically — the "foreign" read is a sprite concern. Two defs sharing a `family` with different stats are allowed only after the MVP. **Dialect and `grade` stay behaviourless:** a dialect never raises a stat, and `grade` is a reserved integer that is 0 everywhere in the MVP; any future behaviour for `grade`, or any stat that differs by dialect or ring, needs a creative-director review before it enters a def (IDB-29 rule 5).

**IDB-24 — "Dead" modules are not variant defs.** No def field encodes "powered-off", and no `_dead`, `_cold` or `_dormant` def exists. A dried-trail derelict fault is the **same `def_id`** authored at `hp` at or below `HP_FAIL` on an unpowered branch — "a `def_id` whose module is powered-off" [LSG:226] — emitting descriptors at `intensity_pm` 0 with a large `since_steps`. Failure & Trail reads the descriptor, not the def (its own Interactions row, FT:305), so the convention is Derelict Authoring's to follow when it authors a dead fault. Pillar 1: the dead module is the live module without power; the world, not the catalogue, says so.

**IDB-25 — Things that have `hp` but no def.** HULL_SEGMENT (empty `def_id` [LSG:68]), edges (no `def_id` [LSG:72]), VALVE (an edge), OPENING and SEAM have no def; a part mends them (IDB-19). This is why a thing with `hp` can lack a `def_id`: the Graph gives a `def_id` to every node that is a *module*, and these are structure. The junction node kinds (DUCT_JUNCTION 23, PIPE_JUNCTION 33, FUEL_JUNCTION 41) are not in the MVP slice list [LSG:228] and get no def until a template uses one. **Door motors (resolved).** LS-3 gives a `def_id` to every node except COMPARTMENT, EXTERIOR and HULL_SEGMENT [LSG:68], and the home-ship has door-motor LOAD nodes among its ~8 LOADs [LSG:228] (tier 2 [LSG:425]), so a door motor has a def: `mod_door_motor` (`type_id` 1013; `node_kind` 13, LOAD subtype `3 DOOR_MOTOR`; 120 W, `power_tier` 2, `waste_permille` 0, all [PH] except the tier; IDB-6, IDB-8, IDB-31). Whether power is a precondition of opening a door stays Interaction's [LSG:192]. The one-sentence reason a thing with `hp` can lack a `def_id` stands: structure (hull segments, edges, openings, seams) is not a module, and the Graph gives a `def_id` only to modules.

**IDB-26 — Data lives in JSON under `assets/data/items/`.** One file per class: `modules.json`, `tools.json`, `consumables.json`, `parts.json`, `curios.json` (`junk.json` in the vertical slice), plus `meta.json` (`catalogue_version`, `retired_ids`) and `ids.lock.json` (IDB-4). There is no lure data in this folder (IDB-22). Integers only; any designer float (a 1.5-tile glowstick radius) is converted once at boot by `int(round(x * scale))` into the immutable table, as `GraphTuning` does [LSG:64, LS-1 (b)]; no `res://` path on any field that can reach a save; sprite and glyph keys follow the art bible's 8.5 naming grammar `<cat>[_<dialect>]_<subject>[_<variant tokens>]_<map>.png` [ART:1117-1148]. This follows the controller's `assets/data/*` convention [PCI:238]. **-> ADR candidates, not decided here:** `Resource` versus plain data for the runtime object (the kernel's ADR [KRN:102]); the export include-filter for `*.json` (a 30-minute spike against the 4.7.2 export templates); `JSON.parse_string` returning numbers as `float`, so every integer field is range- and integrality-checked at load (`ITEM_BOOT_FIELD`: `40000.5` and `1e9` are refused; an integral float such as `40000.0` is accepted and converted once). The engine reference has no module for this domain (`modules/` has animation, audio, input, navigation, networking, physics, rendering, ui; there is no `scripting.md` or `core.md`), so these claims are MEDIUM confidence and spike-bound; the 4.7 typed-return rule (an override of a typed-return method needs an explicit `return`) applies to any loader or validator base class.

**IDB-27 — Versioning, renames, retirement.** The Item DB offers Save/Load one integer, **`catalogue_version`**, bumped on any def removal, rename or field-shape change (F-9), plus `catalogue_hash` (diagnostic only, never gates a load; H64 over canonical bytes sorted ascending by `def_id` — the function is an ADR candidate). Pre-release saves are disposable [LSG:832]. After the first build given to anyone outside the project, a rename needs a `renamed_from[]` entry on the new def, honoured **only at restore**; a removed def must name `replaced_by` or restore fails loudly with the holder's named failure — `GRAPH_RESTORE_UNKNOWN_DEF` for Graph nodes [LSG:832], the holder's own code for instances; never silently. A value tuned within its type (`mass_g`, `req_w`, a glowstick number) is **not** structural and does not bump. The Item DB does not embed itself in saves. The integer is named `catalogue_version`, the name Save/Load's header table uses [SL:159] and its own Item DB row adopts [SL:476] (name history: Save/Load OQ-13 [SL:1301], closed). This is checkpoint ruling A2 applied to Save/Load's final text: the header is Save/Load's namespace (kernel row 3), so the Item DB carries the owner's written name. `catalogue_hash` is kept as diagnostic. `catalogue_hash` sorts the canonical bytes by `def_id` (ascending byte order), not by `type_id`, so a legal renumber never changes the diagnostic hash.

**IDB-28 — The query API (all pure, read-only).** `def(def_id)`, `has(def_id)`, `def_by_type_id(type_id)`, `defs_of_class(class)` (ascending `type_id`, the only order sim code may iterate), `compatible_defs(hardpoint)` (F-6), `repair_part_for(subject_kind, kind_id, loop)` (F-3), `salvage_yield(rec)` (F-5), `weight_class(def)` (F-1), `instance_mass_g(instance)` (F-2), `resolve_alias(def_id)` (restore only), `catalogue_version()`, `catalogue_hash()` and `has_both_frames(def_id, pair) -> bool` (true iff both tokens of `pair` are in the def's `sprite_states`; requested by In-World Instruments IW-17, it implements Sanity AC-SN-152's SPRITE_SWAP eligibility; presentation-only). No query returns a Dictionary iteration order; none mutates; none allocates an instance id. **Pure helpers (orchestrator ruling, QA B2):** `part_cost(subject_kind, kind_id, loop)` (F-4: the count rule only; the channel is Interaction's), `held_mass_g(instances)` (the F-2 holder sum), `validate_instance(instance)` (IDB-15; returns a reason code, never clamps) and `glowstick_reach(rem)` (F-7: equals Emitter State's D.9 [EM:506], which reproduces F-7 tick for tick; a **non-authoritative** reference oracle, Emitter State remains authoritative). The analysis formulas (F-1 margin, F-10, F-11, F-12, F-14, F-15) are CI tools in `tools/ci/item_db_lints`, not runtime queries. **Totality (orchestrator ruling, QA B4/B5):** `def` and `def_by_type_id` of an unknown key return the shared immutable sentinel `NO_DEF` (empty `def_id`, `type_id` 0, mass 0; callers test `has` first, and a sentinel reaching a holder is the holder's named failure); `repair_part_for` of an unknown `kind_id` returns (`NONE`, 0); `salvage_yield` of an ABSENT or SALVAGED record returns an empty list; `resolve_alias` outside the restore path returns `NO_DEF`.

**IDB-29 — The faucet, the sinks, and what the Item DB refuses to be.** Salvage is the only faucet: an item enters the world by an authored placement, by `salvage_yield`, or by the Suit's one-time starting kit, and by nothing else. There is no shop, no currency, no recipe, no crafting, no vendor and no refill station. Anti-pillars applied [CON:285-298]: not a freeform ship-building sandbox (a def fixes its ports and cannot be edited), not a combat game (no weapon stats on any def), no authored story, no win state. Scarcity comes from O2, power, Attention and finite parts — not from tool upkeep.

| Faucet | Mechanism | Bound | Owner of the placement number |
|---|---|---|---|
| Module node | `salvage_node` yields the node's own def at its `hp` | one per authored module node, once | Derelict Authoring |
| Scrap | `salvage_node` on a DESTROYED node yields `scrap_count` of its part | at most one per destroyed node | wear and damage (the Graph) |
| Loose placement | an authored part, glowstick or spare | one per placement, picked up once | Derelict Authoring, Layout |
| Starting kit | tools, starting glowsticks, suppressant charges | one-time | Suit & Equipment (provisional — Suit in flight) |

| Sink | Mechanism | Capacity |
|---|---|---|
| Part | a full repair consumes `parts_per_full` (IDB-20) | finite: equals the parts supply |
| Glowstick | `throw_glowstick`; a landed stick is spent (IDB-12) | finite: equals the stick supply |
| Suppressant charge | use spends one charge (IDB-11) | finite: equals the allotment |
| Installed module | converted into ship capability | open: the hardpoint count is Layout's |
| Discarded section | `discard_section` (M4) discards everything installed [LSG:183] | Layout's and Assembly's |

Five economy rules follow. (1) **A patch is free of items; a part is an upgrade priced in scarcity.** A patch is free of items by locked design [REG:6033], so the part is never the *only* way to survive an MVP fault; it is the only way to stop *paying time* for it. That honest reading of Pillar 5 means **the MVP is always survivable with zero parts: the patch treadmill is a playable, deliberately costly terminal state** (guarantee G-0, an authoring acceptance; it **holds by LS-10 and M7**: `FAILED` is repairable [LSG:125] and M7 rejects only DESTROYED [LSG:186], so a patch works on the broken breaker at `hp` 2000; the LS-26 phrase "dark until replaced" [LSG:228] is prose, routed to the Graph owner as a wording question, and one spare `mod_breaker_junction` placement is a recommendation to Derelict Authoring, not an Item DB guarantee): the terminal state when the parts are spent is the patch treadmill, and that state is designed as playable. It is a treadmill, not a farm: a patch decays (`PATCH_DECAY_S`), a patched scrubber holds one crew at margin zero, and with N >= 2 crew the treadmill loses ground (F-14: one scrubber, even fully repaired, runs a 200 mGU/step deficit at N = 2; holding N crew takes N patched scrubbers at margin zero and N times the re-patch chores), so zero-part survival is pressure and not a safe place to farm (the Pillar 5 design test). What makes the part worth hunting is quantified in F-14 (a patched scrubber at `PATCH_CAP_BP` makes exactly `R_BREATH` for one crew — margin zero — against a margin of +100 mGU/step for a fully repaired one, and the margin vanishes with a second crew). (2) **Part conservation: no verb converts parts.** `scrap_count` is 1 on every module def and at most 1 in the MVP (boot-asserted), no def consumes a part to yield an item (no recycling, no upgrade), and the number of part instances in existence never exceeds the authored loose parts plus the DESTROYED module nodes. (3) **A supply ratio is an authoring target, not a runtime field.** The ratio of parts supplied to full repairs a diligent player could demand, `part_cover_pm` (F-11), is held in an advisory band of 500-800 permille [PH]; below it permanent fixes feel out of reach, above it every chronic fault is solved and nothing pushes inward. (orchestrator ruling: the three specialist proposals gave 750-1000, 500-750 and 500-800; the reconciled band is 500-800.) (4) **No placement without a consumer**: a module names an install target kind, a part a repair family, a consumable a `use_verb`, a tool its `verbs[]` — a def with no consumer in a placement table is the lint `PLACEMENT_NO_CONSUMER` (WARN; the data core, in the MVP). A lint fails any def or file under `assets/data/items/` that carries a forbidden key (IDB-5). (5) **Depth richness is expressed by placement, never by def stats (BINDING on Derelict Authoring and Depth Rings & Scarcity).** "Deeper Is Richer" is delivered through placement density, part supply (`part_cover_pm` by ring: F-11's band is the MVP default and Depth Rings may set a per-ring target) and authored `hp` (better-condition modules deeper). It is never delivered through def stats: no def carries a worth, dialect is a skin (IDB-23), and `grade` stays 0 and behaviourless; any future behaviour for `grade`, or any stat that differs by ring or dialect, requires a creative-director review before it enters a def.

**IDB-30 — Boot order and the closed failure list.** The catalogue is loaded in BOOTING **after** the Graph's type tables and `GraphTuning` are loaded (it needs LS-4 for port-id validation and the pure `connectable` for lint) and **before any block restore** (the Graph's `def_id` validation at restore reads it). Lifecycle: UNLOADED, PARSED, VALIDATED, FROZEN, REFUSED (see States). Failure codes — a closed list, all fatal at boot: `ITEM_BOOT_TYPE_ID(class, id)` (IDB-4); `ITEM_BOOT_FIELD(def_id, field)` (missing, out of range, a forbidden key, a float where an integer is required, a heavy def with `stack_max` above 1, `hp_start` outside 1..`HP_FULL`); `ITEM_BOOT_PORT(def_id, local_index)` (a port-type id not in LS-4, or illegal for the node kind); `ITEM_BOOT_REPAIR(def_id)` (`repair_part_id` not a part def, `parts_per_full` below 1, or `repair_part_for` not total over the five `subject_kind` values [LSG:150]); `ITEM_BOOT_MIRROR(table)` (the LS-3 or LS-4 mirror differs from the Graph's boot table); `ITEM_BOOT_ALIAS(def_id)` (IDB-4). `ITEM_BOOT_FIELD` also carries the three structural invariants of the tuning knobs (orchestrator ruling, QA B7): `WEIGHT_MEDIUM_G` below `WEIGHT_HEAVY_G`, `GLOWSTICK_RADIUS_TILES` below `THROW_RANGE_TILES` (reported as `ITEM_BOOT_FIELD(cons_glowstick, radius_tiles)`) and `WANE_TICKS` at least 1; the published ranges in Tuning Knobs are guidance checked by an advisory lint, not boot assertions. **Content lints (CI, not boot):** a hardpoint with no compatible def [LSG:774]; an authored `def_id` unknown to the catalogue (`PLACEMENT_DEF_UNKNOWN`); `WEIGHT_CLASS_MARGIN_PM` (IDB-21; blocking); the junk tell (IDB-22); `PLACEMENT_NO_CONSUMER` (IDB-29; WARN); the part-cover band (F-11; `PART_COVER_BAND`) and the part-supply cap (`PART_SUPPLY_EXCEEDS`); the content-lint ids are Derelict Authoring's (DA-40; registry: `content_lint_ids`), this GDD supplies the rules they check; the binding port-cap lint (F-12: severed leak and seam capacity per fluid port). The Item DB raises no runtime error: at runtime every query is total.

### States and Transitions

The Item DB has three state machines, none of them per-tick: the **catalogue lifecycle** (boot only), the **def lifecycle** (across builds) and the **instance location** (the states an `ItemInstance` can be in, defined here and *performed* by the named owner). Nothing in this section runs in a kernel step.

**Catalogue lifecycle (boot only; one pass).**

| State | Entry | Exit | Transitions | Who may cause them |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| UNLOADED | BOOTING starts | the data files are read | -> PARSED | the kernel's BOOTING sequence (CR-6) |
| PARSED | every `assets/data/items/*.json` read; every designer float converted once to an integer | tables built, nothing yet checked | -> VALIDATED; -> REFUSED on a parse or type error (`ITEM_BOOT_FIELD`) | the loader |
| VALIDATED | the IDB-4 ledger checks, IDB-7 port checks, IDB-19 repair resolution (total over the five `subject_kind` values), IDB-30 mirror equality and alias checks all pass | end of BOOTING | -> FROZEN; -> REFUSED on any closed-list failure code | the loader |
| FROZEN | end of BOOTING | none in a release build (re-entered only by a new process; no hot reload) | none; all IDB-28 queries answer | nobody — the catalogue is immutable |
| REFUSED | any boot code of IDB-30 | terminal | none; the kernel reports a BOOTING failure and the build does not start | the loader |

**Def lifecycle (content, across builds).**

| State | Meaning | Entry | Exit and transitions | Who may cause them |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| ACTIVE | in the catalogue and offered to authors | a def is added with a fresh `type_id` | a rename keeps it ACTIVE under the new id with `renamed_from`; a removal moves it to RETIRED | a content author, with a `catalogue_version` bump (F-9) |
| RETIRED | removed from play; its `type_id` is in `retired_ids`; `replaced_by` names a successor or is empty | a def is removed | never revived; its `type_id` and its `def_id` are never reused; a save naming it restores through `replaced_by` or fails loudly | a content author; the Save/Load restore consumes it |

Every ACTIVE-to-ACTIVE rename and every move to RETIRED bumps `catalogue_version`.

**Instance location.** The Item DB defines these states and the legal moves between them; the **named owner** performs every move through its own command or mutator, and the Item DB performs none. (Suit, Interaction, Assembly, Boarding and Layout are designed as of the 2026-10-27 consistency pass; each repeats its moves in its own GDD.)

## Formulas

All formulas are integer-only: the catalogue is data, and anything an instance carries into a holder block may be hashed or replicated (the kernel's `state_hash` and the Graph's LS-1 (b) rule), so no float is stored, hashed or compared. Truncation is floor, applied to non-negative operands only [LSG:64, LS-1 (d)]. Every duration is labelled **tick** (60 Hz, `TICK_RATE` 60) or **step** (the Graph's 10 Hz, `GRAPH_STEP_TICKS` 6) or **s**. Formulas the Graph owns (D.4 `eff`, D.5 `out_scr`, D.14 `seam_at_weld`) are cited by name and line and never restated; where an example needs their value it says "evaluated by calling D.n". The ids F-1 to F-15 are those of IDB-32. Formulas F-1 to F-10 and F-12 are **runtime or boot** formulas; F-11, F-14 and F-15 are **design-time checks** (authoring and balance lints), and F-13 is a derivation that proves a rule by lock.

### F-1 `weight_class`

The weight_class formula is defined as:

`weight_class(m) = LIGHT if m < WEIGHT_MEDIUM_G ; MEDIUM if WEIGHT_MEDIUM_G <= m < WEIGHT_HEAVY_G ; HEAVY if m >= WEIGHT_HEAVY_G` — and the blocking margin lint `margin_ok(m) = for T in {WEIGHT_MEDIUM_G, WEIGHT_HEAVY_G}: abs(m - T) * 1000 >= WEIGHT_CLASS_MARGIN_PM * T`

**Variables:**

| Variable | Type | Range | Source | Description |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `m` | int g | 1..500000 | def `mass_g` (IDB-5) | True mass of one unit |
| `WEIGHT_MEDIUM_G` | int g | 3000-8000, default 5000 | data [PH] ★ registry candidate | LIGHT/MEDIUM boundary |
| `WEIGHT_HEAVY_G` | int g | 15000-40000, default 25000 | data [PH] ★ registry candidate | MEDIUM/HEAVY boundary |
| `WEIGHT_CLASS_MARGIN_PM` | int permille | 50-200, default 100 | data [PH] ★ registry candidate | Minimum relative distance of any def's mass from either threshold |

**Output Range:** one of three classes; `margin_ok` is a boolean. Boot assertion `WEIGHT_MEDIUM_G < WEIGHT_HEAVY_G` (`ITEM_BOOT_FIELD`). The margin lint is a blocking CI check (IDB-21), not a boot code.
**Example:** `tool_cutter` 3500 g -> LIGHT (distance to 5000 is 1500 >= 5000 x 100 / 1000 = 500, pass). `curio_data_core` 6000 g -> MEDIUM (1000 >= 500, pass). `mod_battery_cell` 22000 g -> MEDIUM (distance to 25000 is 3000 >= 2500, pass; at the old 25000 g it sat exactly on the threshold, where the `>=` rule made it HEAVY and the lint failed). `mod_coolant_pump` 30000 g -> HEAVY (5000 >= 2500, pass). A def at 24000 g would fail the lint (1000 < 2500) and so cannot ship.

### F-2 `instance_mass_g`

The instance_mass_g formula is defined as:

`instance_mass_g(inst) = mass_g(inst.def_id) * inst.count` ; `held_mass_g(holder) = sum of instance_mass_g over the holder's CARRIED, STOWED and IN_HAND instances` ; `stowed_mass_g(holder) = sum of instance_mass_g over the holder's STOWED instances` (the quantity the binding body cue of IDB-21 reads: `weight_class(stowed_mass_g)` HEAVY means the cue is owed)

**Variables:**

| Variable | Type | Range | Source | Description |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `mass_g(def_id)` | int g | 1..500000 | catalogue | Per-unit true mass |
| `inst.count` | int | 1..`stack_max` | `ItemInstance` (IDB-15) | Stack size; 1 for modules, tools and curios |

### F-4 `part_cost`

### F-7 Glowstick parameters and the curve contract

The glowstick_life formula is defined as:

`LIFE_TICKS = GLOWSTICK_LIFE_S * TICK_RATE` ; `RADIUS_PX = int(round(GLOWSTICK_RADIUS_TILES * TILE_PX))` ; `WANE_TICKS = floor(LIFE_TICKS * GLOWSTICK_WANE_FROM_PM / 1000)` ; `f(rem) = 1000 if rem >= WANE_TICKS else GLOWSTICK_WANE_FLOOR_PM + floor((1000 - GLOWSTICK_WANE_FLOOR_PM) * rem / WANE_TICKS)` ; `reach_px(rem) = floor(RADIUS_PX * f(rem) / 1000)` ; `rem_at_landing = LIFE_TICKS - THROW_ARC_TICKS`

**Variables:**

| Variable | Type | Range | Source | Description |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `GLOWSTICK_LIFE_S` | int s | 60-300, default 150 | data [PH] ★ | Seconds of light, counted from the throw tick |
| `TICK_RATE` | int | 60 | registry (locked) | Ticks per second |
| `GLOWSTICK_RADIUS_TILES` | number | 1.5-3, default 2; strictly below `THROW_RANGE_TILES` 3 [PCI:779] | data [PH] ★ | Radius at full life; converted once at boot |
| `TILE_PX` | int | 48 | registry (locked) | Base pixels per tile |
| `GLOWSTICK_WANE_FROM_PM` | int permille | 200-1000, default 400 | data [PH] ★ | Share of total life remaining at which waning starts (1000 = linear from the throw) |
| `GLOWSTICK_WANE_FLOOR_PM` | int permille | 100-500, default 250 | data [PH] ★ | Reach fraction at the last tick |
| `rem` | int ticks | 0..`LIFE_TICKS` | Emitter State's record (`life_remaining_ticks`) | Life remaining |
| `THROW_ARC_TICKS` | int ticks | 30 | Controller [PCI:611] | Flight time; the stick has burned that long before it lands |

**Output Range:** `LIFE_TICKS` 3600..18000; `f` in `GLOWSTICK_WANE_FLOOR_PM`..1000, monotone non-increasing as `rem` falls (never rises, never pulses); `reach_px` from `RADIUS_PX` down to the floor reach, then the emitter is removed at `rem` 0. Emitter State owns the evaluation (curve id `WANE_LINEAR_25`, EM-15 [EM:196]); this formula is the parameter contract, and Emitter State's D.9 [EM:506] reproduces it tick for tick (its example: `r` 1800 gives `f` 625 and reach 60; `r` 1 gives reach 24 [EM:520]). Build assertions: `GLOWSTICK_RADIUS_TILES < THROW_RANGE_TILES`; `WANE_TICKS >= 1`. `LIFE_TICKS` is a derivation, not a registry constant.
**Example:** `LIFE_TICKS` = 150 x 60 = 9000; `RADIUS_PX` = 2 x 48 = 96; `WANE_TICKS` = floor(9000 x 400 / 1000) = 3600, so the pool is steady for the first 5400 ticks (90 s) after the throw. At `rem` 1800: `f` = 250 + floor(750 x 1800 / 3600) = 625 and `reach_px` = floor(96 x 625 / 1000) = 60 px. At `rem` 3600 the formula is continuous (250 + 750 = 1000). At landing `rem_at_landing` = 9000 - 30 = 8970 (Interaction passes it, EM-15). Floor reach = floor(96 x 250 / 1000) = 24 px, one sixteenth of the full pool's area, which is why Emitter State carries `reach_px` and the shape rather than a radius alone. With `GLOWSTICK_WANE_FROM_PM` 1000 the curve is linear from the throw.

### F-14 Scrubber margin ladder (why a part is worth a sortie)

The scrubber_margin formula is defined as:

`margin(hp, N) = out_scr(hp) - N * R_BREATH` where `out_scr(hp)` is the Graph's D.5 evaluated at `sat` 1000 with `eff` from D.4 (cited, not restated) ; `scrubbers_needed(N, hp) = floor(N * R_BREATH / out_scr(hp)) + 1` (undefined, no finite count, when `out_scr(hp)` is 0, i.e. at or below `HP_FAIL`)

**Variables:**

| Variable | Type | Range | Source | Description |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `hp` | int bp | 0..`HP_FULL` | scrubber condition (the Graph) | Scrubber `hp` |
| `N` | int | 1..4 | the concept's 1-4 players | Crew breathing in the pool |
| `R_SCR` | int mGU/step | 400 (100-1000) | Graph data held in `mod_scrubber.rated_out` [LSG:927] | Scrubber nominal output |
| `R_BREATH` | int mGU/step | 300 (100-600) | Graph placeholder, owned by Suit & Equipment [LSG:929] | Draw per crew; read, never re-valued |
| `out_scr(hp)` | int mGU/step | 0..`R_SCR` | D.5 with D.4 (`HP_OK` 7000, `HP_FAIL` 3000) | Net output |

**Output Range:** `margin` from -N x 300 up to +100 at N = 1 (a fully repaired scrubber). It is an analysis formula, not a runtime rule; if Suit moves `R_BREATH` the ladder moves with it and is not duplicated here.
**Example:** (N = 1, `sat` 1000) `hp` 10000 -> `eff` 1000 -> `out` 400 -> margin +100; `hp` 6000 (`PATCH_CAP_BP` [REG:6021-6037]) -> `eff` 750 -> `out` 300 -> margin **0**; `hp` 5000 -> 200 -> -100; `hp` 4000 -> 100 -> -200; `hp` 3000 -> 0 -> -300. So a patched scrubber supports exactly one crew with zero margin, and the part (which restores `hp_cap` to `HP_FULL` and ends the decay clock) buys +100 and the end of the chore. For two crew with one scrubber at `hp` 10000 the deficit is 600 - 400 = 200 mGU/step and the home-ship's 3,000,000 mGU O2 tank lasts 3,000,000 / 200 = 15,000 steps = 25 min; for four crew the deficit is 800 and the tank lasts 3750 steps = 6.25 min. That is why derelict A's FAILED scrubber is the second scrubber a duo needs (a design reason, not an MVP route: the `scr_a` carry has no MVP destination and the reference carry is `htr_a`, Boarding & Salvage C-BS-3). Lever (reported to the Graph owner, not changed here): at `PATCH_CAP_BP` 5500 the patched `eff` is 625, `out` 250, margin -50 at N = 1, which makes the part mandatory even for a solo player.

### F-15 Salvage-reinstall break-even (the launder regression tripwire)

The patch_launder formula is defined as:

`launder_pays_off <=> T_cycle < floor(H / T_decay_cycle) * (c_patch + w_patch)`

**Variables:**

| Variable | Type | Range | Source | Description |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `T_cycle` | s | Interaction's and Assembly's | cut + carry + `cap_port` on the stubs + install | Time cost of one salvage-then-reinstall of a patched module |
| `H` | s | 1800-7200, design horizon | design | How long the module must stay serviceable |
| `T_decay_cycle` | s | 540 for a machine (to FAILED), 440 for a leak subject | Graph [LSG:769]: 240 s hold (`PATCH_DECAY_S`) plus 30 or 20 wear periods of 10 s at `PATCH_DECAY_BP_PER_100` 100 | Interval between mandatory re-patches |
| `c_patch`, `w_patch` | s | illustrative 8 and 15 | Interaction's channel time and walk (Interaction designed: `interaction-system.md`) | Cost of one re-patch |

**Output Range:** boolean. Under the adopted rule (IDB-18: a reinstall carries `hp_cap` and `patched_at_step`) the launder does not exist, so this check is **no longer the control**; it is a regression tripwire and the Graph owner's fallback arithmetic. It answers one question: if a build or a ruling ever lets a reinstall reset the cap or the clock (`hp_cap` `HP_FULL`, no `patched_at_step`), would cycling pay? The primary control is the persistence rule and its test (AC-32: cap and clock must come back unchanged); this check is the second line. In no case does a cycle launder the damage (`hp` is unchanged) or duty wear.
**Example:** (all times illustrative until Interaction is written) *had the rule been the other way*: for a patched scrubber over `H` 3600 s, `T_decay_cycle` 540 gives floor(3600 / 540) = 6 re-patches x (8 + 15) = 138 s of chore, and a salvage-reinstall in one room at a 35 s illustrative `T_cycle` would be cheaper, so the launder would pay off at the placeholders, at the price of the cut's stub hiss (F-12: the scrubber's one O2 stub, port 101 per IDB-7, leaks 200 mGU/step until capped), the island re-solving without the scrubber and an Attention spike. That is exactly why the rule carries the cap and the clock: a free, repeatable action must not substitute for the finite part. If the Graph owner ever rules that a reinstall resets cap and clock, `T_CYCLE_MIN` 100 s becomes the only control and the placeholders already fail it (35 s against 100 s), so that ruling is not acceptable without a stronger sink and goes to creative-director review.


## Edge Cases

Edge cases are grouped by theme. Diagnostics lines name only the **closed** failure list of IDB-30 (`ITEM_BOOT_TYPE_ID`, `ITEM_BOOT_FIELD`, `ITEM_BOOT_PORT`, `ITEM_BOOT_REPAIR`, `ITEM_BOOT_MIRROR`, `ITEM_BOOT_ALIAS`), the Graph's codes (`GRAPH_RESTORE_UNKNOWN_DEF`, `PORT_INCOMPAT`, `NOT_REPAIRABLE`) or a named CI lint; the Item DB raises no runtime error and rejects no command, so a runtime refusal in a case below is always the owning neighbour's. Specialist proposals that invented other codes (a range code, a non-integer code, a forbidden-field code, a weight-margin code, an over-stack code) are mapped onto these.

**Catalogue boot and ids**

- **If two defs share a `def_id`, or two share a `type_id`**: boot is refused with `ITEM_BOOT_TYPE_ID(class, id)` naming the second occurrence. A catalogue with an ambiguous key cannot be loaded at all, because every save, hash and `SalvageRecord` is keyed by `def_id`.
- **If a `type_id` lies outside its prefix's class range (a `part_` def numbered 1xxx), or a `def_id` prefix disagrees with the declared class**: boot is refused with `ITEM_BOOT_TYPE_ID(class, id)` (F-8). The class ranges are what let the in-memory table be split by class without a lookup.
- **If a shipped def is renumbered (the ledger says `mod_scrubber` is 1001 and the data says 1020), or reuses a retired id**: boot is refused with `ITEM_BOOT_TYPE_ID`. A `type_id` never reaches a save, so a renumber could not break one, but the ledger keeps the in-memory ids stable for the vertical slice's replication summaries and makes reuse impossible.
- **If `hp_start` is 0 or above `HP_FULL`**: boot is refused with `ITEM_BOOT_FIELD(def_id, hp_start)` [IDB-9]. A node installed at `hp` 0 is a DESTROYED node leaking at its maximum [LSG:772].
- **If a JSON number arrives as `40000.5`, `1e9` or any non-integral value in an integer field**: boot is refused with `ITEM_BOOT_FIELD(def_id, field)`; an integral float such as `40000.0` is accepted and converted once at boot (`JSON.parse_string` returns numbers as floats, a MEDIUM-confidence engine claim, spike-bound). Zero floats may survive into the table (LS-1 (b)).
- **If a def carries a forbidden key (`price`, `damage`, `recipe`, `appears_as`, `hallucination_*` and the rest of IDB-5's list)**: boot is refused with `ITEM_BOOT_FIELD(def_id, field)`. The list is the machine form of the anti-pillars, so a later "just one price field" edit fails the build rather than a review.
- **If a def declares a `port_type_id` that is not in the Graph's LS-4 table, or one illegal for its node kind**: boot is refused with `ITEM_BOOT_PORT(def_id, local_index)`; the Graph's own boot would refuse the same table with `GRAPH_BOOT_TYPE_ID(table, id)`. The Item DB reports the def; the Graph reports the table.
- **If the LS-3 or LS-4 mirror in the catalogue differs from the Graph's boot table**: boot is refused with `ITEM_BOOT_MIRROR(table)`. The mirror is read-only; the Graph is the owner.
- **If an alias chain exceeds `ALIAS_DEPTH_MAX` 8, forms a cycle, points at itself, or names a live `def_id`**: boot is refused with `ITEM_BOOT_ALIAS(def_id)`. An alias never creates a def, and a retired string reused for a different def would let an old save silently load the wrong thing.
- **If a def with `mass_g` at or above `WEIGHT_HEAVY_G` is authored with `stack_max` above 1, or a CARRIED/HAND def with `stack_max` above 1**: boot is refused with `ITEM_BOOT_FIELD(def_id, stack_max)`. A heavy stack would let a player carry an unbounded mass outside the single `carry_ref`.
- **If `repair_part_for` has no answer for one of the five `subject_kind` values, or a module names a `repair_part_id` that is not a part def, or `parts_per_full` is below 1**: boot is refused with `ITEM_BOOT_REPAIR(def_id)`. A FULL repair must never cost zero parts, and M7 may be handed any subject kind.

**Zero, maximum and out-of-range values**

- **If a catalogue edit lowers `stack_max` below a count already held in a save**: the restore **keeps the full held count and deletes nothing**; the holder refuses further pickups of that def until the count is at or below the new maximum. This is the one invariant of IDB-15 that is not asserted at restore (it stays a lint at serialise). (orchestrator ruling: the economy proposal's keep-and-refuse-pickup over the systems proposal's clamp, because a silent clamp destroys items without the player doing anything, against Pillar 5's "loss is not total".)
- **If a module `ItemInstance` is serialised with `condition_bp` 0, or above `cap_bp`, or a `cap_bp` that is neither `HP_FULL` nor at most `PATCH_CAP_BP`, or a non-zero `patched_at_step` on a `cap_bp` of `HP_FULL`**: the holder's restore refuses it with the holder's own named failure (the Item DB provides the check, not the code). A module at `hp` 0 is DESTROYED and yields a part, never a module (F-5), so a zero-condition module instance can only be corruption; a non-canonical zero would give one state two byte forms and desync `state_hash`.
- **If `mass_g` or a `WEIGHT_*` threshold is edited between builds, and a def now falls in a different `weight_class`**: nothing migrates and nothing bumps `catalogue_version`. Mass and class are never persisted on an instance (IDB-15), so the next read re-derives them; a boundary case is caught earlier by the `WEIGHT_CLASS_MARGIN_PM` lint (F-1).
- **If an instance's `count` would exceed `stack_max` on a merge**: the merge is refused by the holder (Suit) and the surplus stays where it was; the Item DB supplies the cap and nothing else. A pickup refused for a full stack leaves the item in the world, so scarcity never silently deletes salvage.
- **If a FULL repair targets a subject already at `hp_cap` = `HP_FULL` with `hp` = `hp_cap`**: it consumes nothing and Interaction resolves `PRECONDITION(NOTHING_TO_REPAIR)` [IDB-20 (1); IA-18 statement 7]. A part must change something to be spent.

**Simultaneous rules**

- **If two players cut the same node on the same tick**: the first applied M6 yields the module; the second M6 sees SALVAGED and is rejected under validate-then-apply [KRN:CR-9], so `salvage_yield` runs once on the applied record and the module cannot be duplicated. The yield is computed from the M6 return value, never from a pre-read of the node (F-5).
- **If two players start a FULL repair on one subject, each with one part, on the same step**: commands dispatch sequentially, so the first begin is Applied (its part RESERVED at the begin tick, then PAID at its first productive step) and the second seat's begin is `BUSY` at `validate` and makes no reservation, so the second player's visible stock never falls (Interaction IA-34; registry `full_job_charge`). Each player's part is spent only if that player's first step is applied (IDB-20 (7)).
- **If a FULL repair channel is interrupted (stagger, pod hop) after its first productive step**: the part stays spent and the `hp` already applied stays; the Item DB gives no refund. Interaction's `FULL_JOB_GRACE_S` (60 s, IA-18 statement 6) waives the charge when the same seat re-issues on the same subject inside the window; after it, or for another seat, the second attempt costs `parts_per_full` again.
- **If a PATCH step is issued inside a paid FULL job**: it would re-cap `hp_cap` to `PATCH_CAP_BP` and waste the part [LSG:186]; a FULL job must issue only `kind = FULL` steps (IDB-20 (2)), and the acceptance test asserts it.
- **If a player tries `begin_patch` or `begin_cut` while holding `carry_ref`**: Interaction rejects it `BUSY` under PC-8 [PCI:131, 168]; the player must `drop` first. This is why `part_hull_plate` is STOWED with `stack_max` 1: a stowed part never sets `carry_ref`, so the part itself can never be what blocks the full repair that consumes it.

**Degenerate strategies**

- **If a player salvages a patched module and reinstalls it to shed the patch ceiling and decay clock**: it sheds neither. The module reinstalls with its record's `hp`, `hp_cap` at `PATCH_CAP_BP` and the original `patched_at_step`, so the cap stays and the clock keeps its origin (IDB-18; provisional until the Graph owner rules). Time in transit counts toward the hold: a module carried or shelved longer than `PATCH_DECAY_S` 240 s reinstalls already past its hold and starts decaying at the next wear period. The only way to lift the cap is a FULL repair and its part. The cut still costs the hissing stubs, the island re-solving, a flat-speed carry and an Attention spike, so there is no reason to cycle; AC-32 and the tripwire `T_CYCLE_MIN` guard the regression.
- **If a player deliberately destroys a spare module (overload, dropped into a breach) to harvest its part**: a DESTROYED node salvages into exactly `scrap_count` (1) of its `repair_part_id` and no module, so one module becomes one part at most; the conversion costs a module, which is itself finite salvage. Part conservation (IDB-29 (2)) holds: parts in existence never exceed authored loose parts plus DESTROYED module nodes.
- **If a player salvages the running SCRUBBER or the live BATTERY**: it is allowed with no special case [LSG:773]; the island re-solves, the stubs hiss, the item is delivered whole. A reinstalled store starts empty (IDB-18), so a battery carried home is dark until recharged. The world shows the cost.
- **If a player hoards glowsticks or parts**: the holder caps each stack at `GLOWSTICK_STACK_MAX` 4 and the part `stack_max`; a stack that is full refuses the pickup. Light and permanent fixes stay a decision, not a stockpile, and the stowed mass is a body cue only.
- **If a player tries to run with a carried module**: not an Item DB rule; the Controller forbids it (PC-8) and mass never changes the 1 px/tick carry speed or the carry noise (F-13). A request for mass-scaled speed is a registry conflict, not a tuning change.

**Save, restore and catalogue edits**

- **If a save names a `def_id` that was removed with no `replaced_by`**: the Graph's restore fails with `GRAPH_RESTORE_UNKNOWN_DEF` [LSG:832] for a node, or the holder's own named failure for an instance, with live state untouched. Never a silent drop (IDB-27). Diagnostics: the failing `def_id` and the save's `catalogue_version`.
- **If a removed def has `replaced_by`, or a renamed def has `renamed_from`**: restore maps the instance or node to the successor and keeps `condition_bp` / `hp`. Aliases are honoured only at restore (IDB-4, IDB-27).
- **If a save was written under a higher `catalogue_version` than the running catalogue**: Save/Load's pre-check does not refuse and the holder's restore decides (Save/Load SL-14 [SL:219]: "a header `catalogue_version` above or below the running catalogue's is never a pre-check refusal; the load proceeds and each holder's `restore()` decides; an unknown `def_id` stays the owning block's named failure (`GRAPH_RESTORE_UNKNOWN_DEF`); a `renamed_from` alias honoured only at restore is the Item DB's to offer"; AC-SL-96e [SL:1220]: `catalogue_version` 9 in the header against 7 in the running catalogue); the Item DB offers the integer and states that pre-release saves are disposable [LSG:832].
- **If the catalogue edits `req_w` (or another snapshotted-at-install field) of `mod_scrubber` and a save holds an installed scrubber**: the saved node keeps its old value until it is salvaged and reinstalled (IDB-6); no bump. **If it edits `waste_permille`, `R_SCR`, `BATT_CELL` or `cap_ml`** and the Graph reads those live, the change applies to existing saves at the next restore, a possible mid-game rebalance; the copy-versus-live question is routed to the Graph owner.
- **If the crew dies while carrying a module**: MVP death is restart from the last autosave (user decision; Save/Load owns it, SL-7), so the restore replays every holder block and no item-loss rule exists in the Item DB. The module is wherever the autosave had it.
- **If a section holding loose items or installed modules is discarded**: `discard_section` (M4 [LSG:183]) discards them with the section and destroys their mass into `discarded_*` [LSG:799]; the Item DB defines no refund. It is the loss sink of "ship, not knowledge".

**Salvage, ports and hardpoints (checkpoint-born)**

- **If the player salvages `mod_o2_tank`**: its single fluid stub (103, cap 1200) leaks 600 mGU/step until `cap_port` (F-12), about a DESTROYED duct's 500 [LSG:772], and the tank's stock is discarded into the section's `discarded_*` counters (IDB-18). With the old cap 3000 it would have bled 1500, three times a burst duct; F-12 refuses that value.
- **If the player salvages `mod_reactor_cell` and its fuel stub (402) is capped late**: the stub leaks 1 mL/step (cap 2); at the earlier cap 1 it would floor to 0 and the fuel line would be cut silently, which F-12 refuses. Its heat-jacket port (301, cap 40) is also a fluid stub and bleeds 20 mL/step of coolant until `cap_port`, like a pump stub (F-12).
- **If a SEAM record is the subject of a FULL repair request**: `repair_part_for` returns `NONE` for subject_kind 4, so Interaction offers PATCH only; a seam is restored by V9 re-weld (Assembly's), never for free. Whether M7 is FULL-capable on a SEAM in the MVP is routed to the Graph owner.
- **If a DESTROYED duct, cable or pipe edge is the subject**: `NOT_REPAIRABLE` [LSG:771]; no part is consumed and no def exists to salvage (edges are structure). The loop is cut for the rest of the run unless the Graph defines a replacement path (Open Questions).
- **If a LOAD hardpoint accepts node kind 13 with no subtype filter**: `mod_heater` and `mod_lamp` have the same port signature, so `compatible_defs` (F-6) offers both and a heater could be installed in a lamp socket. The content lint reports an unfiltered LOAD hardpoint; Layout is asked to declare `accepted_subtypes` (Open Questions).
- **If `mod_door_motor` is FAILED, or unserved**: it "does not hold" [LSG:798]; the door's state follows the Graph and Interaction. The def contributes no heat (`waste_permille` 0) to the thermal baseline, so a dead door motor changes no temperature [LSG:700].
- **If a module with `emits` and the default `lantern_on_states` is FAILED**: it is dark; `{0 OK, 1 DEGRADED}` excludes FAILED [LSG:125]. A dead reactor does not glow, so the room's light never lies about the machine.
- **If a player asks for a patch kit**: no def exists; a PATCH consumes no item [REG:6033; LSG:222]. The phrase "patch kits are the Item DB's finite consumable" [LSG:795] is a wording defect routed to the Graph owner.
- **If `curio_data_core` appears in an authored placement in the MVP**: the lint `PLACEMENT_NO_CONSUMER` warns; the item is carryable but has no consumer until the Knowledge Codex (vertical slice). A carry trip with no payoff would break Pillar 1's honesty.
- **If `salvageable` is false on a def**: the Graph does not enforce it [LSG:185]; Interaction's `begin_cut` `validate` reads it. No MVP def sets it.
- **If `tool_suppressant` reaches 0 charges**: it stays on the hand rail, empty; the MVP roster has no refill, so what the player does about it is Suit's and Attention's (Open Questions).

**Hallucination interplay**

- **If the sprite or inspect card of a salvaged scrubber shows a turning rotor while the module is FAILED (false system state, class b)**: the lie is the module-body channel's; the instance's `condition_bp`, the node's `hp` and the Graph's output are unchanged, and the consequence channel (breath, visor fog, lamp brightness from `served_pm`) stays true [LSG:44]. The def holds no believed value to be corrupted.
- **If a phantom crate is dressed as a data core (class d, vertical slice) and picked up**: the carry body cue reports the junk def's true `mass_g` (a different class by F-10); the player can disprove the lie by weight before the trip home. A junk def whose class equals its lure's fails the lint and is not releasable.
- **If a hallucination shows a crewmate carrying a module as a horror (class c)**: `carry_ref`, the instance and its mass are unchanged; the human channel (voice, beam colour and shape) is never faked [CON:217-219]. The Item DB adds no field a lie could attach to.
- **If a player asks what a held item "really is" under hallucination**: the def's identity is never hidden behind an identify step (Player Fantasy); the only uncertainty is Pillar 4 perception, which has a cheaper falsifier (weight) than the trip home.

**Co-op hooks (later)**

- **If a downed player was carrying a module**: the Controller drops it as a loose item at that position (PCI PC-8); the instance persists as LOOSE_WORLD and a teammate may pick it up. The Item DB holds no ownership of an instance.
- **If two players each need a glowstick or a part**: counts are per player in Suit's block; the Item DB defines the stack cap and nothing about sharing or trading. A tool is unique per player (`stack_max` 1) and a loose duplicate of a held tool is an authoring warning.
- **If a replicated summary (vertical slice B) names an item**: it uses `def_id` for anything persisted and `type_id` only for in-memory summaries; an item never replicates a number from its def (mass, class), which each peer derives from its own identical catalogue.

## Visual/Audio Requirements

*Scope note.* The Item DB is data: it renders nothing and plays nothing. This section states what the player must be able to **see and hear because of the catalogue**, which GDD draws or sounds it, and which art bible sections bind it. No art-director proposal file and no audio proposal exists for this stage (the stage brief lists none), so the art-direction questions are routed to Open Questions rather than invented here. Citations use the keys of the Overview; "ART n.n" is an art bible section.

**What the player sees, and the rule behind it.** (1) *Condition is drawn, never labelled.* A salvaged module shows the wear it came with as scuffs worn to bare metal at rounded corners (Foundry, ART 6.1) and a bolted patch plate that straddles panel lines "on-grid and off-rhythm" after a PATCH (ART 6.1, [ART:617]); permitted materials are cold metals only (ART 6.2, [ART:635]). No bar, number or colour code states condition (IDB-21, G3). (2) *A dead module is a stopped machine.* A FAILED scrubber is dark, its rotor still and no medium moving (ART design principle 2); the catalogue never "refreshes" it (IDB-9, IDB-24), so the sprite family is the same def at a lower `hp`, not a `_dead` variant. (3) *Parts read like the loop they mend*: a duct coupling reads O2, a cable splice reads power, a pipe fitting reads coolant by silhouette and loop hue, hue redundant with shape ([CON:343-345]); the hue is the art-director's token, the shape is the `sprite_family` key. (4) *Tools are silhouettes.* A hand tool is a sprite drawn once per tool, never per dialect (ART 5.1, [ART:512]); its hand-rail glyph is that sprite reduced to the 16 px grid (ART 7.2, [ART:815]), so `glyph_id` names a derived asset, not a hand-made icon. (5) *The carry frame keeps the chest lamp clear*: a carried module rides above the helmet and never covers the chest point (ART 5.1, [ART:512]), and a heavy stowed part (the hull plate) is drawn on the back rig under the same rule; no bare skin, no body shown ([ART:508]). (6) *Sane-palette discipline*: no item, part, tool or glowstick uses flesh or violet, which are reserved for sanity leaks ([CON:333-334]); there is no rarity colour, because there is no rarity (IDB-5, IDB-29). (7) *Footprint*: every def draws in one of the five sprite cells (`M96`, `P48`, `P48x96`, `P96x48`, `T80`; ART 8.2) named by the grammar of ART 8.5.

## Game Feel

*Ownership note.* The catalogue has no input, no animation and no frame data, so the feel of its things is owned elsewhere: the **carry** by the Controller (`V_CARRY`, PC-8), the **body cue** for mass by Suit & Equipment, the **channelled repair, cut and throw** by the Interaction System, the **glowstick light** by Emitter State. What this GDD owns is the *data that makes those feels honest*: a true mass, a true class, a true condition and a finite part. Every target below is therefore a requirement on a neighbour, tagged with the neighbour, and nothing here is a number the Item DB can tune except where a knob is named. Ticks are 60 Hz; steps are 10 Hz.

### Feel Reference

The target is the moment in **Project Zomboid** when a player drags a heavy generator or a stack of planks across a yard: the cost is the **trip**, not a number — slower, louder, hands busy, every sound behind you suddenly relevant — and the thing keeps the damage it already had. Borrow that, and borrow **Pandorum's** hands-free chest lamp: the beam stays on the road while both arms hold the load. The anti-reference is the **encumbrance meter** (a weight bar that gently scales speed) and the **inventory grid**: weight here is never a readout and never a gradient, and nothing is "managed" in a menu. A second anti-reference is the **loot pop** (a glint, a chime, a rarity colour at pickup): picking a thing up is a quiet, physical act whose meaning arrives later, at the install.

### Input Responsiveness

The Item DB adds no input. The item actions below are the Controller's and Interaction's; the row states the feel requirement the catalogue's data imposes on them. Latencies are proposals for those GDDs to adopt or replace (Interaction designed: `interaction-system.md`).

|---|---|---|---|
| `pickup` a module (sets `carry_ref`) | the gait change begins on the next tick | 1 tick | The slowdown must be visible at once; a delayed gait reads as a bug, not weight |

### Weight and Responsiveness Profile

- **Weight**: Heavy and deliberate for a module; unremarkable for a part, tool or glowstick. The weight is expressed as a **gait and a cost in sound and exposure**, never as a number, a bar or a pop-up. A 90 kg reactor cell and a 6 kg data core cost the same per tile (F-13); the body cue (Suit) is where their masses differ. The 30 kg hull plate on the hauler's back rig is the other visible weight: it is seen on the back frame and felt as the same closed-list body cue (IDB-21), never as a number.
- **Player control**: High. Even while carrying, the player can steer, aim the chest lamp and `drop` on any tick; the only things they lose are the run and the other `interact` modes. The load commits the player to a path, not to an animation.
- **Snap quality**: Analog for the burn of a glowstick (a smooth waning), binary for installs and part spending (it is done, or it is not).
- **Acceleration model**: Simulation feel. The carry gait is a flat `V_CARRY` 1 px/tick taken the tick `carry_ref` is set (the Controller's ramp, `RUN_RAMP_TICKS`, applies to run only [PCI:296-319]); the catalogue adds no acceleration term of its own.
- **Failure texture**: Fair and readable. A repair fails for a reason the world shows: the part is missing (the hand rail has none), the subject is DESTROYED (no plate to patch), or the cap is reached (the module runs but remains DEGRADED). A lost module is never a surprise: a dropped or abandoned module is still on the floor with its condition unchanged.

## UI Requirements
