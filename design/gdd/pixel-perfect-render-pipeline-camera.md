## Summary

The Pixel-Perfect Render Pipeline & Camera is the surface every visual system draws onto and the window the player sees through: a 640×360 logical world scaled by a whole number to the display, rendered with nearest sampling and pixel snapping so no pixel ever moves without a cause in the world; a HUD on its own layer that world light, the sanity filter and camera motion can never touch; a draw-order contract that makes a 3/4-oblique ship read correctly; and a camera that is a window, not a sensor — it follows the player and leans where their beam points, responds to nothing the world does, holds still when the ship does, and cuts rather than pans. It exists because the art bible's identity (*What You Light, You Can Read*) and three pillars rest on an image that is faithful to the simulation at the scale of one art pixel, and because the lighting-budget spike that decides the renderer's cost cannot run without the pipeline it measures.

> **Quick reference** — Layer: `Foundation` · Priority: `MVP` · Key deps: `None` upstream (contracts: Command & Simulation Layer CR-10/11; art bible 8.1–8.4, 7.0); 10 downstream dependents

## Overview

The Pixel-Perfect Render Pipeline & Camera is the surface every visual system of The Synaptic Sea draws onto, and the window through which the player sees it. It fixes a **logical resolution of 640×360** scaled by an integer to the player's display (3× at 1080p, 4× at 1440p, 6× at 2160p, 2× on a Steam Deck), renders the world into its own viewport with nearest-neighbour sampling and pixel snapping so that every art pixel is a crisp square of screen pixels, keeps the HUD on a separate root-viewport layer that the world's glow, ambient and occlusion can never touch, and owns the **camera**: what it follows, how it moves, where it may not go, and the integer zoom steps it may take. It also owns the draw-order contract that makes a 3/4-oblique ship read correctly — floors never sort, wall faces and actors sort together by Y, caps draw above actors — and the single full-screen hook point where the Perception Filter is allowed to corrupt what a low-sanity player sees, below the HUD and never touching it.

The player never interacts with this system directly. They experience it as an image that is faithful: the one-pixel wander of a lying needle and the two-pixel identity bands on a crewmate's helmet stay legible because nothing ever shimmers or blurs; the edge of their own light is drawn as Lighting computes it, never softened or extended by a mask; the frame holds still when the ship does, and moves only as the simulation moves.

It exists because the art bible's whole identity rests on it: *What You Light, You Can Read* needs true 2D occlusion (not a vignette), legibility at one art pixel, and a HUD exempt from world light (art bible Principle 1, 7.0, 8.1–8.4), and the lighting-budget spike that decides the renderer's cost (8.12) cannot run without the pipeline it measures. Six systems depend on it directly (2D Lighting & Occlusion, Player Controller & Input, Failure & Trail, Perception Filter, HUD & Suit Layer, Chart Table) and every presentation system inherits its contract: subscribe to the kernel as `PRESENTATION`, read last-committed state, interpolate with `tick_alpha`, write only what is drawn (Command & Simulation Layer CR-10, CR-11). Without it, the game would still simulate correctly and look wrong — which, for a game whose pillar is *The World Never Lies*, is the same as being wrong.

**Design-level only.** `SubViewport` vs root rendering, stretch and snap settings, whether lights render at base or native resolution (the spike's pass A/B), `Camera2D` smoothing vs manual interpolation — these are implementation choices → **becomes an ADR** (render pipeline / tick-source ADRs). *(Framing: technical infrastructure, no ADR yet to cite, fantasy felt indirectly — the recommended options for a `Core`/`Foundation` system under `autonomous` automation.)*

## Player Fantasy

*Indirect — the player never meets this system; they meet a stillness it keeps.*

**When the ship is still, the screen is still.** The game teaches the player to read motion as evidence. A needle that shakes a pixel is a stressed hull warning them. A needle that glides slowly with nothing in the ship to explain it is lying. The four trim bands on a crewmate's helmet are who that is. All of this lives at the scale of one or two art pixels, so it only works if no pixel ever moves for a reason that isn't in the world: no shimmer from a non-integer scale, no camera crawl from rounding, no actor jittering a pixel against the floor as the camera follows. Every pixel that changes was changed by something in the world.

The anchor moment is on the first sortie. The player stands in a dead scrubber bay with their beam on a wall gauge. They stop walking and the frame stops with them. Then the needle moves. Because nothing else on screen moved, the player knows the motion belongs to the ship, or to their own mind, and not to the screen. They lean in to see whether it shakes or glides, and that judgement is the game. The comfort is structural: *if I saw it move, something moved it.*

**Pillars served.** *Dread Before Chaos*: "Every breach is preceded by a tell a careful player could have perceived (sound, light, gauge drift, hull stress)" — gauge drift is perceivable only if render noise can neither fake it nor hide it. *Sanity Rewrites Reality*: "the lie extends from the gauge to the sprite" — the art bible's settle/tremor/glide grammar (7.3) is how a player tells the lie from the warning, and a pipeline that adds its own motion corrupts both. *The World Never Lies*: the rendering stack is neither instrument nor perception, so it may not be wrong either. *The Web Watches*: filament movement at ≤ 1 art px/s (art bible 2.11) is readable only on a grid that does not crawl.

**Design tests.**
- If we are debating a presentation effect that moves world pixels with no cause in the world (sub-pixel smoothing, eased camera lag that overshoots, non-integer zoom, a softening filter, screen shake) against holding the grid: hold the grid, or move the effect into the world (a needle, a sprite, a light).
- The fantasy has failed if a player can see a one-art-pixel movement that no system put there. Two cases count: a static scene, and a followed actor that shifts relative to the floor while the camera tracks it. That is a counterfeit tremor or a counterfeit glide — a Pillar 2 and Pillar 4 violation, not a cosmetic bug. It is testable on day one with a frame-diff: hold the sim with no committed change and the camera target still, and the only world pixels allowed to differ between frames are those owned by a declared animating source (flicker, scrolling medium, web drift); then walk an actor across a static room, and the actor-to-floor offset may change only by whole art pixels in step with committed position.

*What this section does not claim.* The pipeline does not make the world honest; the kernel does. It does not make motion meaningful; the art bible's grammar does. It does not make a reading true; the HUD frame stays put and undimmed (HR-1), but the needle inside it may lie under class (b). It does not own the edge of the light — Lighting and Principle 1 do; the pipeline's part is only that no mask, vignette, fog layer or edge-darkening ever sits between a lit world pixel and the screen, which is a prohibition in Core Rules, not a fantasy. The image is up to one tick behind committed state and never ahead of it (kernel `tick_alpha` clamps at 1). Under lighting pass A the cone edge is a 3-px stair-step at 1080p, and this section does not promise a smooth one. *(Alternatives considered: "the edge belongs to the light" — evocative but credits the pipeline with Lighting's guarantee; "a porthole, not a lens" — accurate camera policy, but policy is not a moment the player feels.)*

**Downstream implications carried into Section C and Game Feel.** Any whole-frame shake counterfeits a tremor, the honest Tell of 2.7: hull stress goes into real needles, dust direction and sprites, not into the frame, and whether any shake survives (e.g. breach impact after the tell has landed) is decided explicitly against design test 1. The commonest way this fantasy fails is the followed actor and the world rounding to different integers in the same frame, so QA tests motion during follow, not only a still scene. The HUD exemption is anchoring, not truth.

## Detailed Design

### Core Rules

*Specialists consulted: systems-designer (primary), engine-programmer, gameplay-programmer, game-designer, ux-designer. Disagreements resolved and logged 2026-10-26; items the specialists could not resolve are in Open Questions.*


**Vocabulary.** *Base px* (*art px*): one pixel of the 640×360 logical grid. *Screen px*: one pixel of the window. *k*: the world's integer scale (screen px per base px). *H*: the HUD's integer scale, independent of k. *Picture*: the region of the window that shows the game (the window unless letterboxed). *World viewport*: the `SubViewport` holding every lit thing; its canvas **is the current body's frame** (PR-6 C2). *Body*: a Ship Body & Interior Physics object with its own reference frame (home-ship, derelict, pod, detached section). *Sort origin*: the base-px row an object sorts by on the Y-sorted layer. *Pose*: the camera's output — integer base-px position in the body frame plus the current k. *`present_time`*: the pipeline's render-rate clock (PR-9). *Tick*, *`tick_alpha`*, *read view*, *`PRESENTATION`*: as the kernel GDD defines them (CR-10, CR-11, D.3).

---

**PR-1. Logical resolution and integer scale.** The world is authored at **640×360 base px** (art bible 8.1, decided) and drawn at an integer scale `k = floor(min(W / 640, H / 360))` where `W × H` is the window's **pixel** size — never derived from DPI; `content_scale_factor` stays 1.0 and `allow_hidpi` behaviour on the Deck and on Windows ≥ 125 % scaling is logged by spike step 11 (→ D for the variable table). The **minimum window is 1280×720** (C-R; `Window.min_size` `[BIN]`), so `k ≥ 2` on every display. Nothing in the world is ever drawn at a non-integer scale; the only non-integer text anywhere is the vector reading face on the chart-table layer (7.1). At every window size the player sees square, unblurred base pixels; the trade is the picture size, never pixel integrity. The player may choose k below the automatic value in Settings ("integer world scale: Auto + list", PR-14) — see PR-2 for why a manual k forces `keep`.

*Rationale.* 640×360 is the only common base that lands on an integer at 720p, 1080p, 1440p and 2160p (8.1's table), so the four sizes the bible calls common need no policy at all; PR-2 exists for everything else. *Alternative rejected:* fractional scaling with a sharpening filter — it breaks 8.0's single grid and makes the 1 px contour, the 2 px bands and the 2 px fringe uneven widths, which 5.6's minimum-feature rule forbids. *Engine fact that makes this rule load-bearing:* `project.godot` today has no `[display]` block and no `rendering/textures` or `rendering/2d/snap` keys, so the project currently runs at the engine built-ins — 1152×648, stretch `disabled`, `fractional`, LINEAR-filtered, unsnapped `[BIN]` — and looks "almost right" in the editor at 1×. PR-15 is the guard.

**PR-2. Aspect: `keep` for the spike, `expand` as the IDEAL, both bounded; bars are true black.**

- **`keep` (letterbox).** The picture is `640k × 360k`, centred; the remainder is bars. This is the spike architecture (8.4) and the **default until 8.12 step 2 and step 11 have run**, because the world viewport is a fixed 640×360 and nothing resizes.
- **`expand` (IDEAL, 8.1/8.10, post-spike).** The world viewport's logical size becomes `(floor(W/k), floor(H/k))`, clamped to **≤ 960×540** at default zoom (PROVISIONAL cap = 1.5× base; → D), remainder letterboxed; the base size and k both stay integer at every window (C-R). The camera stays centred on the same target, so an ultrawide player sees more void left and right and a Deck player 20 more rows above and below.
- **Manual k forces `keep`.** A player-chosen k below the automatic value under `expand` would be a permanently wider frame (at 1080p, k = 2 gives 960×540 — exactly the zoom-out view, as the default), which the game-designer's decision forbids ("never a wider default frame"; the frame is changed only by the player's `zoom_in` / `zoom_out` steps, PR-6 C6 *(Amended 2026-10-26 — consistency check)*). So when `k < k_auto`, aspect is `keep` regardless of the setting. *(Systems-designer resolution of a conflict between the Settings list and the camera decision — flagged for the ux-designer and game-designer to confirm.)*

*The honesty caveat on "more void, not more information" (8.1).* The claim holds in the dark states (2.4–2.8), where everything outside the beam is black whichever width the frame is. It does **not** fully hold in two cases: (i) at 3440×1440 under `expand` the half-width is ≈ 9 tiles, so the narrow cone's 8-tile reach (8.3) fits entirely on screen where a 16:9 player's is clipped at 6.7 tiles; (ii) in the home-ship stable state (2.3) every fixture is on and a wider frame shows more lit ship. A 1600×900 player under `expand` sees 25 % more in each axis than a 1080p player. The cap bounds the gap; it does not remove it. **Routed to creative-director** with the recommendation to accept `expand` (a third of the screen black at 1600×900 is a worse experience than a slightly wider frame in a co-op horror game with no competitive stake) and to record the gap in Edge Cases. Second cost: under pass B (PR-7) light is paid per lit screen pixel — 4.95 M px at 3440×1440 against 2.07 M at 1080p; under pass A the cost grows only to 860×360 base px. The spike measures pass B at 1080p only; `expand` under pass B is a post-spike measurement.

*Bars.* Letterbox bars are **true black, outside the world `SubViewport`**, untouched by the Perception Filter pass and by any camera jolt (C-R). Because the void inside the world is also near-black (0–1 % ambient, 2.5), a bar could be mistaken for unlit hull: the ux-designer recommends a **1-HUD-px Graphite hairline at the picture edge** — routed to art-director (it is a UI mark, 4.6 palette). What the engine actually draws in the remainder under `keep` is NOT SOURCEABLE (step 11).

*Deck safe area (PROVISIONAL).* The HUD is authored **anchor-relative to the visible picture rect**, never the window, with the 7.0 safe margin of 5 % of the picture — 32 × 18 base px at 640×360, 32 × 20 at 640×400 — and must survive a fractional HUD canvas (the picture divided by H is not always integer) and both aspects (C-R). The Deck has no notch; the margin is ergonomic and the ux-designer may raise it per device. Deck *text* size is PR-12's problem.

*Fullscreen.* Default `MODE_FULLSCREEN` (borderless window covering the screen, `Window.mode = 3` `[BIN]`) rather than `EXCLUSIVE_FULLSCREEN` for Alt-Tab safety `[UNV]`; any mode or size change fires `Viewport.size_changed` `[BIN]` and is handled as one `RESCALING` frame (C.2) by **one pipeline-root node that is the only writer** of `SubViewport.size`, `size_2d_override`, HUD anchors and camera limits (→ ADR, with PR-7's wiring).

**PR-3. The layer model.** One root viewport, one world viewport, and `CanvasLayer`s above it. The question that assigns a thing to a layer is 7.0's: *who produced the information?* — the ship goes in the world and must be lit to read; the suit and the chart table are screen-space and never lit. **The HUD is in a different viewport, not merely a different layer** (engine-programmer): world lights, glow, `CanvasModulate`, the Filter pass on the container and the camera transform cannot reach it by construction, so HR-1, HR-6 and HR-13 are promised structurally. The trade-off is that nothing that must *mix* (a diegetic element lit by the world) may live there — it goes in the world.

| # | Layer (bottom → top) | Where | Scaled by | Lit / glow / ambient / occlusion | Perception Filter | Camera jolt | Contents |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | **World** | `SubViewport` inside a `SubViewportContainer` | k | **yes** — the only layer that is (`use_hdr_2d`, `use_debanding`, filter, snap set on this viewport's own members `[BIN]`; never on the root, which stays SDR-exact for the 4.6 palette) | per-node effects inside; the full-screen pass sits on the container (PR-10) | yes (bounded, PR-6 C7) | floors, floor marks, the Y-sorted layer, caps, lights, occluders, fringe, dust, horrors, web, in-world instruments, the camera |
| 1 | **Transit / load hold** | `CanvasLayer` | — | no | no | no | one opaque black quad, shown only in `TRANSIT_HOLD` and `BOOTING/LOADING` (C.2) |
| 2 | **Suit layer (HUD)** | `CanvasLayer` | H | **no** (HR-1 by construction) | no — readings are fed *perceived values* by the Filter as data; the frame is fed nothing (7.6 hand-off) | **never** (HR-13); prompts use the unjolted transform (C11) | critical strip, suit panel, hand rail, crew strip, interaction prompts, inspect card |
| 3 | **Chart table / menus** | `CanvasLayer` | H (bitmap face), continuous (reading face) | no | no | no | pause panel over the frozen world (7.4), web map, settings, text chat |
| 4 | **Debug overlay** | `CanvasLayer`, dev builds only | H | no | **no — it reads the true channel** | no | DIAGNOSTIC surface (C.3); may toggle debug draws *inside* the world viewport (note e) |

Rules that follow: (a) **glow is per viewport** (8.11 flag 3; 4.6 reworked glow to run before tonemapping `[REF]`, so no older tuning transfers and `glow_hdr_threshold` is re-found at 8.12 step 8). (b) The ambient-floor hull material (8.11 flag 1) and the HR-15 low-vision raise are **Lighting's**, applied inside layer 0; the pipeline owns no ambient value and **no full-frame gamma or brightness control exists anywhere** (C-R): the only brightness control the game has is HR-15's bounded hull-material step, which can never reveal web, horrors or filaments and never touches the HUD. (c) In-world instruments are layer-0 lit sprites; the inspect card (7.0) is layer 2 — the same reading drawn twice, once lit and once not. (d) Interaction prompts are layer 2 and are *gated* by the Emitter State exposure query, never by the renderer. (e) Debug draws inside the world (occluder outlines, sort origins, the look-ahead box, the pixel grid, the camera limits) are children of a debug node in layer 0 that exists only in dev builds; `draw_line` there needs explicit width ≥ 1 base px because 4.7 lines have no feather `[REF]`. (f) **NEAREST is set in three places with two differently numbered enums `[BIN]`:** the project setting `rendering/textures/canvas_textures/default_texture_filter = 0` and the world `SubViewport.canvas_item_default_texture_filter = 0` use `Viewport.DEFAULT_CANVAS_ITEM_TEXTURE_FILTER_NEAREST = 0`; the `SubViewportContainer.texture_filter` (a `CanvasItem` member, default `0 = PARENT_NODE`) must be set to `CanvasItem.TEXTURE_FILTER_NEAREST = 1`, or pass A's ×k upscale is bilinear-blurred `[UNV]`. Whether project settings propagate into a `SubViewport` is NOT SOURCEABLE; the startup assertion (PR-15) reads every member back. (g) `SubViewport.size` defaults to 512×512 and `TileSet.tile_size` to 16×16 `[BIN]` — both are set explicitly (640×360 / 48×48); a scene that forgets gets silently wrong output.

*Alternative rejected:* HUD inside the world viewport with `LIGHT_MODE_UNSHADED` materials — unshaded items still receive glow and the full-screen Filter pass, so HR-1 would be a per-node discipline that one forgotten material breaks (8.11 flag 3 made this call; recorded here because the pipeline inherits it).

---

**PR-4. Pixel integrity.** Inside the world viewport:

1. **Nearest sampling everywhere** (`light_` textures excepted — sampled by the light, not the item, 8.4). Mipmaps off on every drawn sheet (8.7).
2. **Integer base-px placement by presentation code — the viewport snap flags are a safety net, not the mechanism** (gameplay-programmer, engine-programmer). Every presentation node computes `pos = floor(prev + (curr − prev) × tick_alpha)` in base px **in the current body frame** and writes that integer; `snap_2d_transforms_to_pixel` and `snap_2d_vertices_to_pixel` are on as belt-and-braces (what they snap to under pass B — base px or render px — is NOT SOURCEABLE; step 12 checks for double-rounding flicker at .5 boundaries). `floor` is the one rounding function used everywhere, including the camera and the beam's chest point (which uses the same rounded position as the sprite, so light origin and sprite never disagree). Because the world canvas *is* the body frame (PR-6 C2), no fractional body offset ever enters an interior position; other bodies drawn in this frame (a docked derelict, a detaching section) are placed by one floored offset shared by all their items, so their items never swim against each other.
3. **Interpolate, then quantise.** Rounding before interpolating produces 1 px stalls; interpolating after rounding is impossible. Consequence to state honestly (engine-programmer): **snap is a floor, not a filter** — interpolation changes only the *timing* of one-base-px steps, never their size. A body moving 1 base px per tick shows a k×k screen block step whenever it moves; the promise is "base-pixel steps at interpolated timing, never twice in a frame, never backward", not sub-pixel smoothness. The Player Controller should therefore choose walk and run speeds in **whole base px per tick** when the camera quantum is 1 (PR-6 C3) — routed.
4. **Scale.** Only integer scales, only k (or the zoom step). Horizontal flips are permitted (mirrored facings, 5.6). No per-node scale animation of any kind; `Control.scale` stays `(1,1)` on the HUD. `Camera2D.zoom` stays `(1,1)` **always** (there is no pixel-snap member on `Camera2D` — confirmed by the exhaustive `[BIN]` member list — so zoom is a change of k, PR-6 C6).
5. **Rotation.** `Camera2D.rotation = 0` always, forever (game-designer; also `ignore_rotation = true` default `[BIN]`). Human-built geometry — floors, walls, caps, doors, props, machinery, pipes, seams, characters, tools, overlays, HUD — is **never rotated at runtime** (8.4): a lint asserts `rotation == 0` on every node under the environment, pipe, prop and actor groups. Rotation is permitted for **web pieces** (off-grid by design, 3.3), **horror rig segments** (quantised to 15°, stepped at 12 fps = every 5th tick, 5.4/8.10 — a test), and **lights** (engine light lives off the grid by 8.0's rule). What "rotated segments fall off the grid" means per pass: under pass A a rotated segment is rasterised at base resolution — a stair-step of whole base px, on the grid; under pass B it is rasterised at window resolution with nearest sampling — angled k×k blocks, same density, **off** the grid. The second is tolerated for horrors and web only (8.4); if it fails review the remedies are 8.10's (24-angle pre-rotated sets, or a base-resolution sub-viewport for horrors alone → ADR). `snap_2d_vertices_to_pixel`'s effect on a rotated quad's corners is NOT SOURCEABLE — step 2 judges the rotating segment. Sub-pixel blur is forbidden in every case.
6. **Half-pixel origins.** `Sprite2D.centered = true` by default `[BIN]`: an odd-dimension texture centred puts its origin on a .5 and wobbles 1 px under NEAREST. 8.2's cells are even (48, 80, 96) and the foot-centre origin is set via `offset`, so the asset standard satisfies this; the rule for VFX and one-off sprites is **even cells, or `centered = false` with an integer `offset`**.
7. **No sub-pixel sprite positions** means no item is ever drawn at a fractional base-px offset *relative to the grid the other items are on*. Whether the whole grid may translate by screen pixels is the camera quantum's question (PR-6 C3), not this rule's.
8. **One interpolator.** The kernel's `tick_alpha` with previous/current read views is the only interpolator of sim-owned motion: `physics/common/physics_interpolation = false`, every presentation node `physics_interpolation_mode = OFF`, `Camera2D.position_smoothing_enabled = false`, `limit_smoothed = false` `[BIN]`. Decisive `[BIN]` reason beyond "two clocks": `get_global_transform_interpolated()` exists on `Node3D` only, so no 2D script could read where an engine-interpolated body is actually drawn — the camera would follow the un-interpolated pose and swim 1–3 render px against the sprite at 144 fps while looking fine at 60. The 2D interpolation machinery does exist in 4.7.2 `[BIN]` (items, lights, occluders); its behaviour is NOT SOURCEABLE and it is simply not used for sim-owned transforms. → ADR (interpolation clock). Whether pure-physics debris may use it is Ship Body's.
9. **Light masks.** `Light2D.range_item_cull_mask` and `CanvasItem.light_mask` default to bit 1 `[BIN]`; the unshaded emissive overlay and the fringe sprites go on a light-mask bit the beams do not light (6.2 "unshaded") — behaviour `[UNV]`, checked at 8.12 step 4. The HUD needs none; it is in another viewport.
10. **Lines are sprites.** Hairlines, filaments and seams are sprites, never runtime line draws (8.4, `[REF]`).

**PR-5. Projection convention and the draw-order contract (3/4 oblique).** The projection is 1:1 with no foreshortening (8.2): one base px of floor depth, one of width and one of height are each one screen row or column. Floors are 48×48 squares. A wall has a **24 px footprint** on the floor grid, a **72 px front face** and a **24 px top cap**, occupying 96 screen rows but half a tile of floor.

**The convention this contract requires (flagged — blocking for every dependent that sorts).** Art bible 8.2 says the footprint "is drawn as its 24 px top cap, with its 72 px front face hanging below the cap", and separately that "front faces occlude what lies screen-south of their footprint". Taken literally — cap drawn on the footprint rows, face hanging down over the floor to the south — no Y-sort rule can work with a 24-px collision footprint: an actor walking north toward that wall is either drawn floating partway up the face (if sorted by feet against the footprint's south edge) or hidden behind a wall they are physically in front of (if sorted against the face's bottom row), because the face covers 72 rows of *walkable* floor south of the wall. The convention that keeps every number in 8.2 (24 / 72 / 24 / 96, "5.5 tiles of floor with a back wall") and makes sorting consistent is the standard one: **vertical height projects up-screen.** The front face rises from the footprint's south edge (its floor-contact line) 72 rows up-screen; the cap sits above the face. A wall whose footprint is rows `[y0, y0+24)` occupies screen rows `[y0−72, y0+24)` and **occludes what lies screen-north (behind) of its footprint** — the floor of the room behind it, for 1.5 tiles. This changes one clause of 8.2 (screen-south → screen-north) and is **routed to art-director and technical-artist as a spike-room item**: 8.12 step 2 builds a 20×11 room and meets this on day one; the engine-programmer's step-5 test ("a crewmate standing just south of a wall is drawn over its face; just north, behind it") is exactly this convention's acceptance test. *Kit consequence, routed to level-designer:* under this convention a room's own south wall covers 1.5 tiles of the room's floor along its south edge and an actor standing there is hidden — kits need low or cut-away south walls, south-open rooms, or a cap-only south-wall variant, the ordinary 3/4-oblique remedy. If the art-director instead confirms the literal reading, the collision footprint must grow to the full 96 rows from the south (the floor under the face is not walkable), the sort origins below stand unchanged, and 8.2's "only half a tile of floor" and its room arithmetic become false.

**Sort origin.** Every object on the Y-sorted layer sorts by its **floor-contact line**: the foot row for actors and horrors (the cell origin of 8.6), the south edge of the footprint for walls, doors, props and machinery nodes — so the wall-face `TileMapLayer.y_sort_origin` `[BIN]` is set to the footprint depth, not 0. Anything **mounted on a wall face** — pipes, fittings, seams, wall gauges, the emissive `_msk` overlay, wall-mounted trails — takes **its host face's sort origin** and draws after the face by tree order. Ties (equal row) break by x ascending, then creation order (→ ADR: Godot's own tie rule is tree order `[UNV]` and may need a deterministic wrapper). Sort origins are **integer base px** — an interpolated actor's sort origin is its floored render row, so an actor and a wall never swap order on a sub-pixel difference.

**The per-row z-band fallback (8.6).** If the Y-sorted layer exceeds ≈ 300 draws at the spike (8.12 step 5 — "Y-sort breaks quadrant batching" is `[UNV]` and the 50–300 figure NOT SOURCEABLE until then), wall faces and their wall-mounted layer leave the Y-sort node and are drawn by **row band**: each face tile gets `z_index = BASE + floor((sort_origin − cam_top) / BAND_H)` with `BAND_H = 24` base px (= the wall footprint depth, so a wall is always alone in its band), and every Y-sorted item gets the same formula from its own sort origin, with Y-sort still active *within* a band node. 360 rows / 24 = 15 bands visible plus two margin bands; bands are recomputed per frame relative to the camera top so `z_index` stays far inside `CANVAS_ITEM_Z_MIN/MAX = ±4096` and the lights' ±1024 `[BIN]`. *Alternatives rejected.* Sorting everything (floors included) — floors would then need a sort origin per tile and break batching for no visual gain. A depth buffer / 2.5D approach — not what 2D lighting and occluders expect, and 8.0 says the engine decides how things look, not how they are stacked. Pipes on their own unsorted band — see the Y-sort row.

**PR-6. The camera contract.**

**C1. The camera is a window, not a sensor (game-designer, decided).** The camera responds **only to player-owned inputs** — the avatar's position, the beam aim, the player's `zoom_in` / `zoom_out` steps *(Amended 2026-10-26 — consistency check)*, the vehicle the player is in — and **never to world state**: not Attention, breach phase, horrors, sound, the graph, or sanity. The invariant is testable: **an identical input trace produces an identical camera trace in calm, tell and breach** (acceptance test). The camera's framing — position, zoom, rotation, offset — is a **never-shift anchor** in the sense of art bible §1's Corollary: the Perception Filter may not write any camera or viewport property (C-R; the anchor-list addition is routed to the art bible §1 Corollary / 2.9 and to the Perception Filter GDD). The never-list (C9) follows from this.

**C2. Frame: the world canvas is the current body's frame, interior at origin.** The interior scene of the body the avatar is on sits at the origin of the world viewport's canvas; **ship motion through the void never appears in the interior view** — it appears only on exterior or transit layers (`Parallax2D`/`ParallaxBackground` exist `[BIN]`), and **ship rotation is never rendered inside** (gameplay-programmer, engine-programmer option (a); routed to the Ship Body GDD). Consequences: (i) the render rotation of every human-built body is 0 and two docked bodies are both axis-aligned — a hard constraint exported to Ship Body (→ ADR there: `lock_rotation`, or rotate in simulation while presenting in the body frame); (ii) **detached sections translate without rotation in the interior view** (routed to Modular Ship Assembly); (iii) all pixel snapping is in the body frame (C-R), which PR-4 rule 2 already requires; (iv) the pod in transit is its own body — the camera is pod-local and the pod moves at constant velocity against the exterior layer (gameplay-programmer). The follow target is the local seat's controlled entity (kernel binding table, CR-7); **no shared camera in co-op** — every client has its own window. **The camera is a top-level presentation node, never a child of the body** (engine-programmer): `Camera2D.process_callback` is IDLE by default `[BIN]`, a parented camera would inherit the body's transform on the engine's clock and (without `ignore_rotation`) its rotation, and no 2D API exposes an interpolated global transform; the camera script composes the avatar's interpolated body-local position exactly as the actor's presentation node does, with `Node.process_priority` ordering it after the actor (behaviour `[UNV]`, step 12). If the two compose differently the camera lags the sprite by up to a tick — step 12's zero-render-px-disagreement test.

**C3. The solver: a pure function, first-order smoothing, quantised after.** The camera is a `CameraSolver` — a pure `RefCounted` `solve(input, state, params) → pose` with no scene-tree dependency, headless-testable, deterministic, and **identical at 60 and 144 fps for the same wall time** (gameplay-programmer; → ADR for the node that hosts it). Its follow is a **first-order exponential approach** `cam += (target − cam) × (1 − exp(−dt / τ_follow))` with `τ_follow ≈ 0.18 s` — zero overshoot by construction, so the camera **never reverses direction within 100 ms unless the player did** (C-R). **No dead-zone** (knob `DEAD_ZONE`, default 0 × 0, kept for A/B). The crewmate is confined to an **inner box ≈ 0.60 of the frame** (half-extents 0.30 × frame = ±192 × ±108 base px about centre at 640×360): follow lag plus look-ahead may never push the avatar outside it. The pose is **quantised after smoothing** to the camera quantum `QUANTUM ∈ {1 base px, 1/k base px}`; **MVP ships on 1** (whole base px in the body frame — C-R), and the `1/k` path — render the world viewport 2 base px larger with the camera at `floor(cam) − (1,1)` and offset the `SubViewportContainer` by `−round((1 + frac(cam)) × k)` screen px, so the picture scrolls in screen-pixel steps while every item stays on one shared base grid — is **built as the upgrade if testers name the stutter** (gameplay-programmer). The `1/k` path costs 2 px overdraw, a container clipped to the picture rect (bars stay untouched), root snap off for the container's fractional root position (shares PR-7's wiring ADR), its pass-B behaviour is `[UNV]`, and it needs **art-director sign-off against 8.0** (the grid translates; its contents do not) **and ux-designer sign-off against C-R "camera moves in whole base px in its body frame"**, which the `1/k` path relaxes to whole screen px. *Systems-designer's recorded risk, for step 12:* with exponential follow and camera and avatar floored independently, the avatar's screen position wobbles ±1 px whenever their fractional parts cross — the one artefact 8.0's grid makes most visible, on the one sprite always on screen. A rigid follow (camera = avatar + offset, smoothing only on the offset) avoids it at the cost of the world scrolling in the avatar's cadence; the A/B is `τ_follow` = 0.18 vs 0 with `DEAD_ZONE` = 0, measured in step 12 as camera-vs-sprite jitter in render px.

**C4. Look-ahead toward the beam aim.** The frame's half-height is 180 base px = 3.75 tiles against a 6–8-tile beam (8.3): a north-facing wide cone is clipped at under 4 of its 6 tiles, a narrow cone at under half; a south-facing beam runs into the critical strip (7.0's accepted cost). The remedy is a bounded offset **toward the beam aim — not the facing** (game-designer and gameplay-programmer agree), read from the **local raw aim**, not the coalesced intent command (so it carries no tick latency and no quantisation), eased with `τ_lookahead ≈ 0.40 s`, and bounded at `L_MAX = 72` base px = 1.5 tiles (**default**; safe range 1.0–2.5 tiles = 48–120 px; the gameplay-programmer argued for 2.0 tiles = 96 px because the vertical clip is the real readability problem and 96 px shows 5.75 of a north beam's 6 tiles — recorded; the game-designer's bound is the one that fits C3's inner box: 72 px look-ahead plus the 32.4 px steady-state follow lag at `v_max` (`v_max·τ_follow` = 180 × 0.18, Player Controller GDD) is 104.4 px, inside the ±108 vertical half-extent, whereas a 96 px look-ahead plus 32.4 px = 128.4 would exceed it; at other ladder steps the look-ahead bound and the solver's other length parameters scale with the visible field, `L_MAX_eff = L_MAX × field_h / 360` (D.7), so the same margin holds at every zoom; the inner-box clamp applies to the final pose while the lag cut (`LAG_CUT` 48 px) reads the unclamped follow term — see the Section D amendment) *(Amended 2026-10-26 — consistency check)*. The offset is quantised with the pose. **Reduced motion disables look-ahead** (`L_MAX → 0`; C-R) — tremor, hairlines and audio carry the information instead.

**C5. Pan-speed ceiling and the cut rule (ux-designer, decided).** The camera's pan speed is ceilinged at **1.25 × `v_max`** (the avatar's fastest locomotion; amended from 1.25× walk — see the Section D amendment); if the follow term would exceed it for `T_PAN` = 0.10 s (6 frames at 60 fps), or the follow lag exceeds **`LAG_CUT` = 48 px**, the solver **cuts** (state reset to the target) rather than fast-panning; a **single-frame target jump ≥ 48 base px** (one tile) cuts immediately. This covers the kernel's catch-up residual — up to `MAX_TICKS_PER_FRAME` ticks in one frame (CR-17) can move the avatar several px at once and is **not** fully forgiven by interpolation; the cut rule is what makes it never a fast pan. The camera **teleports (full solver state reset)** on every `LOADING → RUNNING` and on **every body transfer** (gameplay-programmer, ux-designer); where the new body frame places the avatar at the same screen position the cut is invisible, otherwise it is a cut, never a pan. **Camera state is never persisted**; after a restore the solver starts from the target. Because the solver interpolates from previous/current, every owner of a moving read view must set `previous := current` in `post_restore` so the first frame after a load does not interpolate from a stale pose — routed to the kernel (CR-10 / CR-8 `post_restore`) and Save/Load.

**C6. Zoom: integer ladder in both directions, persistent, cut not tween.** `Camera2D.zoom` stays `(1,1)`; "zoom" is a change of the world's integer scale: press `zoom_in` / `zoom_out` (edge-triggered; one step per press; persistent — never held — and reset to default on re-wake) to step `k` through the integer ladder `[1, k_auto + 2]` (user decision 2026-10-26, supersedes the game-designer's single held step out); it is available in every state including breach (it is a player input, C1). At 1080p the ladder is 1×, 2×, **3× default**, 4×, 5× (sprites at 33 / 67 / 100 / 133 / 167 %); 8.1's "one integer step out" is the first `zoom_out` press (amendment routed to art-director). C-R's "no zoom-out below a 2× world" is **superseded**: on a 2× world (720p, Steam Deck, manual 2×) the ladder reaches 1× (decided 2026-10-26, creative director concurs, user ratification pending — Open Question 4; on-device acuity check routed to the accessibility-specialist). The logical size at a ladder step `k_z` is `picture / k_z`, **uncapped** (PR-2's cap applies to default zoom only; at 1440p one step out is 1280×720 logical, and letterboxing it would defeat the step); at 1080p one step out is 960×540 = 20 × 11.25 tiles, so the 8 × 6 machine room of 8.2 fits, and 1× is 1920×1080 base = 40 × 22.5 tiles. The solver's length parameters scale with the field (`s = field_h / 360`, D.6–D.10) so the camera feels the same at every step. The change is a **cut**: one frame at the old k, the next at the new, no intermediate scale (none is integer), no zoom pulse. The HUD does not change (PR-12). Zoom-in **is** offered as integer steps up to `k_auto + 2` (user decision 2026-10-26 — see the zoom-ladder note; the tight default frame remains the default, not the only frame). Every step is implemented by changing the world viewport's **logical size** (pass A: `size`; pass B: `size_2d_override`), never `Camera2D.zoom` — which shares the unresolved wiring question of PR-7 (two integer scales in one window under one root stretch) and is folded into spike step 11. **"Across the room" readability** (Pillar 2's frosting elbow seen to worsen from across the room) is satisfied by trail propagation into frame, look-ahead, wide-and-shallow kits (8.2) and the player's zoom steps — **never by a wider default frame** (game-designer). *Playtest metric:* the share of boarding time spent away from default zoom, in either direction; **> 50 % → revisit the default frame** (routed to game-designer and the playtest plan). *(Amended 2026-10-26 — consistency check: held-step wording replaced by the edge-triggered ladder throughout.)*

**C7. Jolt (shake): a bounded knob, default 0.** No camera shake in MVP (game-designer). A **physical-jolt knob** exists for VS-A revisiting: an integer base-px offset of at most `JOLT_MAX = 2`, bounded duration, applied **as a `Camera2D.offset` inside the world `SubViewport`, never as a container offset** (C-R — so bars and HUD are untouched by construction); caused **only by P1 physical events** the player's body is subject to (hull section separation, docking contact, a detach (the single trigger list; hull impact and vent bursts are deferred to the VS-A decision, Open Question 14)) and **never by Attention, Sanity, the Tell or breach** (C1; 2.9: sanity is never read off the screen's energy; 2.8: breach is more things moving, not the screen fighting the player). Default 0 at MVP; **reduced motion sets it to 0** regardless. World-anchored HUD prompts use the **unjolted** camera transform (C11).

**C8. Limits.** `Camera2D.limit_*` are `int` base px `[BIN]` and, with the world in body-local coordinates, are the loaded body's bounding rect **set by a `PROJECTOR` when sections weld or detach** (kernel CR-10 class), never by the camera itself; `limit_enabled` with no authored rect means the engine defaults (±10 000 000) — effectively none, and the void beyond a hull is honest black. When an authored rect clamps the pose on an axis the camera is `CLAMPED` (C.2). Bounds are the only "fixed frame" the pipeline offers; there is **no cinematic takeover** of any kind.

**C9. Never on the world camera** (game-designer, ux-designer): rotation; non-integer zoom; a zoom pulse or "FOV punch"; blur; chromatic aberration; vignette; uncaused drift; free-look or peek; auto-aim or any aim assist expressed through framing; a revealing pan; a cinematic cut or hold; a minimap or edge marker (prompts anchored to objects **never clamp to the frame edge** — an off-screen object has no prompt); a shared camera in co-op; any write by the Perception Filter or by Attention/Horror/Sanity presentation.

**C10. Transit, crew loss, re-wake.**
- *Pod transit.* The hop is a one-tick data change followed by an `ARRIVING` phase in which a `PROJECTOR` builds the destination (kernel C.3 #8). Hop `Applied` → pipeline enters `TRANSIT_HOLD` (layer 1 black, HUD stays — HR-3) → if Web Map & Transit presents an exterior leg (2.5), **cut** to the pod body and follow it with the ordinary solver, pod-local, constant velocity — **no cinematic cuts within the leg** (game-designer: "the camera follows the pod exactly as the crewmate") → black → destination ready → **cut** to the avatar on the derelict. The two hop boundaries are body transfers and therefore cuts (C5) through black; the pipeline never fades the *world's light* — a world fade is a mask (Principle 1); the plate is above the world and below the HUD, and it is the void. Fade to black `120 ms` out, cut in (PROVISIONAL, 7.3's menu-fade range; game-designer and art-director own the feel).
- *Breach.* **No camera change** of any kind (C1).
- *Crew loss and re-wake.* 2.10's sources going out one by one is Lighting reading state; the camera **holds exactly where it was until the hard cut** (no drift, no pull-in); on `LOADING` the pipeline shows layer 1 black; on `RUNNING` it cuts to the new avatar under the cryo-bay lamp with **framing identical to the first wake — snap, not ease** (game-designer; 2.2: "I know this room" before "something has changed").

**C11. `world_to_hud(pos)`.** The pipeline publishes one projection from body-space position to HUD-layer coordinates — accounting for k, H, the container offset of the `1/k` quantum if active, letterboxing and zoom, using the **unjolted** camera pose — quantised to the HUD's own integer grid. Interaction prompts, the inspect card's anchor and any future ping call it; nobody re-derives the projection. Mouse-to-world (`SubViewportContainer.mouse_target`, `Viewport.get_mouse_position()` `[BIN]`) goes through the same node and is checked at step 11; the pipeline root's input mapping must not test `device == 0` (4.7: `DEVICE_ID_MOUSE/KEYBOARD` `[REF]`).

**PR-7. Render mode: pass A or pass B, one flag, decided by the spike — and the one wiring question three features share.** The two passes are one scene (8.4): **A** renders the world viewport at logical size (640×360 texels; lit pixels 230 400) and the container scales it by k — lights, shadows and glow at base resolution, cone and shadow edges stair-stepped by one base px; **B** renders the world viewport at window size with `size_2d_override = logical size` and `size_2d_override_stretch = true` `[BIN]` — canvas coordinates logically 640×360, sprites integer-scaled **only if item origins land on multiples of k render px** (PR-4 rule 2 guarantees this for sim-driven motion; tile layers and the camera are checked at step 2/12), lights, shadows and glow at window resolution (2 073 600 lit px at 1080p — the 9× lever of 8.11 flag 4). 8.12 step 2 decides on the criteria written there (B wins only inside 16.6 ms with 8.10's budget); **A is the fallback the art-director accepts and the default until the spike runs.** Recorded as `RENDER_MODE ∈ {A, B}` → ADR.

**The wiring question (engine-programmer; NOT SOURCEABLE; step 11).** Under one root `canvas_items` + `integer` stretch the container is 640×360 base units and `stretch = true` plausibly sizes the `SubViewport` to 640×360 (= pass A); `stretch_shrink` only shrinks `[BIN]`. How a world viewport reaches W×H (pass B), or `picture / k_z` (any ladder step, C6), or how the HUD draws at H ≠ k (PR-12 — mandatory on the Deck from MVP), while the root stays in integer stretch, is not derivable from the members. Candidate wirings: **(i)** root `stretch/mode = disabled`, world `SubViewport.size` set by the pipeline root on `size_changed`, container placed and scaled by hand, HUD `CanvasLayer.scale = H` set by hand; **(ii)** root integer stretch kept, container `stretch = false`, `SubViewport.size` driven by script, relying on the final transform to map base units onto render px 1:1. **Pass B, zoom-out and HUD ≠ k are one ADR**, not three; the art bible's "both passes are one scene" glosses this. Whichever wins, the acceptance test is `get_final_transform().get_scale()` exactly `(k, k)` for the world and `(H, H)` for the HUD at every window in PR-1's table.

**What must be identical across A and B** (so no rule in this GDD, and no dependent, needs to know which won): the logical coordinate space and PR-2's policy; k, H and the zoom step; nearest sampling and the placement rules of PR-4; the PR-5 sort contract and z-bands; the whole camera contract of PR-6 (the `1/k` quantum is the one `[UNV]` item under B); the HUD layer and `world_to_hud`; the Filter hook of PR-10 (the container's material receives the world at *screen* resolution in both passes — under A because the container upscales, under B because the viewport is that size); the fringe at exactly 2 base px (under B the shader takes k as a uniform, 8.10); the ambient-floor mechanism; the presentation contract of PR-8. **What may differ**: viewport size and memory (2–3 MB vs 22 MB of HDR render targets at 1080p, 8.6); light-texture `size_limit` and mipmaps (1024, off vs 2048, on — 8.3/8.4); the look of a cone edge and of a rotated horror segment (PR-4 rule 5); frame time.

**PR-8. The presentation contract every visual system inherits.** A visual system — Lighting, Trails, Instruments, HUD, the Filter, the rig animator, the `CameraSolver`'s host node — is a `PRESENTATION` subscriber (CR-10) and:

1. subscribes as `PRESENTATION` (or `INPUT_SURFACE` if it also issues commands — the HUD's prompts do not; the chart table does); the Debug Overlay alone is `DIAGNOSTIC`;
2. reads **last-committed** state through `SIM` and `PERCEPTION_OUT` read views; nodes the Perception Filter's tag contract marks *corruptible* obtain their displayed value from the Filter, truth-channel nodes read `SIM` directly, and the Debug Overlay reads the true channel for everything — which nodes default to which is the Filter GDD's to set (CR-10's two channels);
3. treats a notification as a dirty mark and does its reading and drawing in the render callback — never inside the notification handler, where writes are disabled and a long handler stalls P5 for every other subscriber;
4. writes **only** `CanvasItem`, `Material`, `Light2D`, particle and audio properties (8.11's manifest rule); never a block, never a command (render presentation has no submit API — CR-14 H4), never a physics body (that is a `PROJECTOR`); **never a camera or viewport property unless it is the pipeline root or the camera host** (C1);
5. interpolates every moving value with the kernel's `tick_alpha` and the read view's previous/current pair (D.3), then floors to base px in the body frame (PR-4 rules 2–3) — **one interpolator** (PR-4 rule 8);
6. animates on `present_time` (PR-9), never on wall-clock and never on the tick counter (a presentation that counts ticks would freeze under a render hitch while the world moved — the opposite of the honest hold D.3 wants);
7. **never causes a tick** (CR-11) and never blocks on one; if the state it needs has not been committed yet it draws the last committed state;
8. places every item at an integer base-px position in the body frame and declares its band (PR-5) — a node that sorts declares its sort origin as its floor-contact line, or its host face's if wall-mounted;
9. obeys the flash clamp of PR-13 for anything it animates in luminance.

Rationale: this is the renderer's copy of the concept's "corruption is presentation-only" rule, written so a lint can check it (8.11 routes the manifest rule to the lead programmer). *Alternative rejected:* letting presentation own small bits of state (a trail's fade timer, a gauge's needle lag) — the moment a trail's visibility depends on presentation state it is no longer the truth channel every hallucination is checked against (Failure & Trail's "pure reader" rule in the index). Camera state is the one presentation-owned state the pipeline admits, and C5 makes it non-persistent and reset on every cut so it can never diverge from the sim.

**PR-9. The presentation clock.** The pipeline owns `present_time`: a render-rate clock in seconds that advances by the render delta while the kernel is `RUNNING`, `SAVING` or `LOADING`-with-hold, is **frozen while the kernel is `PAUSED`**, and is the only clock layer-0 presentation may animate on (flow scroll, dust, the fringe's 4–6 s breathing, the beacon's sweep phase, the Filter's stage-2 wobble). Horror rig stepping is the exception: the rig's pose is sim-driven (every 5th tick) and presentation interpolates nothing between steps (5.4: stepped playback is the look). `present_time` reconciles three rules that otherwise collide: CR-16 ("`PAUSED` stops the tick but not rendering"), 7.4 ("the world simply stops, lit exactly as it was" — so the flow in the pipes must stop too, not just the sim) and CR-11 ("pausing presentation effects (reduced motion) never pauses the sim" — reduced motion scales *individual effects*, never `present_time`). Layers 2–4 run on the engine's process time, because the chart table must respond while paused. **`SceneTree.paused` `[BIN]` is not the kernel's pause** (H10 — the kernel is scene-tree independent): the pipeline root maps game pause to kernel `PAUSED` via the control plane and applies the engine's `paused` to presentation nodes only if at all → ADR (tick driver). Under pause the world viewport may stop re-rendering (`render_target_update_mode` `[BIN]`, last frame held → ADR), which is also the `HIDDEN` state's behaviour; step 11 confirms no stale frame survives a resize.

---

**PR-10. The Perception Filter hook point.** The pipeline provides exactly one full-screen hook: **a `ShaderMaterial` slot on the `SubViewportContainer`**, owned by the Perception Filter, receiving the world viewport's output at screen resolution, drawn **below every `CanvasLayer`** — so the Filter pass **never samples or writes** the HUD, inspect card, menus, text chat, pings, letterbox bars or debug overlay (C-R; HR-1, HR-6, R-7 by construction). This is the fifth material of 8.10's "≤ 5 materials" row. Constraints on what the pass may do (ux-designer, adopted; the Filter GDD owns the effects): it **writes colour only** — no displacement, blur, chromatic aberration, pixelation or resolution change; it **only adds** violet or flesh at 4.4's places and never remaps sane hues, erodes shape or trail contrast, or drops hull below the measured floor; it obeys the flash clamp (PR-13); it never writes a camera or viewport property (C1). Its output is **multiplied by a truth-channel mask** — character sprites, hand tools, crewmate beams, trails, the true cone edge — **or decomposed into per-element materials** so truth-channel pixels are exact (C-R). Everything stage 2/3 does to the *whole image* goes in this slot; everything *per thing* — a lying gauge's gleam, a decoy light, a wholly substituted sprite (5.0) — goes on nodes inside layer 0 as the Filter's own materials or `Light2D` modifiers under PR-8. **The mask's production is → ADR**: (a) a second `SubViewport` sharing the camera pose that draws only truth-channel items as unlit silhouettes (cheap, no sort problem because it is a mask, not a composite), or (b) no mask, per-element materials only; the Filter GDD chooses. *Alternative rejected:* compositing truth-channel items in a second viewport **above** the filtered world — those items sort against corruptible ones on the same Y-sorted layer, and splitting a sorted layer across two viewports breaks PR-5. `BackBufferCopy` without the material slot is the engine alternative (8.10) → ADR.

**PR-11. Frame-rate independence.** The sim runs at 60 Hz (CR-1); the pipeline renders at whatever rate the display and settings allow and never changes the tick. **The acceptance test is the kernel's:** identical `state_hash` after a scripted 10-s input replay at `max_fps` 30, 60, 144 and 0 (G3 + CR-11; spike step 13).

| Render rate | Ticks per frame | What presentation shows | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| 144 fps | 0 or 1 (ticks on ≈ 42 % of frames) | interpolated positions between the last two commits at `tick_alpha ∈ [0,1)`, floored: camera and sprites step one base px at the interpolated instant, **never twice in a frame and never backward**; without interpolation 58 % of frames would repeat a pose | no sub-base-pixel motion exists at any fps unless the `1/k` quantum is adopted (C3); `L_render` shrinks to ≤ 6.9 ms, input latency is otherwise unchanged above 60 |
| 60 fps | 1 | with tick-before-render, `tick_alpha` at draw time is ≈ 0, so an **interpolated transform shows approximately the *previous* tick's position**: continuous motion carries **one tick (≈ 17 ms) of visual latency by construction**, because D.3 forbids extrapolation. **Discrete committed state** (a light toggled, a gauge value, a trail appearing) reads `current` and shows in the same frame — D.4's best case applies to it. Look-ahead reads the **raw** aim (C4) and so carries no tick | the design target; the latency is the price of the honest clamp and is the same at every fps |
| 30 fps | 2 (catch-up) | the latest commit; `tick_alpha` clamps at 1 after the second tick; motion shows every second tick's pose — 2-base-px steps for a 1 px/tick body, honest, not smoothed | input-to-pixel worst case 50 ms (D.4); acceptable; **the Deck floor under pass A** |
| 40 / 90 Hz (Deck panels) | 1.5 / 0.67 average | the interpolation path is what keeps motion even at rates that are not multiples of 60 | spike at 40 and 90 fps caps (step 13) |
| < 20 fps | up to `MAX_TICKS_PER_FRAME`, then sim-time lag (CR-17) | the latest commit; the world *slows* rather than extrapolates; a catch-up burst that moves the avatar ≥ 48 px in one frame triggers the camera cut (C5) | the pipeline reports frame time to the Debug Overlay; never a visual compensation |

`tick_alpha` is clamped at 1 so a hitch holds the last pose rather than extrapolating (D.3's Pillar 1 argument). Presentation reads `delta` only to advance `present_time` and the solver's smoothing. **Tick driver → ADR** (engine-programmer): kernel D.4 assumes the physics callback drives the tick (`physics_ticks_per_second = 60`, `max_physics_steps_per_frame = 8` `[BIN]` — if that caps catch-up `[UNV]`, the engine stops catching up below 7.5 fps); kernel D.5 defines a kernel-side accumulator — two accumulators drift, one must be the clock. If the physics callback drives, **`physics_jitter_fix` is set to 0** (default 0.5 `[BIN]`) pending the spike, since a fixed-tick kernel that forbids wall-clock reads (H9) should not receive engine-smoothed steps; `delta_smoothing = true` `[BIN]` affects `_process` delta only `[UNV]` and is harmless. **Tick-before-render ordering** (`_physics_process` → `_process` in the same frame) is silent in `[REF]` and is step 13's check; D.4's best case for discrete state is conditional on it. **Position ownership tension with CR-4:** the kernel says movement "integrates on the physics side from the current intent so the sprite never waits a tick" — a position integrated outside the tick and drawn the same frame has no previous/current pair and no one-tick latency, which is a different interpolator from kernel-alpha. The tick-source ADR (kernel Open Q 2/3) must decide who owns position before "one interpolator" can mean kernel-only; until then both are described and neither is promised. **V-sync and caps (PR-14):** ship `VSYNC_ENABLED` (engine default `[BIN]`); expose `MAILBOX` as "uncapped, no tearing" and `DISABLED` + `max_fps` for benchmarking; never `ADAPTIVE` by default (behaviour on Windows D3D12 vs Vulkan NOT SOURCEABLE — the binary reports `driver.windows = vulkan` `[BIN]` while `[REF]` says D3D12 is the 4.6+ default; 8.12 step 1 prints the active driver and the GDD assumes neither).

**PR-12. HUD scale.** The HUD's integer scale is `H ∈ {H_default, …, 2·H_default}` with **`H_default = max(k, 3)`** — **never below 3× on any display** (C-R), player-raisable to 2× the default in integer steps (HR-12's 100–200 %), labelled in Settings **by resulting cap height in screen px** (e.g. "21 px / 28 px / 35 px / 42 px" at 1080p), not by percentage (ux-designer). At 1080p the steps are 3×–6×; on the Deck and at 720p **3×–6× over a 2× world**; at 1440p 4×–8×; at 2160p 6×–12×. H is **independent of k and of zoom** (8.1: "the frame grows, the world does not"). The reading face (7.1) scales continuously and is the only non-integer text in the game. Consequences: (i) because the Deck ships at H = 3 over k = 2, **two integer scales in one window is an MVP requirement**, not a post-spike nicety — it is PR-7's wiring ADR; the engine-programmer's note that "the HUD's integer scale comes from the root stretch with `Control.scale = (1,1)`" holds only when H = k, and `gui_snap_controls_to_pixels = true` `[BIN]` stays on the root in every wiring; (ii) **7.0's critical-strip size cap (12 % × 5 % of frame) is exceeded by default on the Deck** at H = 3 over a 2× world (the plate is 1.5× larger relative to the picture than at 1080p) — routed to art-director as a known, accepted cost of C-R's 3× floor; (iii) the arcminute floors per device class (desk monitor, TV, handheld) that justify the 3× floor are the accessibility-specialist's table — routed. HUD elements are anchor-relative to the picture (PR-2) and survive a fractional HUD canvas.

**PR-13. Photosensitivity and brightness.** The pipeline hosts the frame-level invariants; Lighting owns the per-light clamp through its modifier contract. (a) **Per light:** no light may produce more than **3 flashes per rolling 1-s window**, a flash being a pair of opposing luminance changes ≥ 10 %, red included (C-R); the alarm beacon's 2–3 s sweep (2.8) and the cryo lamp's gutter (0.3–0.5 s dips no more often than every 10–20 s, 2.2) pass; **the deployable's flicker (3.2) must be specified as a number and pass the clamp** — routed to art-director and technical-artist. (b) **Regional and frame-mean:** the same ceiling applies to any ≈ 213×120 base-px region (a ninth of the frame) and to the frame mean, so overlapping lights cannot sum past it; **no full-frame flash in any state, on any layer** (7.3, HR-13). (c) **A "reduce flashing" toggle, separate from reduced motion**: deployables steady, power arcs held as glow, the beacon becomes a static mark + sound + HUD glyph (HR-8's form), the cryo gutter kept. (d) **No full-frame gamma or brightness**; HR-15's bounded hull-material step is the only brightness control (PR-3 b). (e) A **first-launch photosensitivity notice** (chart-table register). (f) A **flash scan** of the 2.8 breach composition and the 2.4 lost state is a spike step (routed to the technical-artist's 8.12 list). The Filter pass obeys (a)–(b) (PR-10).

**PR-14. What Settings may expose (all integer; no non-integer option anywhere).** Display mode (windowed / fullscreen / borderless); resolution; **integer world scale** (Auto + list; a manual k below Auto forces `keep`, PR-2); HUD scale (labelled by cap height, PR-12); reading-face scale (continuous — the one exception, and it is text, not pixels); aspect `keep` / `expand` (post-spike); frame cap (30 / 60 / 120 / 144 / 240 / off); V-sync (enabled / mailbox / off); reduced motion (disables look-ahead, jolt → 0, world effects per HR-8 — never trail propagation, never `present_time`); reduce flashing (PR-13); the HR-15 ambient-floor step (Lighting's, surfaced here). **PR-15. Guard rails (engine-programmer, adopted).** (1) The `[display]` stretch block (`mode = canvas_items`, `aspect = keep` for the spike, `scale_mode = integer`, 640×360), `default_texture_filter = 0`, both `snap_2d_*` keys, `hdr_2d` and `use_debanding` are **written explicitly into `project.godot`** (approval path 8.11 flag 7 — not Section C's to write). ## Formulas

*Every value below is **PROVISIONAL until the lighting spike** (art bible 8.12 steps 2 and 5; the engine-programmer's proposed steps 11 "stretch and window", 12 "motion integrity", 13 "pacing"). Values that depend on the avatar's locomotion speeds — `v_walk`, `v_run`, `v_max` — are additionally **bound to the Player Controller GDD**, which owns them and fixes `v_walk` = 2 px/tick = 120 base px/s and `v_run` = `v_max` = 3 px/tick = 180 base px/s (registry `V_WALK` / `V_RUN`; whole base px per tick per Section C PR-4 rule 3); they are not this system's knobs, and every derived number below was re-run against them *(Amended 2026-10-26 — consistency check)*. At a zoom-ladder step the solver's length parameters scale by `s = field_h / 360` (D.6, D.7, D.10) so every invariant that holds at the 360-row field holds at every reachable field. Notation: base px unless stated; `dt` is the render-frame delta in seconds; `W × H` is the window in screen px; `L_w × L_h` is the world viewport's logical size (640×360 by default). Formulas D.6–D.11 are the `CameraSolver` (Section C PR-6 C3) and are required to give identical poses at 60 and 144 fps for the same wall time, which is why every predicate is written in seconds, never frames.*

**Four reconciliations found while deriving these formulas, adopted into Section C's meaning (adopted — decision log 2026-10-26):**
1. *Pan ceiling and lag cut.* Under first-order follow the steady-state lag is exactly `v × τ_follow` and the camera's steady-state speed equals the avatar's, so a ceiling of "1.25× walk" is unreachable by a legal follow of a running avatar, and a 24 px lag cut fires continuously at run speed (180 × 0.18 = 32.4 px *(re-run 2026-10-26 — consistency check)*). The ceiling is **1.25 × `v_max`** (the avatar's fastest locomotion), `LAG_CUT` is **48 base px**, and the invariant `LAG_CUT > v_max × τ_follow` is written into D.10 so Section G flags it whenever either knob moves.
2. *Cut predicates evaluate the follow component only; look-ahead has its own slew clamp.* A 180° aim flick under `τ_lookahead = 0.40 s` would otherwise start a 360 px/s look-ahead swing that exceeds any ceiling for ≈ 16 frames and cut the camera on every flick. Look-ahead is clamped to `L_SLEW_MAX = 144` base px/s (D.7); a flick is the player's input, so C-R "never reverses within 100 ms unless the player did" is satisfied by construction.
3. *Look-ahead reads the aim regardless of beam lit state.* The game-designer's rule is "beam aim", not "beam on"; a camera that shifted a tile on beam toggle would read as a jump.
4. *Predicate order.* The inner-box clamp (D.8) is applied to the pose; the lag and pan cuts (D.10) are evaluated on the **unclamped** follow term — otherwise, since `LAG_CUT + L_MAX = 120 > 108` (the vertical half-extent), the lag cut would be unreachable on the vertical axis.

**D.1 World scale**

The `world_scale` formula is defined as:

`k = floor(min(W / 640, H / 360))`

**Variables:**
| Variable | Type | Range | Source | Description |
|----------|------|-------|--------|-------------|
| W | int | 1280 – unbounded (screen px) | engine (window pixel size, never DPI) | Window width |
| H | int | 720 – unbounded (screen px) | engine | Window height |
| 640, 360 | int | fixed | art bible 8.1 (decided) | Base logical resolution |
| k | int | 2 – unbounded | calculated | Screen px per base px for the world |

**Output Range:** Bounded below by the minimum window (1280×720 → `k = 2`; Section C PR-1); unbounded above (3840×2160 → 6; 7680×4320 → 12). Never fractional by construction; a window that is not an integer multiple of 640×360 yields a remainder (D.2) or an expanded field (D.3), never a fractional scale. `k` is re-derived on every `size_changed` (one `RESCALING` frame).
**Example:** 1920×1080 → `floor(min(3.0, 3.0)) = 3`. 1280×800 (Steam Deck) → `floor(min(2.0, 2.22)) = 2`. 3440×1440 → `floor(min(5.375, 4.0)) = 4`.

**D.2 Letterbox remainder under `keep`**

The `letterbox_bars` formula is defined as:

`bars_x = (W − 640·k) / 2`, `bars_y = (H − 360·k) / 2` (an odd remainder splits `floor` left/top, `ceil` right/bottom)

**Variables:**
| Variable | Type | Range | Source | Description |
|----------|------|-------|--------|-------------|
| W, H, k | int | as D.1 | D.1 | Window and world scale |
| bars_x | int | 0 – unbounded (screen px) | calculated | Black bar width on each side |
| bars_y | int | 0 – unbounded (screen px) | calculated | Black bar height top and bottom |

**Output Range:** On the axis that limited `k` the remainder is strictly less than one base-picture step, so `bars_x < 320` or `bars_y < 180` on that axis; the other axis is unbounded (an ultrawide limited by height has 440 px bars each side). Bars are true black, outside the world `SubViewport`, never touched by the Filter pass or a jolt (Section C PR-2). What the engine actually fills the remainder with is NOT SOURCEABLE — step 11.
**Example:** 1600×900, `k = 2` → `bars_x = (1600 − 1280)/2 = 160`, `bars_y = (900 − 720)/2 = 90` (36 % of the screen black — the case `expand` exists for). 1365×768, `k = 2` → `bars_x = 42.5` → 42 left, 43 right; `bars_y = 24`.

**D.3 Expanded logical size under `expand`**

The `expanded_field` formula is defined as:

`L_w = min(floor(W / k), CAP_W)`, `L_h = min(floor(H / k), CAP_H)`; residual bars `(W − L_w·k) / 2`, `(H − L_h·k) / 2`

**Variables:**
| Variable | Type | Range | Source | Description |
|----------|------|-------|--------|-------------|
| W, H, k | int | as D.1 | D.1 | Window and world scale |
| CAP_W, CAP_H | int | 960, 540 (safe 640–960 × 360–540) | tuning (G) | Cap on the default-zoom field = 1.5× base |
| L_w, L_h | int | 640–960 × 360–540 (base px) | calculated | World viewport logical size at default zoom |

**Output Range:** Never below 640×360 (because `k` was chosen as the largest integer that fits) and never above the cap; the cap exists to bound the information gap between displays (Section C PR-2) and the pass-B lit-pixel cost. When `k` is set manually below `k_auto`, aspect is forced to `keep` and this formula is not applied. A half-column from an odd window is letterboxed (1 screen px), never rendered fractionally.
**Example:** 3440×1440, `k = 4` → `L_w = min(860, 960) = 860`, `L_h = min(360, 540) = 360`; residual 0. 1280×800, `k = 2` → `640×400` (+40 rows). 1600×900, `k = 2` → `800×450`; residual 0.

**D.6 Follow (first-order exponential approach)**

The `follow` formula is defined as:

`a = 1 − exp(−dt / τ_follow_eff)`, `τ_follow_eff = τ_follow × s`, `s = field_h / 360` (D.5; `s = 1` at the default step); `f_n = f_{n−1} + a · (target_n − f_{n−1})`; steady-state lag `lag_ss = v · τ_follow_eff`; settle time `t_95 = 3 · τ_follow_eff` *(Amended 2026-10-26 — consistency check: field scaling added)*

**Variables:**
| Variable | Type | Range | Source | Description |
|----------|------|-------|--------|-------------|
| dt | float | 0.004 – 0.05 s | engine (render delta) | Frame time |
| τ_follow | float | 0.18 s (safe 0 – 0.30) | tuning (G) | Follow time constant; 0 = rigid follow (the step-12 A/B) |
| target_n | float pair | body px | kernel read view, interpolated (D.9) | Avatar's interpolated position this frame |
| f_n | float pair | body px | calculated | The follow component of the camera pose, before look-ahead, clamp and quantisation |
| v | float | 0 – v_max (base px/s) | Player Controller GDD (`v_walk` 120, `v_run` = `v_max` 180; registry `V_WALK` / `V_RUN`) | Avatar speed |
| s | float | 0.6 – 4.0 | D.5 | Field-scale factor; lag as a *fraction of the frame* is therefore constant across the ladder |
| lag_ss | float | 0 – v_max·τ_follow | calculated | Distance the camera trails a constant-velocity avatar |
| t_95 | float | 0 – 0.9 s | calculated | Time to close 95 % of a step |

**Output Range:** `a ∈ (0, 1)` for any positive `dt`, so `f` moves monotonically toward the target with zero overshoot — the camera never reverses unless the target does (C-R). Frame-rate independent: two 144-fps frames and one 60-fps frame of equal total `dt` give the same `f` to floating-point precision, so the per-frame factor is 0.0885 at 60 fps and 0.0378 at 144 fps. `lag_ss` must stay below `LAG_CUT` (D.10) or a steady run cuts forever — 180 × 0.18 = 32.4 px < 48 at the default field, and both sides scale by `s` at other steps *(re-run 2026-10-26 — consistency check)*.
**Example:** Avatar steps 40 px (a dash start) at 60 fps, default field: frame 1 `f` moves 3.54 px, after 0.1 s (6 frames) 17.3 px, after `t_95 = 0.54 s` 38 px. Running at `v_max` = 180 px/s the camera trails by 32.4 px; at walk (120 px/s) 21.6 px. At the 270-row zoom-in field (`s = 0.75`, `τ_follow_eff = 0.135 s`) the same run trails by 24.3 px and the walk by 16.2 px — 9 % and 6 % of the field in both cases.

**D.7 Look-ahead from aim**

The `look_ahead` formula is defined as:

`L_MAX_eff = L_MAX × s`, `L_SLEW_MAX_eff = L_SLEW_MAX × s`, `s = field_h / 360` (D.5); `L_target = L_MAX_eff · û(aim)` where `û(aim) = aim / |aim|` if `|aim| ≥ ε`, else the previous `û`; `ΔL = clamp((L_target − L_{n−1}) · (1 − exp(−dt / τ_lookahead)), −L_SLEW_MAX_eff·dt, +L_SLEW_MAX_eff·dt)`; `L_n = L_{n−1} + ΔL` *(Amended 2026-10-26 — consistency check: `L_MAX_eff` per the zoom-ladder decision)*

**Variables:**
| Variable | Type | Range | Source | Description |
|----------|------|-------|--------|-------------|
| aim | float pair | any (body px direction) | Player Controller, **local raw aim** (not the coalesced intent); read regardless of beam lit state | Where the chest-mounted beam points |
| ε | float | 0.01 | fixed | Dead threshold against a zero or NaN aim |
| L_MAX | float | 72 base px at the 360-row field (`LOOKAHEAD_PX`; safe 48 – 75 — the inner-box invariant `INNER_BOX_FRAC·field_h > v_max·τ_follow + L_MAX_eff` (INNER_BOX_FRAC = 0.30, D.8) binds at 75.6 for `v_max` = 180, so the 2.5-tile bound is unreachable; 0 under reduced motion) | tuning (G) / Settings | Maximum look-ahead distance = 1.5 tiles |
| L_MAX_eff | float | `L_MAX × s`: 43.2 at 216 rows, 54 at 270, 72 at 360, 80 at 400 (Deck), 108 at 540, 144 at 720, 160 at 800 (Deck 1×), 216 at 1080 | calculated | The bound actually applied at the current ladder step |
| τ_lookahead | float | 0.40 s (safe 0.2 – 0.8) | tuning (G) | Ease time constant (time, not length — does not scale) |
| L_SLEW_MAX | float | 144 base px/s at the 360-row field (safe 96 – 240); `L_SLEW_MAX_eff = L_SLEW_MAX × s` | tuning (G) | Rate clamp so a flick never out-paces the pan ceiling |
| L_n | float pair | |L| ≤ L_MAX_eff | calculated | Look-ahead offset added to `f_n` |

**Output Range:** `|L| ≤ L_MAX_eff` always (the target is on the circle of radius `L_MAX_eff` and the approach is contractive); as a fraction of the frame the bound is the same 20 % of the field height at every step. Without the slew clamp a 180° flick starts at `2·L_MAX / τ = 360 px/s`; with it, the first `(144 − 57.6)/144 ≈ 0.6 s` run at the clamp and the exponential tail takes over when the gap falls below `L_SLEW_MAX · τ = 57.6 px`, so a full reversal reaches 95 % in ≈ 1.4 s. Look-ahead is player-caused and is excluded from the cut predicates (D.10).
**Example:** Aim north, `L = (0, −72)`: a 6-tile north beam shows 3.75 + 1.5 = 5.25 of its tiles instead of 3.75. Flick to south: `L_target = (0, +72)`; frame 1 at 60 fps the unclamped step would be `144 × 0.0408 = 5.9 px`, the clamp allows `144 × 0.0167 = 2.4 px`, so the clamp governs; after 0.6 s `L ≈ (0, +14)`; after 1.4 s ≈ `(0, +68)`.

**D.8 Inner-box clamp**

The `inner_box_clamp` formula is defined as:

`B = (floor(INNER_BOX_FRAC · L_w), floor(INNER_BOX_FRAC · L_h))` with `INNER_BOX_FRAC = 0.30`; `c = clamp(f + L, target − B, target + B)` (per axis); steady-state invariant `INNER_BOX_FRAC · field_h > v_max · τ_follow_eff + L_MAX_eff` *(Amended 2026-10-26 — consistency check: constant named for the registry)*

**Variables:**
| Variable | Type | Range | Source | Description |
|----------|------|-------|--------|-------------|
| L_w, L_h | int | any ladder field — 216 to 1080 rows at 1080p; 200 to 800 on the Deck (D.5) | D.3 / D.5 | World viewport logical size at the current step |
| INNER_BOX_FRAC | float | 0.30, fixed (box = 0.60 of frame; registry `INNER_BOX_FRAC`) | gameplay-programmer (decided) | Half-extent fraction |
| B | int pair | (192, 108) at 640×360; (258, 108) at 860×360 | calculated | Half-extents of the box the avatar may not leave |
| f + L | float pair | body px | D.6 + D.7 | Unclamped pose |
| target | float pair | body px | D.6 | Avatar position |
| c | float pair | body px | calculated | Clamped pose, before quantisation |

**Output Range:** `|c − target| ≤ B` per axis, so the avatar is never drawn outside the central 60 % of the frame whatever the lag or look-ahead. At the defaults the clamp is inactive in steady state (`lag_ss + L_MAX = 32.4 + 72 = 104.4 < 108` at the 360-row field; because `τ_follow`, `L_MAX` and `B` all scale by `s`, the same inequality holds at every ladder field — 270: 24.3 + 54 = 78.3 < 81; 216: 19.4 + 43.2 = 62.6 < 64.8; 540: 48.6 + 108 = 156.6 < 162; 720: 64.8 + 144 = 208.8 < 216; Deck 400: 36 + 80 = 116 < 120; Deck 800: 72 + 160 = 232 < 240 *(re-run 2026-10-26 — consistency check)*); it engages only during the transient after a target jump below the cut thresholds, where it is the thing that keeps the avatar in frame. The clamp acts on the pose; the cut predicates read the unclamped `f` (reconciliation 4).
**Example:** Avatar jumps 40 px north while `L = (0, −72)`: unclamped pose trails by 40 + 72 = 112 > 108, so `c` is pinned at 108 px north of the avatar for the first frames, then released as `f` catches up.

**D.9 Quantisation and sprite placement**

The `quantise` formula is defined as:

`pose_q = floor(c / Q) · Q` with `Q ∈ {1, 1/k}`; sprite `p = floor(prev + (curr − prev) · tick_alpha)`; container offset `o = −round((1 + frac(pose_q)) · k)` screen px if `Q = 1/k`, else 0

**Variables:**
| Variable | Type | Range | Source | Description |
|----------|------|-------|--------|-------------|
| c | float pair | body px | D.8 | Clamped pose |
| Q | float | 1 (MVP) or 1/k | tuning (G, set-once per build) | Camera quantum |
| pose_q | float pair | multiples of Q | calculated | Camera position written to `Camera2D` (floored; `floor`, never `round`, everywhere) |
| prev, curr | float pair | body px | kernel read views (CR-10) | Committed positions of the previous and current tick |
| tick_alpha | float | 0.0 – 1.0 | kernel D.3 (clamped) | Interpolation fraction |
| p | int pair | body px | calculated | Integer base-px position every presentation node writes |
| o | int pair | −2k … −k screen px | calculated | `SubViewportContainer` position under the `1/k` quantum (world viewport rendered 2 base px larger) |

**Output Range:** `p` is always an integer in the body frame — the viewport snap flags are a safety net (Section C PR-4 rule 2). With `Q = 1` the picture scrolls in whole base px (k screen px); with `Q = 1/k` it scrolls in screen px while every item stays on one shared base grid. `tick_alpha` is produced clamped by the kernel driver; a value outside [0, 1] is impossible by D.3 and is asserted (E).
**Example:** `prev = 100`, `curr = 102`, `tick_alpha = 0.42` → `p = floor(100.84) = 100`; at `tick_alpha = 0.6`, `p = 101`. Camera `c = 311.7`, `Q = 1` → `pose_q = 311`; `Q = 1/3` → `311.667`, `o = −round(1.667 × 3) = −5` screen px.

**D.10 Pan-speed ceiling and the cut predicates**

The `camera_cut` formula is defined as:

`v_follow = |f_n − f_{n−1}| / dt`; `P_pan = (v_follow > 1.25 · v_max)` held continuously for `≥ T_PAN`; `P_lag = (|target_n − f_n| > LAG_CUT)`; `P_jump = (|target_n − target_{n−1}| ≥ JUMP_CUT)`; `cut = P_pan ∨ P_lag ∨ P_jump ∨ body_transfer ∨ loading_to_running`; at a ladder step `LAG_CUT_eff = LAG_CUT × s`, `JUMP_CUT_eff = JUMP_CUT × s` and `τ_follow_eff = τ_follow × s` (D.5, D.6) while the ceiling `1.25 · v_max` does **not** scale (it can never sit below `v_max`); invariants `LAG_CUT > v_max · τ_follow` and `INNER_BOX_FRAC · field_h > v_max · τ_follow_eff + L_MAX_eff` (D.8; INNER_BOX_FRAC = 0.30) — both scale-free, every length term carrying `s` *(Amended 2026-10-26 — consistency check)*

**Variables:**
| Variable | Type | Range | Source | Description |
|----------|------|-------|--------|-------------|
| f_n | float pair | body px | D.6 (unclamped follow term, without look-ahead or jolt) | What the predicates read |
| v_max | float | 180 base px/s (3 px/tick) | Player Controller GDD (registry `V_RUN`) | Avatar's fastest locomotion |
| 1.25 | float | fixed (safe 1.1 – 1.5) | ux-designer (decided; "walk" → `v_max`, reconciliation 1) | Pan ceiling multiplier |
| T_PAN | float | 0.10 s (safe 0.05 – 0.2) | tuning (G) | Sustain time before a pan cut (= 6 frames at 60 fps, 15 at 144, 3 at 30) |
| LAG_CUT | int | 48 base px at the 360-row field (safe > v_max·τ_follow = 32.4 … 96); `× s` at other steps | tuning (G) | Lag beyond which the camera cuts instead of panning |
| JUMP_CUT | int | 48 base px at the 360-row field (safe 24 – 96); `× s` at other steps | tuning (G) | Single-frame target displacement that cuts immediately |
| cut | bool | — | calculated | Solver state reset to the target this frame (look-ahead at full value, jolt cleared) |

**Output Range:** Because D.6 never exceeds the target's speed in steady state, `P_pan` can only fire on a target discontinuity: a jump `J` gives an initial follow speed `J / τ_follow` that decays as `exp(−t/τ)`; it stays above the ceiling for `T_PAN` only if `J > J_pan`, where the named quantity **`J_pan = 1.25·v_max·τ_follow·exp(T_PAN/τ_follow)`** = `225 × 0.18 × 1.743 ≈ 70.6 px` at the defaults (84.7 px at multiplier 1.5; at the 270-row field with `τ_follow_eff = 0.135`: `225 × 0.135 × 2.097 ≈ 63.7 px` against `JUMP_CUT_eff = 36`) *(re-run 2026-10-26 — consistency check)* — so at the defaults `P_jump` (48 px) fires first and `P_pan` is the backstop for multi-frame drifts (e.g. a `MAX_TICKS_PER_FRAME` catch-up burst delivering 20 px per frame for several frames). `P_lag` covers a target that out-runs the follow for any reason. The invariant guarantees a legal run never cuts: 48 > 32.4 at `v_max` = 180; Section G flags any `(τ_follow, v_max)` pair that breaks it.
**Example:** Re-wake: the target moves ≈ 2 000 px in one frame → `P_jump` → cut. A 40 px catch-up burst: `P_jump` false; initial `v_follow = 40 / 0.18 = 222 px/s`, **below** the 225 px/s ceiling, so `P_pan` never arms → no cut; D.8 pins the avatar inside the box while D.6 eases. A 47 px burst (just under `JUMP_CUT`): initial `v_follow = 261 px/s > 225`, back below the ceiling at `t = 0.18 × ln(261/225) = 0.027 s < T_PAN` → no cut. Running at 180 px/s: lag 32.4 px, `v_follow = 180 < 225` → no predicate fires. *(Re-run 2026-10-26 — consistency check.)*

**D.11 Jolt (default 0)**

The `jolt` formula is defined as:

`j(t) = round(J_0 · max(0, 1 − t / T_J) · s(t))`, `s(t) = (−1)^{floor(2 · f_J · t)}`; on overlapping events `J_0 = max(J_0, J_new)` (never summed); `|J_0| ≤ JOLT_MAX`; applied as `Camera2D.offset` inside the world `SubViewport`, excluded from `world_to_hud` (D.14)

**Variables:**
| Variable | Type | Range | Source | Description |
|----------|------|-------|--------|-------------|
| J_0 | int | 0 – JOLT_MAX | event (P1 physical only) | Initial amplitude; **default knob 0 at MVP**; 0 under reduced motion |
| JOLT_MAX | int | 2 base px (safe 0 – 2) | tuning (G) / Settings | Hard amplitude cap |
| T_J | float | ≤ 0.30 s (safe 0.1 – 0.3) | tuning (G) | Decay duration |
| f_J | float | ≤ 8 Hz (safe 4 – 8) | tuning (G) | Alternation frequency |
| t | float | 0 – T_J s | `present_time` since the event | Elapsed time |
| j(t) | int | −2 … +2 base px | calculated | Integer offset added after quantisation |

**Output Range:** Integer base px, bounded by `JOLT_MAX`, zero after `T_J`; alternation at `f_J ≤ 8 Hz` is positional, not luminous, so the flash clamp (D.15) does not apply — flagged for the accessibility-specialist as 7.3 flags the tremor. Never caused by world state (Section C C1/C7).
**Example:** `J_0 = 2`, `T_J = 0.3`, `f_J = 8`: `j = +2, −2, +2, −1, +1, −1, 0 …` in 62.5 ms half-periods, gone by 0.3 s.

**D.12 Y-sort key (up-screen convention)**

The `sort_key` formula is defined as:

`sort_y(actor) = y_foot`; `sort_y(wall with footprint rows [y_0, y_0 + 24)) = y_0 + 24`; `sort_y(wall-mounted item) = sort_y(host face)`; face rows `[sort_y − 72, sort_y)`; cap rows `[sort_y − 96, sort_y − 72)`; draw order ascending `sort_y`, then **layer order** (tile layers before free items — wall faces, then the wall-mounted layer, then free items), then `x` and `creation_id` among free items only

**Variables:**
| Variable | Type | Range | Source | Description |
|----------|------|-------|--------|-------------|
| y_foot | int | body px | presentation (D.9 `p`) | Actor's floored foot row |
| y_0 | int | multiples of 24 | layout | North edge of a wall's floor footprint |
| 24, 72, 96 | int | fixed | art bible 8.2 (set-once) | Footprint depth, face height, total screen rows |
| sort_y | int | body px | calculated | The floor-contact line every Y-sorted item sorts by |
| layer order | enum | tile layers < wall-mounted < free items | layer stack (Section C PR-5) | First tie-breaker on an equal row — a tile's `x` is its left edge and an actor's is its foot-centre, so `x` cannot order a tile against an actor |
| x, creation_id | int | — | layout / instantiation | Tie-breakers among free items only |

**Output Range:** Integer rows, so an interpolated actor and a wall never swap on a sub-pixel difference. Predicate the spike tests (step 5): an actor is hidden by a wall iff `y_foot < sort_y(wall)` and their rows and columns overlap — standing just north of a wall (behind it) is hidden; standing just south (in front, `y_foot ≥ sort_y`) draws over the face. Under the convention the face covers 72 rows of floor **north** of the footprint (the room behind); Section C PR-5 routes the one-clause amendment of 8.2 to the art-director.
**Example:** Wall footprint rows [240, 264): `sort_y = 264`, face rows [192, 264), cap rows [168, 192). Rows 240–263 are collision, so the first legal row south of the wall is `y_foot = 264`: it sorts equal to the wall and the layer-order tie-break draws the tile layer first and the free item after → the actor is drawn over the face, standing at the wall's base — correct. Actor at `y_foot = 239` (just north, in the room behind): drawn before the wall; its rows [184, 239) overlap the face rows [192, 264) → hidden behind the wall — correct. An actor reported at `y_foot = 250` is inside the footprint and is a collision defect, not a sorting case (E).

**D.13 z-band fallback**

The `z_band` formula is defined as:

`z = Z_BASE + floor((sort_y − cam_top) / BAND_H)`; `N_bands = ceil(L_h / BAND_H) + 2`; bound `Z_BASE + N_bands ≤ range_z_max`

**Variables:**
| Variable | Type | Range | Source | Description |
|----------|------|-------|--------|-------------|
| sort_y | int | body px | D.12 | Item's sort row |
| cam_top | int | body px | D.9 `pose_q.y − L_h/2` | Top row of the camera view this frame |
| BAND_H | int | 24 base px (set-once; = wall footprint depth) | tuning (G) | Band height — a wall is always alone in its band |
| Z_BASE | int | 10 (proposed) | → ADR | Base `z_index` of the Y-sort band |
| L_h | int | 360 – 800 | D.3 / D.5 | Logical height, including the zoomed field |
| range_z_max | int | 1024 `[BIN]` default | `Light2D.range_z_max` | Lights ignore items outside their z range |
| z | int | Z_BASE … Z_BASE + N_bands | calculated | Per-frame `z_index` written to banded items |

**Output Range:** At `L_h = 360`, 17 bands; at the Deck's 1× field `L_h = 800`, 36 bands → `z ≤ 46`; at the largest ladder field (1440 rows at 1× on 1440p) 62 bands → `z ≤ 72` *(re-run 2026-10-26 — consistency check)*, far inside ±1024 and `CANVAS_ITEM_Z_MAX = 4096` `[BIN]`. The fallback is only engaged if 8.12 step 5 shows the Y-sorted layer above ≈ 300 draws; it changes nothing visible when the formula is right. Recomputed every frame because `cam_top` moves.
**Example:** `cam_top = 180`, wall `sort_y = 264` → `z = 10 + floor(84/24) = 13`; an actor at `y_foot = 270` → `z = 10 + 3 = 13`, same band, Y-sorted within it; an actor at `y_foot = 290` → band 14, drawn after every band-13 item.

**D.14 World-to-HUD projection (unjolted)**

The `world_to_hud` formula is defined as:

`s = (p − pose_q + L/2) · k + picture_origin + o`; `h = floor(s / H)`

**Variables:**
| Variable | Type | Range | Source | Description |
|----------|------|-------|--------|-------------|
| p | int pair | body px | D.9 | Object's integer body-frame position |
| pose_q | float pair | body px | D.9, **without** D.11's jolt | Camera position (unjolted — C-R) |
| L | int pair | logical size | D.3 / D.5 | World viewport logical size |
| k | int | D.1 / D.5 | D.1 | World scale (`k_z` at a ladder step) |
| picture_origin | int pair | screen px | D.2 / D.3 (bars) | Top-left of the picture in the window |
| o | int pair | screen px | D.9 | Container offset (0 unless `Q = 1/k`) |
| s | int pair | screen px | calculated | Screen position |
| H | int | D.4 | D.4 | HUD scale |
| h | int pair | HUD px | calculated | Position on the HUD layer, quantised to the HUD grid |

**Output Range:** `s` is integer because every term is; `h` is floored to the HUD grid so a prompt never sits on a fractional HUD pixel. An object outside `[0, L)` projects outside the picture and **gets no prompt** (never clamped to the frame edge, Section C C9). The function is the single projection every HUD anchor uses; nobody re-derives it.
**Example:** 1080p, `k = 3`, `H = 3`, `L = (640, 360)`, `pose_q = (1000, 500)`, object `p = (1100, 440)`: `s = ((100, −60) + (320, 180)) × 3 = (1260, 360)`; `h = (420, 120)`.

**D.15 Flash-count predicate**

The `flash_count` formula is defined as:

`t_i` is a *transition* when `|Y_i − Y_ref| ≥ 0.10 · Y_scale` and it reverses the sign of the previous transition; `F(window) = floor(n_transitions / 2)` over any sliding 1 s window; constraint `F ≤ F_MAX` evaluated per light, per region (3 × 3 grid of ≈ 213 × 120 base px), and on the frame mean

**Variables:**
| Variable | Type | Range | Source | Description |
|----------|------|-------|--------|-------------|
| Y_i | float | 0 – 1 (normalised luminance) | render frame sample | Luminance of the light's contribution, region mean or frame mean at frame i |
| Y_ref | float | 0 – 1 | last transition's level | Reference level since the last counted transition |
| Y_scale | float | 1.0 (frame/region) or the light's own peak | fixed | Denominator of the 10 % rule |
| F_MAX | int | 3 | ux-designer (decided; WCAG 2.3.1-style) | Flashes allowed per second |
| region | int pair | 213 × 120 base px | fixed (= frame / 3 per axis) | Regional ceiling area |
| F | int | 0 – unbounded | calculated | Opposing-pair count in the window |

**Output Range:** Compliant lights: the beacon sweep (one bright pass per 2–3 s → `F ≤ 0.5`), the cryo gutter (one dip per 10–20 s). Two individually compliant lights overlapping a region can sum to `F > 3` in that region; the regional and frame-mean checks exist for exactly that, and the lighting modifier contract (Lighting GDD) is what clamps — the pipeline hosts the measurement (flash scan, spike step). The **deployable flicker is unspecified** and must be given a number that passes (routed, Section C PR-13).
**Example:** A light alternating 0.9 / 0.4 every 100 ms: 10 transitions/s → `F = 5 > 3` → non-compliant; the clamp holds it at the last compliant level and diagnostics record `(light_id, F, window_start_tick)`. The same light at 250 ms per state: 4 transitions → `F = 2` → passes.

**D.16 Draw-call budget shape per viewport (art bible 8.6, cited)**

The `draw_budget` formula is defined as:

`D_world = D_floor + D_marks + D_ysort + D_caps + D_fx`; `D_root = D_hud + D_filter + D_hold`; `D_world + D_root ≤ D_BUDGET`

**Variables:**
| Variable | Type | Range | Source | Description |
|----------|------|-------|--------|-------------|
| D_floor | int | ≈ 4 | 8.6 (`rendering_quadrant_size = 16`) | Floor `TileMapLayer` |
| D_marks | int | 4 – 20 | 8.6 | Floor trails and stains |
| D_ysort | int | 50 – 300 (now **including** pipes + emissive 10 – 40, Section C PR-5) | 8.6; NOT SOURCEABLE until step 5 | The single multiplier |
| D_caps | int | ≈ 4 | 8.6 | Cap `TileMapLayer` |
| D_fx | int | ≤ 22 | 8.6 (fringe ≤ 10, ≤ 12 live particle emitters) | Effects |
| D_hud | int | 20 – 60 | 8.6 | HUD on the root |
| D_filter, D_hold | int | 1, 0–1 | PR-10, PR-3 | Container material pass; hold quad |

**Output Range:** `D_world ≈ 72 – 350`, `D_root ≈ 21 – 62`; the sum sits well inside 1000 unless `D_ysort` blows past 300, which is the trigger for D.13. Measured **per viewport** with `Viewport.get_render_info(RENDER_INFO_TYPE_CANVAS, RENDER_INFO_DRAW_CALLS_IN_FRAME)` `[BIN]` so the HUD's number is never confused with the sorted layer's. Zoomed out, `D_ysort` scales roughly with visible content — at the Deck's 1× field (D.5) about 4× the default content is in view, and at 1× on 1080p about 9×, so step 5 must be run at the largest ladder field of each target, not only at 1080p default (routed to technical-artist) *(Amended 2026-10-26 — consistency check)*.
**Example:** Spike room at 1080p default: `4 + 10 + 180 + 4 + 15 = 213` world, `40 + 1 = 41` root → 254 of 1000.

**D.17 Repeated-pose frames**

The `repeated_pose_fraction` formula is defined as:

without interpolation `R_0 = max(0, 1 − f_tick / f_render)`; with interpolation and quantum `Q` at speed `v`: `P_move = min(1, v / (Q · f_render))`, `R = 1 − P_move`

**Variables:**
| Variable | Type | Range | Source | Description |
|----------|------|-------|--------|-------------|
| f_tick | float | 60 Hz | kernel CR-1 | Tick rate |
| f_render | float | 30 – 240 Hz | engine | Render rate |
| v | float | 0 – v_max (base px/s) | Player Controller | Avatar speed |
| Q | float | 1 or 1/k | D.9 | Camera quantum |
| R_0, R | float | 0 – 1 | calculated | Fraction of frames that show the same pose as the previous frame |

**Output Range:** At 144 fps without interpolation 58.3 % of frames repeat (visible judder). With `tick_alpha` interpolation and `Q = 1` the repeat fraction is set by the speed: a 1 px/tick body (60 px/s) moves on 41.7 % of frames and repeats on 58.3 % — the same number, because interpolation cannot create motion smaller than one base px (snap is a floor, not a filter); at `v_walk = 120` it repeats on 16.7 %, at `v_run = 180` on 0 % (≥ 1 px every frame) *(re-run 2026-10-26 — consistency check)*. With `Q = 1/k` at `k = 3` the camera moves every frame above 48 px/s. At 60 fps every speed ≥ 60 px/s moves every frame. This is the honest promise: "base-pixel steps at interpolated timing, never twice in a frame, never backward" — step 12 measures it.
**Example:** 144 fps, `v = v_walk = 120`, `Q = 1`: `P_move = 120/144 = 0.833`, `R = 0.167`. Same at `Q = 1/3`: `P_move = min(1, 120/48) = 1`, `R = 0`. At `v = 60` (1 px/tick): `P_move = 0.417`, `R = 0.583`.

**D.18 Visual latency of continuous motion**

The `motion_latency` formula is defined as:

`latency = (1 − tick_alpha_draw) · tick_duration + L_render`

**Variables:**
| Variable | Type | Range | Source | Description |
|----------|------|-------|--------|-------------|
| tick_alpha_draw | float | 0 – 1 | kernel D.3, sampled when the frame is composed | Interpolation fraction at draw time |
| tick_duration | float | 16.67 ms | kernel D.1 | One tick |
| L_render | float | 0 – frame_duration | kernel D.4 | Wait from compose to present |
| latency | float | ≈ 0 – 33 ms | calculated | How far behind the committed state a moving sprite is drawn |

**Output Range:** With tick-before-render at 60 fps, `tick_alpha_draw ≈ 0` → one full tick (≈ 17 ms) plus `L_render` — the price of forbidding extrapolation (Pillar 1, kernel D.3). Discrete committed state (a light toggled, a trail appearing) and look-ahead (raw aim, D.7) carry no tick. At 144 fps the average `tick_alpha_draw ≈ 0.5` → ≈ 8 ms + `L_render`.
**Example:** 60 fps, `tick_alpha_draw = 0.05`, `L_render = 5 ms` → `0.95 × 16.67 + 5 ≈ 20.8 ms`.

## Edge Cases

*Each case states the exact outcome and what diagnostics record (the Debug Overlay's DIAGNOSTIC surface, Section C row 11; `diag.*` names are provisional).*

**Window, scale and aspect**

- **If the window is 1366×768 under `keep`**: `k = 2`; picture 1280×720 centred; bars 43/43 left/right and 24/24 top/bottom, true black, outside the world viewport. Under `expand`: logical 683×384, no bars, 14.2 × 8.0 tiles. Diagnostics record `diag.k = 2`, `diag.picture`, `diag.bars`. *D.1–D.3.*
- **If the window is 1365×768 (odd width)**: under `keep` bars 42 left / 43 right (floor/ceil split), 24/24; under `expand` logical 682×384 and a 1-screen-px residual bar split 0/1 — the half base column is never rendered fractionally. What the engine does with a non-integer base under `expand` is NOT SOURCEABLE; step 11 screenshots it and the GDD promise is "integer base size or letterbox". *D.2–D.3.*
- **If the window is 1600×900 under `keep`**: `k = 2`, bars 160/160 and 90/90 — 36 % of the screen black; the player is told in Settings that `expand` removes the bars once it ships. Under `expand`: 800×450, 16.7 × 9.4 tiles, inside the 960×540 cap. *The creative director recommended accepting the information gap and the user ratified it on 2026-10-26 (Open Question 9).*
- **If the window is 3440×1440 under `keep`**: `k = 4`, bars 440/440 left/right, none top/bottom. Under `expand`: 860×360, 17.9 × 7.5 tiles — the narrow cone's 8-tile reach fits entirely at ≈ 9 tiles half-width where 16:9 clips it at 6.7; recorded as the information caveat. Pass-B lit pixels 4.95 M (post-spike measurement).
- **If the window is 1280×800 (Steam Deck) under `keep`**: `k = 2`, bars 40 top / 40 bottom. Under `expand`: 640×400, 20 extra rows above and below; HUD anchors to the 640×400 picture; `H_default = 3` (D.4). Diagnostics log `screen_get_dpi()`, `screen_get_scale()`, `get_final_transform()` for the Deck's Linux session (step 11).
- **If the window is smaller than 1280×720** (user drags it, or a 1024×768 display): the window is refused below the minimum (`Window.min_size`); if the OS still delivers a smaller size (a display change), `k` is clamped to 2 and the picture is cropped centred rather than scaled below 2× — the only case where picture edges leave the window. Diagnostics record `diag.window_below_min = true` and the delivered size. *PR-1.*
- **If a resize arrives mid-frame**: the current frame completes at the old `k`; the next frame is one `RESCALING` frame (held, re-derive `k`, `H`, logical size, bars, limits, `world_to_hud`), then `PRESENTING`. No frame ever carries a fractional transform (step 11 screenshots the first frame after every change). A continuous edge drag is a sequence of one-frame `RESCALING`s debounced to the final size (→ ADR). Diagnostics record each `size_changed` with old and new `k`.
- **If a resize arrives mid-tick**: nothing — the sim never sees the window; `RESCALING` is a pipeline state and the kernel's tick is unaffected (CR-11). The first `_process` after the resize re-derives; `present_time` keeps advancing.
- **If fullscreen is toggled during breach**: identical to any resize — one `RESCALING` frame, no sim effect, no camera reaction beyond re-derivation; the camera pose is unchanged (the picture re-centres on the same `pose_q`). The beacon and vents keep their phase on `present_time`. Diagnostics record the mode change and that no cut fired (`diag.cut_reason` empty).
- **If the DPI changes** (`dpi_changed`, moving the window between monitors): `k` is re-derived from the new window **pixel** size only; DPI never enters D.1; `content_scale_factor` stays 1.0. If the pixel size did not change, nothing changes. Diagnostics record the DPI event and the (unchanged) `k`.
- **If `k_auto` changes while at a ladder step** (a resize while `k_z ≠ k_auto`): `k_auto` is re-derived in the same `RESCALING` frame and the ladder `[1, k_auto_new + 2]` rebuilt; `k_z` is kept if it is still on the ladder, else clamped to the nearer end; the step persists (it is never a hold); the field and the solver's `s` recompute. Example: 1920×1080 → 1280×720 while at `k_z = 2` keeps `k_z = 2`, which is now the default (`diag.zoom_step = 0`); the same resize while at `k_z = 5` clamps to 4 (`diag.zoom_step = +2`). Diagnostics record `diag.k_auto`, `diag.k_z`, `diag.zoom_step = k_z − k_auto`. *(Amended 2026-10-26 — consistency check.)*

**Zoom**

- **If the zoom input is pressed during a cut**: the cut completes first (the solver reset is one frame); the zoom step applies on the following frame as its own one-frame `RESCALING`. Two cuts in two frames are acceptable; a cut and a scale change in the same frame are not (two transforms to screenshot). Diagnostics record both with their frame numbers.
- **If `zoom_in` or `zoom_out` is pressed during `LOADING`**: the press is discarded (edge-triggered, never queued); the first `RUNNING` frame is the cut to the avatar at `k_auto` after a re-wake, or at the pre-load `k_z` after an in-session load (zoom is pipeline state, never saved, reset only on re-wake); a press after that steps normally. The player never sees a zoomed re-wake frame. *(Amended 2026-10-26 — consistency check.)*
- **If `zoom_in` is pressed at `k_auto + 2`, or `zoom_out` at `k_z = 1`**: ignored — no `RESCALING`, no cut; `diag.zoom_step_refused` increments. *(Added 2026-10-26 — consistency check.)*
- **If re-wake occurs while `k_z ≠ k_auto`**: the re-wake `CUT` resets `k_z` to `k_auto` so the cryo bay frames identically to the first wake; `diag.zoom_reset = REWAKE`. A body transfer, transit or in-session load leaves `k_z` unchanged across its cut. *(Added 2026-10-26 — consistency check.)*
- **If `zoom_out` is pressed down to 1× on a 2× world** (720p, Deck, manual 2×): `k_z = 1`, field = the full picture at 1 screen px per base px (Deck under `expand`: 1280×800 base = 26.7 × 16.7 tiles); 5.6 tier-2 marks are defined to survive 50 % so readability holds; the on-device acuity check is the accessibility-specialist's. **Draw-call consequence:** about 4× the default content is in view, so the Y-sorted layer's ≈ 300-draw ceiling (D.16) is tested at the zoomed Deck field, not at 1080p default — spike step 5 must include it (routed to technical-artist). **Lit pixels:** under pass B unchanged (the window is the window); under pass A — the default and the fallback — the world viewport renders at logical size, so the zoomed Deck target is 1280×800 texels = **4× the lit pixels of 640×400**, and light and shadow cost scales with it — the second reason steps 3 and 5 must be run at the largest ladder field of each target (1× on 1080p is 9× the default field). Diagnostics record `diag.k_z = 1`, the per-viewport draw count and the lit-pixel count. *(Amended 2026-10-26 — consistency check.)*
- **If a zoom step is pressed with reduced motion on**: the step still happens — it is a cut, not a motion, and it is the player's input. Look-ahead is already 0 under reduced motion, so the zoomed frame is centred on the avatar.
- **If a zoom step is pressed during a pause**: the press is discarded (the chart table owns input while paused and the world viewport is not re-rendering); the frozen frame stays at its current `k_z` (the world simply stopped, 7.4), and that `k_z` persists on resume with no `RESCALING`. *(Amended 2026-10-26 — consistency check.)*

**Follow, cuts and target discontinuities**

- **If the follow target teleports on re-wake**: `P_jump` fires (D.10) → cut; the solver resets to the target with look-ahead at full value from the current aim and jolt cleared; framing is identical to the first wake (same room, same lamp, same `pose_q` offset) — snap, not ease. Diagnostics record `diag.cut_reason = REWAKE` (the `LOADING → RUNNING` trigger) and the pose.
- **If the follow target teleports on pod arrival**: the arrival is a body transfer and a scene load → cut through `TRANSIT_HOLD`'s black; the first `PRESENTING` frame is already composed at the new body's frame. `diag.cut_reason = BODY_TRANSFER`.
- **If the avatar's body changes without a load** (weld merges the avatar's section into the ship; detach puts the avatar on a new body): the solver resets (`BODY_TRANSFER`); the new body's frame is placed so the avatar's screen position is unchanged where the layout allows, making the cut invisible; where it does not, it is a cut, never a pan. The detached section translates without rotation in the interior view. Diagnostics record the old and new body IDs and whether the screen position moved.
- **If a detach moves the camera's *limits* rather than its body** (the avatar stays on the ship, a section leaves): no cut; the `PROJECTOR` updates `limit_*` on the next tick's notification; if the current pose is now outside the rect, the solver enters `CLAMPED` and eases to the rect edge within `t_95 = 0.54 s` (D.6 on the clamped target). Diagnostics record the limit change.
- **If the target exceeds the pan ceiling for exactly `T_PAN` minus one frame**: no cut; D.8 holds the avatar inside the inner box and D.6 eases. The predicate is "held continuously for ≥ `T_PAN`" in seconds — at 60 fps that is 6 frames (5 do not fire), at 144 fps 15 (14 do not), at 30 fps 3 (2 do not). Diagnostics record the peak `v_follow` and duration as `diag.pan_near_miss`.
- **If the lag is exactly `LAG_CUT` (48 px)**: no cut — the predicate is strictly greater. At 48.01 the solver cuts. Diagnostics record the lag sample. The invariant `LAG_CUT > v_max·τ_follow` (48 > 32.4) means a legal run never approaches it; if Player Controller raises `v_max` to 200 px/s or more at `τ = 0.18` and `L_MAX = 72`, Section G's inner-box check fails the build (the inner-box invariant binds before the 266 px/s lag-cut bound).
- **If `prev == curr` on the first frame after a restore**: `p = floor(prev)` for every `tick_alpha` — the correct outcome (nothing moved). The camera's `P_jump` sees the target jump from its pre-load position → cut (`LOADING → RUNNING` also forces it). Diagnostics record `diag.cut_reason = LOAD`.
- **If an owner forgot `previous := current` in `post_restore`**: the first frame after a load interpolates from the pre-load `previous` toward the restored `current` — a sprite drawn partway between two unrelated places for up to one tick, and the camera cuts anyway. This is a defect, not a tolerated state: a debug assertion in the pipeline root checks, on the first `RUNNING` frame after `LOADING`, that every moving read view reports `previous == current`, and diagnostics name the offending block (`diag.stale_previous = [block_id]`). Routed to the kernel (CR-8 `post_restore`) as a required step.
- **If `tick_alpha` is reported > 1 or < 0**: impossible by kernel D.3 (clamped at the driver); the pipeline asserts `0 ≤ tick_alpha ≤ 1` in debug and, in release, clamps and increments `diag.tick_alpha_out_of_range`. A non-zero counter is a kernel defect.

**Jolt**

- **If two jolt events arrive in one frame or overlap**: amplitude is the **max**, not the sum (`|j| ≤ JOLT_MAX = 2` always); the decay restarts from the later event. Diagnostics record both event IDs and the chosen `J_0`.
- **If a jolt event arrives with reduced motion on**: `J_0 = 0`; the event is logged (`diag.jolt_suppressed = true`) so audio and the HUD glyph still carry it. Also at MVP the knob default is 0 regardless.
- **If a jolt is active during a cut**: cleared by the reset (D.10). The cut frame has `j = 0`.
- **If a jolt is requested by anything other than a P1 physical event** (Attention, Sanity, horror presentation): refused at the API — the jolt entry point accepts only tagged physical events; a debug assertion fires and diagnostics record the caller (`diag.jolt_refused = [caller]`). This is C1's anchor enforced.

**Look-ahead**

- **If the beam is off**: look-ahead follows the aim exactly as when it is on (reconciliation 3) — toggling the beam never moves the camera. Diagnostics record nothing special.
- **If the mouse aim flicks 180° in one frame**: `L_target` reverses; the slew clamp (D.7) limits the first frames to `144 × dt` px (2.4 px at 60 fps); 95 % of the reversal completes in ≈ 1.4 s; no cut predicate reads look-ahead, so no cut. The reversal is player-caused, satisfying C-R's 100 ms rule. Diagnostics record `diag.lookahead_slew_clamped = true` for the clamped frames.
- **If the aim is a zero vector or NaN** (no input yet, a disconnected device): `|aim| < ε` → `û` holds its previous value; on the very first frame of a session `û` defaults to the avatar's facing (down-screen south if none) and `L_target = L_MAX · û`. NaN is treated as zero. Diagnostics increment `diag.aim_invalid`.
- **If reduced motion is on**: `L_MAX = 0`, so `L_target = 0` and `L` eases to zero at the slew clamp when the setting is toggled mid-play; thereafter the camera is centred. Tremor, hairlines and audio carry the beam's forward information.
- **If look-ahead plus lag would push the avatar outside the inner box** (a 40 px target jump while aiming north: 40 + 72 = 112 > 108): D.8 pins the pose at the box edge for the first frames; the avatar stays inside the central 60 %; no cut (the jump is below `JUMP_CUT`). Diagnostics record `diag.inner_box_engaged = true`.

**Frame rate**

- **If the render rate is 30 fps**: two ticks per frame; `tick_alpha` clamps at 1 after the second; a 1 px/tick body shows 2-px steps; input-to-pixel worst case 50 ms (kernel D.4); the solver's `T_PAN` is 3 frames; `present_time` advances 33 ms per frame. The sim is unchanged (`state_hash` identical across caps — the acceptance test).
- **If the render rate is 144 fps**: ticks on ≈ 42 % of frames; with interpolation and `Q = 1`, at `v_walk = 120` 16.7 % of frames repeat the pose (D.17) *(re-run 2026-10-26 — consistency check)* — honest, not a bug; never a backward or doubled step (step 12). `T_PAN` is 15 frames. If testers name the stutter, the `Q = 1/k` path removes it above 48 px/s.
- **If the panel is 40 Hz or 90 Hz** (Steam Deck options): rates not multiples of 60; the interpolation path keeps steps even — at 40 fps `tick_alpha` alternates ≈ 0.5 / 1.0, at 90 fps ≈ 0.33 / 0.67 / 1.0. Spiked at both caps (step 13). Diagnostics record `Engine.get_frames_per_second()` and the repeated-pose count.
- **If the render rate falls below 20 fps**: the kernel runs up to `MAX_TICKS_PER_FRAME` ticks then lets sim time lag (CR-17); a catch-up burst that displaces the target ≥ 48 px in one frame cuts the camera (`P_jump`); smaller bursts ease under D.6/D.8. The pipeline never compensates visually. Diagnostics record frame time, ticks per frame and the kernel's lag counter.

**Sorting and drawing**

- **If two Y-sorted items have the same sort origin** (two actors on one row; an actor on the first legal row south of a wall): tile layers draw before free items on an equal row (layer order), so an actor sorts after a wall of equal `sort_y` and draws over its face; among free items the order is `x` ascending, then creation order. Deterministic across runs (→ ADR for the tie-break wrapper if Godot's tree order is insufficient). Diagnostics do not record ties (they are normal).
- **If an actor stands exactly on a wall footprint line** (`y_foot = y_0 + 24`, the footprint's south edge): this row is the first legal row south of the wall (the footprint rows are collision); `sort_y` equal → tie → actor over face. If physics ever places `y_foot` inside `[y_0, y_0 + 24)` the actor is behind the wall by D.12 and visibly wrong — a Ship Body collision defect, caught by the debug tree-walk's "actor inside footprint" check. Diagnostics record `diag.actor_in_footprint`.
- **If a prop is taller than the 72 px face** (`f > 72`, e.g. a 96-px-tall machinery node placed flush against a wall): its top rows overlap the cap band (D.12 cap rows), and because caps draw above the Y-sort band the cap is drawn over the prop's top — visibly wrong. Outcome: the kit rule "face height ≤ 72 for anything flush against a wall" is enforced by the asset checklist (8.9 addition, routed to art-director/level-designer); at runtime the debug tree-walk flags any Y-sorted item whose top row is above `sort_y − 72` while within 24 px of a wall footprint (`diag.cap_overlap = [item]`). Machinery nodes' own caps (96×96 cells) are part of the node sprite, not the cap layer, so a node *not* against a wall is fine.
- **If a horror segment is rotated under nearest sampling**: pass A — stair-stepped on the base grid, accepted; pass B — angled k×k blocks, tolerated for horrors and web only (8.4); the rotation is quantised to 15° (a test) and stepped at 12 fps. If the step-2 review rejects the pass-B look, 8.10's remedies apply (24-angle sets or a base-resolution sub-viewport for horrors → ADR). A human-built node with `rotation ≠ 0` is a lint failure, not a tolerated state (`diag.rotated_human_geometry = [node]`).
- **If the z-band index would overflow**: with `BAND_H = 24` and `L_h ≤ 800`, `z ≤ Z_BASE + 36`; overflow past `range_z_max = 1024` is unreachable at any supported field (D.13). If a future field or a smaller `BAND_H` approaches it, the pipeline clamps `z` to `range_z_max − 1` and increments `diag.zband_clamped` — items in the clamped band still sort within it. Section G flags `BAND_H < 12` as unsafe for this reason.
- **If the Y-sorted layer exceeds ≈ 300 draws at the spike**: the D.13 fallback is enabled by a build flag; wall faces and the wall-mounted layer leave the Y-sort node; nothing visible changes. Diagnostics record per-viewport draw counts before and after (D.16).

**Filter, layers and the HUD**

- **If the Perception Filter pass runs with no truth-channel mask available** (mask route not yet built, or the mask viewport failed to allocate): the pass **is not applied** — the container's material is set to null and the world is shown unfiltered rather than letting trails, sprites, beams or the true cone edge be touched (Pillar 1: a lie that corrupts the truth channel is the one failure the game cannot have). Per-element Filter materials inside layer 0 still run. Diagnostics record `diag.filter_pass_disabled = NO_MASK`; the Debug Overlay shows it in red.
- **If a HUD element is accidentally parented into the world `SubViewport`**: it is lit, glowed, filtered and scrolls with the camera — immediately visible; the debug tree-walk lints "any `Control` or node in the `hud` group under the world viewport" and fails the dev build (`diag.hud_in_world = [node]`). Release builds have no automatic remedy; the lint is in CI.
- **If a stray script sets `Camera2D.zoom` to anything but `(1,1)`**: the debug tree-walk (every frame behind the console toggle, and always at startup) resets it to `(1,1)`, logs the writer's stack and increments `diag.camera_zoom_violation`; the one frame rendered at the wrong zoom is accepted in dev and impossible in release because the startup assertion fails the build if any script references `Camera2D.zoom` as a writer (lint). Zoom-out is a logical-size change, never this property.
- **If `physics_interpolation` is turned on by a setting or a scene**: the startup assertion fails (`physics/common/physics_interpolation == false` is asserted); if toggled at runtime, the tree-walk logs `diag.second_interpolator = PHYSICS_INTERPOLATION` and the pipeline root sets `physics_interpolation_mode = OFF` on every presentation node it owns. The symptom it prevents: a sprite swimming 1–3 render px against the camera at 144 fps while looking fine at 60.
- **If the letterbox bars are touched by a post-process** (a Filter pass or a `BackBufferCopy` sized to the window instead of the picture): the bars are outside the world viewport and the container, so a correctly placed pass cannot reach them; if a pass is found to cover the window, it is a defect — the startup assertion checks the container's rect equals the picture rect, and the debug tree-walk flags any `CanvasItem` on the root whose rect exceeds the picture (`diag.bar_overdraw = [node]`). Bars stay true black; the optional Graphite hairline is a HUD element inside the picture edge, never in the bar.
- **If the HR-15 ambient-floor step is raised to its maximum**: hull geometry only brightens by the measured floor + 2–3 steps (Lighting's material); web, horrors and filaments stay beam-only; the HUD is untouched; the pipeline changes nothing — recorded here because it is the one brightness control the player has and a tester will look for a gamma slider that does not exist (`diag` records the HR-15 step in the frame header).

**Flash clamp and photosensitivity**

- **If a light's flash count would exceed `F_MAX = 3` in any 1 s window**: Lighting's modifier contract holds the light at its last compliant luminance until the window admits the next transition (the clamp *delays* transitions, it never brightens or darkens beyond the requested levels); the pipeline's flash scan records `(light_id, F, window_start_tick, clamped_transitions)`. The alarm beacon and the cryo gutter never trigger it; an unspecified deployable flicker might — hence the routed number.
- **If two individually compliant lights overlap one 213×120 region and their sum exceeds the regional ceiling**: the regional check catches it; the newer light's transitions are delayed first (it is the one that pushed the region over). Diagnostics record `(region_index, F_region, light_ids)`. The frame-mean check is evaluated after the regional one and catches the case where many regions are each just under.
- **If "reduce flashing" and "reduced motion" are both on**: both apply — deployables steady, arcs held as glow, beacon static mark + sound + HUD glyph, cryo gutter kept (flashing); look-ahead 0, jolt 0, world effects per HR-8 (motion). Neither touches trail propagation or `present_time`. No conflict: one is luminance, the other is motion.
- **If a full-frame flash is requested by any layer** (a hypothetical "white-out" effect): refused by construction — no API exists to write the whole frame's luminance; a shader that attempts it on the container violates PR-10 ("only adds violet/flesh at 4.4's places") and the flash scan's frame-mean check records it as a defect (`diag.frame_mean_flash`).

**Startup and configuration**

- **If `project.godot` is missing a required key** (the `[display]` block, `default_texture_filter`, either `snap_2d_*` key, `hdr_2d`, `use_debanding`): the debug startup assertion fails loudly with the key name, and the headless gdUnit4 parse test fails in CI before any window exists. A release build cannot be produced from a tree whose CI is red. Diagnostics (if the dev build is run anyway) print the full read-back table: `content_scale_mode`, `content_scale_stretch`, `content_scale_size`, world viewport filter/snap/hdr/debanding members, container `texture_filter`, `physics_interpolation`, `position_smoothing_enabled`, `Camera2D.zoom`, driver name.
- **If the world `SubViewport` does not read back the project's filter/snap settings** (propagation is NOT SOURCEABLE): the pipeline root sets the viewport's own members explicitly at boot regardless, so the read-back passes either way; step 11 records whether the explicit set was necessary.
- **If the driver reported at startup is not the one the spike measured** (`vulkan` `[BIN]` default vs D3D12 `[REF]`): nothing in this GDD assumes a driver; the name is logged (`diag.driver`) and V-sync/swap-chain behaviour is re-measured once on the other driver (step 13).

- **If the window is wider than 16:9 and `expand` is enabled**: the visible base field widens up to the 960×540 cap at the same integer `k`; the extra columns show whatever is there — honest void in every dark state, and extra lit floor only where loops are running (chiefly the stable home-ship). No information the beam has not paid for is revealed, because unlit geometry remains at the ambient floor and web content is never drawn unlit. Diagnostics record the expanded base size. *(Creative-director recommendation, ratified by the user 2026-10-26 — Open Question 9.)*

## Visual/Audio Requirements

The pipeline draws nothing of its own and plays nothing. What it *requires* of visual systems is the contract in Section C: every presentation element lives in the world `SubViewport` or on the root HUD layer, never both; every sprite and tile is placed at integer base pixels by presentation code (the viewport snap flags are a safety net, not the mechanism); human-built geometry never rotates at runtime; horror rigs and web pieces rotate only in the quantised steps the art bible fixes; VFX and one-off sprites use even cell sizes or `centered = false` with an integer offset so no origin lands on a half pixel; hairlines are sprites, never 1-px line draws (4.7 draws lines without an antialiasing feather). Two art-bible rules become pipeline rules here: the only brightness control in the game is the bounded ambient-floor step implemented as the hull-material raise (HR-15; no full-frame gamma or brightness exists), and letterbox bars, where they occur, are true black outside the world and get a 1-HUD-px Graphite hairline at the viewport edge so a bar is never read as unlit hull (routed to art-director). The art bible's 8.2 projection wording needs a one-clause amendment to the Y-sortable convention (height projects up-screen; faces occlude screen-north) — routed to art-director and technical-artist with the spike's Y-sort room as its test. Audio: none, except that the pipeline's render-rate loop is not the sim clock and audio cues keyed to tells must come from the simulation's notifications, never from frame events. No asset-spec flag: there are no assets.

## Game Feel

The camera is the one piece of this system the player feels, and its feel target is **absence**: a window bolted to the ship, not a lens that performs.

- **Stillness.** When the crewmate stops, the frame stops — within the follow time constant and then exactly. No bounce, no overshoot (the first-order follow cannot overshoot by construction), no idle drift, no breathing. The frame is the still thing readings move inside (art bible 7.3).
- **Follow.** First-order exponential follow, τ ≈ 0.18 s, no dead-zone by default (a dead-zone starts and stops discontinuously, which reads as "something happened"); the crewmate is held inside the central ~60 % of the frame at walking and running speed. Lag at run speed ≈ 0.6 tile.
- **Look-ahead.** The frame leans where the beam points: 1.5 tiles (72 px) along the aim direction by default, range 1.0–2.5, eased over τ ≈ 0.40 s from the *local raw* aim so a mouse flick does not yank the frame. This buys back cone rim on the short axis exactly when the player is looking there — because they looked, not because the game did.
- **Motion quantum.** The camera moves in whole base pixels (MVP). Motion at walking speed is uniform stepping, which reads as smooth; the 1/k-screen-pixel quantum is the named upgrade if testers name the stutter, and needs art-director and ux-designer sign-off because it lets the hero sprite wobble ±0.5 base px against the frame.
- **Rhythm independence.** Identical camera constants in boarding and breach; the frantic feeling comes from more things moving in the world, never from the frame fighting the player (art bible 2.8). Identical input traces produce identical camera traces in calm, tell and breach — an acceptance criterion, not a hope.
- **Cuts, not pans.** Re-wake, pod arrival, body transfer and any follow failure (pan-speed ceiling exceeded, lag over one tile — `LAG_CUT` 48 px, a 48-px jump) are cuts. The camera never pans across geography the player did not walk.
- **No shake in MVP.** A bounded physical jolt (≤ 2 base px, decayed, on hull separation / docking / detach only, never on Attention or sanity) exists as a knob defaulting to 0, to be revisited at VS-A once the HUD-still-over-moving-world read is tested. Hull stress goes into real needles, dust and hairlines.
- **Latency.** Input-to-pixel is the kernel's ≤ 1 tick plus one render frame; acknowledgement is same-frame on the presentation side. At 30 fps motion shows every second tick's pose honestly; at 144 fps interpolation with `tick_alpha` places each base-pixel step at the right instant and never twice in a frame.
- **Accessibility couplings.** Reduced motion disables look-ahead, zeroes the jolt and applies the HR-8 world-effect reductions listed in PR-14 and Tuning Knobs (static direction shapes, static alarm mark); it never suppresses trails, tremor, hairlines or audio. "Reduce flashing" is a separate toggle. Neither alters the tick.

*Anti-targets*: cinematic takeover, revealing pans, auto-aim, free-look, sanity wobble, handheld energy, Dutch tilt, non-integer or tweened zoom, sub-pixel camera position.

## UI Requirements

No player-facing UI of its own, but the pipeline defines three surfaces other systems' UI must fit:

1. **The HUD layer contract** (consumed by HUD & Suit Layer, Chart Table, Crew Strip, Interaction prompts): a root-viewport `CanvasLayer` outside the world `SubViewport`, integer-scaled at `H_default = max(k, 3)` — never below 3× on any display, player-raisable to 2×H in integer steps, labelled by resulting cap height; authored anchor-relative to the visible viewport edges with a 5 % safe margin in HUD pixels, surviving a fractional logical canvas (Steam Deck 1280×800 ÷ 3) and both `keep` and `expand` aspects; exempt by construction from world glow, ambient, occlusion, the Perception Filter pass, camera motion and jolt (HR-1, HR-13). World-anchored prompts project through the *unjolted* camera transform and are drawn only for lit objects; they never clamp to the frame edge. *Consequence routed to art-director*: on the Deck the 3× HUD over a 2× world exceeds 7.0's 12 % × 5 % critical-strip cap by default.
2. **The Settings surface** (consumed by Settings & Input Rebinding and Accessibility Settings — Alpha systems, but the hooks exist from MVP): display mode (fullscreen / borderless / windowed), resolution, world scale (Auto = largest integer that fits, plus an explicit integer list; manual scale below Auto forces `keep`), HUD scale, reading-face scale, aspect `keep` / `expand` once `expand` ships post-spike, frame cap and VSync (`ENABLED` default; `MAILBOX` as uncapped-no-tear; never `ADAPTIVE` by default), look-ahead strength (off / short / default; added to PR-14 by the amendment note below), reduce flashing (jolt strength is hidden until the VS-A jolt decision — a setting for a feature that ships at 0 is not exposed), reduced motion, and the bounded HR-15 ambient-floor step. No non-integer world scale is offered anywhere. Minimum window 1280×720. A first-launch photosensitivity notice is shown and reachable from settings, and a first-launch **guide-only** black-level calibration screen (a barely-visible reference swatch on the real floor values; writes no game value — user decision 2026-10-26) precedes it.
3. **The diagnostic surface** (consumed by the Debug Overlay, dev builds only, `DIAGNOSTIC` class): current k and H, window and viewport sizes, letterbox remainder, pass A/B mode, camera state (follow / clamped / zoom step / cut reason), quantum, frame time, per-viewport draw calls (world and HUD counted separately via `Viewport.get_render_info`), flash-clamp engagements per light and per region, fractional-transform tree-walk findings, startup-assertion results including the rendering driver name. Absent from release builds.

### Gaps / untestable as written

- **Headless runner.** `commands.test` runs `--headless` with no rasteriser, so every pixel-level criterion (frame diffs, checkerboard, HUD-layer diffs, anchor-pixel identity, band width, draw-call counts, the 60 s flash scan) is `(manual)` with retained evidence until a windowed CI runner exists. The `(auto)` set is confined to pure functions, scene-tree walks over unrendered scenes, `project.godot` parsing and lints. A windowed runner would promote roughly 20 criteria above to `(auto)`; recorded as a `/test-setup` item.
- **Open Question 1 (SubViewport wiring).** The `get_final_transform()` criteria are deliberately wiring-agnostic, but *how* the Deck reaches `(2,2)` world + `(3,3)` HUD in one frame, how a ladder step reaches `picture / k_z`, and the `1/k` quantum's container offset all depend on wiring (i) or (ii). Until the render-pipeline ADR lands those criteria can be written but not run; the HUD ≠ k case is MVP, so this is the first blocker.
- **Player Controller GDD (landed).** Every number derived from `v_walk` / `v_run` / `v_max` was re-run on 2026-10-26 against the Controller GDD's `V_WALK` 2 / `V_RUN` 3 px/tick (120 / 180 px/s): `lag_ss = v·τ_follow` = 21.6 / 32.4 px, `J_pan` 70.6 px (the 40 px catch-up burst now sits below the 225 px/s ceiling, so a 47 px burst is the worked near-miss), D.17's walk repeat fraction 16.7 % at 144 fps, and the `LAG_CUT > v_max·τ_follow` margin 48 > 32.4. If the Controller changes its knobs the criteria are re-run by the auto suite, not re-derived by hand. *(Amended 2026-10-26 — consistency check.)*
- **Ship Body & Interior Physics GDD (pending).** The body-transfer "same screen position → invisible cut", the `PROJECTOR`-set `limit_*` rect, and the "camera reads the body-local position pair" criteria assume the ship-local frame decision (Open Question 3). Fixtures stand in for Body; the tests re-point when it lands.
- **Perception Filter GDD (pending) and the mask route.** The anchor-pixel criterion assumes route (a) (a mask viewport) or (b) (per-element materials); the sampling method differs (mask coverage vs per-node material presence). "Sanity stage 3" is this GDD's name for the Filter's maximum; the Filter GDD must confirm the stage count.
- **Lighting GDD (pending).** The per-light flash clamp and regional delay-ordering criteria run against a Lighting stub; the clamp lives in Lighting's modifier contract. The HR-15 criterion depends on the hull material Lighting defines.
- **Deployable flicker (Open Question 6).** No number exists; the breach-composition scan can only pass once one is authored. Until then the scan's deployable row is `NOT ASSESSED — flicker unspecified`, not a pass.
- **Identical-input-trace test needs two things that do not exist:** a recorded input-trace format for the presentation side, and a way to force calm / tell / breach as read-view fixtures — which depends on the Attention and Horror GDDs. The criterion is written against stand-in fixtures.
- **Determinism tolerance.** D.6 claims identity "to floating-point precision" across 60 / 144 fps; two `exp()` steps are not bit-equal to one. The criterion asserts the quantised `pose_q` is identical and the raw `f` agrees within 1e-4 px — the gameplay-programmer should confirm that tolerance or name a different one.
- **Odd-window `expand` behaviour (1365×768) is NOT SOURCEABLE.** The criterion asserts the GDD's promise (integer base size or a 1 px letterbox); what the engine actually does is step 11's finding and may force a rewrite.
- **Y-sort tie-break wrapper (→ ADR).** The "identical on 10 runs" criterion holds only if the ADR delivers a deterministic wrapper; Godot's tree-order tie rule is `[UNV]`.
- **`diag.*` names are provisional.** Every criterion that reads a diagnostics key (`cut_reason`, `inner_box_engaged`, `camera_zoom_violation`, …) depends on names Section E marks provisional; the Debug Overlay GDD or the pipeline ADR should freeze them.
- **Performance figures are unsourced.** The GDD gives no pipeline CPU/GPU budget; the block above proposes 0.5 ms / 0.05 ms / 0.3 ms / 4 ms / 2 ms and cites 8.6 for memory. All are provisional until the spike; a miss logs, it does not fail.
- **`state_hash` across fps caps 30 / 60 / 144 / 0.** Written above under Pixel integrity & motion, but it is the kernel's G3 criterion (PR-11 says so) and runs in the kernel suite with the pipeline attached; a headless run cannot vary a real render rate, so the fps variation is a synthetic frame-time sequence fed to the driver — the on-build confirmation is spike step 13.
- **Release-build reactions** ("dev build halts", "release returns to the chart table") are observable only in a dev build or an export; the criteria test the dev halt and the export's file list, not the chart-table return.
- **Playtest metric (> 50 % away from default zoom).** A reporting criterion, not pass/fail; the game-designer owns the threshold's consequence.
- **Two additions this section makes that the GDD does not state.** The `(auto)` test directory `tests/unit/render/` is this section's proposal (the kernel GDD uses `tests/unit/kernel/`; the directory-structure doc should confirm the sibling). The `project.godot` parse test checks `physics/common/physics_interpolation = false`, which PR-15 (1) does not list among the written keys — it is in the read-back assertion (2) only; accept or strike.
- **Camera quantum `1/k` (Open Question 11).** The D.9 `o` offset criterion is `(auto)` on the formula only; the `1/k` path's container clipping and pass-B behaviour are `[UNV]` and untested until the quantum is adopted.
