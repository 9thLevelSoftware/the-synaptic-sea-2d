## Summary
Emitter State is the headless, integer-only simulation record of every light and the one question it answers (how much light reaches a registered point, and is it enough to work by); the GDD defers six implementation questions to ADRs and this ADR closes the four that belong to the Core tier (the integer cone and occlusion test, the pair cache, the work-unit budget, the lit hysteresis), plus the record and read-view shape (candidates 1 to 3 and 6). It fixes one SIM block `emitter.state` and one hook `emitter.place` whose eight work steps leave **no pending work past the tick**; an exposure kernel of pure functions in `src/shared/emitter/` (Q14 direction table, Q28 squared-cosine cone test, doubled-coordinate segment-versus-box test against ADR-0014 `QueryBuffer` spans, saturating illumination sum, hysteresis); a subject-major **pair-row cache** for resting subjects that is a pure optimisation (every answer equals the cache-free oracle), with moving subjects re-evaluated and the dynamic layer recomputed every tick; an Emitter-owned **section-local point grid** for subject and static-emitter candidates (the subject index ADR-0014 disclaims); a **counted-work-unit budget** that is a deterministic function of canonical inputs, never of time; and a restore that cold-builds the table the last `emitter.place` committed over end-of-tick upstream state (the `lit` bits copied verbatim and never recomputed, derived reads allowed to differ for one run, D14.4), which needs **pending-intent columns** the GDD's canonical list omits (without them a late-hook registration breaks kernel save-equivalence G4).

## Context

### Problem Statement
Five things must be true at once. (1) Every consumer that asks "is this lit" (Interaction's lit-only rule, Horror targeting, Attention's light stimulus, Suit's beam drain, Lighting's drawn edge) must get the same answer from one place, and that answer must be a pure function of committed integer state so that a hallucination can never move the edge (P1) and Attention can run headless and on the host (kernel H10). (2) The answer must be exact under the project's determinism contract (ADR-0005): integer-only, order-independent, identical across machines and across save/restore (kernel G4). (3) It must fit a per-tick budget the GDD prices at the very top of its range already (D.14: the typical design point sits at 99.9 % of `B`), in a language whose op cost is not sourceable from the pinned engine reference. (4) The geometry must agree with what Lighting draws, to the pixel, at a hard edge. (5) The GDD left six implementation questions open and tagged them for ADRs; stories cannot be cut from an Emitter that has no decided occlusion convention, cache, budget or read-view layout.

### Constraints
- **Binding Foundation stances (Proposed ADR-0001..0010, registry `docs/registry/architecture.yaml`):** simulation code is scene-tree free and integer-only (`float_or_wall_clock_in_sim`, `scene_tree_or_engine_server_in_sim_layers`, SIM-01..06); division and modulo only through `IntMath` (`integer_division_outside_int_math`); no `assert(` in `src/` (SIM-11); no physics API on any simulation path (SIM-06); a block is a `RefCounted` with typed fields, restored by copying into the existing object, and its snapshot payload is a String-keyed Dictionary of duplicated packed columns (ADR-0003 § 2, ADR-0006 § 1.2); dirty keys are marked explicitly inside mutators (`write_outside_writescope_or_setter_dirty`); `Packed*Array` is never returned from an accessor and every read view is getter-only (`packed_array_exposed_or_setter_tracked`, CNT-03); injected seams are `@abstract` ports (ADR-0010 § 3) and the work counter is a `WorkCounter` port with `NullWorkCounter` in every shipped and benchmarked build (ADR-0010 § 4.4); "zero allocation" is claimed only as HOT-01 plus CNT-01..03 (ADR-0010 § 7).
- **Wave B drafts this ADR builds on (all Proposed):** ADR-0014's one rect set behind `OccluderSource.rects_in(body_id, ax0, ay0, ax1, ay1, out)` with `QueryBuffer` columns `keys/spans/flags` and counters, `version/static_version/leaf_version` plus `version_epoch`, and `changed_region_kind`/`changed_region_box_into`; ADR-0013's section-local anchors, `body_of`, `section_offset_x/_y`, `anchor_geometry` and `subject_anchors`; ADR-0012's three Graph hooks, M14 schedule, persisted `n_served` and descriptor-store window; ADR-0016's two-phase restore, Tier S/D reads, flat `binding_source` and V9 verifier row; ADR-0015's `BodyView` and ADR-0019's `ControllerView`.
- **GDD invariants that do not move:** the Emitter never obtains a `PERCEPTION_OUT` view and no record has a `phantom`/`perceived`/`decoy` field (EM-29); the owner stands in their own light (EM-21); the beacon never gates actions (EM-16); `lit` is a canonical hysteresis bit with no dwell (EM-22); an unregistered ref answers 0/false in release (EM-21).
- **Budget:** `emitter.place` plus exposure at the registry value `EXPOSURE_BUDGET_MS` (P99, release export, RM-DESKTOP and RM-DECK), rare scenarios at `EXPOSURE_TICK_CEILING_MS`, cold rebuild at `COLD_REBUILD_CEILING_MS`; kernel P3 target 2.0 ms P99 shared with the Graph and Controller/Body; the hook owner's whole-hook figure is its row in the ADR-0003 P3 share ledger (CT-20), so this ADR's row is the whole `emitter.place` hook including the Emitter-box share of ADR-0014's `solid_in` and `rects_in` queries. Every GDScript cost is a model parameter until EM-SPIKE-1 (D.14).
- **GDScript constraints found by probing (section 17):** no `const PackedInt32Array`; the floor root is `sq.bsearch(d2, false) - 1` and never the default `bsearch` form (P7); a `PackedByteArray` element is assigned only an int in 0..255 (`1 if x else 0` for flags), never a `bool` (P8); `static var` is banned; `/` and `%` are banned in `sim`; `min`/`max`/`clamp`/`abs`/`sign` untyped builtins are banned and `mini/maxi/clampi/absi/signi` are the sanctioned forms (ADR-0011 probed them under the warning policy).

### 1. Module layout, purity split and what is data (TR-emitter-003, -006, -016, -017, -030, -034, TR-itemdb-034, TR-derelict-038)

| Location | Contents | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| `src/shared/emitter/` (L0) | `emitter_kind.gd` (closed enums and transition tables), `dir_table.gd` (**generated**, `const UX: Array[int]`, `const UY: Array[int]`), `cone_math.gd`, `segment_math.gd`, `illumination.gd`, `lamp_math.gd`, `wane_math.gd`, `beacon_math.gd`, `exposure_probe.gd` | static functions over ints only; no state, no I/O, no `Node`; imports nothing but `IntMath`. This is the "published table and kernel" that `src/data` and the content lint may name (ADR-0007 section 1.9: `src/data` never imports `src/sim`). |
| `src/data/emitter/` | `shape_table_schema.gd`, `hue_map_schema.gd`, `emitter_tuning_schema.gd`, `glow_table_schema.gd`; catalogs `ShapeCatalog`, `HueMap`, `EmitterTuning`, `GlowTable` | stage-1 closed schemas and frozen catalogs (ADR-0007 sections 1.4, 1.5); array exposure per CNT-03 (`Array[int]` read-only, or index accessors) |

**D1.1 Purity split.** The exposure kernel is pure functions of ints so that unit tests, the content lint (TR-derelict-038: "console, arrival and wake-room lighting proved headlessly") and the block call the same code. `ExposureProbe` composes the kernel with a caller-supplied `OccluderSource` double and plain emitter fields; it is **not** the VS-A `probe(body_id, point)` read (deferred and lint-banned in MVP source) and is importable from `src/sim/` only inside `testing/` and the boot checks.

**D1.2 What is data (decided).** The shape table (11 rows, EM-8), the hue token set and every tunable are **stage-1 content**, not code constants: `assets/data/emitter/{shape_table,hue_map,emitter_tuning}.json` plus the Failure & Trail glow rows in `assets/data/failure_trail/glow_table.json` (ADR-0007 section 1.1; architecture IC-12). Reasons: gameplay values are data-driven (coding standards); the technical artist owns the placeholders and re-tunes after the Lighting spike; and the Item DB `emits` clause (TR-emitter-043, TR-itemdb-034) needs a table to validate against at load, which ADR-0007 section 1.4 says the loader must **read from the Emitter's published table and not copy** (stage 1 loads before the Item DB, section 1.7). **`ShapeCatalog` is that published table.** Its authored columns are `shape_id`, `curve_class`, `geometry` (`ONE_CONE`, `TWO_CONES`, `OMNI`), `half_angle_units`, `split_axis_units`, `reach_px`, `gates_actions`, `shadow_casting` (informational) and `art_ref`; the freeze **derives** `cos2_q28` from `DirTable` (the square of the Q14 cosine at `half_angle_units`) instead of storing a second hand-typed integer, so the table, the direction table and the I-OVERFLOW bound cannot disagree. The five derived values equal the GDD's EM-8 column exactly (probed, section 17). The art-bible degrees stay in the art bible; `tools/emitter/check_shapes.gd` checks each `half_angle_units` against its degree figure (float allowed there) and fails the build on drift over its tolerance.

**D1.3 The direction table is generated code, not content (decided).** `DirTable.UX`/`UY` are mathematical constants (two `AIM_UNITS`-entry `Array[int]` consts), not designer data, so they bypass the content loader: a lint or test can use them with no `ContentSet`, and an engine change cannot reorder a JSON array under them. ADR-0007 Alternative 2 (code-as-data) is rejected for *content*; it does not cover a derived mathematical table, and `tools/emitter/gen_dir_table.gd` plus a CI byte-diff gate (`EMT-GOLD-01`) make regeneration a build step with a golden hash. **Source of truth (E6):** the committed `dir_table.gd` is the source of truth, and the golden hash of its contents is checked on every CI platform; the generator runs on every CI platform but only one designated platform's output is byte-diffed against the committed file, because GDScript `cos`/`sin` call the platform libm (MSVC on Windows, glibc on Linux) and a last-ulp difference at a `round()` tie is possible with no real defect behind it (P2 was probed on Windows only). The named fallback, if a cross-OS mismatch is ever seen, is an integer-only CORDIC or Taylor generation of the first quadrant in the tool, so that the table does not depend on libm at all. Generation: the first quadrant `a = 0..AIM_UNITS/4` is computed in the tool as `round(16384 * cos/sin(a * TAU / AIM_UNITS))`, and the other three are filled **by reflection**, never recomputed (`DIR[AIM_UNITS/2 - a] = (-ux, uy)`, `DIR[AIM_UNITS - a] = (ux, -uy)`), then `uy` is negated so that aim `3 * AIM_UNITS / 4` is `(0, +16384)` (south, screen y down, EM-19). I-MIRROR is therefore true by construction and re-asserted at boot. **Probed:** `const X: PackedInt32Array = PackedInt32Array([...])` fails to parse ("isn't a constant expression"); `const X: Array[int] = [...]` loads (4,096 elements, `is_read_only()` true). The kernel reads `DirTable.UX[a]` directly in `@hot` code (an indexed read of a typed constant array: no copy, no allocation). The table length must equal `AIM_UNITS` (boot invariant `I-DIRSIZE`).

**D1.4 Square-root table (decided).** The distance `d = floor(sqrt(d2))` of D.7 is the largest `k` with `k*k <= d2`, found by the native `bsearch` of a boot-built `PackedInt32Array` of squares (`SQ[k] = k*k`, `k = 0..REACH_MAX_PX + 1`) owned by `EmitterDerived` and passed to the kernel (`static var` is banned, so the table is an instance field, not a global). **Call form (E1):** `floor_root(sq, d2) = sq.bsearch(d2, false) - 1`, a single shared function (`ConeMath.floor_root` in `src/shared/emitter/`; if Foundation prefers it in `IntMath` as `IntMath.isqrt_table`, the signature is unchanged) used by the block, the content lint and the tests, with a fixture over every `d2` in its precondition range. The default `bsearch(v, before = true)` returns the lower-bound insertion index and is **wrong for every non-square `d2`** (probed on squares `[0,1,4,9,16,25,...]`: `bsearch(16)` = 4 is right, `bsearch(17)` = 5 is wrong, the floor root is 4; with `before = false` and `- 1` there were 0 failures over `d2` 0..81, P7). **Precondition:** `0 <= d2 <= (REACH_MAX_PX + 1)^2`, so the result stays in `0..REACH_MAX_PX + 1` and the table has `REACH_MAX_PX + 2` entries. Exactness is a boot invariant (`I-SQRT`: for every `d2` in `0..(REACH_MAX_PX + 1)^2` the result `r` of the shared function satisfies `r*r <= d2 < (r+1)*(r+1)`, so the invariant tests the same code the hot path calls). Probed 2026-10-29 (P4; the call form of the timed `bsearch` was not recorded, so the normative form is the one above and EM-SPIKE-1 re-times it): 0 failures over all 147,457 values, **63 ns** per call against **397 ns** for a binary-search loop and 144 ns for `bsearch` on the read-only `Array[int]` (so the packed table is built once at boot, cold, and held by the instance). No `sqrt`, float or `Vector2` appears on the path (SIM-03/04).

### 2. The block: what is canonical and what is derived (TR-emitter-001, -005, -007, -028 (canonical part), -030, -037)

**D2.1 Descriptor values** (the per-block values ADR-0003 leaves to the owner; TR-emitter-001): `block_id &"emitter.state"`, `owner_name &"emitter"`, `schema_version 1`, `supported_versions [1]`, `block_class SIM`, `persist true`, `persist_scope WORLD`, `scope SHARED`, `gameplay true`, `interpolated false` (so `commit_baseline`/`snap_baseline` are not registered), `repl_hint SHARED_SUMMARY`, `restore_after [layout.state, graph.state, body.state, controller.state]`, `added_in_format 1` (the format data file entry of Save/Load SL-15; the boot lints `SL_BOOT_NO_SUPPORTED_VERSIONS` and `SL_BOOT_NO_ADDED_IN_FORMAT` therefore pass), `init_world` **not** registered (Web Map's `init_world` calls E1, E9, E4 and E10 under the shared INIT scope in the order of the ADR-0026 row of Cross-ADR Assumptions, D3.4), `binding_source` registered (D3.5), and `default_state` registered (mandatory, ADR-0003 section 2; T13): empty columns, `next_emitter_id` 1, `last_place_tick` -1, `fault_step_seen` -1, passing `EmitterVerify` and accepted by this block's own `restore` (ADR-0016 asks each owner for a `default_state` its own `restore` accepts). `read_view` returns the long-lived `EmitterView` (section 13); `empty_view` its `EmitterViewEmpty` twin.

**D2.2 One class per concern (decided).** `EmitterState` holds the persisted columns only (typed packed arrays and ints; no cache, no scratch, no `Dictionary`); `EmitterDerived` holds every rebuildable structure; `EmitterScratch` holds per-tick temporaries sized once; `EmitterDiag` holds counters (never hashed). This is ADR-0011's four-class pattern applied to a smaller block. It makes "persisted versus derived" checkable by reflection and by a **zero-and-diff** test (zero every `EmitterDerived` column, call `post_restore`, assert the block hash and every read-view answer unchanged), which is the executable form of TR-emitter-007.

**D2.3 The persisted set (closed; the snapshot key set is generated from this table and an allow-list test walks it, as ADR-0013 AC-130).** `snapshot(ctx)` calls `ctx.put` with a String-keyed Dictionary, every packed column a `duplicate()`, rows in ascending id or ref, no `Dictionary` keyed by an id, no `StringName`/`PackedStringArray`/`Vector2i`/`Rect2i`/`Resource` (ADR-0006 section 1.2, ADR-0013 section 2).

| Key | Type | Contents |
|---|---|---|

**Column write rule (E2).** The byte columns (`kind`, `subkind`, `shape_id`, `switch_on`, `beacon_dir`, `priority`, `pending` on emitters; `kind`, `moving`, `lit`, `pending`, `has_pend_anchor` on subjects) are written only as `1 if x else 0` (flags) or as an int already known to be 0..255, and read as `b[i] != 0`. On 4.7.2 `b[i] = true` on a `PackedByteArray` is not caught at parse time and fails at run time ("Invalid assignment of property or key 0 with value of type bool on a base object of type PackedByteArray"), and an int above 255 wraps silently (300 stores as 44; the kind-default `priority` BEAM = 255 sits exactly at the limit and the `priority` knobs are tunable data, P8). The `EmitterTuning` schema therefore gives `priority`, and the shape table gives `subkind` and `shape_id`, a closed 0..255 range, and `restore` range-checks every byte column (`EMITTER_RESTORE_RANGE`, D14.1). The `Int32` and `Int64` columns also wrap silently on overflow, so the int32 bounds (`I-OVERFLOW`, `EMITTER_ID_EXHAUSTED`) cover **every** Int32 column and not only `next_emitter_id` (D2.4, D15). Lint `EMT-08` flags a `bool` right-hand side assigned to a `Packed*Array` element (D16.3).

Everything else is `EmitterDerived`: `body_id` of every record and subject (never stored, never hashed, EM-4), `origin_px`, `aim_units`, `half_angle_units`, `reach_px`, `output_pm`, record `lit`, `r_lit_px`, `r_off_px`, `curve_class`, top ids, the slot stores, grids, pair rows, raw sums, per-subject `lux_pm`/`act_lux_pm`/`top_emitter_id`, the section cache, the section-emission array, last-emitted dirty buckets, the version cache (D11.4) and every scratch buffer. `state_hash` over this block is the kernel's `block_hash` of `canonical_encode(snapshot payload)` (ADR-0005 section 4.3); **the block exposes no `canonical_bytes()` and no `block_hash()`** (EM-25 as amended 2026-10-29), and the round-trip test uses the kernel-side reads `canonical_encode` and `block_hash(&"emitter.state", uncached = true)` (TR-emitter-038, owned by ADR-0003/0005; this ADR supplies the payload).

**D2.4 Capacity and size.** Row counts are bounded by `MAX_EMITTERS` and `MAX_EXPOSURE_SUBJECTS` (registry); a snapshot at both caps is tens of KB of column data at the very most (about 96 x 100 plus 192 x 40 bytes), far below the payload ceiling (ADR-0002 `payload_size_check`, ADR-0006 `MAX_BLOCK_BYTES`); `snapshot()` computes the byte estimate from the counts and raises the one-shot diagnostic `emitter_block_soft_bytes` over `EMITTER_BLOCK_SOFT_BYTES` (an `EmitterTuning` key). **Int32 column sufficiency (CT-24):** stored anchors and pending anchors are section-local px bounded by section extent, derived origins are body-frame px bounded by `BODY_COORD_LIMIT` (ADR-0015: at most 2^24, derived from the maximum section extent times the maximum sections per body, restore-checked, repeated by Layout in `can_attach`), ticks are `Int64`, ids are bounded by `EMITTER_ID_EXHAUSTED`, and `I-OVERFLOW` proves the bound for every Int32 column at boot; ADR-0015 also publishes the canvas bound `|world_a - world_b| + |pos| < 2^23` under `WEB_COORD_BUDGET_PX`, which concerns presentation and not this block.

**D2.5 Pending-intent columns: the finding that changes the canonical list (decided; GDD sync).** EM-28 lists the canonical set without them, and EM-5/EM-23 say the subject table "first reflects spawns, E7 unregisters and E8 anchor moves at the first `emitter.place` at or after that tick". Hooks ordered **after** `emitter.place` call mutators in the same tick (Interaction's landing hook: E3 and E6/E7; Horror's hook: E6/E7/E8). A snapshot taken at the end of that tick holds the late mutation in the record columns while the live subject table does not yet reflect it; a restore that cold-builds the table from every row would reflect it at once, so `validate` at tick T+1 would answer differently in the live and the restored run: **kernel G4 breaks** under the GDD's own wording. This ADR therefore makes the lag canonical and small: `emitters.pending` (1 = created since the last committed `emitter.place`), `subjects.pending`/`has_pend_anchor`/`pend_x`/`pend_y` (a registration, an unregistration or an anchor move not yet applied to the table) and `hdr.last_place_tick`. Rules: a creation mutator sets `pending = 1` and fills the record's **derived** fields synchronously (TR-emitter-037: the read view is complete from P4 of the creating tick) but the record contributes to no pair row, no subject sum and no `section_emission` until W3 of the next run, which clears every `pending`; E6 on a new ref sets `subjects.pending = 1` (counted against the caps and the duplicate check at once, absent from the table: `lux`/`lit` read 0/false); E7 sets `pending = 2` (the row stays in the table until the next run, then is removed); E8 writes `pend_x/pend_y` and `has_pend_anchor = 1` (the applied `anchor_x/y` is what the table was built from). A mutator that runs **before** `emitter.place` in its tick (any P2 apply; a hook the sort places earlier) is applied by that tick's own W3, so no `pending` row survives a tick in which its creator ran earlier. `post_restore` builds the table from the **applied** view (emitters with `pending` 0; subjects with `pending` 0 or 2; the applied anchor) and derives with `T_derive = hdr.last_place_tick`; the next run applies the pending rows exactly as the uninterrupted run would. The cost is one byte per row, a header int and ten bytes per subject; the gain is that G4 holds with no exception list. `post_restore` reads **end-of-tick** upstream state, not the state the last run saw, which is why only the canonical-backed reads are G4-exact straight after a restore (D14.4).

**Pending combinations (`TRANSITIONS_PENDING`, T11).** The combinations a late hook can produce within one tick are a closed table in `src/shared/emitter/emitter_kind.gd`, next to `TRANSITIONS_RECORD` and `TRANSITIONS_BIT`; each changes the caps, `subject_headroom()` and the restore validation below, so each is a fixture. Subject pending state `p` (0 applied, 1 register-pending, 2 unregister-pending): **E6** on 0 or 1 is a no-op reporting `DUPLICATE`; E6 on 2 returns the row to 0 with its applied anchor unchanged, or to 0 with a pending anchor (`has_pend_anchor` 1) if the anchor differs, and reports `DUPLICATE`. **E7** on 1 drops the row at once with no table effect (the headroom is returned immediately); E7 on 0 sets `p = 2` and clears `has_pend_anchor`; E7 on 2 is a no-op. **E8** on 0 writes `pend_x/pend_y` and sets `has_pend_anchor`; a repeated E8 overwrites `pend_x/pend_y`; E8 on 1 writes the applied anchor directly (the row is not in the table yet); E8 on 2 is a no-op. Emitter `pending`: E2 on a `pending` 1 record removes it with no table effect. `EMITTER_RESTORE_SUBJECTS` additionally rejects `has_pend_anchor` set on a row whose `pending` is not 0, and non-zero `pend_x/pend_y` with the flag clear.

**Mandatory fixtures for the ADR-0016 permutation harness (KRN-RST-05; T1, T11).** (a) An E6, an E7, an E8 and an E3 issued by a hook ordered after `emitter.place` on the snapshot tick. (b) Every combination of the table above on the snapshot tick (E6 then E7 on a register-pending row; E7 then E6 on an unregister-pending row; E8 on a register-pending row; a second E8 on a row that already has `has_pend_anchor`). (c) Interaction's hook calls Graph M6 (`salvage_node`) on a lit lamp's node on the snapshot tick. (d) A late hook sets a `loco_state` to DEAD on the snapshot tick. Each asserts the live run and the restored run agree on `lit(ref)`, `beam_of`, `subject_headroom()` and the registration set at T+1, and that the pending set equals the live run's.

**D2.6 Ids and iteration (TR-emitter-005).** `next_emitter_id` is allocated by the block (kernel CR-8), starts at 1, is persisted, is never reused and is checked at restore (`EMITTER_RESTORE_COUNTER`: it exceeds every stored id). Records are appended in ascending id and removed by deleting the row; every iteration is over rows in ascending id or subjects in ascending ref (the persisted order), through the rank indexes of the slot stores (D10.1). No `Dictionary` is iterated anywhere in `src/sim/emitter/` (lint `EMT-02` extends SIM-09 and DET-01 to the directory); `sort_custom`, `shuffle` and `pick_random` are banned by DET-01 already.

**D2.7 Closed kind set and the no-perception-field rule (TR-emitter-006, EM-29).** `EmitterKind` is a closed enum: `BEAM, FIXTURE, NODE_LANTERN, DEPLOYABLE, BEACON, FAULT_GLOW`; subkinds `FIXTURE_EMERGENCY`, `FIXTURE_DOCK_STATUS`, `DEPLOYABLE_GLOWSTICK` (`FLARE`, `TORCH`, `DECOY` are VS-A and absent until a schema bump). Ids are code constants locked by `assets/data/emitter/ids.lock.json` (ADR-0007 section 3 ledger), so an enum reorder cannot silently change a saved byte. The column list above is the whole record; lint `EMT-04` fails any identifier `phantom`, `perceived`, `decoy`, `hallucinated` and any `PERCEPTION_OUT`/`PerceptionRef`/`true_value` token in `src/sim/emitter/` and `src/shared/emitter/`.

### 3. Registration, commands and mutators (TR-emitter-002, -004, -009, -010, -011, -012, -013, -035, -037, TR-interact-036, TR-boarding-013, TR-webmap-028, TR-light-033)

**D3.2a Who checks reach, focus and `NOT_LIT` for a fixture switch (decided; T3, CT-47).** A command type has exactly one `validate` (IA-3, ADR-0002), and for `set_switch` it is this block's. **D3.6 Closed transition table and the invalid-transition counter (TR-emitter-045).** The record lifecycle (ABSENT, OFF, ARMING, ON, STARVED, WANING) and the subject bit (UNREGISTERED, UNLIT, LIT) are two small tables in `src/shared/emitter/emitter_kind.gd` as data (`TRANSITIONS_RECORD`, `TRANSITIONS_BIT`: a `from * N + to` bitmask over a closed list). Every state change in `src/sim/emitter/` goes through `EmitterDerived.move_state(row, to)`, which checks the table: an unlisted pair leaves the state unchanged, increments `emitter_invalid_transition[from * STATES + to]` (a `PackedInt32Array` of `STATES * STATES` counters in `EmitterDiag`, labelled by from and to in the dump) and reports through `Diagnostics` in a debug build (no `assert(`). The record state itself is **derived** (computed from `switch_on`, `arm_tick`, `served_pm`, liveness and life); `move_state` is the choke point that audits each derived change against the closed table and is what AC-109 drives with an injected illegal request (for example WANING to OFF).

**D3.7 Commit-spacing guard lives in the commit path too (TR-emitter-011, TR-light-033).** `toggle_beam.validate` rejects `TOO_SOON` and `set_switch.validate` rejects `COOLDOWN` when `last_toggle_tick >= 0` and `commit_tick - last_toggle_tick < SWITCH_MIN_SPACING_TICKS`, with `commit_tick` per D.2b: the apply tick for a beam toggle, for an OFF that darkens a lit or armed lamp and for every switch of a self-powered fixture; `arm_tick` (D5.2) for the ON of a Graph-powered lamp; an OFF applied while ARMING is exempt and writes nothing; E10 and `force_off` are exempt. **The guard is also enforced where the transition is committed** (TR-light-033: "a command-level spacing guard alone is proven insufficient under arming-latency jitter"): W1 writes `last_toggle_tick = arm_tick` at the run in which an ON is realised and compares it with the previous value; a realised transition closer than `SWITCH_MIN_SPACING_TICKS` (reachable only through arming-latency jitter that `validate` could not foresee, or a non-`validate` writer) increments the release counter `emitter_flash_rate_defect`, is **still committed** (refusing here would make the light disagree with the committed switch, and Lighting's flash governor may not retime a committed switch) and reports through `Diagnostics` in a debug build. The "at most three flashes per second" arithmetic is the I-SPACING derivation (D15.1).

**D3.8 `toggle_beam` and Suit.** `toggle_beam.validate` reads Suit's charge view for `CHARGE_EMPTY` (`SuitView.beam_charge_pm(player_id)`, an ADR-0027 assumption in `cross_adr_assumptions`); a turn-OFF is never rejected for charge. Suit's `suit.vitals` hook (before `emitter.place` in the compiled order) reads the beam record `lit` and calls E5 at charge zero, so a `force_off` takes effect in the same tick's run. A BEAM record's `lit` and `output_of` derive from its own canonical `switch_on` and `BEAM_BASE_PM` and from no late-mutable upstream block, so this read is **exempt** from the derived-read restriction of D14.4 (the restriction covers lamp-kind records, subjects and `section_emission`). The beam is a suit load and never calls M14.

### 4. The hook: order of work, and nothing pending past the tick (TR-emitter-002, -021, -022, -025, -037, TR-interact-038)

**D4.1 Eight steps, fixed order, one pass each.** `emitter.place` does exactly the following, in this order, and nothing else; each step is a bounded loop over a count or a literal (no iterate-to-convergence, no recursion, no catch-up):

| Step | Work | Reads | Writes (derived unless marked) |
|---|---|---|---|

The static layer's contract is **never stale and synchronous** (EM-23): W3 and W4 finish every update inside the tick, before W6 writes the table, so the staleness bound of D.13 (age 0 for a reader `after: [emitter.place]`, age 1 for P2 `validate`, a render-rate poll and a hook ordered earlier) holds for every reader; a tick whose updates exceed the ceiling still completes them and counts `emitter_rebuild_over_ceiling` (D12.4). GDD alternative (b), a counted-unit cap with a deferral queue, is rejected for the same reasons as in EM-23: it would make `lit` depend on a derived queue that a restore (which rebuilds cold and complete) cannot reproduce.

**D4.2 No snapshot copy (decided; revises the mechanism of TR-emitter-025, not its intent).** EM-25 specifies a per-tick snapshot "built once in W7" so that readers see stable columns. This ADR does not build one. The subject table and the emitter derived columns are written **only inside `emitter.place`** (outside the hook, the only derived writers are E10 and the creation mutators, each for the record's own derived fields, D2.5, D3.4); the kernel is single-threaded and the presentation stages run between ticks (ADR-0009), so any read outside `emitter.place` already sees a committed table, and no reader runs *inside* the hook. A copy would cost `W_SNAP` units per emitter every tick (D.14 prices it at about 4 % of `B` at the typical point) to protect against a race that cannot occur. What TR-emitter-025 asks for survives: reads are pure table lookups (typed scalar getters over packed columns, ascending id by construction), at most one tick stale unless the reader declares `after: [emitter.place]`, allocation-free, never callable from a notification handler that decides state, with the no-retain rule kept for list reads (they fill a caller-owned buffer, D13.1). `T_snap` leaves the budget model; the per-section emission array of D.12 is accumulated in W2 and made current in W7, and a late-created (`pending`) emitter is absent from it until the next run, the same one-tick lag as the subject table.

### 5. Output derivations (TR-emitter-013, -014, -015, -016, -029, TR-itemdb-034)

**D5.1 Lamp output (TR-emitter-014).** `LampMath.lamp_output(base_pm, served_pm, switch_on, armed, live) -> int` is `IntMath.floor_div(base_pm * served_pm, 1000)` when `switch_on`, `armed` and `live` hold, else 0. `served_pm` is the tier's satisfaction read live each run from `GraphView.served_pm(node_id)` (ADR-0011 section 9.1; persisted as `n_served` per ADR-0012, so it is exact straight after a restore); the Emitter's own `switch_on` gates the light (a switched-off lamp can still read `served_pm` 1000). A lamp is **live** iff `GraphView.node_live(node_ref)` (ADR-0011 section 9: O(1), allocation-free, false for an unknown or tombstoned id; every other getter returns its documented neutral value for a non-live id and `node_state` returns `GraphIds.STATE_NONE`, -1, which is outside the enum, so liveness is decided by the guard and never from a neutral value; CT-09), the node is not dormant (`section_dormant`), and `node_state` is not FAILED, DESTROYED, SALVAGED or ABSENT (EM-13). A not-live lamp keeps `switch_on` and relights on repair with no command. A node with `node_ref` 0 (the two self-powered subkinds) has no liveness test, no ARMING and a fixed `base_pm`.

**D5.2 Arming latency (TR-emitter-013).** `arm_tick` is the first tick at or after the apply tick `T_a` on which a power hook runs: `arm_tick = T_a + IntMath.posmod_i(power_offset - T_a, GRAPH_STEP_TICKS)`, with `power_offset` and `GRAPH_STEP_TICKS` read from the frozen `GraphTuning` catalog injected at `setup()` (ADR-0012 makes the hook offsets tuning data, so the GDD's `T_a mod GRAPH_STEP_TICKS` form, which assumes offset 0, is generalised; at offset 0 the two are identical, which is a unit test). This agrees with ADR-0012's drain rule (a P2 apply on a power-hook tick is drained by the same tick's phase 0; an apply on any other tick by the next power-hook tick), so a lamp is ARMING with output 0 for 0 to `GRAPH_STEP_TICKS - 1` ticks and is lit by the first `emitter.place` at or after `arm_tick`. OFF is immediate with M14 `(node_ref, 0)`. M14 is called **inside the apply** through the injected Graph mutator object (`views` cannot write; the Graph's published mutators are wired by the composition root, ADR-0003 section 7). **Hard dependency (T5, CT-36):** E1 calls M14 under the INIT scope at Web Map's `init_world`, so ADR-0012 must list M14 as `APPLY, HOOK, INIT` and carry the INIT drain row: an INIT deposit drains at phase 0 of the first `graph.propagate.power` run after LOADING to RUNNING, which is the first RUNNING tick `T` with `T mod GRAPH_STEP_TICKS == hook_offset_power`. **That tick is `arm_tick` for an INIT-scope creation:** `T_a` is the first RUNNING tick (one more than the N2 clock `T` the INIT pass is stamped with, ADR-0016 section 1.1, CT-43), so the formula above gives exactly ADR-0012's drain tick. The fallback of creating authored-on Graph lamps with `switch_on` set and no M14 call is not taken (ADR-0012 grants M14 in INIT scope). The negative case of D.2 (a Graph that consumes only strictly later requests) is a `GraphTuning` fact the unit tests parameterise, not a second code path.

**D5.3 Beam pose and the occlusion origin (TR-emitter-016).** For the seat's BEAM: `origin` (the apex of the cone and the distance origin) is the chest point read at the time of the run as two scalars, `ControllerView.get_chest_x(player_id)` and `get_chest_y(player_id)` (ADR-0019, CT-28: absolute body-frame ints computed on every read from the same tick's `BodyView.pos_x/pos_y` plus the frozen `CHEST_POINT[sheet][row]` for the current `loco_state`, mirrored by facing; O(1), allocation-free, so it cannot lag the position; the Emitter takes the two values once per beam per run); the axis is the committed `ControllerView.get_aim_angle(player_id)` (`AIM_UNITS`; the beam follows aim, not facing); `ControllerView.get_loco_state(player_id)` gives the DEAD/DOWNED/STAGGERED/IN_POD behaviour below; reach, `half_angle_units` and curve class come from the shape row of `shape_id` (`ShapeCatalog`); `BEAM_BASE_PM` is the output while on (no low-charge dimming in the MVP); the `HAND_POINT` is never read. **The occlusion segment starts at the foot** (`pos_curr`, `BodyView.pos_x/_y(actor)`), because `pos_curr` is outside every footprint by construction (`LocomotionStep` keeps the avatar's box out of them) and is free of the chest jitter (EM-6, the technical-director's ruling). The whole rule lives in one function, `EmitterDerived.occlusion_origin(row, out_xy)`; ADR-0018 (the Lighting spike) consumes `EmitterDerived.occlusion_origin(row, out_xy)` (CT-30), and **outcome D replaces `occlusion_origin` and adds a hot, allocation-free `EmitterView` read of the beam's previous and current occlusion origin (two int pairs, derived each tick) and the `OCC_ORIGIN_MODE` tuning row, introduced together with ADR-0018's combination invariant; there is no change while B or A ships** (the reads and the tuning row are not reserved now: the only reachable mode pairs at ship are legal, so the invariant could never fire). Other kinds use their floor-plane anchor for both apex and occlusion. The BEAM_SPILL (shape row 11, present only while the beam is on and `BEAM_SPILL_RADIUS_PX` is above zero) is a **second dynamic item of the beam record**, SOFT, omnidirectional, centred on the foot origin, output `BEAM_SPILL_PM`, gating actions, not stored as an emitter, absent from `emitters()` and from `section_emission`; it runs through the same radius-then-occlusion pipeline and its changes dirty the beam's own key. DOWNED keeps the beam committed (aim frozen); DEAD forces it off in W1; STAGGERED leaves it unchanged; IN_POD follows the actor into the pod body's frame.

**D5.4 Node lantern (TR-emitter-015).** `output_pm = IntMath.floor_div(base_pm * clampi(run_pm, 0, 1000), 1000)` when the node is live and its `node_state` is in the Item DB's `lantern_on_states` bitmask, with `run_pm` the Graph-published `GraphView.run_pm(id)` consumed as published and clamped (never recomputed from `out_w`, never `served_pm` alone, no fallback form: a lantern that cannot read `run_pm` is a boot defect). The lantern roster is Item DB data (`emits.shape == LANTERN`), not a list here.

**D5.5 Beacon (TR-emitter-029).** `BeaconMath.aim(phase, dir, T, sweep_ticks) = IntMath.posmod_i(phase + dir * IntMath.floor_div(T * AIM_UNITS, sweep_ticks), AIM_UNITS)` with `T` the kernel tick and `dir` +1 or -1: no per-tick angle state, so save-equivalence is automatic (the kernel clock is persisted). `T * AIM_UNITS` stays below 2^62 (I-OVERFLOW bounds `T` below 2^50). The beacon's brightness is the lamp formula (it carries a `node_ref` in the MVP), `gates_actions` is **false** (`ShapeCatalog` row 8), it is a dynamic-layer item, and a `set_beacon` ON starts from the angle the pure function gives at that tick. `BEACON_SWEEP_TICKS` is `EmitterTuning`; any positive integer is valid.

### 6. Deployables, evict-wane and fault glow (TR-emitter-027, -028, TR-itemdb-022, TR-ftrail-041, TR-boarding-037, TR-interact-036)

**D6.1 Glowstick curves (TR-itemdb-022).** The record carries `life_remaining_ticks`, `life_total_ticks` (from the def at spawn), `evict_tick`, `reach_at_evict`. `WaneMath.reach(r_full, rem, total, wane_from_pm, floor_pm) -> int` is the Item DB F-7 `reach_px(rem)` verbatim (curve id `WANE_LINEAR_25`): `W = IntMath.floor_div(total * wane_from_pm, 1000)`; `r >= W` holds full reach, else `f = floor_pm + IntMath.floor_div((1000 - floor_pm) * rem, W)` and `reach = IntMath.floor_div(r_full * f, 1000)`. Output stays constant `GLOWSTICK_BASE_PM`; the reveal radius shrinks, and the waning is **sim state**, not presentation. The life is counted from the throw tick: Interaction passes `life_ticks` = the life remaining at landing (E3), and a stowed or in-flight stick has **no record** (inert by construction; a save with a throw in flight lands the stick on the same absolute tick with the same remaining life, TR-interact-036). The stick is spawned only at landing, from Interaction's hook, which is ordered after `emitter.place`; its record is therefore complete in the read view from P4 of the landing tick, it enters the subject table at the next run, and **`lit(ref)` is false on every tick up to and including the landing tick and true from the first table committed after it** (TR-boarding-037; the landing tick calls `spawn_deployable` exactly once).

**D6.2 Cap eviction is a visible wane (D.9b).** `EmitterTuning` `GLOWSTICK_EVICT_WANE_S` gives `E = ticks_for_seconds(...)` (at least 1, I-WANE). The evict-wane reach is one non-negative numerator and one floor division, `max(R_floor, IntMath.floor_div(R_0 * E - (R_0 - R_floor) * (T - t_e), E))` capped by the F-7 value, ending at `T - t_e >= E`; writing it as `R_0 - floor_div(...)` computes the ceiling and is 1 px high on almost every tick (GDD D.9b), so the kernel test fixes the exact sequence. `evict_tick` and `reach_at_evict` persist, so an evict-wane in progress survives a save. Over `MAX_DEPLOYABLES_EVICTING` simultaneous evict-wanes the spawn is still applied in full (the item is spent) and `emitter_deployable_evict_overflow` counts; `MAX_DEPLOYABLES_LIVE` bounds non-evicting sticks.

**D6.3 Cap on records (TR-emitter-023 part).** At `MAX_EMITTERS` a creation is **refused**, never made by removing a light with a physical cause (D12.5).

**D6.4 Fault glow: derived, reconciled once per completed Graph step, never in `post_restore` (TR-emitter-027, TR-ftrail-041; answers ADR-0012 Open Question 3).** FAULT_GLOW rows are canonical (they carry `glow_key`, `sev`, `placed_tick`), so a restore restores them; they are created and removed by W1 and by nothing else. W1 reconciles **iff `GraphView.last_step_no() != hdr.fault_step_seen`**, that is, once for each Graph step the failure hook has completed (`last_step_no` is the last step whose failure hook completed, LS-8, ADR-0012 section 1; it trails `step_no()` by up to one step), in the same tick, after the failure hook completes (`emitter.place` is `after: graph.propagate.failure`, so it reads that step's final descriptor store, and the glow appears on the failure tick and never later, TR-ftrail-041), and then writes `fault_step_seen = last_step_no`. `post_restore` does not reconcile and does not touch the rows. Consequences: in the uninterrupted run and in the restored run the reconciliation happens at the same ticks against a descriptor store freshly produced by the same phase 11, so the one-step window ADR-0012 section 11 leaves in the *store* between a restore and the next phase 11 is never read by the Emitter; the glow of a descriptor appears on the failure tick of the step in which it appears, never later (EM-1). Reconciliation reads `GraphView.failures(filter, out)` with a reusable `FailureFilter` (`loaded_only`, `min_sev` from the glow table) and compares it, in ascending descriptor id, with the existing FAULT_GLOW rows keyed by `glow_key`: new descriptors with a glow row (`GlowTable`, keyed by `(mode, medium)`) create a row, vanished ones remove it, `sev` changes update `sev` (and the derived output `IntMath.floor_div(GLOW_BASE_PM[key] * SEV_GLOW_PM[sev], 1000)`), subject to `sev >= sev_min[key]` and output above zero. Position, section and frame: `section_id = GraphView.section_of(anchor)`, `anchor_px` = the head point `base_px_polyline[0]` of `LayoutView.anchor_geometry(anchor)` read once at creation (a cold read; an empty answer for a discarded section creates no row, counts `emitter_fault_glow_no_anchor` and retries at the next step), and `body_id` derived. Cap: `MAX_FAULT_GLOW`; beyond it the evicted rows are the unprotected ones of lowest `sev`, ties by the higher descriptor id first, and a row younger than `ticks_for_seconds(W_MIN_WARNING_S)` (age `T - placed_tick`) is protected and never evicted: protection beats the cap (`emitter_fault_glow_overcap`, `emitter_fault_glow_evictions`). The trail's emissive layer adds nothing to exposure (a FAULT_GLOW is the only fault light the sim knows). The Failure & Trail glow table is stage-1 content (`GlowTable`), authored by Failure & Trail, read here as data (architecture IC-12). **Hot-path scoping (T14):** FAULT_GLOW row creation and removal, E1/E2 and the slot-store insert and remove are untagged cold helpers called from the tagged W1 (`LayoutView.anchor_geometry` is a Tier C read that allocates); CNT-01 therefore covers ticks with no structural change, and its 600-tick fixture includes a steady fault set.

### 7. The integer cone test (TR-emitter-017; Emitter candidate 3)

**D7.1 Test.** `ConeMath.in_cone(rx, ry, d2, ux, uy, cos2_q28) -> bool` where `r = anchor - origin` (body frame), `d2 = rx*rx + ry*ry` (the caller has already established `d2 <= reach * reach`, a closed edge), `(ux, uy)` is the Q14 axis `DirTable.UX/UY[IntMath.posmod_i(aim + off, AIM_UNITS)]`:

```gdscript
## @hot
static func in_cone(rx: int, ry: int, d2: int, ux: int, uy: int, cos2_q28: int) -> bool:
	if d2 == 0:
		return true                       # the apex is inside every cone of its emitter
	var dd: int = rx * ux + ry * uy       # Q14 dot product
	return dd > 0 and dd * dd >= d2 * cos2_q28
```

The `dd > 0` guard is required (the squared test alone accepts the opposite half-plane); a 360-degree shape (`OMNI`) skips the call. **BEAM_SPLIT is the OR of two such tests** at axes `aim +- split_axis_units` (both axes are posmod-wrapped); the dark wedge never closes (I-WEDGE), so a point lies in at most one cone and its contribution is never doubled. `cos2_q28` is the squared Q14 cosine of `half_angle_units` derived by the catalog freeze (D1.2). **Mirror exactness is a test, not a hope:** exposure of a mirrored scene (x to -x, aim `a` to `AIM_UNITS/2 - a`, `flip_h` toggled) equals the mirror image of the original exactly, for the five drawn and three mirrored facings, every `CHEST_POINT` animation row and all four beam shapes. No float, trig or square root is on the path. Magnitudes: `|r| <= REACH_MAX_PX + CHEST_X_MAX`, `|dd| <= |r| * 16384`, `dd * dd` and `d2 * cos2_q28` stay below 2^62 by a wide margin (the product bound of the registry reach range is about 4 x 10^13); `I-OVERFLOW` proves it at boot for the declared constants, not for a hand-picked case.

**D7.2 Quantisation.** The direction table is Q14 and the half-angles and the split offset are not integral in `AIM_UNITS`, so the rim is misplaced by under 0.1 degree (under 0.7 px at the longest reach); the quantisation sits far inside the accepted committed-versus-drawn divergence (PC-5 tolerance) and is the same on the sim and drawn sides because Lighting derives its cone from the same `ShapeCatalog` rows (a build lint, TR-emitter-016: the rendered reach of every hard-edged row equals the table reach within one pixel). Probed 2026-10-29: the generated table has 0 mirror mismatches over all 4,096 entries; the squared length of an entry deviates from 2^28 by at most 22,314 (a relative length error below 5 x 10^-5, under 0.02 px at the longest reach); the five `cos2_q28` values derived from it equal the GDD's EM-8 column exactly.

### 8. The integer occlusion test (TR-emitter-018, -044; Emitter candidate 1)

**D8.1 Source.** Occlusion is an integer segment-versus-rectangle test over the OCCLUDES_LIGHT rectangles of one body, obtained through the `OccluderSource` port of ADR-0014 and **nothing else**: no physics backend, no direct-space-state query, no rendering occluder (Alternative 1). Actors and horrors never occlude (they are not rectangles). Door state is the Layout projection of the Graph's `door_state` (the leaf rectangle is already in the OCCLUDES_LIGHT set at its current extent, ADR-0014 section 1), so the Emitter holds **no door flag** and no open-fraction threshold exists. One `QueryBuffer` is owned by `EmitterScratch`, sized at the freeze to its worst result (`MAX_FOOTPRINT_RECTS_PER_BODY`, ADR-0014 section 4), and reused for every pair; `out.grew` stays 0 after warm-up (a test asserts it).

**D8.2 Box convention (the cell-unit rule, GDD EM-20 a).** Layout's coordinates name pixel cells; a cell is the unit square centred on its integer coordinate. `rects_in` returns the occupied span `(x0, y0, x1 - 1, y1 - 1)` of each rectangle in `out.spans`; the box tested is the **closed** continuous box `[x0 - 1/2, (x1 - 1) + 1/2] x [y0 - 1/2, (y1 - 1) + 1/2]`, computed in **doubled coordinates** as the integer box `[2*x0 - 1, 2*(x1-1) + 1] x [2*y0 - 1, 2*(y1-1) + 1]` (equivalently `[2*x0 - 1, 2*x1 - 1]` in the stored half-open terms). Abutting rectangles share a box face, so no wall seam, door-leaf/jamb joint or corner join leaves a strip; a touch (corner graze, edge-on pass) is blocked; a segment endpoint is an integer cell, doubled to an even number against odd box bounds, so it is never exactly on a box face and is inside a box iff its cell is occupied. The candidate query box is the segment's cell bounding box, half-open: `ax0 = min(sx, px)`, `ax1 = max(sx, px) + 1`, likewise y. **This is exactly the set of rectangles whose closed box can touch the segment** (a rectangle's box meets the segment's doubled bounding box iff `x0 <= maxx` and `x1 > minx`, which is the half-open overlap `rects_in` computes), so the broadphase neither misses a blocker nor the doubled test needs a second filter.

**D8.3 Test (slab method on integers, no division).** `SegmentMath.blocked(sx, sy, px, py, spans, count) -> bool` translates every coordinate relative to `S` and doubles it, then for each box clips the parameter interval `[t_lo, t_hi]` (initially `[0, 1]`) against each axis slab by **cross-multiplication of fractions with positive denominators**; the box blocks iff `t_lo <= t_hi` at the end (closed). An axis with zero extent is `outside` (no hit) when `S'` lies outside the slab and unbounded otherwise.

```gdscript
## @hot   spans: QueryBuffer.spans (4 ints per record: x0, y0, x1-1, y1-1), count: QueryBuffer.count
static func blocked(sx: int, sy: int, px: int, py: int, spans: PackedInt32Array, count: int) -> bool:
	var dx: int = (px - sx) * 2
	var dy: int = (py - sy) * 2
	for i in count:
		var b: int = i * 4
		var x0: int = (spans[b] - sx) * 2 - 1           # box in doubled coordinates, relative to S (S' = 0)
		var y0: int = (spans[b + 1] - sy) * 2 - 1
		var x1: int = (spans[b + 2] - sx) * 2 + 1
		var y1: int = (spans[b + 3] - sy) * 2 + 1
		var ln: int = 0                                  # t_lo = ln / ld   (ld > 0)
		var ld: int = 1
		var hn: int = 1                                  # t_hi = hn / hd   (hd > 0)
		var hd: int = 1
		var hit: bool = true
		# x slab
		if dx == 0:
			hit = x0 <= 0 and 0 <= x1
		else:
			var en: int = x0 if dx > 0 else -x1          # entry numerator (denominator |dx|)
			var ex: int = x1 if dx > 0 else -x0
			var den: int = dx if dx > 0 else -dx
			if en * ld > ln * den:
				ln = en
				ld = den
			if ex * hd < hn * den:
				hn = ex
				hd = den
		if hit:
			if dy == 0:
				hit = y0 <= 0 and 0 <= y1
			else:
				var deny: int = dy if dy > 0 else -dy
				var eny: int = y0 if dy > 0 else -y1
				var exy: int = y1 if dy > 0 else -y0
				if eny * ld > ln * deny:
					ln = eny
					ld = deny
				if exy * hd < hn * deny:
					hn = exy
					hd = deny
		if hit and ln * hd <= hn * ld:                     # closed interval: a touch blocks
			return true
	return false
```

The listing is the probed algorithm with the segment translated to the origin (the probe used absolute doubled coordinates; translation changes no comparison). Every value is an integer relative to `S`; with `I-BOUNDS` (body-frame coordinates below `BODY_COORD_LIMIT`, which ADR-0015 defines in `BodyTuning` and its bounds ledger at no more than 2^24, derives from the maximum section extent times the maximum sections per body, restore-checks, and Layout repeats in `can_attach`; CT-24, T6) the doubled relative values are at most 2^26, so every cross product is at most 2^52, well inside `I-OVERFLOW`. **Probed 2026-10-29:** 300,000 random segment/box cases (24,519 blocked, including touches) produced **0 mismatches** against an independent separating-axis implementation (bounding-box overlap plus corner-side signs, exact integers), and the GDD's oblique seam-leak example (rectangles `[0,24)` and `[24,48)`, segment `(23,300)` to `(24,140)`) is blocked.

**D8.4 Anchor clearance (TR-emitter-044).** An anchor is at least `ANCHOR_CLEARANCE_PX` outside every occluding rectangle (Chebyshev distance in cells from the occupied span, ADR-0013 `rect_algebra_closed_span`). It is a **data-load lint** `EMITTER_ANCHOR_CLEARANCE` over every authored FIXTURE, NODE_LANTERN and BEACON placement and every subject anchor the content can declare (the lint uses `ExposureProbe` and `section_rects_into`, D1.1; **`section_rects_into` answers in the BODY frame** (ADR-0014 section 5), so the results are converted to section-local px by subtracting `section_offset_x/_y` read in the same call, and the data-load lint, which has no running Layout, uses a template-frame double of `OccluderSource`, where template frame equals section-local; T7) and a creation-time check for FAULT_GLOW anchors. At run time an anchor found strictly inside a footprint is **treated as blocked from every emitter** (`lux` 0, `lit` false, fails safe to dark), **still counts in `section_emission`** (an emitter anchored in its wall lights nothing but is still emission), and increments the per-record counter `emitter_anchor_clearance_fail`. The runtime check is one `solid`-free test: an anchor cell inside any returned span is "inside" (a zero-length segment test, `S == P`, which the same function answers).

**D8.5 Hard parts handled once, here.** (a) A zero-length segment (`d2 == 0`, the subject at the emitter's occlusion origin) is answered by the same function (both axes zero-extent). (b) The owner's own beam on the owner's ACTOR subject contributes its apex value with no cone and no occlusion test (D9.4). (c) The segment test is called only for pairs that survived the radius and cone rejects; the call count and the rectangle count are the `W_SEG`/`K_OCC` units of D12.

### 9. Illumination and the lit hysteresis (TR-emitter-019, -020; Emitter candidate 3)

**D9.1 Contribution.** For one emitter and one subject `c` is 0 unless all hold: equal `body_id`, `d2 <= reach_px^2` (closed), inside the cone for cone shapes, unblocked. Otherwise `Illumination.contribution(output_pm, curve_class, edge_floor_pm, d, reach) -> int` (E5: `curve_class` is the `ShapeCatalog` id and the HARD or SOFT split is a `match` on it **inside** the function, so a replacement curve keeps the signature; `radius_for` takes the same `curve_class`): `F = IntMath.floor_div(1000 * (reach - d), reach)`; SOFT `f = F`; HARD `f = edge_floor_pm + IntMath.floor_div((1000 - edge_floor_pm) * F, 1000)`; `c = IntMath.floor_div(output_pm * f, 1000)`, with `d = ConeMath.floor_root(SQ, d2)`, the largest `k` with `SQ[k] <= d2` (D1.4). All operands are non-negative (`d <= reach`), so `floor_div` is exact; `reach >= REACH_MIN_PX > 0` (I-REACH), so no division by zero occurs. Curve classes are rows of `ShapeCatalog` (`LINEAR` is the MVP curve id; a replacement 17-knot interpolation table keeps the signature and must be monotone, integer and non-negative).

**D9.2 Aggregation.** `lux_pm = mini(1000, Sum c)` over every emitter, `act_lux_pm = mini(1000, Sum c)` over emitters whose `gates_actions` is true (the BEACON does not gate, so it adds to `lux` and not to `act_lux`), with **no ambient term** (`AMBIENT_PM` is fixed at 0 in the sim). Integer addition is commutative, so the sum is independent of candidate order. `top_emitter_id` is the emitter of the largest contribution, ties by **ascending id**, an explicit comparison and not an iteration-order accident.

**D9.3 Published radii.** `r_lit_px` and `r_off_px` are the largest `d` in `0..reach` whose integer chain gives `c >= LIT_ON_PM` and `c >= LIT_OFF_PM` respectively (-1 when `c(0)` is below the threshold), found by binary search because `c` is monotone non-increasing in `d`; the second search is bounded to the window above `r_lit_px` (`Illumination.radius_for`). The closed form `floor(R * (output - LIT_ON_PM) / output)` runs 1 px high for some dimmed outputs and is not used. They are derived on an output or reach change and in `post_restore`, never persisted. For a full-output hard-edged emitter the lit boundary **is** the drawn edge (invariant I-EDGE); a dimmed one has an honestly smaller actionable area.

**D9.4 The owner stands in their own light (EM-21).** The owner's own beam adds its full `output_pm` (the `d = 0` apex value, which every cone contains) to the owner's ACTOR `lux_pm`, independent of facing and occlusion; it is **not** added to `act_lux_pm`; every other beam lights the owner, and the owner's beam lights everyone else, by the ordinary tests. Attention reads the beam as emission and does not add it again.

**D9.5 Lit hysteresis (decided; the "lit hysteresis" of the task scope).** `LitHysteresis.next(prev, act_lux_pm, on_pm, off_pm) -> int` is `1` iff `act_lux_pm >= LIT_ON_PM`, or `prev == 1` and `act_lux_pm >= LIT_OFF_PM`; there is **no dwell** (`LIT_OFF_DWELL_TICKS` is fixed at 0: holding `lit` after the light is gone would be a window where the sim says lit and the world is dark, a P1 lie). Decisions: (a) **the bit is canonical**, one `PackedByteArray` entry per subject, snapshotted and hashed (EM-22, checkpoint ruling CP-2): a subject saved at 130 permille and lit is lit after the restore, and rebuilding every bit against `LIT_ON_PM` is rejected because `validate` at T+1 would accept in one run and reject `NOT_LIT` in the other; (b) `post_restore` recomputes `lux_pm`, `act_lux_pm` and `top_emitter_id` from the restored state and **copies each subject's `lit` bit verbatim; it never calls `LitHysteresis.next`** (T1): a hook ordered after `emitter.place` may write an upstream block on the snapshot tick (Interaction calling Graph M6 on a lamp's node, a late hook setting `loco_state` to DEAD), so the `act` a rebuilt table sees can differ from the one the last run saw, re-applying `next` to a different `act` could rewrite a canonical bit, and a canonical write in `post_restore` is what ADR-0016 section 4.2 forbids. The hysteresis runs only in the W6 of a live run; the bit is therefore canonical-backed and G4-exact, and `lux_pm`, `act_lux_pm` and `top_emitter_id` after a restore may differ from the live run's for one run (D14.4); (c) a hard rim is a step (a full-output rim contributes `EDGE_FLOOR_PM` then 0 across one pixel) that the hysteresis never sees, so it bites only for dimmed or soft light (brownout, waning sticks, fixture pools); rim chatter under a sweeping beam is the Controller's committed-aim quantisation and `FOCUS_STICKY_MARGIN` (PC-6); (d) `lit(ref)` and `lux(ref)` stay separate answers (the beacon must not strobe prompts, EM-16); (e) a newly registered subject starts unlit and is evaluated at the next run; (f) an unregistered ref answers `lux` 0 and `lit` false in a release build, indistinguishable from an unlit registered one (no existence oracle; the debug-only `registered(ref)` is for tests and the overlay). The two `lit`s keep their GDD names verbatim: the record field `lit` (`output_pm > 0`, the beam draws only when it is true) and the query `lit(ref)`.

**D9.6 What the kernel is not.** It is not `probe(body_id, point)` or `lit_by` (deferred to VS-A, banned by lint `EMT-06`), not a shadow or light renderer (Lighting conforms to the published fields), and it never takes a Filter-wrapped value.

### 10. Subjects, frames and the subject index (TR-emitter-020, -021, -044, -046, TR-interact-022, -023, TR-horror-009, -039; Emitter candidates 2 and 4 (index half))

**D10.1 Slot stores (decided).** Emitters and subjects are held in two **slot-stable stores** in `EmitterDerived`: arrays of fixed capacity (`MAX_EMITTERS`, `MAX_EXPOSURE_SUBJECTS` plus the moving cap) allocated once at the freeze, a free list, and a **rank index** (`by_id` / `by_ref`: the ids or refs in ascending order with their slots, kept sorted with `bsearch` and native `insert`/`remove_at`, which ADR-0013 probed to preserve order). A slot never moves while its row lives, so the grid links, the pair rows' subject index and the scratch lists can hold slots; the persisted columns are the same rows read in rank order, which is ascending id or ref by construction (D2.6). Registration and removal are mutator-path (cold, untagged) work; the per-tick loops index slot arrays directly.

**D10.2 Subjects.** A subject has one `anchor_px` (the only sampled point: a large object half in a beam is lit iff its anchor is), a `kind` (OBJECT, ACTOR, HORROR, ITEM), a `moving` flag (ACTOR and HORROR are always moving; an ITEM registers static, a carried item is unregistered at pickup and re-registered at drop) and the canonical `lit` bit. Only moving registrations count against `MAX_MOVING_SUBJECTS`. **Anchor frames by kind** (EM-21, unchanged): OBJECT and ITEM anchors are section-local, as Layout's `subject_anchors` returns them; an ACTOR's anchor, `section_id` and `body_id` are read from Body every run (`BodyView.pos_x/_y`, `actor_section`, `body_of_actor`) and converted to section-local px with `section_offset` (so a subject always lives in exactly one section's frame and a weld moves nothing); a HORROR passes a **body-frame** anchor with its `section_id` through E6/E8, the Emitter converts it to section-local px **at apply time (W3)** using the offsets of that moment, and Horror re-bases and re-sends E8 after a weld or cut (the one-run lag the GDD accepts). Registrants (Interaction, Horror, loot spawners, the actor boot) own the thing registered. **Registrations are persisted and restore verbatim; a well-formed save needs no re-registration. A registrant's `post_restore` only compares them with its own window (ADR-0016 V10), without writing. Any drift is repaired by the registrant's first ordinary hook run through E6/E7/E8, and the D2.5 pending columns absorb it with at most a one-run lag** (T2, CT-38: `post_restore` holds no `WriteScope` and never writes another block, ADR-0016 section 4.2); the Emitter declares no edge from Interaction, and Interaction is ordered after `emitter.place`. E6 on an already-registered ref is `DUPLICATE`, not a defect (TR-horror-039; the pending combinations are in D2.5). A HORROR is a registered subject that emits nothing, adds no occluder and never changes `lux`/`lit` of any subject: there is no code path by which a subject contributes light (TR-horror-009). Registration over `MAX_EXPOSURE_SUBJECTS` or `MAX_MOVING_SUBJECTS` returns `OVER_CAP`/`OVER_MOVING_CAP`, the subject reads dark and the release counters `emitter_subjects_overflow`/`emitter_moving_overflow` increment: the caps are content budgets held upstream (I-CAPS (d), Interaction's `INTERACT_FIXTURE_BUDGET + PLAYER_DROP_RESERVE` lint of TR-interact-023 per window, `subject_headroom()` for `drop`/`place_loose`), never gameplay states. `subject_headroom()` counts registrations that are not pending-unregister, so a drop that would exceed the cap refuses `OBSTRUCTED` on Interaction's side.

**D10.3 Frames (TR-emitter-021).** Stored anchors are section-local. The derived `origin_px` of an emitter is `anchor + section_offset(section_id)` (body frame); an emitter and a subject are compared **only if their `body_id` is equal**; `body_id` is never stored, is `LayoutView.body_of(section_id)` for every record and subject but a BEAM/ACTOR (Body's `body_of_actor`), and is re-read in W0 for every record and non-ACTOR subject of a section whose `static_version` differs from the cached one. A weld or cut therefore moves every record to its new body in the tick the Layout change applies with **no Emitter mutator call**. Light crosses a weld (sections of one body share a `body_id`); unwelded bodies and a docked pod are dark to each other (cross-body light is VS-A).

**D10.4 The subject index is an Emitter-owned section-local point grid (decided; the half of "candidate 4" ADR-0014 left here).** ADR-0014 section 7 disclaims "the Emitter's subject index", and TR-emitter-046 forbids a linear scan (at the typical point it costs about 13,000 units against a `B` of 10,000). The index is a `PointGrid` per loaded section, in **section-local px**, derived, never persisted: a dense grid of `EMITTER_CELL_PX`-sized cells sized from the section's footprint bounding box (read once, cold, with `section_rects_into`, whose results are in the BODY frame and are converted to section-local px by subtracting `section_offset_x/_y` read in the same call, T7) plus a one-cell margin; a head array per cell and a `next` link per slot (intrusive singly linked buckets) for each of two populations, **subjects** and **static emitters**; points outside the grid clamp into an edge cell, as do query boxes, so a clamped point is never missed (the radius test removes the extras). A moving subject updates its bucket only when its cell changes (`W_BROAD` per move); a section transfer (an actor crossing a door) is a remove from one grid and an insert in the other. A candidate query takes a body-frame box (an emitter's reach disc, or a subject's reach to the statics), and for each section of that body whose offset-translated bounding box meets the box (a short loop over the section cache, ascending `section_id`) walks the clamped cells; candidates are in grid order, **which no answer depends on** (sums are commutative, the top emitter is an explicit comparison, rows are inserted sorted by emitter id), and a test asserts answers are independent of insertion history. Because every grid is section-local, **a weld or cut re-indexes nothing**; only derived origins and body ids change (D10.3, D11.5). `EMITTER_CELL_PX` is a new `EmitterTuning` knob (initial value two tiles; range one to four tiles; owner technical-director; registry candidate): smaller cells mean fewer candidates and more cells per query, and the `W_CELL` unit prices the traversal (D12.6).

**D10.5 Hard parts handled once.** (a) An actor section of 0 keeps the previous value and counts `emitter_actor_section_none` (a defect-only state). (b) A subject whose section is dormant or unknown at E6 returns `NO_SECTION` and registers nothing. (c) A section discard (E2) removes its grid and every row and subject entry in one pass. (d) A subject registered at an anchor inside a footprint reads dark from every emitter (D8.4).

### 11. The pair cache, version cache and frame changes (TR-emitter-022, -046, TR-emitter-021 (trigger), Emitter candidate 2)

**D11.1 What is cached (decided).** The **static layer** (FIXTURE, NODE_LANTERN, FAULT_GLOW, resting DEPLOYABLE) is cached as **pair rows** `(emitter id, subject) -> {d, f_pm, c, act}` for **resting** subjects only, plus per subject the **unsaturated** raw sums `raw_all = Sum c` and `raw_act = Sum c over gating emitters` (clamped to 1000 only when the answer is formed, D9.2) and the static top `(c, emitter id)`. A saturated partial sum would be exact for adding an emitter but cannot remove one, so the unsaturated sum plus rows is what makes every change a delta (EM-23). Rows hold **no coordinates**. Storage is subject-major with a fixed stride `PAIR_ROWS_PER_SUBJECT` (an `EmitterTuning` knob; initial 16): per slot `pr_n`, and `pr_emitter`, `pr_d`, `pr_f`, `pr_c` columns of `slots * stride` entries, rows sorted by emitter id (so a subject's rows are ascending and its top emitter is a scan of at most the stride). All of it is derived and preallocated at the freeze; it is never persisted, never hashed, and rebuilt in `post_restore`. The **dynamic layer** (BEAM cones, BEAM spill, BEACON) is recomputed every run with the cheapest rejects first and caches nothing (a pose-unchanged skip is a pure optimisation and is rung 1 of the ladder, not MVP).

**D11.2 The cache is a pure optimisation: the transparency invariant (decided; the keystone).** For every state reachable by any mutation sequence, every cached answer (`lux`, `act_lux`, `lit` transition input, `top_emitter`, `r_lit_px`) equals the answer of `TestBruteExposure` (`src/sim/emitter/testing/`), a cache-free oracle that re-derives every pair from canonical state with no grid, no row and no history. A subject whose in-range unblocked static emitters exceed the stride holds **no rows** and is evaluated fresh each run (`uncached`; the transition counts `emitter_pair_rows_overflow`), and returns to rows when the count falls back to the stride; the rule is a function of current state, so a cold build and an incremental history agree. The oracle is run in the fuzz suite (random sequences of E1..E10, switches, brownouts, door travel, weld and cut, restore at a random tick), once with the production stride and once with a stride of 2 to force overflow on every room. **An answer never depends on the cache, so a row-table overflow changes cost, never an answer.**

**D11.3 Deltas (synchronous, in W3/W4, ascending emitter id then subject ref).** Each is a function of the change list from W3.

| Trigger | Work | Units |
|---|---|---|
| (i) an emitter's `output_pm` changes (including to or from 0) | for each resting subject in range (grid), find the row by emitter id and recompute `c = floor_div(output * f_pm, 1000)` from the stored `f_pm`; adjust `raw_all`/`raw_act` by the difference; an output of 0 stores `c = 0` and **keeps the geometry rows**, so a blackout is a zeroing and the switch back on is a delta too (no occlusion test) | `W_DELTA` per row; `W_PUB` per key; `W_RLIT` once per emitter whose output changed and stays above 0 |
| (ii) an emitter's integer `reach_px` changes (a waning stick) | recompute `d` and `f_pm` for its rows, drop rows beyond the new reach (reach only shrinks, so no pair is added and no occlusion test runs) | `W_CON` per row |
| (iii) an emitter is created (W3 apply of `pending`) or removed | created: resting subjects in range by radius, then occlusion (`rects_in` once per surviving pair), rows added; removed: its rows dropped | `W_REJ` per candidate; `W_CON + K_OCC * W_SEG + W_RECTS` per surviving pair |
| (iv) a resting subject is registered, unregistered or has its anchor moved (the `pending` apply) | its rows are replaced: static emitters in range from the emitter grid, radius, occlusion | `T_sub` (below) |
| (v) an occluder changes (D11.5) | only pairs whose segment bounding box meets the changed region are re-tested | `T_leaf = P_leaf * (W_REJ + K_OCC * W_SEG + W_RECTS)` |
| (vi) the layer is cold (`post_restore`, a section build, an `ALL` or epoch change) | everything for the affected body is built | `N * T_sub` plus `N_rl * W_RLIT` |
| (vii) a frame change (weld, cut) | D11.5 | `W_FRAME` per record or subject, cross-seam candidate tests |

`T_sub = U_sub + W_TAB`, `U_sub = E_cand * W_REJ + E_surv * (W_CON + K_OCC * W_SEG + W_RECTS)` as in D.14, with the candidate step now `W_CELL` per grid cell visited in addition (D12.6).

**D11.4 Version cache (decided).** `EmitterDerived` keeps, per section in the section cache, the triple `(version_epoch, static_version, leaf_version)` read through `OccluderSource` and the cached `body_id` and `section_offset`. In W0: an **epoch** change means "rebuild everything for that section's body" by definition (ADR-0014 section 6: a restore or `default_state` replaced the counters, so numeric equality with an old cached `version` proves nothing); a `static_version` change reads `changed_region_kind(section)` and takes the frame or region path of D11.5; a `leaf_version`-only change expects `REGION`. `post_restore` performs the cold rebuild and then **seeds the section cache with the current `(version_epoch, static_version, leaf_version)`, offset and `body_id`, read after Layout's `post_restore`** (which runs first in the DAG, ADR-0016 section 4.1), so the first W0 sees no change, repeats no cold build and charges nothing to `B` (T10, CT-45: an epoch change gates only result-neutral cache rebuilds and must never defer anything that affects lit state, or a restored run would diverge from an uninterrupted one). **The Emitter never relies on `changed_region == ALL` after a restore** (ADR-0014 section 6 says the stamp can be missed); the epoch is the authoritative signal, and a version that changed while the kind reads `NONE` (a missed stamp) is treated as `ALL` for that section, safe, and counts `emitter_region_missing`. Counters are compared for inequality, never ordering.

**D11.5 Narrowing and frame changes (decided: option L).** The GDD leaves the choice between (L) Layout publishes the changed region and (D) every leaf-pair is a dynamic-layer pair, to this ADR on Layout's answer. ADR-0014 section 6 delivers (L): `changed_region_kind` and `changed_region_box_into(section_id, RegionBox) -> bool`, valid for the current tick, merged by a lattice. **Option L is adopted; option D is the named fallback and is not built.** Rules: `REGION(box)` re-tests only the pairs whose segment bounding box meets the box (candidates found from the emitters whose reach box meets the region and, for each, the resting subjects in range whose segment box meets it, including pairs that have no row because they were blocked); `FRAME` (translation only, `rot_q` is 0 in the MVP) keeps every row whose emitter and subject lie in the same moved component (distances are invariant under a common translation, and rows hold no coordinates), **drops** rows whose emitter and subject `body_id` now differ (a cut: the rows that crossed the severed seam), and **adds** rows by radius-first candidate tests between the two sides now on one body (a weld: only subjects within the largest static reach of the seam, from both sides); a socket-host section that also moves reports `REGION` and the Emitter detects its translation by comparing `section_offset` with the cached value; `ALL` (build, discard, restore, or an attach/detach Layout declines to classify) is a cold rebuild of the affected bodies, priced as scenario S8b. After a restore `changed_region` is advisory and `version_epoch` is authoritative (CT-43); Layout's `post_restore` runs first by the DAG (ADR-0016 section 4.1), so the Emitter's cold build already sees the bumped leaves and is correct for open doors without a self-heal.

**D11.6 Moving subjects and `static_epoch` (decided).** A moving subject (ACTOR, HORROR) holds **no pair rows**; its static contribution is evaluated in W5 as a sweep over the static emitters in range from the emitter grid, radius first, then occlusion, whenever its anchor changed since the last run **or** `static_epoch` changed. `static_epoch` is a derived counter bumped in W3 whenever anything static changed (an emitter's output, reach, existence; an occluder region; a frame change); a stationary mover with an unchanged epoch reuses its last `static_raw`/`static_act_raw` scalars. This is exactly D.14's `T_mov = M * U_sub` (a mover re-evaluated when it moved) without the row bookkeeping that would be rewritten every tick anyway.

### 12. Counted work and the budget (TR-emitter-023, -024, -032, -046, TR-interact-038; Emitter candidate 2 (budget half))

**D12.1 Units.** Work is counted in the integer unit classes of D.14, defined as constants in `EmitterUnits` (`REJ, CONE, CON, SEG, RECTS, POSE, DER, TAB, PUB, DELTA, BROAD, FRAME, RLIT` and, new in this ADR, `CELL`; `SNAP` is retired, D4.2). Each is a count of an operation the code performs (candidates rejected by radius, cone tests, distance-falloff-accumulate evaluations, rectangle tests, `rects_in` calls, ...). The **weights** (`W_*`, `K_OCC`, `U_OP_NS`) are `EmitterTuning` keys: spike parameters used to classify a tick against `B`, `C_hot`, `C_cold`; they never alter state, an answer or a hash, and changing them is not a schema change.

**D12.2 Counted, never timed (TR-emitter-023).** Counts are deterministic integer functions of the canonical inputs (the same seed and command stream give the same counts on any machine); a time-based governor would be nondeterministic and is forbidden (lint `EMT-01` bans every clock token here, SIM-02 already does). No tick is ever cut short, skipped or deferred because of its count: **mandatory work is never skipped** (D4.1). The per-tick budget in milliseconds is a P99 on a **release export on RM-DESKTOP and RM-DECK** and is not a per-commit gate; the per-commit gates are the deterministic counts (CNT-04).

**D12.3 The injected counter and the telemetry tally (decided; TR-emitter-032).** The hook takes an injected `WorkCounter` port (ADR-0010 section 3.2/4.4): a counting double in `tests/support/` in tests, `NullWorkCounter` in every shipped and benchmarked build; no conditional compilation (`EM_WORK_COUNTER` is retired). Tests assert, per scenario, that the counted units equal the **D.14 closed form exactly** (CNT-04), including `S1` door travel, `S2` one-room blackout, `S3` ship blackout, `S4` restore with inrush, `S5` eight sticks waning, `S6` cold restore, `S8` weld, `S8` cut and `S8b` fallback weld. Because a release build injects the null counter, the release telemetry counter `emitter_rebuild_over_ceiling` (the GDD names it a release counter) cannot be fed by the port. The tick therefore also keeps an **always-on tally** in `EmitterDiag.units_tick`, computed at phase boundaries (at most about ten integer multiply-adds per run: `count * weight` per unit class) and closed in W7, compared with `C_hot`; it feeds telemetry only and never a branch of the simulation. Two independent counts of the same operations (the port in tests, the tally everywhere) must agree in the CNT-04 suite, which is also the test that the tally is not drifting from the code.

**D12.4 Budget classes and what a breach means.** Steady state is `T_mand + T_static <= B` with `B = EXPOSURE_BUDGET_MS * 10^6 / U_OP_NS`; hot scenarios (rare events: door travel, blackout, restore with inrush, waning sticks, weld, cut) are held to `C_hot` from `EXPOSURE_TICK_CEILING_MS`; the cold build (`post_restore`, the `init_world` build, the `ALL` fallback) is held to `C_cold` from `COLD_REBUILD_CEILING_MS`. A breach completes the tick, counts `emitter_rebuild_over_ceiling` and is a hitch to be removed by content or by the ladder (D12.7), never deferred.

**D12.5 Creation at the cap (TR-emitter-023).** When the record count equals `MAX_EMITTERS` a creation is **refused**: E1 refuses the section's remaining records, `spawn_deployable` returns `REFUSED_CAP` (the item was already spent), and the fault-glow reconciliation creates nothing for the lowest-ranked descriptors; each refusal counts `emitter_create_refused` per kind and reports through `Diagnostics` in a debug build. Within one pass creations are admitted in `priority` order, highest first (the kind defaults BEAM 255, BEACON 200, FIXTURE 120, DEPLOYABLE 100, FAULT_GLOW 80, NODE_LANTERN 60 are `EmitterTuning`; ties by ascending creation order); the seat beams are never refused because their slots are reserved (I-CAPS (a) counts the bound seats). The other caps (`MAX_DEPLOYABLES_LIVE`, `MAX_DEPLOYABLES_EVICTING`, `MAX_FAULT_GLOW`, `MAX_EXPOSURE_SUBJECTS`, `MAX_MOVING_SUBJECTS`) are the registry values; a runtime overflow of any is a defect of its upstream budget, is counted by a release counter and reports in a debug build, and no design relies on it.

**D12.6 What this ADR changes in the D.14 model, and what it does not claim (decided; honesty).** (a) `W_SNAP` leaves the model (D4.2): about 4 % of `B` at the typical point. (b) A new unit `CELL` (one grid cell visited by a candidate query, initial model weight 2, range 1 to 4) prices the traversal of D10.4, which D.14 did not price (it priced candidates "after the broadphase"). (c) The distance step uses the native `bsearch` of D1.4. (d) Everything else, the pair-row deltas, the narrowing rule and the scenario table, is D.14 as written. **The typical design point of D.14 sits at 99.9 % of `B` with 11 units of headroom, so the model has no margin for (b) and this ADR does not claim the MVP fits.** What it claims: every cost is counted, the counts are closed-form, and the decision table below fires on measured numbers. Indicative evidence only (4.7.2 editor binary, headless, dev machine; **not** a release export, **not** the budget pass, UNVERIFIED for RM-DESKTOP and RM-DECK): the cone test cost 82 ns, the segment-versus-box test 342 ns (one box), the square-root `bsearch` 63 ns; the model weights `W_CONE`, `W_SEG`, `W_CON` at `U_OP_NS` 40 are 0.8, 1.6 and 2.5 microseconds, so the primitives alone sit well below their model weights, but a unit also pays for the surrounding candidate loop, column reads and writes, which the probe did not include. **The gate is split (T4; the ADR-0006 SAVE-WRK-1 pattern), because scenario hot maxima need the real W0..W7 hook, which cannot be written before Acceptance.** *EM-SPIKE-1-ACCEPT* gates Acceptance: a release-export harness in `prototypes/` or `tools/bench` (the architecture S1 stub harness: a stub Graph and `emitter.place` at realistic counts) running the shared kernel functions and a W0..W7 skeleton at the D.14 typical point on RM-DESKTOP and RM-DECK, with a stated pass threshold: steady state at or below `B` with at least 15 % margin at the measured weights, or a named ladder rung of D12.7 selected that meets it. *EM-SPIKE-1-SLICE* runs the real hook's S1..S8b scenario hot maxima and gates the Vertical Slice milestone, not Acceptance. Both are tied to S1 / SP-ENG-1.

**D12.7 The ladder (cheapest first; each rung is a data-layout or loop step behind the same API, and "the number moves, not the design").** 0 as designed. 1 evaluate in W6 only subjects touched this run or the last, and skip a dynamic item whose pose, output and occlusion origin are unchanged (a pure optimisation; the oracle is the proof). 2 evaluate only movers that changed cell (idle movers reuse their last result; the GDD's first mitigation). 3 raise `EXPOSURE_BUDGET_MS` within its range only while the ADR-0003 P3 share-ledger total (the Core hooks' whole-hook figures, CT-20) stays within 2.0 ms P99; the Emitter-box line of the Body and Layout queries is charged inside this ADR's figure. 4 shrink candidate sets: smaller `EMITTER_CELL_PX`, then a cone-tight cell mask for the dynamic items. 5 bake a per-room illumination grid for the static layer (the brief's option c; derived, rebuilt on the same triggers). 6 split the cold build across LOADING's verification window (never across ticks). 7 a GDExtension for the exposure kernel (ADR-0010 section 9, requires S1 evidence and an accepted amendment; the GDScript implementation stays the golden reference). A hot max in 1 to 2 ms is accepted if the scenario occurs less than once a minute of play, above 2 ms blocks the Vertical Slice milestone (EM-SPIKE-1-SLICE; move the static layer to rung 5); the worst-corner point (all caps) is recorded whatever it measures.

### 13. The read view, dirty keys and the Lighting contract (TR-emitter-025, -026, TR-sanity-038, TR-audio-037, TR-webmap-040; Emitter candidate 6)

**D13.1 `EmitterView` (decided; the read-view layout of candidate 6).** `read_view(ctx)` is called once at the registry freeze and returns a long-lived getter-only `EmitterView` (`src/sim/emitter/views/emitter_view.gd`) over the block and its derived columns, with an `EmitterViewEmpty` twin (neutral values; `lit` false). **`EmitterViewEmpty extends EmitterView`, restates `-> T` on every override and returns the neutral value on every path** (under the 4.7 rule an override of a typed-return method inherits the return type, so a twin that omits a return, or declares `-> int` where the base returns `bool`, is a GDScript error); one test compares the method sets and return types of the two classes by reflection (`get_script_method_list`). It is **typed scalar getters over the packed columns, keyed by emitter id or ref, plus caller-owned buffers for lists**; no getter returns a `Packed*Array` or a live container (CNT-03; registry `packed_array_exposed_or_setter_tracked`), none allocates, and a list read overwrites the caller's `IdBuffer` (`ids: PackedInt32Array`, `count`, authoritative; the QueryBuffer discipline of ADR-0014 section 4). Because the columns are written only inside the hook (D4.2) and rows are in ascending id, `emitter_count()` and `id_at(rank)` iterate in ascending id with no sort and no Dictionary. The surface:

**D13.3 Dirty keys (TR-emitter-026, the Emitter half).** `KeySpaceDescriptor{block_id &"emitter.state", kind_ids [1], kind_names [&"EMITTER"], event_ids [], event_names [], quiet_writes_allowed true}`: the kind number is fixed here (ADR-0004 left it to this ADR), the family is `E/<emitter id>` as `DirtyKey.make(1, id)` (`(1 << 32) | id`), and **there are no events at the MVP**. A key is marked, inside the mutator or in W7, on exactly these discrete changes of one emitter: create, remove, a `switch_on` flip, a `shape_id` change, `output_pm` crossing a bucket of `OUTPUT_DIRTY_QUANTUM_PM` (including to or from 0), and an integer `reach_px` step. Continuous pose (origin, aim) never dirties; a subject's `lux`/`lit` never dirties (they are poll reads); the beam spill's changes mark the **beam's** key. The last-emitted bucket and reach per emitter are derived, seeded from the restored values in `post_restore` so a restore marks nothing spurious (keys marked in INIT and RESTORE scopes are discarded and one `ALL` batch is delivered on LOADING to RUNNING, ADR-0003 section 4). The worst case is every emitter in one tick, at most `MAX_EMITTERS`, which I-CAPS (b) holds at or below `MAX_DIRTY_KEYS`, so a sweeping beam over 192 subjects cannot collapse notifications to `ALL`. `quiet_writes_allowed` is true because the pose and sub-quantum output writes legitimately mark nothing (ADR-0004 section 7 does not audit quiet blocks; the explicit marking tests and the key-space CI test are the audit here). **Which events fire which key is this ADR's; the encoding and the collapse rule are ADR-0004's; Lighting's render-rate poll of the view plus the dirty batch is ADR-0009/0028's.**

**D13.4 The Lighting contract.** Lighting reads and never writes: per emitter the 17 fields plus `shapes()` and `beam_spill_into`. This block publishes facts and Lighting conforms; the build lint of TR-emitter-016 checks that a hard-edged row's rendered reach equals the table reach within 1 px and that the drawn soft pool edge equals `max(r_off_px, POOL_RESIDUAL_PX)` within 1 px. The sim never culls by view: an off-screen emitter and an off-screen subject are fully simulated and counted (P3), and authoring lint keeps in-view emitter counts under Lighting's hypotheses; every record carries a deterministic `priority` so Lighting's "emitters are cut before occlusion" is reproducible.

### 14. Restore, save-equivalence and the verifier (TR-emitter-007, -008, -028 (restore part), -038 (payload side))

**D14.1 `restore(data, stored_version, ctx)`.** Two-phase per block (ADR-0016 section 3): `restore` validates and copies the persisted columns **into the existing block object** (never replaces it, so `EmitterView` stays valid) and touches nothing else. **Every incoming column is duplicated (E3, CT-41):** `restore()` owns nothing in the payload (ADR-0016 section 3.1), so it assigns `state.col = staged["col"].duplicate()` (or resizes and copies into the existing array) for every `Packed*Array` and `duplicate(true)` for any nested container, and never keeps a reference to a payload array (packed arrays are shared by reference in GDScript and `Dictionary.duplicate()` is shallow, ADR-0006 probe 16; an aliasing restore would let the live block mutate the kernel's staged payload and a retry or second restore of the same payload would see live state). The 4.7 rule that a packed-element write skips the setter does not affect this block: no setter is used and dirty keys are marked explicitly; every derived structure is the job of `post_restore`. Validation order, first failure wins, each through `ctx.fault(code)` with no partial application: unsupported `stored_version` or a key set that differs from the allow-list (`EMITTER_RESTORE_SCHEMA`; a reported failure, never a silent default); ragged column lengths, non-ascending or duplicate ids or refs (`EMITTER_RESTORE_COLUMNS`); `next_emitter_id` not above every id, `last_place_tick` or `fault_step_seen` out of range (`EMITTER_RESTORE_COUNTER`); an enum, flag or numeric column out of its range, including every byte column against 0..255 (`priority`, `subkind`, `shape_id`) and every Int32 column against its declared bound, since a wrapped value is a corrupt save (E2), a count above a cap (`EMITTER_RESTORE_RANGE`); a `section_id`, `shape_id`, `hue_id`, `glow_key` or `node_ref` that does not resolve against the catalogs and the already-restored Layout and Graph (`EMITTER_RESTORE_REF`); a subject-table violation: an ACTOR row with non-zero derived columns, `pending` outside 0..2, a pending anchor without its flag, the moving cap exceeded (`EMITTER_RESTORE_SUBJECTS`). The verifier of D14.3 is the same function the runtime tests reuse.

**D14.2 `post_restore(ctx)`: the cold build of the table the last run committed, over end-of-tick upstream state.** It runs after every predecessor in the `restore_after` DAG has restored **and** post-restored (ADR-0016 section 4.1), so Layout's door leaves, the Graph's intensive caches (`P`, `p_o2`, `o2_pm`, the pressure trend) and topology caches, and Body are valid (Tier D reads of DAG predecessors only); `served_pm`, the band memory and the Controller's chest point are not Tier D (persisted `n_served` and band columns, and a read-time getter with nothing derived, ADR-0016 section 3.4). It writes derived members only: no clamp, no prune, no repair, no cross-block write, and no canonical column either (the `lit` bits stay as `restore` set them). In ascending id: the section cache and frames (after the cold build the cache is seeded with the current version triple, offset and `body_id` read after Layout's `post_restore`, D11.4); the record derived fields with `T_derive = hdr.last_place_tick`; the slot stores, grids and `by_id`/`by_ref` ranks; the static layer for the **applied** view (D2.5) as the cold class (scenario S6); rows pending at the snapshot stay pending until W3 of the first run at T+1, as in the live run, so the pending set equals the live run's at T+1; the movers' static sweep; the dynamic layer as it stood at that run; the W6 sums (`lux_pm`, `act_lux_pm`, `top_emitter_id`) with each `lit` bit copied verbatim and `LitHysteresis.next` never called (D9.5 (b)); the emission array; the last-emitted dirty buckets and reaches; `static_epoch` and the tally reset. FAULT_GLOW rows are not touched (D6.4). **Idempotence:** calling `post_restore` twice in a row on the same restored block leaves every derived column and the block hash unchanged (tested); two restores of the same bytes under different valid linear extensions of the `restore_after` DAG produce identical per-tick `state_hash` traces and read-view answers (the ADR-0016 KRN-RST-05 battery, extended with `lit`, `lux`, `top_emitter`, `r_lit_of`, `section_emission` and the fixtures of D2.5).

**D14.3 The verifier (ADR-0016 row V9, decided here).** `EmitterVerify.check(block, catalogs, layout_view, graph_view) -> int` is a pure owner-held function listed in the ADR-0016 table at DAG position 6 (slot `restore` for `EmitterVerify`, plus slot `post_restore` for the rebuild; the V9 row): it returns 0 or the first `EMITTER_RESTORE_*` code, performs the checks of D14.1, and is called by `restore` and by tests; it reads no block's mutable state and writes nothing. The six codes are the "`EMITTER_RESTORE_*` (6)" of ADR-0016 section 5, row V9.

**D14.4 Why G4 holds, and exactly what it does not promise (the argument the tests falsify; T1, CT-04, CT-39).** (1) The canonical set is exactly what no other source can supply: the counter, the records' discrete facts, the subjects' registrations, the `lit` bits, the pending intents and two tick stamps (D2.3). (2) Every derived *structure* is a pure function of that set, the catalogs and the restored upstream blocks as they stand at the **end of tick T**. (3) The table the live run holds at the end of tick `T` is the output of the run at `T` over the upstream state **that run saw**; pending rows are by construction not in it. A hook ordered after `emitter.place` may write an upstream block on the same tick (Interaction calling Graph M6 `salvage_node`, an immediate mutator allowed in HOOK scope; a late hook setting `loco_state` to DEAD), so the derived values `post_restore` rebuilds from end-of-tick upstream state **can differ from the values the last run held by any write made by a hook ordered after `emitter.place`**. G4 is therefore claimed where it is true and not wider. (a) *Canonical-backed reads are G4-exact before the first run:* `lit(ref)` (the bit is copied verbatim, D9.5 (b)), `beam_of`, `subject_headroom()` and the registration set (with the pending set). (b) *Derived reads may differ for one run:* `lux`, `act_lux`, `top_emitter`, `output_of`, `r_lit_of`, `r_off_of` and `section_emission` (the first run at T+1 recomputes them from the same state in both runs). (c) *The rule that makes (b) safe:* no `validate` and no hook ordered before `emitter.place` decides state on a derived read of (b) for a lamp-kind record or a subject (a BEAM record's `output_of` and `lit_of` derive from its own canonical `switch_on` and `BEAM_BASE_PM`, with no late-mutable upstream, and are exempt; Suit's `suit.vitals` read, D3.8); this is `EMT-09` (D16.3) plus the KRN-RST-05 fixtures of D2.5. The alternative to the restriction, persisting the per-emitter live and output inputs the last run used, is rejected as heavier than the restriction. (4) FAULT_GLOW reconciliation happens at the same ticks in both runs (D6.4). (5) The hysteresis bit is canonical and is **never recomputed in `post_restore`**; only a live W6 applies `LitHysteresis.next`. (6) Arming reads `arm_tick` and the Graph's persisted node column `n_served` (ADR-0011 section 3.2; with `n_draw`, the CABLE `e0` and `power_use_w5` persisted as hook products, ADR-0012 section 11, CT-04, so there is no "if ADR-0011 declines" fallback): a lamp lit at the snapshot tick is lit after the restore unless a late hook changed its node on that tick, which is case (b) and is covered by the rule (c). (7) Counters (`EmitterDiag`) are not canonical and are not compared. The block does nothing while the kernel is RESTORING or FAULTED: the hook does not run and every mutator is `REFUSED` because the scope is closed (TR-emitter-038, ADR-0003).

**D14.5 Fault domain 6 (decided; pre-packed, append-only, `EmitterFaults`, `DOMAIN_ID` 6 per the registry's static domain table).** Restore: `EMITTER_RESTORE_SCHEMA` 1, `_COLUMNS` 2, `_COUNTER` 3, `_RANGE` 4, `_REF` 5, `_SUBJECTS` 6. Boot checks (D15): `EMITTER_BOOT_EDGE` 7, `_HYST` 8, `_WEDGE` 9, `_OVERFLOW` 10, `_MIRROR` 11, `_CAPS` 12, `_WANE` 13, `_REACH` 14, `_DOCK` 15, `_SPACING` 16 (warn-only), `_DIRSIZE` 17, `_SQRT` 18, `_BOUNDS` 19, `_SHAPE_REF` 20. Runtime defects: `EMITTER_ID_EXHAUSTED` 21 (a mutator would overflow `next_emitter_id` beyond int32; returns `-21` and calls `scope.fault`), `EMITTER_ROW_INVARIANT` 22 (a rank index or slot invariant found broken by the debug verify). Every code is packed `(DOMAIN_ID << 16) | n`; mutators return `-code` on a defect; there is no use of `assert(`.

### 15. Boot invariants as fault codes (TR-emitter-031)

**D15.1 Every invariant is a function returning a fault code, never `assert()`** (GDScript `assert()` is stripped from release exports, so an assert disappears in the shipped build and a debug test cannot see it; SIM-11 bans `assert(` in `src/` and the lint bans it in the boot-check file). Each lives in `EmitterBoot` (`src/sim/emitter/emitter_boot_checks.gd`) and takes the frozen catalogs, the registry-fed tuning and an injected `Diagnostics` seam carrying a `build_is_debug` flag, so a test runs the same code with the flag false as in a release build; the composition root refuses to start on any non-OK code in debug **and** release, **except `I-SPACING`, a build warning** (it depends on a value the Player Controller owns and has no promotion step). ### 16. Isolation, determinism, lints and the test plan (TR-emitter-003, -030, -034, -041, -031)

**D16.1 Isolation by construction.** `emitter.state` never obtains a `PERCEPTION_OUT` view; no field or symbol named for a perception fact exists (lint `EMT-04`); a Perception Filter decoy light is a Lighting visual and is not an emitter; the beam, the real reveal radius and lamp brightness are on the closed consequence channel. Nothing in `src/sim/emitter/` or `src/shared/emitter/` references `Light2D`, `LightOccluder2D`, `Node`, `PhysicsServer2D`, a physics space, `Camera2D`, a viewport, `RenderLayout`, a zoom rung, a visible rect or any `present`-side symbol (lint `EMT-01`, which also bans every wall-clock and frame-counter token already banned by SIM-02), and `setup()` takes none of them. **Camera independence (TR-emitter-041)** is therefore structural, and is also tested: the same seed and command stream run with different injected presentation settings produce an identical `state_hash` trace and identical `lux`/`lit`/`top_emitter`/`section_emission` answers; an off-screen subject and lamp are fully simulated and counted.

**D16.2 Determinism.** No RNG at all is drawn (no stream, no `RngFacet`); floats appear nowhere in `src/` for this module (SIM-03) and only in `tools/emitter/`; all division is `IntMath.floor_div`/`posmod_i` on non-negative or posmod-wrapped operands (the checked helpers; `trunc_div` is not used because no truncating quotient is meant here), enforced by SIM-04/05 and a committed bad/good fixture pair per banned token (FIX-01, TR-emitter-034); iteration is ascending id or ref; `state_hash` covers the canonical set only; the same seed and command stream give an identical hash and identical answers on any machine (lint AC-2 plus the replay test). Untyped numeric builtins are replaced by `mini/maxi/clampi/absi/signi` (SIM-04's sanctioned forms).

**D16.3 Lint pack `EMT` (decided; same conventions as ADR-0010 section 6; every rule has bad and good fixtures, FIX-01).**

### Implementation Guidelines
- **Must** keep `src/shared/emitter/` pure (static functions over ints, no state, no I/O, imports only `IntMath`) and have the block, the content lint and the tests call the same kernel; **must never** duplicate the cone, segment, contribution or hysteresis arithmetic in a second file.
- **Must** register exactly one block (`emitter.state`), one hook (`emitter.place`, `after` both edges with the `tolerates_stale` flags of D3.1), three commands, one fault domain (6), one key space (`EMITTER` kind 1, no events, `quiet_writes_allowed` true) and one binding source, only through `ModuleFacet.registrar` in `BOOTING`; **must never** register a second Emitter hook or block.
- **Must** hold the persisted columns of D2.3 in `EmitterState` only and every rebuildable structure in `EmitterDerived`; **must never** put a derived value, a scratch buffer, a counter, a `body_id`, a `StringName` or a `Dictionary` keyed by an id in the snapshot, and **must never** store a float.

## Alternatives Considered

### Alternative 1: Engine physics for occlusion (`PhysicsDirectSpaceState2D.intersect_ray`, shape casts, `Area2D`), or a physics backend as a test-only cross-check (GDD Open Question 11)
- **Description**: Build the Layout footprints into a physics space and ask the engine whether a segment is blocked; optionally keep it only as a cross-check of the integer test.
- **Pros**: Less code; the engine's tested broadphase.
- **Cons**: SIM-06 bans every physics token in `src/` and kernel H10 requires scene-tree-free simulation; the 2D physics engine makes no determinism claim, so a physics-derived `lit` flowing into a persisted block would be an observation to record for replay (kernel CR-13 G3); a direct-space-state query is valid only during the physics step while prompts read at render rate; the 2D ray API is not sourceable in the pinned engine reference (its physics module carries only a 3D example); engine version churn (Jolt changes in 4.6 and 4.7) would sit under the P1 truth.
- **Rejection Reason**: Not allowed on a simulation path. **GDD Open Question 11 is answered: no physics backend exists, not even test-only.** The cross-check is an independent integer implementation (a separating-axis test), which found 0 mismatches over 300,000 cases (P5) and runs headless.

### Alternative 2: Float trigonometry and `Vector2` for the cone (`cos`, `dot`, `angle_to`)
- **Description**: Compute the cone with floats, round at the end.
- **Pros**: Obvious code, no table.
- **Cons**: SIM-03 bans floats and float-producing members in `sim`; results differ across machines and compilers at a rim, and the project requires identical `state_hash` and mirror-exact exposure.
- **Rejection Reason**: Violates the determinism contract. The Q14 table plus a Q28 squared-cosine test is exact and cheap.

### Alternative 3: Reuse the rendering occluder (`LightOccluder2D` polygons) as the sim's occluder
- **Description**: Query Lighting's occluder nodes.
- **Cons**: A presentation object read by the simulation inverts the dependency; the sim must run headless; the rendering polygons are merged and capped (ADR-0028) and are not the collision footprints.
- **Rejection Reason**: Rejected outright (GDD EM-20); the sim reads Layout's rectangles, Lighting builds its polygons from the same set (ADR-0014 `section_rects_into`).

### Alternative 4: A saturated per-subject partial sum (the GDD first draft)
- **Description**: Cache `min(1000, Sum c)` per subject.
- **Cons**: Exact for adding an emitter, wrong for removing one (a saturated 1000 has forgotten what it summed), so every output change forces a full re-evaluation of the subject.
- **Rejection Reason**: The unsaturated sum plus pair rows makes every change a delta (D11.1).

### Alternative 5: Pair rows in a hash table keyed `(emitter << 32) | subject`, or emitter-major runs
- **Description**: A `Dictionary` or one sorted array of pair keys.
- **Cons**: A `Dictionary` is iterated in insertion order (not ascending after a restore) and is banned in this module by `EMT-02`; one sorted array makes a subject's rows scattered and every register/move a mid-array insert; emitter-major needs the per-subject sums rebuilt by a second index.
- **Rejection Reason**: Subject-major fixed stride keeps a subject's rows contiguous and sorted, gives the per-subject sums in place, and bounds memory (D11.1). Emitter-side deltas find rows with a short scan of one stride per candidate subject, which the grid already enumerates.

### Alternative 6: No broadphase (a linear subject scan), or a linear scan of static emitters per subject
- **Cons**: TR-emitter-046 states the linear scan costs about 13,000 units at 174 subjects against `B` 10,000; a linear emitter scan per mover adds about 780 units at the typical point and pushes the cold build to the ceiling.
- **Rejection Reason**: Required to be a broadphase; two section-local grids (D10.4).

### Alternative 7: A Layout-owned subject index, or a room-gated broadphase
- **Description**: Put the point index in Layout, or gate candidates by room adjacency.
- **Cons**: Layout does not know subjects (ADR-0014 Alternative 5 rejected an Emitter-owned *rect* index and left the subject index here); a room gate fails for a beam that crosses an open door and for a glowstick in a corridor, and rooms are not frames.
- **Rejection Reason**: Subjects change at the rate of gameplay; the Emitter owns the index of its own populations.

### Alternative 8: One body-frame grid per body, rebuilt on every weld and cut
- **Cons**: A weld or cut moves whole sections by a translation, so every grid cell of the moved side is rewritten; the rebuild is the S8b "ALL" path on every weld.
- **Rejection Reason**: Section-local grids make a weld re-index nothing (D10.4); the per-query loop over the few sections of a body is cheaper than the rebuild.

### Alternative 9: A deterministic counted-unit cap per tick with a rebuild queue (GDD alternative (b) of EM-23)
- **Cons**: `lit` would depend on a queue that is derived and not canonical; a restore (which rebuilds cold and complete) would answer differently from the uninterrupted run (kernel G4); fail-safe-dark would blink a room's prompts on every door bump; the age bound that three designed GDDs rely on would widen.
- **Rejection Reason**: Synchronous rebuild with a ceiling counter (D4.1, D12.4).

### Alternative 10: A per-tick read-view snapshot built in W7 (GDD EM-25)
- **Cons**: A copy of every record each tick (`W_SNAP` per emitter, about 4 % of `B` at the typical point) to protect readers from a writer that cannot run concurrently with them.
- **Rejection Reason**: Columns are written only inside the hook; typed getters over them already give committed, allocation-free reads (D4.2).

### Alternative 11: Rebuild every `lit` bit against `LIT_ON_PM` in `post_restore` (the earlier ruling R6), or add a dwell
- **Cons**: A subject between the thresholds flips lit to unlit across a restore (G4 broken); a dwell keeps `lit` true after the light is gone (a P1 lie). Re-running `LitHysteresis.next` in `post_restore` over end-of-tick upstream state is rejected too: a hook ordered after `emitter.place` can write an upstream block on the snapshot tick (Interaction's M6, a DEAD `loco_state`), the `act` then differs from the one the last run saw, and the call could rewrite a canonical bit (T1).
- **Rejection Reason**: The bit is canonical, `post_restore` copies it verbatim and never calls the hysteresis, and only a live W6 applies it (D9.5).

### Alternative 12: Persist nothing pending and cold-build every row (the GDD canonical list)
- **Cons**: Breaks G4 for any mutation made by a hook ordered after `emitter.place` on the snapshot tick (D2.5).
- **Rejection Reason**: A byte per row and a header int are cheaper than an exception list.

### Alternative 13: The shape table and direction table as GDScript `const` data only, or the direction table as content JSON
- **Cons**: A code-only shape table cannot be retuned by the technical artist and cannot be validated against by the Item DB loader (`src/data` may not import `src/sim`); a JSON direction table is a derived mathematical constant that an engine or loader change could reorder, and `const PackedInt32Array` is not a constant expression (P1).
- **Rejection Reason**: Shape table, hue map and tuning are stage-1 content; the direction table is generated `Array[int]` code with a golden gate (D1.2, D1.3).

### Alternative 14: Class B mutators that return `bool`, as the GDD writes E3 and E6
- **Cons**: ADR-0003 section 5 fixes Class B as `-> void`; a bool cannot distinguish a duplicate from an overflow, which the budget counters need.
- **Rejection Reason**: `-> void` plus a caller-owned `EmitterOutcome` (D3.4); a clarification is requested of ADR-0003.

## Consequences

### Positive
- One truth for "is this lit": a pure integer kernel, a single block, one hook, and an oracle that proves the cache, the grid and the stride never change an answer.
- Save-equivalence holds for late-hook writers (D2.5) for the pending set and every canonical-backed read, with the derived-read restriction of D14.4 stated instead of an exception list; FAULT_GLOW is insensitive to the Graph's restore window by construction (D6.4).
- Weld and cut cost the Emitter no re-indexing (section-local grids) and no mutator call.
- The kernel, shape table and tuning are testable and lintable with no scene tree (TR-derelict-038), and the Item DB `emits` validation reads one published catalog.
- The budget is expressed as deterministic counts that CI can gate, with a decision ladder that fires on measured numbers rather than on a redesign.
- Two defects in the GDD's wording are found and fixed before code: the unsaturated-sum requirement for removal (kept), and the missing canonical pending state (added).

### Negative
- **The D.14 model has no margin**: the typical point is at 99.9 % of `B` before this ADR adds the grid traversal unit; the MVP may need ladder rungs 1 to 5. This ADR does not hide that (D12.6).
- Canonical state grows by pending bytes, a pending anchor pair per subject and two header ints; the GDD's EM-28 list and its acceptance criteria must be amended.
- Two slot stores, two grids per section and the oracle are more machinery than the GDD's prose suggests; the oracle and the fuzz suite are mandatory work, not optional.
- The cone and segment kernels are `## @hot` GDScript with a plain-integer style that is harder to read than vector math.
- The shape table becomes content with a freeze-time derivation, so a shape change is a data change plus a golden test, not a code edit.
- Derived reads (`lux`, `top_emitter`, `output_of`, `r_lit_of`, `r_off_of`, `section_emission`) may differ from the live run's for one run after a restore; `validate` and hooks ordered before `emitter.place` are barred from deciding state on them (D14.4, `EMT-09`).
- Acceptance waits on EM-SPIKE-1-ACCEPT (a release-export stub harness), not on the real hook, which is gated later by EM-SPIKE-1-SLICE (D12.6).

## Risks
- **R1 Budget (high, owner technical-director).** Every GDScript cost is UNVERIFIED on a release export. Mitigation: counts are CI-gated now (CNT-04), EM-SPIKE-1-ACCEPT (the S1 stub harness on RM-DESKTOP and RM-DECK, D12.6) gates Accepted and EM-SPIKE-1-SLICE (the real hook's scenario maxima) gates the Vertical Slice milestone, the ladder of D12.7 is ordered by cost, and VS-B (four beams) is already known to exceed the MVP budget (D.14: about 215 % of `B`) so it is a separate decision.
- **R2 The pending-intent columns deviate from the GDD (medium).** Mitigation: the deviation is justified by G4 with a mandatory permutation fixture and is listed as a GDD sync item for user review; if rejected, the alternative is to forbid E3/E6/E7/E8 from hooks ordered after `emitter.place`, which constrains Interaction and Horror.
- **R3 Cross-ADR assumptions (medium).** ADR-0011's persisted hook products (ruled by CT-04), ADR-0014's same-tick `changed_region` (advisory after a restore, CT-43), the Interaction repair model (ruled by CT-38 and CT-39), ADR-0020's `verb_precondition` predicate (CT-47), the Layout reads owed under CT-17 and the unspecified `WorkCounter` file; each is listed in `cross_adr_assumptions` and a change reopens the named section.
- **R4 `Array[int]` direction reads versus packed reads in a release export (low).** The probe shows 82 ns per cone test with `Array[int]`; if EM-SPIKE-1 shows a gap, the kernel can take a boot-built `PackedInt32Array` copy held by the instance (the same pattern as the square table) with no interface change.
- **R5 Stride and cell size are guesses (low).** `PAIR_ROWS_PER_SUBJECT` and `EMITTER_CELL_PX` change cost only; the oracle proves answers independent of both, and `emitter_pair_rows_overflow` reports a bad stride.
- **R6 The section grid's bounding box (low).** It is derived from `section_rects_into` at section add; a section whose anchors lie far outside its footprints is clamped to edge cells and loses efficiency, never correctness.
- **R7 Mover cost with many horrors or seats (medium).** `T_mov` scales with movers (I-CAPS (d) bound is 126 % of `B` at the GDD's pricing); the first mitigation is rung 2.
- **R8 The occlusion origin may change (low).** ADR-0018's spike may select the chest walk-back; outcome D replaces `occlusion_origin` and adds a hot, allocation-free `EmitterView` read of the beam's previous and current occlusion origin (two int pairs, derived each tick) and the `OCC_ORIGIN_MODE` tuning row, introduced together with ADR-0018's combination invariant; there is no change while B or A ships (CT-30).
- **R9 One-run derived-read divergence after a restore (medium, owner technical-director).** `post_restore` reads end-of-tick upstream state, so derived reads can differ from the live run's for one run when a late hook wrote upstream on the snapshot tick (D14.4). Mitigation: the canonical-backed reads are exact, `EMT-09` and the D2.5 fixtures (Interaction M6, a late DEAD) police the restriction, and the alternative (persist the live and output inputs of the last run) is the named fallback.

## Performance Implications
- **CPU**: `emitter.place` plus exposure at the registry P99 budget on a release export on RM-DESKTOP and RM-DECK, rare scenarios at their ceiling, cold rebuild at its ceiling; **all UNVERIFIED** (EM-SPIKE-1, S1). Deterministic counts are the per-commit gate (CNT-04). The typical D.14 point has no margin; the ladder of D12.7 and the removal of `T_snap` (D4.2) are this ADR's levers. The whole `emitter.place` hook figure is this ADR's row in the ADR-0003 P3 share ledger (CT-20).
- **Memory**: subject and emitter slot stores, pair rows (`MAX_EXPOSURE_SUBJECTS x PAIR_ROWS_PER_SUBJECT` entries in four columns: about 50 KB at stride 16), section grids (a few KB per section), one `QueryBuffer` (about 50 KB, ADR-0014), the direction table (two constant arrays of `AIM_UNITS` ints) and the squares table: roughly 200 KB in all, none of it persisted.
- **Load Time**: the cold build (`post_restore` and the `init_world` build) is the S6 class, held to `COLD_REBUILD_CEILING_MS`; the one-time boot checks (`I-SQRT` over 147,457 values, `I-MIRROR` over `AIM_UNITS` entries) are boot work, not per-run.
- **Network**: none; `SHARED_SUMMARY` contents for VS-B are an open question.

## Migration Plan
No Emitter code exists. Implementation order (each step lands with its tests and lint fixtures): (1) `tools/emitter/gen_dir_table.gd`, `dir_table.gd`, `EMT-GOLD-01`; (2) the shared kernel with its unit tests and the separating-axis oracle; (3) stage-1 schemas and catalogs (`ShapeCatalog`, `HueMap`, `EmitterTuning`, `GlowTable`) and the boot checks; (4) `EmitterState`/`EmitterDerived`, snapshot/restore/verifier and the zero-and-diff test; (5) mutators and commands with the AC-33 matrix; (6) the hook W0..W7 against `TestBruteExposure` (no grid, no rows first: the oracle path is also the cold-build path); (7) **conditional on step 6 measured against `B` on the S1 harness (EM-SPIKE-1-ACCEPT)**: the point grids, pair rows and `static_epoch` mover sweep, built only if the oracle and cold-build path misses `B` with margin at the D.14 typical point; if it meets `B` with margin, ship it and keep step 7 and the later rungs as ladder rungs behind the same API; the transparency fuzz is the entry condition for step 7 (T16); (8) `EmitterView`, keys and the Lighting contract; (9) the CNT-04 scenario suite and EM-SPIKE-1-SLICE (EM-SPIKE-1-ACCEPT is measured earlier, at step 6, on the S1 harness). Steps 6 and 7 are ordered so that the slow, obviously correct implementation exists before the optimised one, and the optimised one is built only on evidence.

## Open Questions
1. **Budget margin (R1).** The D.14 typical point has about 11 units of headroom and this ADR adds an unpriced traversal unit; EM-SPIKE-1-ACCEPT (the S1 stub harness) selects the ladder rung before Acceptance and EM-SPIKE-1-SLICE confirms it with the real hook's scenario maxima. 5. **VS-B**: `SHARED_SUMMARY` contents (beam origin, aim, shape, switch) and the four-beam budget (the GDD prices it at over twice `B`); not decided here.
6. **Closed (CT-24): `BODY_COORD_LIMIT`.** ADR-0015 defines `BODY_COORD_LIMIT` (BodyTuning plus the bounds ledger, at most 2^24, restore-checked, repeated by Layout in `can_attach`); `I-BOUNDS` cites it and fails the boot above 2^24 (D8.3, D15).

8. **ADR-0018 outcome D** (chest walk-back) replaces `occlusion_origin` and adds a hot, allocation-free `EmitterView` read of the beam's previous and current occlusion origin (two int pairs, derived each tick) and the `OCC_ORIGIN_MODE` tuning row, introduced together with ADR-0018's combination invariant; no change while B or A ships (CT-30); confirm after the Lighting spike.
