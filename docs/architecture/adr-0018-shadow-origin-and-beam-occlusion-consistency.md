## Summary
The simulation starts every beam occlusion segment at the foot (`body.state` `pos_curr`, as returned by `EmitterDerived.occlusion_origin(row, out_xy)`, ADR-0017 D5.3) while the cone's apex and reach start at the chest, and the engine draws shadows from the position of the light node, so a drawn beam can disagree with the `lit` answer the prompts use, or render black while the simulation reports it lit. This ADR decides that the **drawn shadow origin must be the simulation's occlusion origin at the same lattice point** (a cell centre, which is half a base pixel from an integer node position), selects the mechanism by a fixed ladder **B (node at the foot, `PointLight2D.offset` carries the apex), then B-pad (B with a beam texture padded so its quad contains the foot), then A (node at the chest, a control arm), then D (the simulation walks its origin back), C rejected**, records that the 2026-10-29 probes support the origin and lattice rule on a centred test texture and predict A fails but **do not yet show B on the real edge-apex texture** (engine culling of occluders by the light's quad is untested, SO-P10), and keeps the ADR `Proposed` until lighting spike step 3b reports on the reference machines and a release export.

## Context

### Problem Statement
Two things are true at once, by user decision. The beam is a chest lamp: its apex and reach are measured from the Controller's chest point, `ControllerView.get_chest_x/get_chest_y` (ADR-0019 § 2.3; there is no `chest_sim` field, CT-28; the GDD's EM-6 name `chest_sim` is a sync mapping), 25 to 45 px above the feet. And the simulation's occlusion segment, the one that decides whether a prompt is lit, starts at the foot (`EmitterDerived.occlusion_origin` returns `pos_curr`, EM-6 and EM-20 (g)), because `LocomotionStep` keeps `pos_curr` outside every footprint by construction while the chest point can stand inside a wall.

The renderer draws a shadow from where the light node sits. If the node is at the chest, the shadow starts at the chest. With the feet 10 to 34 px south of a north wall the chest lies inside the 24 px footprint; in the probe the beam then renders black everywhere while the simulation reports 24,191 cells lit (P-9). With the feet closer than that the chest lies beyond the wall and the shadows are cast from the next compartment (66 % of cells wrong at s = 1). Both are the P1 lie the GDD exists to prevent: a prompt lit where the screen shows dark, or the opposite, "every time a player walks up to a wall" (Emitter Open Question 5).

The lighting GDD (LO-10) already names the candidates (B: node at the foot, `offset` moves the texture; A: node at the chest; C: two lights; D: the simulation occludes from the chest) and the order of preference, and makes the choice a spike result (step 3b). What is missing, and what this ADR supplies, is (1) a contract precise enough to fail, (2) the lattice and frame facts that make the contract achievable and that nobody has written down, (3) the selection rule so the spike is mechanical, (4) the interface so the Avatar Presenter, Lighting and the Emitter can be built before the spike, and (5) the decision whether to keep this as its own ADR.

### Constraints
- The Emitter is integer, scene-tree-free and never references a light class (TR-emitter-003). It cannot read what the renderer drew. Agreement is therefore enforced by construction (same origin, same lattice, same rectangles) and measured by test, never by feedback.
- One rect set serves collision, light and shadow (TR-section-011, ADR-0014). The occluder polygons are the `OCCLUDES_LIGHT` rectangles and invent no geometry (LO-11).
- Presentation places every node through `Placement` at an integer base-pixel position (ADR-0009 § 4, TR-pxl-005; a `Light2D` position is the one lattice exception, § 3); the Avatar Presenter writes exactly `position` and `rotation` of the beam node (ADR-0019 § 7.2, TR-pc-026, TR-pc-040); Lighting writes every value member, including `offset` (LO-2, TR-light-006). The Presenter runs in stage AVATAR, Lighting in stage LIGHTING, always in that order (ADR-0009 § 3).
- At most `SHADOW_SLOTS_MAX` shadow-casting lights (TR-light-013): outcome C (two lights per beam) is rejected on that budget by the GDD and is not re-opened.
- The engine reference says nothing about these classes. Every engine claim carries a probe or UNVERIFIED.
- The world viewport renders at base resolution under pass A (the MVP build, ADR-0008 § 4), so light positions are sampled at base-pixel centres.

### Requirements
- TR-light-015: the shadow origin matches the simulation's segment origin (foot, via `body.state` `pos_curr` / the occlusion origin) versus the chest apex, by a spike-decided mechanism (preference B, then B-pad, A, D; C rejected).
- AC-57 / AC-LO-15: for s in {1, 10, 20, 34, 44}, aim N, S, E, W and every `CHEST_POINT` row (idle, walk, run, carry, work, stagger, down), the drawn beam and the simulation agree to within 2 base px on whether the wall blocks the cone, and no row shows a beam that renders black while the simulation reports it lit. A failing row selects outcome D.
- The mechanism must not change the number of shadow-casting lights, must allocate nothing per frame, and must be selectable by data so that the spike result flips a constant rather than rewrites code.

### Why the contract has to be an identity of origin, not a tolerance
Take a silhouette vertex V of a wall at distance d_V from an origin O. Displacing the origin by delta perpendicular to OV rotates the tangent ray through V by about delta / d_V radians. A subject at distance r beyond V is misclassified over a band of lateral width about r * delta / d_V. With the foot and the chest 45 px apart, d_V = 20 px (the AC-57 geometry) and r = 150 px that band is hundreds of pixels wide; even the 0.71 px that separates an integer node position from a cell centre gives about 5 px of band at those numbers. A criterion of the form "within 2 px at the subject" therefore cannot be met by *approximately* the same origin near a wall; it is met only when the two origins are the same point on the same lattice, and the residual is then the engine's own shadow sampling (`SHADOW_ATLAS_PX`, the filter). The probes agree with the arithmetic: moving the origin by half a cell changed the mismatch count by 3 to 10 times (P-7) and moved some mismatches beyond 2 px of the simulation's boundary (P-8).

### The two frame facts nobody wrote down
1. **The lattice.** The simulation's integer coordinates name pixel cells, each a unit square *centred* on its integer coordinate (EM-20 (a); Layout LY-12 (b)). A stored rectangle `[x0, x1)` therefore has the continuous box `[x0 - 1/2, x1 - 1/2]` in simulation coordinates. In the render frame a tile or rectangle at integer origin `x0` covers `[x0, x1)`, and the occluder polygon's vertices are those integers (LO-11). So **render = simulation + 1/2** on each axis: the polygons need no shift, and the simulation's cell centre `n` is the render point `n + 1/2`. A light node written at the integer `n` (what `Placement.put` writes) sits at the *corner* of the foot's cell, 0.707 px from the cell centre the simulation uses and, when the foot cell touches a wall's east or south face, **exactly on the polygon face**, where P-6 shows whole regions flip.
2. **The interpolation lag.** The Presenter places the drawn foot at `Placement.at(prev, curr, alpha)`; the simulation uses `pos_curr`. They are equal at rest and when `alpha` is 1, and differ by at most one tick of travel (`V_RUN` per axis per tick, registry) otherwise. This is the same one-tick presentation lag every drawn thing has (the prompts too, LO-7), not a shadow-specific error.

### 1. Scope, and the decision not to fold into ADR-0028

**Decided: ADR-0018 stays a separate, small ADR.** The architecture note allows folding into ADR-0028 "if the lighting spike settles both at once". It is not folded because:

1. **The spike has not run.** The fold condition is not met; folding on a forecast would hide the one result that can still change the Core tier.
2. **Outcome D edits the simulation.** If B, B-pad and A all fail, `EmitterDerived.occlusion_origin` changes (EM-6, EM-20 (g), D.4, D.6, AC-10 are re-derived). That is a Core (ADR-0017) change; ADR-0028 is a Presentation-tier budget ADR (node pools, shadow slots, the modifier chain) and the layering rule forbids a Presentation ADR from carrying a simulation contingency.
3. **Two stories need the interface before ADR-0028 exists.** The Avatar Presenter writes the beam node (ADR-0019 § 7) and the Emitter computes the occlusion origin (ADR-0017). Both need § 2 to § 4 now.

**Fold-down rule (what moves, when).** Once this ADR is Accepted with outcome B (or B-pad) recorded, nothing in the simulation depends on it any more; the Lighting-side implementation detail (§ 4.2 `BeamOffset` scheduling, § 4.4 the occluder set, the probe pack) is then *carried by* ADR-0028 by reference, and this ADR stays as the short decision record with its Spike Result. **What ADR-0028 must carry:** (a) every `Light2D` position, shadow-casting or not, is written through `Placement.put_light_origin` (§ 3); (b) `PointLight2D.offset` of every hard-shaped light is written every render frame, write-on-change, by `BeamOffset.offset_for` with v = (0, 0) for non-beams (§ 4.2); (c) the merged occluder polygons use `CULL_DISABLED` and integer vertices, with no shift (§ 3.3), and the builder tests that assert those constraints (cull mode, integer vertices, vertex-set equality with the `OCCLUDES_LIGHT` rectangles, `closed` true, `sdf_collision` false, explicit masks; formerly SHO-05 and SHO-06) live in ADR-0028's occluder-builder tests, because ADR-0014 and ADR-0028 own that builder and its LO-11 set-equality contract; (d) the shadow-reach set is measured from the apex (§ 4.4); (e) the probe pack of § 8.2 is a Lighting regression gate on every engine patch bump; (f) `SHADOW_ORIGIN_MODE` is a `LightingTuning` row with values 0 and 1 (the combination invariant arrives only with outcome D, § 7); (g) the shadow-light placement check of § 3.5 (SHO-07).

### 2. The identity contract and its error budget

**Definitions.** `O_sim` is the integer cell returned by `EmitterDerived.occlusion_origin(row, out_xy)` (ADR-0017 D5.3) for the emitter that casts the light: for a BEAM the foot (`pos_curr`), for every other emitter kind its floor-plane anchor (EM-6: "other emitter kinds use their floor-plane anchor for both"). `render(p)` is `Vector2(p) + CELL_CENTRE` with `CELL_CENTRE = (0.5, 0.5)`. `O_draw` is the global position of the shadow-casting `Light2D` node in the body frame, which P-1 shows is the point the engine casts shadows from.

**Contract (SO-1).** For every shadow-casting light at rest (`prev == curr`, or `alpha` 1), `O_draw == render(O_sim)` exactly (a zero-error lattice identity; `x + 0.5` is float32 exact for integer `|x| < 2^23` px, probed, and the bound that matters is the light's *canvas* coordinate, the body-frame position plus the body's canvas offset: ADR-0015 publishes `BODY_COORD_LIMIT` and the canvas bound `|world_a - world_b| + |pos| < 2^23` under `WEB_COORD_BUDGET_PX`, and lowers `WORLD_COORD_LIMIT` otherwise (ruling CT-24); this ADR assumes both). In motion `|O_draw - render(O_sim)|` is at most one tick of travel per axis, the presentation-wide lag of §"The two frame facts" (2). The cone *apex* is separate: it is the beam's chest point and is placed by `offset` (§ 4).

**Error budget (what is and is not a term).**

| Term | Bound | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Lattice (integer node against cell centre) | 0 after § 3; 0.707 px (0.5 per axis) without it | removed by decision (P-7, P-8) |
| Interpolation lag | at most `V_RUN` per axis per tick, zero at rest | accepted, not corrected (the same lag as every drawn thing; correcting only the shadow would make the shadow lead its own beam) |
| Apex placement | float32 only (probe P-3: centroid error 0.00 at five angles); up to `|v| * sin(delta_theta)` (1.7 px for PC-5's 2.8 degrees) *if* `offset` is computed from a rotation other than the one on the node | removed by ordering (§ 4.3) and checked every frame by SHO-03 |
| Aim lead (PC-5, up to 2.8 degrees) | applies to the **cone edge**, not to the origin: probe P-1 shows the shadow origin does not depend on rotation | not an origin term; accepted by PC-5 and out of scope here |
| Engine shadow sampling (`SHADOW_ATLAS_PX`, `shadow_filter`) | measured by SO-P6 and SO-P8; NONE gave 8 to 16 boundary mismatches of 88,031 cells and PCF13 179 | spike-decided (§ 8) |

**Measure (SO-2, the boundary-robust metric).** A mismatching cell counts at radius r when every cell within Chebyshev distance r has the same simulation answer; the metric is the count of mismatches *at radius r*, i.e. disagreements more than r px from any simulation boundary. "Agree to within 2 base px" (AC-57) is **zero mismatches at radius 2**; the tighter envelope that matches the project's locked +/- 1 px contracts (I-EDGE, the prompt agreement of LO-7) is **zero at radius 1**. P-8 gives the shape of the answer: with the lattice applied both are zero in all four scenes; with an integer node position radius 1 is not zero (21 cells in one scene); with a node half a cell the wrong way radius 2 is not zero (40 cells). AC-57's tolerance is therefore read as the radius-2 criterion and the radius-1 count is recorded beside it, not waived; the spike decides whether the radius-1 requirement holds under the shipped `shadow_filter` (§ 8, SO-P8).

### 3. The lattice: where the origin goes

**3.1 Decision (SO-3).** Every shadow-casting `Light2D` node is positioned at `Placement.at(...)` (or the anchor, for a static emitter) **plus `CELL_CENTRE`**, written only through one new `Placement` function:

```gdscript
# src/presentation/pipeline/placement.gd (ADR-0009 § 4 gains this function and this constant; Foundation ask F1)
## The simulation's cell n is centred on n; the render frame's pixel n spans [n, n + 1). A point-like origin on the simulation's lattice is therefore at n + CELL_CENTRE.
const CELL_CENTRE: Vector2 = Vector2(0.5, 0.5)

## The only assignment point for a Light2D position. p is an integer cell (already floored by Placement.at, or an anchor). Skips an unchanged write.
static func put_light_origin(node: Light2D, p: Vector2i) -> void:
	var v: Vector2 = Vector2(p) + CELL_CENTRE
	if node.position != v:
		node.position = v
```

It applies to **every** `Light2D` Lighting or the Presenter positions, not only the six shadow-casting ones (the beam, the beacon, the lent fixtures, slot 6): one rule is cheaper to lint than a per-kind table (`put_light_origin(node: Light2D, ...)` is valid on the abstract type because it touches only `position`), and it is also the more accurate origin for the non-shadow pools, whose radius test in the simulation is measured from the same cell centre (EM-22). A shadow-casting light on a floor-plane anchor must not stand inside a closed occluder (§ 3.5, SHO-07).

**3.2 Why this and not the alternatives** (Alternatives 3 and 4). Probed: a fractional light position is honoured by the shadow (P-5) and the viewport snap flags do not round it; the half cell cuts mismatches by 3 to 10 times (P-7) and removes every beyond-radius-1 disagreement (P-8). Shifting the occluder polygons by -1/2 instead puts a polygon face through every pixel centre in pass A, which is the degenerate case, breaks LO-11's integer set equality and puts the shadow edge half a pixel off the wall art.

**3.3 The polygon side is unchanged.** Occluder polygon vertices are the stored integer corners of the `OCCLUDES_LIGHT` rectangles in the body frame node, with no shift; the node's cull mode is `CULL_DISABLED` (P-4: `CULL_CLOCKWISE` leaks through walls for lights outside them, 35.94 % at s = 34 in P-9). The body frame offset is one floored integer per body (TR-shipbody-033), so the relation holds on every live body. Under this rule a shadow-casting light is never on a polygon face: faces are on integers and origins on half-integers, which removes the degenerate case of P-6 by parity.

**Node settings (engine gotchas, probed or UNVERIFIED as stated).** Every pooled `LightOccluder2D` sets `sdf_collision = false` (default true; it would make the pool SDF contributors and nothing samples the SDF; cost UNVERIFIED) and sets `occluder_light_mask` explicitly; every shadow-casting `Light2D` sets `shadow_item_cull_mask` explicitly to include that mask (both default to 1; explicit values stop a later layer change from silently dropping shadows). `OccluderPolygon2D.closed` stays true (the default, probed), which is what makes § 3.5 a hazard. Shadow resolution is bounded by `rendering/2d/shadow_atlas/size`, shared by all shadow-casting lights; the value is recorded with every SO-P6 and SO-P8 count. The tests that assert these constraints belong to ADR-0028's occluder-builder tests (§ 1 (c)).

**3.4 Consequences for the placement contract (Foundation ask F1).**
- `PRES-03` and its named allow-list (ADR-0009 § 4, ADR-0010 § 6.6) gain exactly two entries: `Placement.put_light_origin` as the only writer of a `Light2D` `position`, and Lighting's `beam_offset_writer.gd` as the only writer of a `PointLight2D` `offset` (SHO-01, SHO-02).
- The `PLACEMENT_FRACTIONAL` post-frame sweep ("`global_position` integral within epsilon 0") exempts nodes in the group `light_origin` and asserts the *lattice* instead: the fractional part of `global_position` is exactly 0.5 on both axes (SHO-03). The sweep's own bound (integer exactness to 2^24) becomes 2^23 for these nodes, which the canvas bound ADR-0015 publishes (`|world_a - world_b| + |pos| < 2^23`, ruling CT-24) respects.
- ADR-0009 § 4's sentence "the beam's chest point and the Avatar sprite use the same rounded position" is true of the *integer cell* both start from; the light node is that cell plus `CELL_CENTRE`, the sprite is that cell. The two never disagree by more than the constant.
- **Registry rows and TR rows this contradicts, requested for amendment together with the ADR-0009 § 4 and ADR-0010 § 6.6 changes (F1, F2; `docs/registry/architecture.yaml`).** Row at line 961 (presentation coordinates are integer-valued float32, converted from `Vector2i` only at `Placement.put` and the `canvas_transform` write) gains `Placement.put_light_origin` as a conversion site for `Light2D` lattice positions. Row at line 971 (`sim_motion_interpolation`: positions are written only through `Placement.put` or `put_control`) notes that a `Light2D` position is `Placement.at` plus `CELL_CENTRE` through `put_light_origin`. Row at line 1398 (forbidden pattern: the position, global position, offset and transform of any `Node2D` are written only in `placement.gd` and the named allow-list) gains `placement.gd` `put_light_origin` (`Light2D` position) and `lighting/beam_offset_writer.gd` (`PointLight2D` offset); `Light2D` is a `Node2D`, so the `Sprite2D.offset` distinction of F2 does not exempt these and they must be named. TR-pxl-005 ("every node at integer base px, incl. the beam chest point") and TR-pc-026 (`Light2D` position by a "floored lerp with zero fractional part") are re-worded to read "`Light2D` position is the floored cell plus `CELL_CENTRE`", as are the GDD rules behind them (PR-4 rule 2; the zero-fractional-part statement of PC-1 (c) / AC-PCI-208). **Acceptance of this ADR is conditional on ADR-0009 accepting F1** (OQ-A).

**3.5 A shadow-casting light must not stand inside an `OCCLUDES_LIGHT` box (SHO-07).** Probed (P-4): with `CULL_DISABLED` a shadow-casting light whose origin lies inside a closed polygon renders the whole view dark (luminance 0.00), and `OccluderPolygon2D.closed` is true by default and stays true. The beam foot is outside every footprint by construction (`LocomotionStep`), but a beacon, a lent fixture or a wall lamp anchored on a floor-plane anchor in or next to an `OCCLUDES_LIGHT` rectangle is covered by no other check. **Rule:** for every shadow-casting light anchor `p`, the cell centre `p + CELL_CENTRE` must not lie inside any `OCCLUDES_LIGHT` box (the same doubled-box test as EM-20 (b)); otherwise the light is demoted to non-shadow, or the content fails validation. SHO-07 enforces it at the boot walk and as a content-validation rule.

### 4. The beam: node, offset, ordering

**4.1 Node topology and writers (fixed; this is LO-2 plus the lattice).** One `PointLight2D` per bound seat beam. Its parent is the **translation-only `BodyRoot` of `BodyView.body_of_actor(actor)`** (never the avatar, the tool layer or any node that rotates). The **Lighting presenter** (stage LIGHTING 6, ADR-0028) builds the node, is the only caller of `BodyScenes.attach_light(actor_id, beam_id, node)` (namespace `LIGHTING`, keyed by the seat's beam id; ADR-0034 § 7.1), owns the node's lifetime (it calls `detach` and frees the node when the beam unbinds) and leaves the node disabled until the Avatar Presenter's first stage-2 position write has landed (ruling R9, confirmed); ADR-0028 must carry the Lighting presenter's create, `attach_light` and `detach` duties (forward reference: ADR-0028 is not yet written); the Avatar Presenter never creates, attaches or frees it. The stage-0 `BODY_TRANSFER` reparent of ADR-0034 § 8 moves the attached node, with `reparent(p, false)`, in the same frame as the Presenter node and before the stage-2 position write; the owning Avatar Presenter then writes the position from the new frame before draw (ruling CT-32; the cross-ADR owner of the attach path is ADR-0034). Without this, a pod hop or a weld rebase leaves the light in the wrong frame and `O_draw` differs from `render(O_sim)` by the inter-body canvas offset, the P1 drawn-against-simulated lie that SO-1 exists to prevent (SHO-04, SHO-T6). The Avatar Presenter writes `position` (through `put_light_origin`) and `rotation` (raw aim for the local seat, committed aim for a remote one, ADR-0019 § 7.1); Lighting writes `offset` and every other value member. The two member sets are disjoint (TR-light-006, owned by ADR-0028; this ADR depends on it and does not restate its lint).

**4.2 `BeamOffset`, the apex term (SO-4).** With the probed node-local, unscaled `offset` (P-2), the apex lands at `node + Rot(theta) * (offset + a)`, where `a` is the vector from the texture centre to the texture apex in the quad's local frame, in base px at the drawn scale (the sidecar's `apex_offset_texels / D_tex`, LO-15). Requiring the apex at the chest, `node + v`, gives:

```gdscript
# src/presentation/lighting/beam_offset.gd  (pure; no Node; golden-vector testable headless)
class_name BeamOffset extends RefCounted

## offset = Rot(-theta) * v - a. v is the integer vector from the occlusion origin to the apex (the chest offset for a beam, (0, 0) for every other light);
## theta is the rotation READ BACK from the node this frame; a is the texture apex displacement from the texture centre at the drawn scale.
static func offset_for(theta: float, vx: int, vy: int, ax: float, ay: float) -> Vector2:
	return Vector2(float(vx), float(vy)).rotated(-theta) - Vector2(ax, ay)
```

The same function serves every hard-shaped light: for a beacon or a lent fixture `v = (0, 0)` and the offset is the texture's own apex term (LO-2: "under both outcomes Lighting writes `offset` every render frame"). `OFFSET_FRAME` (LO tuning knob, "discovered") is **LOCAL** on 4.7.2 (P-2, both renderers). Only the LOCAL branch is implemented: the GDD's WORLD formula `v - Rot(theta) * a` would be dead code on every observed build; if a spike machine disagrees, the branch is added by amendment and the probe pack (SO-P2) is what reports it. Write-on-change applies (LO-3); `Vector2` is a value type, so the function allocates nothing. `beam_offset_writer.gd` types its light parameter `PointLight2D`: `offset`, `texture` and `texture_scale` do not exist on `Light2D`, and a writer typed `Light2D` fails at run time on the abstract type. Every hard-shaped light is a `PointLight2D`; a `DirectionalLight2D` never goes through `BeamOffset`.

**4.3 Ordering.** The Presenter's `rotation` write (stage AVATAR, 2) precedes Lighting's `offset` write (stage LIGHTING, 6) in every frame (ADR-0009 § 3; no hook, no `after:` entry, no new mechanism). Lighting reads the rotation back from the node (`light.rotation`), not from a copy, so a stale value cannot exist. The apex-equals-chest check (SHO-03) runs every frame in dev builds: it recomputes the apex from the node's actual `global_transform` and `offset` and compares it with `render(o) + v` to 0.01 px.

**4.4 The shadow-reach set is measured from the apex.** LO-11 caps `OCCLUDER_POLYS_VIEW_MAX` over "occluders within `reach_px + OCC_MARGIN_PX` of an active shadow-casting light". For a beam under B the node is at the foot and the lit region is a function of the apex. **Decided: the set is measured from the apex (the chest point), for every outcome.** Proof that nothing blocking is missed: let P be a lit point (within `reach_px` of the apex C) and F the foot; a rectangle that blocks F to P meets the segment FP at some Q, and the distance from C is convex along a segment, so `|QC| <= max(|FC|, |PC|) <= max(|v|max, reach_px)`; every beam shape's `reach_px` exceeds `|v|max` (about 45), so the rectangle is within `reach_px` of C and in the set with `OCC_MARGIN_PX` 0. The knob `OCC_MARGIN_PX` keeps the GDD's B-value under B and its A-value under A, unchanged. **This rule is necessary but not sufficient.** `OCC_MARGIN_PX` sizes Lighting's own emitted set; it cannot change which occluders the engine attaches to a light. Whether the engine culls an occluder against the light's texture-quad extent is UNVERIFIED (the recollection for the 4.x canvas renderer is that an occluder attaches to a light only if its bounds intersect the light's quad, which includes `offset` and `texture_scale`; not checkable headless). If it does, B is exactly the wrong geometry: the node (shadow origin) is at the foot, the quad is moved up to the chest apex and, for an edge-apex cone, extends away from the foot, so a wall wholly between foot and apex (the AC-57 s = 10 to 33 rows) lies outside the quad and casts no shadow, and the beam lights through a wall the simulation reports as blocking. That is a **pass condition for B**, not a margin-sizing question: SO-P10 tests it, and rung B-pad (§ 5) is the pre-registered texture-side fallback.

### 6. The mode is data

`LightingTuning.SHADOW_ORIGIN_MODE` is a `ScalarTable` row (ADR-0007 § 1.5) with `FOOT_OFFSET = 0` (B and B-pad) and `CHEST_NODE = 1` (A). The Presenter chooses the node's origin cell from it, once at setup: the foot (`Placement.at(body.prev, body.curr, alpha)`) for 0, the chest cell for 1. There is **no combination invariant and no Emitter-side mode row at ship**: ADR-0017 implements the foot origin as the function `EmitterDerived.occlusion_origin` with no tuning row, so the only reachable pairs, (foot, 0) and (foot, 1), are both legal and a check could never fire (ruling CT-30; the earlier `OCC_ORIGIN_MODE` reservation, the `LIGHT_SHADOW_ORIGIN_COMBO` invariant, its F3 boot-code reservation and SHO-T5 are removed from this decision). The outcome-D amendment (§ 7) adds value 2, the Emitter `OCC_ORIGIN_MODE` row and the combination invariant together, so the check exists only when it can fail. The spike result therefore changes one constant and nothing else; under B nothing in the simulation changes.

### Implementation Guidelines

- **Must** position every `Light2D` through `Placement.put_light_origin` and no other path; **must never** write a shadow-casting light's `position` with `Placement.put`, `position =`, `global_position =` or any relative-move call (lint SHO-01, PRES-03).
- **Must** write `PointLight2D.offset` of a hard-shaped light every render frame, write-on-change, from `BeamOffset.offset_for` with the rotation read back from the same node, in stage LIGHTING; **must never** write it from the Presenter or from a cached rotation (SHO-02, SHO-03).
- **Must** parent the beam node to the translation-only `BodyRoot` of `BodyView.body_of_actor(actor)` through `BodyScenes` `LIGHTING` (re-parented at stage 0 with `reparent(p, false)` on `BODY_TRANSFER`, position rewritten by the owning Presenter the same frame) and **must never** parent a `Light2D` to the avatar, the tool layer or any node that rotates (LO-2, SHO-04).
- **Must** build occluder polygons from the integer corners of `OCCLUDES_LIGHT` rectangles with `CULL_DISABLED` and no shift; **must never** use `CULL_CLOCKWISE` or `CULL_COUNTER_CLOCKWISE` (P-4, P-9), a `TileSet` occluder layer, or a polygon vertex off the integer lattice.
- **Must** measure the shadow-reach occluder set from the apex (§ 4.4); **must never** measure it from the node.
- **Must** read the foot from `body.state` (`pos_x`, `pos_y`, `prev_x`, `prev_y`, ADR-0015 § 11.3) under B and **must never** read `controller.state` for the origin; the chest offset is the only Controller read.
- **Must** select the mode from `LightingTuning.SHADOW_ORIGIN_MODE` once at setup; **must never** branch on it per frame inside a hot loop (a strategy chosen at `setup()`).
- **Must** set `sdf_collision = false` and an explicit `occluder_light_mask` on pooled occluders and an explicit `shadow_item_cull_mask` on every shadow-casting light (§ 3.3); **must never** anchor a shadow-casting light where its cell centre lies inside an `OCCLUDES_LIGHT` box (§ 3.5, SHO-07).
- **Must not** add a second shadow-casting light per beam (C), a physics query, or a renderer-to-simulation feedback of any kind (TR-light-002).
- **Must** rerun the probe pack of § 8.2 when the engine patch version, the renderer method, the driver pin or `shadow_filter` changes, and keep its log in `production/qa/evidence/`.

## Alternatives Considered

### Alternative 1: Fold the decision into ADR-0028
- **Description**: ADR-0028 (2D lighting budget and modifier chain) carries the shadow origin as one section, as the architecture note allows if the spike settles both at once.
- **Pros**: one fewer ADR; the Lighting-side detail lives with the rest of Lighting.
- **Cons**: the spike has not run, so the fold condition is false; outcome D edits the Core simulation and cannot live in a Presentation ADR; the Avatar Presenter and `emitter.place` stories need the interface before ADR-0028 is written.
- **Rejection Reason**: § 1. The fold-down rule keeps the benefit (ADR-0028 carries the implementation by reference after Acceptance) without the layering cost.

### Alternative 2: Outcome A as the default (node at the chest, accept the discrepancy)
- **Description**: the GDD's second preference: simplest scene, the node follows the chest point.
- **Pros**: no `offset` choreography beyond the texture's own apex term; no ordering dependency between AVATAR and LIGHTING.
- **Cons**: probed 2026-10-29 to fail exactly where AC-57 looks: a black beam while the simulation reports 24,191 cells lit (s = 20), 27 % to 66 % of cells wrong for s <= 20, and no cull mode repairs it (`CULL_CLOCKWISE` leaks 35.94 % even with the chest outside). The parallax argument (§ Context) says the failure is structural near walls, not a tuning matter.
- **Rejection Reason**: kept as the control arm (§ 5), not as a candidate; shipping it would knowingly ship the P1 lie.

### Alternative 3: Integer node position, accept the half-cell error
- **Description**: write the beam node with `Placement.put` (no `CELL_CENTRE`) and rely on the 2 px AC-57 tolerance.
- **Pros**: no new `Placement` function, no allow-list entry, no sweep exemption; the half cell is invisible to the eye.
- **Cons**: P-7 shows 3 to 10 times more mismatches; P-8 shows disagreements more than 1 px from the simulation's boundary (21 cells in one scene) which breaks the project's +/- 1 px contracts; a foot cell touching a wall's east or south face puts the light exactly on the polygon face, where P-6 shows whole regions flip.
- **Rejection Reason**: the fix is one constant and one function, and it is exact; the cost is a reviewed carve-out in ADR-0009 (Foundation ask F1), not a design cost.

### Alternative 4: Shift the occluder polygons by -1/2 instead of the light by +1/2
- **Description**: keep integer light positions and move every wall polygon half a pixel up and left.
- **Pros**: no fractional node positions anywhere.
- **Cons**: puts a polygon face through every base-pixel centre under pass A (the degenerate case); breaks LO-11's integer set equality ("the union of the merged polygons equals the union of Layout's rectangles exactly"); the shadow edge no longer coincides with the wall art edge by half a pixel; every consumer of the polygons needs the shift.
- **Rejection Reason**: moves the error onto the one set that must be exact.

### Alternative 5: Outcome D as the default (the simulation occludes from a chest walk-back)
- **Description**: change EM-6 now: the simulation's occlusion origin is the chest, walked back to the first clear point; the node follows it.
- **Pros**: the drawn and simulated origins coincide whatever the engine does with `offset`; no `offset` choreography for the origin.
- **Cons**: edits the Core Emitter contract before there is any evidence it is needed (B passes the origin and lattice probes; the real-texture rows are SO-P10); a per-tick walk query for every beam; the walk-back point jumps at wall depth; EM-6's own ruling prefers the foot ("`pos_curr` is outside every footprint by construction and free of the 2 px mirror jitter").
- **Rejection Reason**: it is the contingency (§ 7), specified so it is cheap, and adopted only if B and A fail.

### Alternative 6: Two lights per beam (outcome C)
- **Description**: a cone at the chest plus a shadow-only caster at the foot.
- **Pros**: exact origin for the shadows.
- **Cons**: four beams become eight lights against `SHADOW_SLOTS_MAX`; breaks the shadow-slot topology (LO-9) and the lend mechanism.
- **Rejection Reason**: rejected by the GDD on budget; unchanged.

### Alternative 7: A hand-built shadow (own shadow polygons drawn as dark meshes)
- **Description**: compute the shadow in script from the same integer rectangles and the exact origin, and draw it as geometry, bypassing the engine's shadow pass.
- **Pros**: exact by construction; no dependence on how the engine casts shadows.
- **Cons**: a second shadow system, per-frame polygon work in GDScript for up to six lights (UNVERIFIED cost, S1), no soft edge or filter, and it competes with the engine light pass it must composite against; contradicts LO-11's use of engine `LightOccluder2D`.
- **Rejection Reason**: unnecessary while the engine's shadow, fed the right origin, is measured (on a centred test texture) to agree to within 1 px of the simulation's boundary.

### Alternative 8: A tolerance-only contract (no origin identity)
- **Description**: leave the origin free and state only "agree to within 2 px" (AC-57 as written).
- **Pros**: no lattice rule, no ordering dependency.
- **Cons**: § Context's parallax arithmetic shows a tolerance on the subject cannot be met by an approximately equal origin near a wall; it makes the spike result a coin-flip.
- **Rejection Reason**: the identity contract is what makes the tolerance achievable; the tolerance is kept as the measure (SO-2).

## Consequences

### Positive
- The spike is mechanical: a ladder (B, B-pad, A, D), ten probes and one table, with the answer pre-registered. The origin and lattice rule is shown on one machine; B on the real edge-apex texture is not yet shown (SO-P10), and its fallback is pre-registered.
- The simulation does not change under B. The only presentation change is one `Placement` function, one lint allow-list entry and one sweep exemption.
- One lattice rule covers every light, and it removes the polygon-face degeneracy by parity.
- D is specified and cheap: the Presenter already takes its origin from a named source.
- The interface unblocks the Avatar Presenter and Lighting stories before ADR-0028 exists.

### Negative
- A fractional `Light2D.position` is a deliberate exception to "every written position has a zero fractional part" (ADR-0009 § 4), which needs Foundation sign-off (F1) and a second lint rule (SHO-03 lattice check) beside the integral sweep.
- B has an ordering dependency (AVATAR before LIGHTING) that A would not; it is satisfied by an existing stage order, but a future change to the stage list must preserve it (SHO-03 catches a violation every frame in dev builds).
- B keeps a two-origin model on screen: a cone drawn from the chest with shadows cast from the feet. It is the same model the simulation uses, so they agree, but a wall footprint that lies between the chest and the foot can shade a region the chest alone would light. Whether that reads naturally is UNVERIFIED (playtest).
- All pre-spike evidence is from one machine and an editor build.
- B depends on an unverified engine behaviour (culling of occluders by the light's texture-quad extent, § 4.4). The pre-registered fallback B-pad costs texture memory and quad overdraw.
- The beam light's frame depends on a cross-ADR attach path (ADR-0034 `BodyScenes` `LIGHTING` and the stage-0 reparent); until that lands the identity contract fails on a pod hop or a weld rebase.

## Risks
- **R1: the release export or the reference GPUs behave differently from the dev machine** (shadow origin from `offset`, the frame, the lattice response). *Mitigation*: the probe pack is the gate; A and D are specified; nothing is Accepted without it.
- **R2: snapping rounds light positions on a path not probed** (the probe covered pass A with only the `SubViewport` flags in a minimal scene; the project settings `snap_2d_*`, the root viewport, a `CanvasLayer` and the shipped tree were not exercised, and SHO-03 checks node `global_position`, which cannot see a renderer-side snap). If snapping rounds 100.5 to 101 the lattice identity silently becomes an integer node, which P-7 and P-8 show is worse. *Mitigation*: SO-P5 runs both the settings and the flags as ADR-0008 ships them, in the shipped tree, passes A and B, with the drawn-lit comparison as the assertion; ADR-0008 states that snapping applies to `CanvasItem` draw transforms and was probed not to apply to `Light2D` positions; if snapping does round lights, `put_light_origin` is replaced by a node-parent offset (an amendment) or the flags are turned off for the light parent.
- **R3: merged multi-vertex polygons and real edge-apex textures behave differently from the one-rectangle, centred-texture probes.** *Mitigation*: SO-P3 and SO-P9 use the shipping builder and textures; failure stops Acceptance.
- **R4: `shadow_filter` PCF widens the disagreement beyond radius 1.** *Mitigation*: SO-P8 sets the shipped level; the agreement statement is then the measured one, and a PCF level that breaks radius 2 is not shipped.
- **R5: the half-cell exception spreads** (other presenters copy it). *Mitigation*: `put_light_origin` takes a `Light2D`, not a `Node2D`; SHO-01 bans the pattern elsewhere.
- **R6: a later change to the occluder polygon builder shifts vertices or changes the cull mode.** *Mitigation*: ADR-0028's occluder-builder tests (cull mode, vertex lattice, vertex-set equality; formerly SHO-05 and SHO-06) and the probe pack rerun.
- **R7: ADR-0017 changes how `EmitterDerived.occlusion_origin` is computed or published.** *Mitigation*: re-verified against the ADR-0017 text on disk (Cross-ADR assumptions); the identity contract needs only one integer origin per beam from one named function; outcome D is the only change that adds a published pair, and it is specified in § 7.
- **R8: the prediction that A fails is wrong on the reference machines.** *Mitigation*: A is run as a control; if it passes every AC-57 row B still passes first by the ladder, so nothing is lost.
- **R9: the engine culls occluders by the light's texture-quad extent, which breaks B on the real edge-apex texture** (§ 4.4). `OCC_MARGIN_PX` cannot repair it. *Mitigation*: SO-P10 is a B pass condition; B-pad is pre-registered ahead of A and D.
- **R10: the beam light is parented to the wrong body root for a frame** (pod hop, weld rebase). *Mitigation*: § 4.1 attach through `BodyScenes` `LIGHTING`; SHO-04 `shadow_origin_wrong_frame` and SHO-T6.
- **R11: a shadow-casting light anchored in or next to a closed occluder renders the view black** (P-4). *Mitigation*: § 3.5, SHO-07.
