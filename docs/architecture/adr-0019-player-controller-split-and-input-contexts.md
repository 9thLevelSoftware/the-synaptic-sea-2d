## Summary
The Player Controller & Input GDD binds forty-odd requirements from fifteen GDDs to one question that no Foundation ADR answers: which object reads a device, which object owns the player's intent, and which object draws the body, with what rights each holds. This ADR fixes a three-part split (an `INPUT_SURFACE` Input Adapter in `src/presentation/controller/` that is the only reader of `Input.*`, `InputMap` and `InputEvent`; a Controller Sim in `src/sim/controller/` that owns block `controller.state`, four commands, two hooks and four status-returning mutators; and a `PRESENTATION` Avatar Presenter that only draws), the change-based intent contract with its single resync rule, the context machine and its cursor policy, device identity by event type for Godot 4.7, the open-loop aim path, the Adapter-owned MenuInput stream, and the lints, trace format and budgets that keep each part inside its rights.

## Context

### Problem Statement
The GDD (PC-1, PC-2, PC-14) names three things that sound like one: the system that turns a device into intent, the system whose state the simulation reads and saves, and the system that draws the crewmate. Read as one "controller", it would be a node that reads `Input`, writes a block and moves a sprite, which breaks three Foundation rules at once (presentation never writes a block; the simulation has no `Node`; a MIRROR peer must be able to draw a remote body from state alone). The GDD defers seven engine-integration choices to an ADR: `InputMap` versus a custom layer, edge detection by event versus polling, device identity under the 4.7 change, the context machine, the resync mechanism, the aim path across a camera whose pose depends on aim, and who owns position (kernel open question 3, closed provisionally as PC-14 pending technical-director ratification). The Foundation ADRs left their halves open and point here by name: ADR-0001 § 3 (devices identified by event type), ADR-0002 Risks (the Adapter's rate rule), ADR-0008 § 3.1 and § 3.3 (zoom call and cursor), ADR-0009 § 7 (pre-play keyed by `correlation`), ADR-0010 § 6.6 (the Adapter's file list is this ADR's allow-list entry).

### Constraints
- **Presentation never writes a block or constructs a command** (kernel H4, registry `presentation_submit_without_handle`, `command_constructed_outside_kernel`). The Adapter holds the one `SurfaceHandle` named `input_adapter`; nothing else submits PLAYER commands (ADR-0002 § 1, ADR-0009 § 7).
- **The Adapter samples and submits in its own `_process` at `ProcessPriority.INPUT_ADAPTER` and never in `_physics_process`, a signal handler or `_input`** (ADR-0001 § 3). One tick of latency, never two.
- **Integer-only simulation** (ADR-0005). The sim sees `octant`, `aim_angle` in `AIM_UNITS`, ticks and ids; a float never crosses `submit`.
- **The sim never reads Sanity, Attention or a `PERCEPTION_OUT` block from the Controller path, and the Adapter never requests them** (HR-6; TR-pc-025). Input mapping, deadzones and aim source cannot lie.
- **`restore` copies into the existing block object; the snapshot holds no derived cache and no Adapter state** (ADR-0003 § 2, ADR-0006 § 2.2).
- **Every kernel-invoked owner callable returns `ctx.complete()` last** (ADR-0003 § 1 and § 3; registry `void_or_value_returning_owner_callable`).
- **`Input.*`, `InputMap`, `InputEvent`, `_input` and `_unhandled_input` appear only in listed files** (TR-pc-032; `READ-01` mechanism of ADR-0010 § 6.6; the list is this ADR's).
- **Godot 4.7 device ids** (HIGH): `device == 0` is wrong for keyboard and mouse and ambiguous for pads; bindings built in code are device-specific (probe 3).
- **No input buffering** (`ACTION_BUFFER_TICKS` is 0; PC-2): a press during a channel is submitted and answered honestly.
- **Gameplay values are data** (coding standard): tuning in the ADR-0007 pipeline, bindings and the context matrix in committed tables, user rebinds and Settings host-local.
- **Release-export budgets** (R12): Adapter, `LocomotionStep`, `controller.step`, Presenter and diagnostics sub-budgets are advisory and measured on RM-DESKTOP and RM-DECK only (TR-pc-029).
- **No autoload, no static variable, no `Kernel` global** (ADR-0001 § 5): the Adapter and the Presenter receive facets through `setup()`.

### Requirements
- A single place where a device becomes ints, so a trace can replay it and a lint can fence it (TR-pc-032, TR-pc-039).
- Intents are change-based and always recoverable: the simulation's intent equals the player's hands within one tick of the kernel being `RUNNING` (TR-pc-009).
- A context machine in which exactly one context is active, with the forced returns and cancel rules of PC-2, and a cursor policy that follows it (TR-pc-010, TR-pc-033, TR-hud-041).
- An aim path that cannot feed the camera back into itself (TR-pc-031, TR-pxl-014).
- One field list for `controller.state` that the registered schema, the read view and the snapshot payload must all follow (TR-pc-037).
- The Presenter writes only the allow-listed members and carries no clamp (TR-pc-026 is ADR-0009's; TR-pc-046 is here).
- Per-tick Controller and Presenter outputs identical across 30, 60, 120 and 144 frames per second (TR-pc-039).

### 1. The split, the rights of each part, and position ownership

**1.1 Three parts, two directories (TR-pc-001; PC-1).** `src/sim/controller/` holds the Controller Sim (block, commands, hooks, mutators, `controller_faults.gd`, `views/`). `src/presentation/controller/` holds the Input Adapter (`input/`) and the Avatar Presenter (`avatar/`); ADR-0010 § 1.1 already lists `controller` under presentation with exactly these two occupants. The three parts have disjoint rights, enforced by facet type and by lint:

| Part | Kernel class and facet | Reads | Writes | Submits |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Input Adapter** | `INPUT_SURFACE` facet; holds the `SurfaceHandle` named `input_adapter` (ADR-0002 § 1, ADR-0009 § 7); `PresentationClock`; `PresentationViews` with `perception_access` false | Devices through `InputSource`; `controller.state`, `body.state`, `emitter.state` (beam id), `suit.state` (`tool_in_hand`, held slots, `stowed_heavy`), `interaction.state` candidates and the Chart Table's `ChartPick` through published views; `RenderLayout`, `f_q`, `layout_version` from `PipelineRoot` | Nothing in the simulation. Adapter-local fields, the cursor (mode, shape, warp), and `AdapterViews` | PLAYER commands, from its own `_process` only, plus the host-local `PipelineRoot.request_zoom_step` |
| **Controller Sim** | A `ModuleFacet` owner (ADR-0003); fault domain 7 | `suit.state` (`locomotion_cap`, `stowed_heavy`), `body.state` through `BodyView` (`body_of_actor`, `actor_of_seat`, `seat_of_actor`, `pos_x/pos_y`; **not** a pod-body test, which Body disowns), ADR-0026's `is_pod_body(body_id) -> bool` and `home_body` (assumptions on ADR-0026, which is not on disk) for IN_POD, the clock | `controller.state` only, in its own `validate`/`apply`/hooks/mutators, inside a `WriteScope` | `SYSTEM` commands: none. **1.2 Position ownership: PC-14 is ratified as an architecture decision (TR-pc-003).** Ship Body owns ship-local integer position (`pos_curr`, `pos_prev`, `sub`, `odometer`, `blocked`) and runs `LocomotionStep` in `body.integrate`; the Controller Sim owns intent, gait, committed aim, facing, locomotion state, carry and channel references, and **publishes** the chest and hand points as read-time scalar getters (§ 2.6; they are computed from Body's position when read and stored nowhere). The Controller **never writes a `body.state` field and holds no mutator on Body**. The reasons are the GDD's and are restated because they are architectural: the welded composite ship is Body's; an actor moves between bodies (pod hop, detach) which only Body tracks; with no physics body the step is deterministic, so replay needs no physics observations; and putting the Layout projection inside an input system would invert the layering. The technical-director ratification the GDD asks for is the review of this ADR; until it is Accepted the split is Proposed with the rest. ADR-0015 is asked to adopt it verbatim (`body.integrate` `after: [controller.step, layout.doors]`).

**1.3 Reading TR-pc-035 (the body edge).** The registered text says `controller.step` "sets `pos_prev == pos_curr` and resets `sub`" when the actor changes body. Those are Body fields, the GDD says in two places that "the Controller does not write `sub`" (Edge Cases, AC-PCI-132) and TR-shipbody-048 requires a transfer to change only `body_id`, `pos_curr`, `pos_prev` and `sub` of an actor row and leave `controller.state` bytes identical. This ADR resolves the contradiction in Body's favour: on the tick in which `body_of_actor(actor)` differs from the stored `channel_body`, `controller.step` clears **only** `channel_ref`, `channel_face` and `channel_body` (§ 2.4 step 1); the position baseline and `sub` reset are written by Body's transfer mutators in P2 of the transfer (ADR-0015, Cross-ADR assumptions), so a same-tick Controller write after a pod hop cannot displace the arrival cell because the Controller has no such write. A weld that leaves the actor's body id unchanged clears nothing. The assumption on ADR-0015, stated exactly: **BM3 keeps `sub` and `last_octant`; BM4 (new side) and BM5 set `pos_prev := pos_curr` and zero `sub`; TR-pc-035 is read as covering a pod hop and a detach.** ADR-0015 corrects its TR-pc-035 row to match (CT-27).

**1.4 Registered file list and the Input fence.** Only five files hold engine input tokens. They are the `READ-01` allow-list entry for the symbols `Input`, `InputMap`, `InputEvent` and subclasses, `_input`, `_unhandled_input`, `_unhandled_key_input`, `_shortcut_input` (§ 8.1). Every other file of the module (quantisers, context machine, router, aim solver, menu queue, ledgers) is pure over ints and takes an `InputSource`, so it is testable with a scripted source and cannot read a device.

**Row lifecycle (T7).** `default_state(ctx)` returns the MVP payload: **one row for the host PlayerId at the rest values of § 2.2** (the PlayerId of the single seat Body binds at new game; the constant is ADR-0015's and ADR-0002's, an assumption), written through the same column encoder as `snapshot`, so a new game and a restore take the same path (ADR-0016 § default-state-through-restore). There is no other creation path at MVP: no command creates a row and no mutator of § 2.5 does. A seat bound in `body.state` with no `controller.state` row after restore is a restore fault `PC_ROW_MISSING` (local code 10, § 2.8), raised by ADR-0016's cross-block verifier (added to its verifier list as an assumption), not by `post_restore`, which therefore reads Suit only. At run time a missing row is not silent: `set_move_intent` and `set_aim` reject `NO_SEAT_ROW` (§ 2.3) and ADR-0015 counts `BDY_SEAT_UNBOUND` from `has_row(player_id)` (§ 2.6). Entity ids are block-local (ADR-0003 § 2); the row key is the PlayerId and the **Body actor id is not stored here**: the Controller resolves `actor ↔ PlayerId` through Body's published `seat_of_actor(actor)` / `actor_of_seat(player_id)`, so the seat is stored once (Body's `seat`, BDY-7) and the kernel's binding rebuild (`binding_source`, ADR-0016) stays Body's and Emitter's.

That is the whole of `post_restore` (ADR-0016 V8 becomes "derived recompute of `cap_seen` only").

**2.3 Commands (TR-pc-006, TR-pc-023, TR-pc-024).** Four command types are registered. `actor_source` is `&"body.state"` for the three PLAYER types, so the authority check is `(body.state, actor)` against the issuer's PlayerId (ADR-0002 § 4). The `max_age_ticks` values are the GDD's two classes (PC-2: one for the two intents, one for every action), held as named constants of `controller_commands.gd`, and a boot check asserts invariant 7 (the largest effective hold times the tick rate stays below the action `max_age_ticks`, so a hold-to-confirm submit is never `STALE`).

| `type` | `allowed_origins` | `actor_binding` / `actor_source` | `coalesce` | `max_age_ticks` | `allowed_when_paused` | `writes_blocks` | `payload_schema` (ADR-0002 § 3 form) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `set_move_intent` | PLAYER | `OWNED_BY_ISSUER` / `body.state` | `LATEST_PER_ACTOR_PER_TICK` | intent class | false | `[controller.state]` | `{octant: INT required 0..8, run: BOOL required}` |
| `set_aim` | PLAYER | `OWNED_BY_ISSUER` / `body.state` | `LATEST_PER_ACTOR_PER_TICK` | intent class | false | `[controller.state]` | `{aim_angle: INT required 0..AIM_UNITS-1, live: BOOL required}` |
| `give_up` | PLAYER | `OWNED_BY_ISSUER` / `body.state` | `NONE` | action class | false | `[controller.state]` | `{}` |
| `stagger` | SYSTEM | `NONE` / empty | `NONE` | action class | false | `[controller.state]` | `{actor: INT required min 0}` |

The `give_up` actor is the command's `actor` argument (the Body actor id); `stagger` carries the target in its payload because `submit_system` has no actor parameter (ADR-0002 § 1). No type allows both origins, so `COALESCE_DUAL_ORIGIN` cannot fire. The payload bound `AIM_UNITS - 1` is compiled from the constant in `controller_constants.gd`, and a boot assertion checks it equals the registry value (`AIM_UNITS`; it is a multiple of 8 so every octant centre is an integer).

*Validation (reasons are closed per command):*
- `set_move_intent`, `set_aim`: resolve the row through `seat_of_actor(cmd.actor)`; a seat with no row is `reject(&"NO_SEAT_ROW")` (the binding table already proved ownership, so this means Body and Controller disagree about the seat, a defect the diagnostics count); `loco_state == DEAD` is `reject(&"DEAD")`. **Intents are accepted and stored in every other state, including STAGGERED, DOWNED and IN_POD**: the locks are in `controller.step` (it ignores `octant` outside the locomotion class), so the held direction is already in the row on the tick a stagger or a pod stay ends and the "no intermediate IDLE tick" rule of the States table holds.
- `give_up`: valid only when `loco_state == DOWNED` and the block holds exactly one row (solo, MVP; no other bound seat could revive), else `reject(&"NOT_DOWNED")`. Apply: `death_tick := min(death_tick, ctx.tick)`; the existing step order does the rest in the same tick's `controller.step` (cap read first, then death).
- `stagger`: `reject(&"NOT_STAGGERABLE")` in DOWNED, DEAD or IN_POD; otherwise apply sets `stagger_end_tick := ctx.tick + STAGGER_TICKS` unless a stagger is already running, in which case the timer is unchanged (a stagger never extends the lock). A `stagger` in STAGGERED is `Applied` and changes nothing.
- **A DEAD seat accepts no command (TR-pc-022).** `DEAD` latches in the precedence function and only a restore replaces the block contents. The Controller's own `validate`s reject with `DEAD`; the Adapter submits nothing while the published `loco_state` is DEAD; other owners' `validate`s consult the same published `loco_state` (ADR-0020 and ADR-0021 assumption). The death hand-off itself (one `request_restore`, honoured after `EPOCH_DURABLE`) is Save/Load's death stub observing `loco_state` (ADR-0006, registry `save_observation_set`); this ADR's half is that `loco_state` stays DEAD with no further transition and that the seat's commands are rejected until the restore.

**2.4 Hooks and the step order.** Two hooks, both divisor 1, offset 0: `controller.step` with `after: [{id: &"suit.vitals", tolerates_stale: true}]` (the cap is read up to five ticks old) and `controller.noise` with `after: [{id: &"body.integrate", tolerates_stale: false}]`. Both are `Callable`s returning `ctx.complete()`. `controller.step` runs the seven steps of the GDD's normative order (PC-15, States and Transitions) for each row in ascending PlayerId; the formulas are the GDD's (D.4, D.5, D.8, D.12, D.14) and are not restated:
1. **Cap edge and body edge.** Read `locomotion_cap` and compare it with `cap_seen`; then `body_of_actor(actor)` against `channel_body`. A mismatch with a set `channel_ref` clears `channel_ref`, `channel_face` and `channel_body` in every state before step 4; `octant`, `carry_ref` and `facing` are kept. `cap_seen := cap` last, in every state.
2. Timers. 3. Movement wins over a channel (a non-NONE `intent_octant` clears the channel refs on the same tick). 4. The total `loco_state` precedence function (DEAD, DOWNED, IN_POD, STAGGERED, CHANNELLING, class function of `(octant, run, cap, stowed_heavy, carry_ref)`); IN_POD is true when `is_pod_body(body_of_actor(actor))` and the pod is the actor's `home_body` (ADR-0026's published predicate and field, assumptions on ADR-0026; `body.state` supplies only `body_of_actor`, because ADR-0015 disowns the pod-body test). 5. `v_eff`. 6. Facing. 7. Bookkeeping. **There is no derived chest or hand point in step 7** (§ 2.6): those are read-time getters, so they are never a tick behind `body.integrate`.

*Facing (TR-pc-016).* Facing is recomputed every tick from committed state only (the stored `facing` is the previous value the 2-quanta hysteresis compares against; `FACING_HYSTERESIS` is the registry constant) with the six-level priority order of D.8. No input, no raw aim and no presentation value enters the function. **A MIRROR peer never recomputes facing or `channel_face`:** a MIRROR kernel does not tick, does not apply and runs no hook (ADR-0002 § 8), so both fields arrive only as replicated committed state, and the Presenter has no HOST/MIRROR branch (CR-10).

*Speed and weight (TR-boarding-007, TR-boarding-008).* `gait_effective` is a function of the class member, the cap and the ramp only. The carry speed is `V_CARRY` whatever the module is; the Controller never reads a mass, a weight class or a roster: the only inventory input of the step is the boolean `stowed_heavy(player_id)` that Suit publishes (registry `stowed_heavy`, user ruling D18), which makes the class function return WALK instead of RUN. The tokens `mass_g`, `instance_mass_g`, `weight_class` and `salvage_yield` are forbidden in `src/sim/controller/` and `src/presentation/controller/` (rule `CTL-03`, § 8.2).

*Noise.* `controller.noise` calls Attention's published `push_stimulus` mutator once per moved tick with the class from `loco_state`, and the BURDEN push of PC-10 on every qualifying tick (ADR-0022 owns the mutator; this ADR owns when it is called). The hook reads Body's `moved_x(actor)` and `moved_y(actor)` (ADR-0015 § 11.3; there is no single `moved(actor)` getter) after `body.integrate`, from a hook registered `after: [body.integrate]` and listed in `body_readers.json` (BDY-02). The mutator reaches the hook as an injected object (not through a view lookup), and the call is made **only in `controller_noise.gd`**; it is the one allowed Attention symbol in the module (rule `CTL-08`, § 8.2). Assumption on ADR-0022: it publishes the mutator's class name and the `push_stimulus` signature that this call uses.

**2.5 The four mutators (TR-pc-018).** `ControllerMutators` (`src/sim/controller/controller_mutators.gd`, a `RefCounted` wired by the composition root into the callers' `setup()` arguments, ADR-0003 § 5) publishes:

```gdscript
func set_channel_ref(scope: WriteScope, actor: int, owner_system: int, channel_id: int, face_octant: int) -> int
func clear_channel_ref(scope: WriteScope, actor: int) -> int
func set_carry_ref(scope: WriteScope, actor: int, item_id: int) -> int
func clear_carry_ref(scope: WriteScope, actor: int) -> int
```

The GDD calls these "Class B"; in ADR-0003's taxonomy a mutator that returns a status and calls `scope.fault` on a defect is **Class A** (status return, fail-closed). This ADR therefore classes them as Class A, each with a pure `check_*` twin (`check_set_channel_ref(views, actor, owner_system, face_octant) -> StringName`) that the caller's `validate` may use to reject early, and amends the GDD wording (GDD sync). Status values are ADR-0003's: `OK`, `REFUSED` (scope not open for `controller.state`; a counted violation, never escalated) or `-fault_code`. `set_channel_ref` returns `-PC_FACE_OCTANT_RANGE` and writes nothing for `face_octant` outside 0 to 8 (`OCTANT_NONE`, 8, is legal: the owner passes it for `ACTOR_SELF` and for a zero anchor offset, and it is stored as `NO_ID`), `-PC_CHANNEL_OWNER_UNKNOWN` for an owner outside the closed enum, and `-PC_NO_SUCH_ROW` for an actor with no row (a caller defect, because the caller's own validate proved ownership). `set_carry_ref` returns `-PC_CARRY_REF_INVALID` for a negative item id. The `clear_*` mutators are idempotent: no row or nothing set is `OK`. `set_channel_ref` stamps `channel_body` from `body_of_actor(actor)` in the same call, so the callers' four-argument shape in the Interaction and Assembly GDDs is unchanged. Every mutator begins with `scope.open_for(&"controller.state")`, marks `P/<player_id>` dirty through `scope.mark_dirty` and nothing else, and callers stop at the first non-`OK` status and return `ctx.complete()` (ADR-0003 § 5). These four are the **only** cross-block write path into `controller.state`; a lint rejects any assignment whose receiver has static type `ControllerRow`, and any member access through the `controller.state` block object, outside `src/sim/controller/` (`CTL-04`, an AST rule, not a lexical one, so Body's and Horror's own `last_octant` are untouched).

**2.6 The published read view (TR-pc-017).** `read_view(ctx)` is called once at the registry freeze and returns a long-lived getter-only `ControllerView` (`src/sim/controller/views/controller_view.gd`) over the block object, with an `empty_view` twin. Getters are keyed by PlayerId and return scalars or fill a caller-owned buffer. Every `published` field of § 2.2 has a `get_<noun>(player_id)` getter (the complete signatures are in Key Interfaces): `get_octant`, `get_intent_octant`, `get_intent_run` (the GDD's "run"; the block has no separate `run` field), `get_gait_effective`, `get_aim_angle`, `get_aim_live`, `get_facing`, `get_loco_state`, `get_carry_ref`, `get_salvage_carry`, `get_channel_ref(player_id, out)`, `get_channel_face`, `get_stagger_end_tick`, `get_t_downed`, the four point getters below, and the non-field accessors `has_row(player_id) -> bool` (ADR-0015 consumes it for `BDY_SEAT_UNBOUND`), `row_count()` (kept for the `give_up` solo test) and `is_empty()`. One style throughout (ADR-0010 accessor naming, CT-54): `get_<noun>` for per-seat values, and the three named non-field accessors above. The owners' `validate` reads `intent_octant` (PC-7).

**The chest and hand points (CT-28; TR-pc-017).** `get_chest_x(player_id)`, `get_chest_y(player_id)`, `get_hand_x(player_id)` and `get_hand_y(player_id)` each return an `int`: an **absolute integer point in the body frame**, equal to `BodyView.pos_x(actor)` (or `pos_y`) plus `CHEST_POINT[sheet][row]` (or `HAND_POINT`), the offset's x mirrored by `facing` under the GDD's flip rule, where `actor = actor_of_seat(player_id)`, `row` is taken from the published `loco_state` through the table's `row_of_loco_state` map, and `sheet` is the catalogue's single MVP avatar sheet (a per-seat sheet would be a new persisted column and is not decided here, OQ-G). The getter is **O(1) and allocation-free**, computed on every read from the same tick's `pos_curr` that `body.integrate` just wrote, so the point cannot lag the foot origin: ADR-0017 D5.3 reads the apex as `get_chest_x/get_chest_y` in `emitter.place` (after `body.integrate`) and ADR-0018 reads the foot cell from `BodyView.pos_x/pos_y`, and the two agree by construction. Scalars in (`pos_x/pos_y`) and scalars out; no `Vector2i` is built on the per-tick Emitter path, as a style choice (ADR-0010 § 5.5 does not ban `Vector2i`; SIM-03 is not the reason). `CHEST_POINT` and `HAND_POINT` come from a **frozen ADR-0007 sim table, `ControllerCatalog` (loaded at pipeline stage 1), injected through `ControllerTuning`**; the § 7.3 presentation sidecar and its bounds checks read that same table, so there is one source of the offsets. Unit test (T1): on a moving tick, the apex used by `emitter.place` minus the chest offset for the tick's `(sheet, row)` equals that tick's `pos_curr`.

**Who reads what.** **Attention and Horror read only `loco_state`:** `ControllerView` is one facade (ADR-0003 § 7 issues one view per block), so the restriction is a lint, `CTL-05`: in `src/sim/attention/` and `src/sim/horror/` the only `ControllerView` member name that may appear is `get_loco_state`. **Body reads `octant` and `gait_effective` only** (ADR-0015, TR-shipbody-016; Body refuses `intent_octant`); Suit reads nothing; Interaction and Assembly read `intent_octant`, `loco_state`, `carry_ref`, `channel_ref` (they remain `intent_octant`'s readers); the Emitter (ADR-0017) reads the chest point, `aim_angle` and `loco_state`.

**2.7 Snapshot, restore, post_restore (TR-pc-020, TR-pc-021, TR-pc-037).**
- `snapshot(ctx)` calls `ctx.put` with a `Dictionary` with `String` keys (ADR-0006 § 1.2 forbids `StringName` keys) whose entries are **one `PackedInt64Array` column per persisted field**, named by `ControllerSchema.fields()`, rows sorted by `player_id`, every array a fresh duplicate (ADR-0003 snapshot contract). Columns rather than a flat stride make a `dump-save` self-describing and make the field-agreement test a key-set comparison. Bools are 0 or 1; sentinels are the persisted `NO_ID` and `NO_TICK`; **no `Vector2i`** and no derived cache (there is no stored chest or hand point at all, § 2.6). The normalisation lives here: the rest column values of § 2.2 are written, the `loco_state` column maps the five transient states to IDLE, and `restore` normalises nothing.
- `restore(data, stored_version, ctx)` decodes and validates into scratch (shape, column lengths equal, enum and range checks, duplicate `player_id`), and only on success copies the contents into the existing `ControllerRow` objects and the existing array, never replacing the block object (ADR-0003 § 2, ADR-0006 § 2.2). Failure is `ctx.fault(PC_RESTORE_SHAPE | PC_RESTORE_RANGE | PC_RESTORE_VERSION | PC_ROW_DUPLICATE, detail)` with no clamp. `snapshot(restore(snapshot()))` is canonical-byte identical; `controller.state` is **not** in `REBASE_BLOCKS`, restore verification is `EXACT`, and a restore leaves `octant` NONE until the resynced hands arrive.
- `post_restore(ctx)` sets `cap_seen := locomotion_cap` and nothing else (no chest or hand point exists to recompute, § 2.6; ADR-0016 V8 is "derived recompute of `cap_seen` only"). It runs after every `restore` (`restore_after` lists `body.state` and `suit.state`, § 2.1) and, if Suit's own `post_restore` derives the cap, after that one (assumption on ADR-0016).
- **The payload holds no Adapter-local state** (TR-pc-021): `last_acked`, hold timers, the run latch, the device latch, the context, pre-play tokens, the menu latch and the last NAV direction are fields of Adapter classes that the Controller Sim cannot name; the field-agreement test (§ 8.4) asserts the column set equals the persisted schema fields exactly.
- **Normalisation and the hash (T6, CT-49).** The `snapshot` payload writes rest values for `octant`, `intent_octant`, `intent_run`, `gait_effective`, `ramp_ticks`, `walk_ticks`, `decel_k`, `stagger_end_tick` and the channel fields, and IDLE for the transient `loco_state`s, so `block_hash` over the payload (ADR-0005 § 4.3) cannot see most per-tick Controller output; a divergence would surface only later through `body.state`. § 8.5 therefore compares per-tick `ControllerView` samples across cadences, and a G4 or KRN-RST fixture that snapshots while an intent is held diverges by construction unless it follows the input model in § 8.5. This is recorded as a note against ADR-0005 (its G3/G4 definition does not change).
- The held item itself is not here. `carry_ref` is an id from **Interaction's instance counter, held as Suit's carried instance**; `validate_instance` (TR-itemdb-013) is not called by this block. The restore checks are **V7** (`carry_ref` equals Suit's carried id, or both are NONE) and, if ADR-0020 accepts it, **V11** (the id is below the counter and not in `loose[]`); both are ADR-0016's cross-block verifier (CT-51). The earlier reading of `carry_ref` as a bare id into the `loose[]` counter is withdrawn.

**2.8 Key space and faults.** `KeySpaceDescriptor` for `controller.state`: kinds `PLAYER` 1 (`P/<player_id>`: every published field) and `BOOK` 2 (`B/<player_id>`: `ramp_ticks`, `walk_ticks`, `last_octant`, `decel_k`, `cap_seen`, `death_tick`, `channel_body`: the persisted fields no presenter reads), no events, `quiet_writes_allowed` false. Every field write goes through a `ControllerRow` setter-free method that calls `scope.mark_dirty` (never a property setter, ADR-0004), and a step that writes a value equal to the current one marks nothing, so a standing avatar marks no key. `ControllerFaults` (`DOMAIN_ID` 7, ADR-0003 § 6, append-only, pre-packed, `NAMES` indexed by local code): 1 `PC_NO_SUCH_ROW`, 2 `PC_FACE_OCTANT_RANGE`, 3 `PC_CHANNEL_OWNER_UNKNOWN`, 4 `PC_CARRY_REF_INVALID`, 5 `PC_RESTORE_SHAPE`, 6 `PC_RESTORE_RANGE`, 7 `PC_RESTORE_VERSION`, 8 `PC_STEP_BAD_ENUM`, 9 `PC_ROW_DUPLICATE`, 10 `PC_ROW_MISSING` (a seat bound in `body.state` with no `controller.state` row after restore; raised by ADR-0016's verifier, § 2.1). The `PC_*` diagnostics counters the GDD freezes (`PC_RESYNC`, `PC_CHANNEL_ABORT`, `PC_FOREIGN_ACTOR_REJECT`, `PC_LINT_*`) are dev-only channels in `*_diag.gd` files reached by the guarded dev load (ADR-0031), so a release export file list contains none of them (AC-PCI-216).

### 3. The Input Adapter

**3.1 Node, priority and ports.** `InputAdapter` (`src/presentation/controller/input/input_adapter.gd`, `extends Node`) is constructed by the composition root, receives its facets through `setup()` (the `INPUT_SURFACE` `PresentationFacet`, the `SurfaceHandle`, `PresentationClock`, `PresentationViews`, `PipelineRoot` for `layout`, `f_q`, `layout_version` and `request_zoom_step`, the frozen `InputCatalog` and `ControllerTuning`, the Settings-sourced `InputSettings` view, the local PlayerId, the injected monotonic `Clock` port, an `InputSource`) and sets `process_mode = PROCESS_MODE_ALWAYS` (ADR-0001 § 6: it must keep running while the scene tree is paused and while the kernel is `PAUSED`, because the Chart Table is driven from it) and `process_priority = ProcessPriority.INPUT_ADAPTER`; it never writes a literal. It submits only from its own `_process` (ADR-0001 § 3, KRN-04).

**`_input` order is tree order, not priority (E2, CT-52; probe 14).** The engine delivers `_input` in reverse tree order (a later sibling first) and `process_priority` does not affect it; `INPUT_ADAPTER -100` therefore orders only the Adapter's `_process`. A later node that handles an event first (the Chart Table layer, HUD, the ADR-0031 debug overlay, `DevInputSink`) would silently starve the Adapter's ring: a missed press with no error. Rules: (1) the composition root adds `InputAdapter` as the **last child** of its parent (ADR-0009's `INPUT_SURFACE` last-child rule), so its `_input` runs first; (2) a **boot assertion** checks that the Adapter is the last child of its parent and fails the boot lane otherwise; (3) a **two-node unit test** (a copy of the probe: two sibling nodes, the Adapter added last, a handling node added before it) asserts the Adapter sees the event first and the earlier sibling still receives it; (4) **only the Adapter may call `get_viewport().set_input_as_handled()`** for action-bound events, and no other layer defines `_input` or calls it (an assumption on ADR-0030 and ADR-0031, Cross-ADR assumptions); the dev overlay latches through the Adapter's `DevInputSink` rather than its own `_input`. `set_input_as_handled()` does not change `Input.*` poll state (actions update before dispatch), so only the ring is at risk. ADR-0001 § 3 item 3b records "`_input` ordering is tree order, not priority" (Foundation asks).

Every read of a device goes through the injected port `InputSource` (an `@abstract` class, ADR-0010's mechanism; fallback a plain base class whose stubs call `push_error` **and return a value of the declared type on every path**). `EngineInputSource` implements it over `Input`, `InputMap`, `Viewport`, `DisplayServer`; a scripted `FakeInputSource` in `tests/support/` implements it for unit tests and for trace replay (§ 8.5). **Both implement all ten abstract methods** (a subclass that implements only some is a parse error, probe 16), and every override restates its return type and returns on every path (under the 4.7 override rule an omitted annotation inherits the typed return, so a stub such as `func cursor_px(): pass` fails; the Fake returns `0`, `false` or `Vector2i.ZERO`). **Every analogue value crosses the port as an integer in `ANALOG_UNITS` (one thousandth)**, every position as `Vector2i` window pixels, every edge as an integer record, so the Adapter's decisions are a function of ints and a recorded trace reproduces them exactly. Float arithmetic in the Adapter is confined to the aim angle (`atan2`) and the deadzone scalings that D.6 and D.7 define in floats, and their outputs are ints before they touch a decision or a submit.

**3.2 Edges by event latch, continuous state by poll (closes the GDD's "edge detection by event vs polling").** `_input(event)` converts each device event to an integer record and appends it, in arrival order, to a preallocated `EdgeRing`: a press or release of a registered action **from a key, a mouse button or a pad button event** (resolved with `InputMap.event_is_action`, never by device number), a wheel step (below), a pointer move or button for MenuInput (a move record carries the integer `relative`, for the virtual cursor of § 6.3), the device class of the event (§ 3.3), and key echo (recorded and ignored). Records for a control are de-duplicated against an Adapter **held-set**: a press for a control already held adds no record. It submits nothing and writes nothing else (ADR-0001 § 3: `_input` may latch, only `_process` submits). `_process` drains the ring and polls `get_action_raw_strength` for the continuous families (move, aim keys, sticks, triggers).

**Pad axes are continuous-only (E4; probe 13).** `InputEventJoypadMotion` makes `is_action_pressed` true on *every* motion event while the stick is past the engine's threshold, so recording it would put a duplicate "press" per motion event per axis per frame into the ring and never a release (an overflow and ordering hazard; `PC_EDGE_OVERFLOW` would become the normal path while a stick is held). `RawEvent.classify` therefore marks `InputEventJoypadMotion` **continuous-only: it produces no edge record** (it still classifies as `DeviceClass.PAD`). Every stick and trigger edge (NAV directions, `MOVE_DEADZONE` crossings, a trigger-bound action) is derived in the Adapter, once per frame, from `get_action_raw_strength` with the existing hysteresis (`MOVE_DEADZONE`, `MOVE_DEADZONE_EXIT`), and enters the same ordered stream at the frame's end. Edge records exist for key, mouse-button and pad-button events only.

**Wheel (E7).** A wheel is an `InputEventMouseButton` (`WHEEL_UP` / `WHEEL_DOWN`) delivered as a press immediately followed by a release; wheel events are not coalesced by `use_accumulated_input`, a high-resolution wheel or precision touchpad reports a fractional `factor`, and `WHEEL_LEFT` / `WHEEL_RIGHT` also exist. The latch counts **only `pressed == true` `WHEEL_UP` / `WHEEL_DOWN` events**, sums their signs into the frame's `wheel_net`, ignores the paired release, ignores `factor` (one zoom step per frame is already the cap, § 6.4) and ignores the horizontal buttons. `InputEventPanGesture` and `InputEventMagnifyGesture` classify as `OTHER` and are ignored: **on a touchpad, two-finger scroll and pinch do not zoom at MVP**; zoom there goes through the keys or pad d-pad (a driver that synthesises wheel buttons will work; that is UNVERIFIED). The burst size per frame is recorded by spike item (c). The reason both are used is the probe: a press and a release in one frame leave `just_pressed` and `just_released` both true and `pressed` false (probe 6), so a poll cannot order them; the latch-until-released and fresh-press rules (PC-2a (d), TR-charttable-012) and the hold-mode cancel need the order. Echo does not re-fire `just_pressed` (probe 5), so echo suppression (PC-2a (a)) is the ring ignoring `echo` records, and the real OS repeat flag is verified by PC-INPUT-SPIKE-1 (d). `_input` is chosen over `_unhandled_input` because a focused `Control` consumes an event before `_unhandled_input` runs and the Adapter would silently miss a press in `chart_table`; in `chart_table` and the boot context the Adapter calls `get_viewport().set_input_as_handled()` (the method is `Viewport`'s, probe 15) for every event it turns into MenuInput so a `Control` never acts on the same press (ADR-0030 builds the layer with no GUI navigation of its own, TR-charttable-010; the Adapter's `_input` runs first, rule 1 above). On `EdgeRing` overflow the Adapter counts `PC_EDGE_OVERFLOW`, drops the frame's edges and derives press state from `is_action_pressed` for that frame only (fail-safe to the poll).

**3.3 Actions, bindings and device identity under the 4.7 change (TR-pc-011, TR-pc-033, TR-hud-029).**
- **Named actions, runtime registration.** The game reads named actions (`StringName`) from `InputActions` constants; it never reads a raw key and never compares a device id. The action set, the context matrix, the default bindings and the keycap tokens are four committed tables under `assets/data/input/` (`actions.json`, `context_matrix.json`, `default_bindings.json`, `keycap_tokens.json`), loaded by ADR-0007's pipeline into a frozen `InputCatalog`. `project.godot` `[input]` carries no gameplay action; `BindingFactory` registers every action with `InputMap.add_action(name, ENGINE_ACTION_DEADZONE)` at start-up, before the first poll (probe 8), where `ENGINE_ACTION_DEADZONE` is a named **non-zero** constant in `binding_factory.gd` (the engine default 0.5, or the existing 0.2; the engine review's reading, probe 13, is that a **0.0 deadzone makes a centred stick or a trigger at rest report its action pressed**, so zero is forbidden). It is the engine's own is-pressed threshold only: `MOVE_DEADZONE` and `AIM_DEADZONE` stay the Adapter's gameplay deadzones, applied as scalings over `get_action_raw_strength`, which ignores the engine deadzone and so stays raw whatever value is registered; no gameplay decision on an analogue family reads `Input.is_action_pressed`. A boot lint (domain 2) fails when a catalogue action is missing from the `InputMap` afterwards **or when `InputMap.action_get_deadzone(action)` is 0.0 for any catalogue action**. User rebinds are host-local overrides applied by `BindingFactory` on top of the defaults; persisting them is the Chart Table `SettingsStore`'s (ADR-0030), not a save block. Action ids are stable integers with an append-only ledger under ADR-0007 § 3.
- **Physical keycodes.** Keyboard bindings are built with `physical_keycode` (and `keycode` left unset), so WASD is the same four keys on AZERTY and QWERTZ (probe 4: a physical binding matches physical events). The keycap label shown by HUD comes from `DisplayServer.keyboard_get_label_from_physical` (4.4; exists, probe 9; values on AZERTY and QWERTZ are UNVERIFIED, PC-INPUT-SPIKE-1 (i)).
- **The wildcard.** Every binding `BindingFactory` builds sets `device = BINDING_ANY_DEVICE` (-1) for keys, mouse buttons, pad buttons and pad axes. Probe 3 shows why: a binding keeps the device its constructor gave it (keyboard 16, mouse 32, **pad 0**), so a pad that enumerates as 1 or 2 matches nothing. The constant is a named `const BINDING_ANY_DEVICE: int = -1` in `binding_factory.gd`; -1 is the engine's any-device binding wildcard (probed: a pad-0 binding misses pad 2; -1 matches pads 0 and 2 and keyboards 16 and 5; 4.7.2 reports `DEVICE_ID_EMULATION` -1, which the code does not use); the code depends only on the named constant. That the wildcard matches every pad on a real machine is UNVERIFIED (PC-INPUT-SPIKE-1 (g)).
- **User rebinds and captured events (E8).** A fresh `InputEventKey` carries device 16 and a mouse button 32, and a real captured event carries the real device (or pad index); storing a captured event directly would make the new binding device-specific, the probe-3 trap again. The Settings rebind capture therefore goes through `BindingFactory.from_capture(event)`, the only path: it **duplicates** the captured event, sets `device = BINDING_ANY_DEVICE` and clears `pressed` and `echo`, and only then is it passed to `InputMap.action_add_event`.
- **Devices are identified by event type.** `RawEvent.classify(event)` (the only function that tests an event's class) returns `DeviceClass.KEYBOARD` for `InputEventKey`, `MOUSE` for `InputEventMouseButton` and `InputEventMouseMotion`, `PAD` for `InputEventJoypadButton` and `InputEventJoypadMotion`, else `OTHER` (ignored). `InputEventMouse` is a base class of the mouse events and is tested with `is`, which does not involve a number. **No code compares `InputEvent.device` with an integer literal or a `DEVICE_ID_*` constant** (rule `CTL-02`, § 8.2; this subsumes the pipeline root's input mapping, TR-pxl-032, because the root reads no input at all, § 6.4). The one legitimate numeric use of `device` is a pad's index: the Adapter stores the `device` of the last pad event as `active_pad` and compares later pad events against that variable (never a literal) to detect a reconnect or a second pad; `Input.joy_connection_changed(device, connected)` is connected in `engine_input_source.gd` and its handler only sets a latched flag (ADR-0001 § 3: no submit from a signal handler).
- **The device latch (PC-13).** `AdapterViews.device_latch()` ∈ {KBM, PAD} follows the last button or key event, switches within one frame, and honours `DEVICE_SWITCH_HYSTERESIS` as a minimum interval between switches (the GDD's resolved reading, K24), the pending device taking effect when the interval ends. The aim source follows the last aim axis independently (§ 6.2). Mixed input is permitted: Godot merges devices per action, so an `interact` held on a pad survives a touch of the mouse.
- **Conflicts and reachability.** `BindingValidator.conflicts(bindings, matrix)` is a pure function that reports, per context, two actions on one physical control; a rebind that creates a conflict is refused with the swap offered, never silently overwritten; a profile with no path to the `chart_table` action is refused (PC-13). The same function runs as a boot lint over the defaults (`INPUT_BINDING_CONFLICT`, `INPUT_NO_CHART_PATH`), and a further boot lint requires a pad binding for every action of the core loop (`INPUT_NO_PAD_PATH`, gamepad-complete play, HR-14, TR-hud-029).
- **Keycap tokens.** `AdapterViews.glyph_token(action, device)` returns a member of the closed `KeycapToken` enumeration defined by `keycap_tokens.json`. A control with no token maps to `KeycapToken.UNKNOWN` (the `?` keycap); HUD draws it and counts `hud_unmapped_key_glyph`. The Adapter never returns a string or a texture.
- **Dev actions.** The ten `debug_overlay_*` actions are registered only under the dev feature tag by `dev_actions.gd`, forwarded host-locally to the Overlay through a `DevInputSink` port, never a command, never in a rebind or accessibility enumeration, and absent from the release file list (PC-2, AC-PCI-216).

**3.4 Intents: the change filter, the order and the rate (TR-pc-006, TR-pc-007).**
- **`set_move_intent`.** From the four move actions' raw strengths, the Adapter forms an octant (D.6: keyboard composition with opposite keys cancelling; a stick under `MOVE_DEADZONE`, with `MOVE_DEADZONE_EXIT` and `OCTANT_HYSTERESIS_DEG`) and the `run` flag (hold, or the toggle latch). It submits **only when the quantised `(octant, run)` differs from `last_acked.move`**, or when a resync is pending for it (§ 3.6). The submit carries `{octant, run}`; 8 is `OCTANT_NONE`.
- **`set_aim`.** The send predicate is D.7's unchanged: `T - last_send_tick >= AIM_SEND_INTERVAL` and (`delta >= AIM_SEND_THRESHOLD` or the source changed or `live` changed or the settle clause), where `T` is `PresentationClock.T`, a committed tick, so the interval is counted in ticks and is independent of render rate. At rest this sends nothing. A sweep is at most `TICK_RATE / AIM_SEND_INTERVAL` aim sends a second plus the move changes, which stays far inside `MAX_CMDS_PER_TICK` and inside the 26 commands a second budget of TR-pc-007 (AC-PCI-221).
- **Order inside a frame (normative).** (1) `set_move_intent`; (2) `set_aim`; (3) the frame's commands in the fixed router order. The kernel stamps `seq` and `host_order` from submission order (ADR-0002 § 2 and § 5), so a stop and a `begin_*` in one frame are applied in that order and the owner's `validate` reads `intent_octant` already set to NONE (PC-7). The Adapter never chooses `seq`.
- **Coalescing and age.** Both intents are `LATEST_PER_ACTOR_PER_TICK`: two submits to one tick keep only the later; the earlier gets `Superseded`, which is not a rejection and triggers no resend. An intent older than its `max_age_ticks` is `STALE` (§ 3.6).

**3.5 Commands, the router and "no buffering" (TR-pc-008).** `ActionRouter` is a pure function from `(ordered edges, context, loco_state, focus, tuning)` to a list of submits, driven by the committed `context_matrix.json` rows: for each action family and context a policy of `LIVE`, `IGNORE`, `NAV_ONLY`, `CLOSES_CARD` or `ACCEPT_ONLY` (§ 4.2). A press whose policy is `IGNORE` is dropped before submit with no pre-play. A press that is live is **submitted at once and answered honestly** by the owner (`BUSY`, `MOVING`, `OUT_OF_RANGE`); the Adapter keeps no queue of presses and no "after the channel" memory (`ACTION_BUFFER_TICKS` is 0). The optional lever `CHANNEL_BEGIN_GRACE_TICKS` (default 0) is the only deferral and, when raised by a playtest finding, holds one `begin_*` press until the Adapter's own last-submitted octant has been NONE; it is a tuning value, not a buffer. `interact` submits **the command type Interaction declares for the focused object** and its ref (PC-6): the Adapter never chooses a verb, so a new `interact` verb changes no Adapter file (AC-PCI-215). The focus score is Adapter-side presentation (PC-6, D.9) over the candidate query that Interaction publishes (ADR-0020 assumption); the Adapter publishes its chosen `focus_ref` and `self_target` in `AdapterViews`, which Interaction's `focus_view` echoes (IA-4). The four presses that are dropped by Adapter-local knowledge alone (`toggle_beam` inside its rate window, `interact` with no focus, `run` while `carry_ref` is set, `run` while `stowed_heavy`) play the single `cue_input_ignored` id and publish nothing else; the Adapter publishes no per-reason ignored-input view because that would be the unlit-subject oracle Pillar 3 refuses.

**Hold and toggle are Adapter policy over the same two commands (TR-assembly-043, PC-7).** For a channel verb the player's `channel_mode` setting (HOLD default, TOGGLE option) changes only *when* the Adapter submits: HOLD submits `begin_<verb>` on the `interact` press edge and `cancel_channel` on the release edge; TOGGLE submits `begin_<verb>` on the first press and `cancel_channel` on a second press or on `cancel`. A weld or cut therefore runs to DONE with the same duration, noise and abort set in either form, and a movement abort is sim-side in both (the owner's hook reads `octant`, so it is identical in a MIRROR peer). The two forms produce the same command types in the same order, which is what ADR-0020's policy-independence test (TR-interact-042) compares. Hold-to-confirm for a destructive verb is the Adapter's bar of D.10 (`CONFIRM_HOLD_S`, the per-verb `H_v = min(m_v * confirm_hold_s, cap_v)` with `cap_v <= HOLD_EFF_MAX_S`), submitting the owner's declared command once at fill and requiring a release before re-arm; `confirm_mode = TAP` is two taps inside `CONFIRM_TAP_WINDOW`.

**3.6 Correlation, pre-play, results, and the one resync rule (TR-pc-009, TR-pc-024, TR-shipbody-050).**
- **Correlation.** Every submit carries a non-zero `correlation` from a per-Adapter monotonic counter; the kernel echoes it unchanged in the terminal result (ADR-0002 § 7). The Adapter's `PrePlayLedger` is a preallocated ring (`PREPLAY_LEDGER_CAPACITY`, data) of `{correlation, kind, pre-play token, frame}` entries, where `kind` is a command type index or one of the two intent kinds; intents carry a correlation so that a rejection can be attributed to a kind, because the result record has no type field.
- **Pre-play is body and sound only, never the effect, never the beam light.** The Adapter records a token (the hand's switch animation, the tool click, the bar starting) in the ledger; the Avatar Presenter and Audio read the token list through `AdapterViews` and draw or play it. Light and effects change only on committed state (ADR-0009 § 7; kernel anti-target: no client-side prediction at MVP). A result for an *immediate* reason (the reason class is declared by Interaction's table, ADR-0020; the Adapter calls its pure `ReasonClass.is_immediate(reason)`) withdraws the token within one frame; a `PRECONDITION` outcome is an `Applied` command and is never withdrawn; a `Superseded` or `Dropped` result withdraws silently; a token without a result is withdrawn at `PREPLAY_MAX_TICKS`. The ack frame of a command whose channel will complete and of one that will resolve to `PRECONDITION` is the same token and therefore pixel-identical.
- **Result handler.** The result handler is a method `Callable` on the Adapter (ADR-0002 § 1: no lambda), registered with `SurfaceHandle.set_result_handler` and cleared in `_exit_tree`. It copies the record's scalars into a fixed `ResultRing` and returns `ctx.complete()`; every consequence (withdraw a token, schedule a resync) happens in the next `_process` (ADR-0009 § 7).
- **No shared or assisted control.** There is one Adapter, one local PlayerId and one seat; a command whose `issuer_peer` does not own the actor is `Rejected(ACTOR_NOT_OWNED)` at P2 with no state change (ADR-0002 § 4), and the Adapter has no code path that submits for an actor other than `actor_of_seat(local_player)`.
- **The resync rule: one mechanism, five labelled triggers.** The Adapter keeps `last_acked` per intent kind (`UNKNOWN` initially). A **resync** sets `last_acked := UNKNOWN`, which makes the next sample's value differ and so bypasses the change filter exactly once; the resend is rate-limited to one per `AIM_SEND_INTERVAL` ticks per kind so a persistent rejection cannot flood. For a hold-mode channel it also compares the real state of `interact` with the last submitted one and submits `cancel_channel` if the button is up. The triggers, each with its observable source:

| # | Trigger | Observed from | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| i | An intent result arrives not-ok (`STALE` is the case that matters; any not-ok other than `DEAD` and `NOT_ACCEPTING` counts) | the `ResultRing` | D.7: `last_acked` is "set on submit, reverted on any rejection" |
| ii | The kernel leaves `PAUSED` | `PresentationClock.state` changed from `PAUSED` to `RUNNING` | the kernel rejects intents immediately while paused, so a release during the pause was lost |
| iii | `LOADING -> RUNNING` | `PresentationClock.load_seq` changed | a restore or a new game |
| iv | Window focus returns, or the active pad reconnects | focus notification latch; `joy_connection_changed` latch | § 3.7 |
| v | `TRANSFER`: the actor's `body_of_actor` changed | `BodyView.body_of_actor(actor)` differs from the last seen | a pod arrival, a section detach |

Triggers ii and iii are one code path: the Adapter resyncs on the first frame in which it observes `RUNNING` after any frame that was not `RUNNING`, and labels the cause for the `PC_RESYNC` counter. **Why this lands in P2 of the first post-restore tick (TR-shipbody-050):** the frame that carries the kernel into `RUNNING` clears the accumulator and discards its own delta, so it runs no tick (ADR-0001 § 6; architecture.md Data Flow 1); the Adapter, which runs at -100 before the driver, has not yet seen that frame's end state, observes the new `load_seq` at the start of the next frame, and submits its resync before the driver runs that frame's first tick; the commands enter that tick's P1 batch and apply in its P2. Body's `sub` therefore survives the restore if a key is still held and is reset once by a later resync only in the case the rule gives (at most one sub-pixel remainder lost); a restore with no key held resets `sub` as a stop does. The invariant is testable: the simulation's intent equals the player's hands within one tick of the kernel being `RUNNING`.

**3.7 Focus loss, pad loss, pause silence and render timers (TR-pc-009, TR-pc-038, TR-pc-044).**
- **Focus loss** while `RUNNING` submits `set_move_intent {NONE}` and, if channelling, `cancel_channel`, and marks every held action released in the Adapter's own held set (there is no `Input.release_pressed_events` in GDScript, probe 9, so the Adapter keeps its own ledger rather than trusting the engine to release). `NOTIFICATION_APPLICATION_FOCUS_OUT` is app-level (alt-tab to another app); a window drag or an OS modal dialog may fire only `NOTIFICATION_WM_WINDOW_FOCUS_OUT` or `Window.focus_exited`, so the focus handler **connects all three** (the two notifications, forwarded as ints by the Adapter's `_notification`, and the root window's `focus_exited` signal, connected in `engine_input_source.gd`) to **one latch** (E6; spike item (e) records which fire in which case). The engine is believed to call `Input.release_pressed_events` itself on main-window focus-out (UNVERIFIED), which would deliver synthetic release events through `_input` *after* the Adapter has zeroed its intents; therefore **a release record for a control absent from the held ledger is idempotent in the ring** (no error, no counter), and spike item (e) records whether synthetic releases arrive through `_input`. On focus return the first click or key press is consumed (never reaches a binding, so an `LMB` alias of `interact` cannot fire on refocus), controls physically held at return are ignored until released and pressed again, and trigger iv resyncs. **The loss of the active pad** (a disconnect mid-weld included) is handled identically: the zero intent and cancel are submitted, the held pad actions are released, keyboard and mouse stay live, and a reconnect resyncs.
- **Pause silence (TR-pc-044).** While `PresentationClock.state` is not `RUNNING` the Adapter submits no intent and no command; it keeps tracking device state, the device latch and the MenuInput stream. The kernel would reject with `PAUSED` at admission anyway; the Adapter's silence avoids rejected-result noise and the revert-on-reject loop. In solo, a `chart_table` press makes the Adapter enter the `chart_table` context (submitting `set_move_intent {NONE}` and, if a channel is running, `cancel_channel` first, in that frame) and publish the context change; the Chart Table layer then opens and its `PauseGate` issues `request_pause` through the control plane (ADR-0002 § 6, ADR-0030). The Adapter holds no `ControlHandle`. **VS-B (reserved, not implemented at MVP):** in co-op the same press opens the overlay with the kernel `RUNNING`, so the channel cancel and the zero intent are what stops a body the player can no longer see.
- **Render-time timers (TR-pc-038, D.10).** The hold-to-confirm bar and the tap-to-confirm window (the world verbs and `give_up`) advance by the Adapter's own `_process` delta **clamped to `CONFIRM_DT_MAX_S`** and are frozen (effective delta zero) whenever `PresentationClock.state` is not `RUNNING`; a freeze does not drain, so the bar resumes where it stopped and a destructive hold cannot complete during a pause and be rejected `PAUSED`. This is one notch stricter than the GDD (which freezes under `PAUSED` only): `SAVING` and `LOADING` freeze it too, which changes no tested behaviour because the Adapter is silent in them. The delta is the `_process` argument, never `Time.*` and never `get_process_delta_time` (rule `CTL-12`, AC-PCI-211); ADR-0009's `PresentFrame.render_delta` is not used because the Adapter runs before the frame object is filled. **The device-switch interval is not frozen**: the Chart Table is open while the kernel is `PAUSED` and a player who changes from keyboard to pad there must see the glyph set follow, so the interval and the name-reveal timer read the injected monotonic `Clock` port (ADR-0010 Key Interfaces: the port described as for "the Pipeline, Controller"), which runs through a pause. The Chart Table's own menu hold fills (Delete, Retire, quit without saving) are the layer's, advanced on engine time from the MenuInput stream (ADR-0030), not the Adapter's bar. All of these are Adapter or layer state and are never read by the simulation.

**3.8 Tool cycle and the console seam (TR-suit-040, TR-charttable-009).**
- **`tool_cycle`** submits exactly one `equip_tool {slot}` per press, over the held slots 1 to 3 only (the slots Suit publishes as held, ADR-0027), next or previous per the pad d-pad direction. Because two presses can land in one frame before either is committed, the Adapter cycles from its own `pending_tool` (the last slot it submitted, cleared when `tool_in_hand` reaches it or a result rejects it), not from the committed value, so each press advances once. The rail highlight moves within one tick plus one render frame of the press: the submit is queued in frame N, applied in P2 of that frame's first tick, and HUD draws the committed `tool_in_hand` in frame N's presentation stage. `tool_1` to `tool_6` submit `equip_tool` for that slot directly.
- **The console seam.** At the pod console the `interact` press resolves through `ConsoleSeam`, which asks the Chart Table layer's pure resolver `ChartPickSource.resolve(prompt_verb) -> SeamResolution` (the CT-10 rows R1 to R8 live in that resolver, ADR-0030, not in the Adapter). `SUBMIT` makes the Adapter inject the host-local `ChartPick` into the declared command's payload key (`target_id` for `pod_hop`, `derelict_id` for `berth`; `unberth {}` needs none) from a two-row data table; `OPEN_CHART` makes it switch to the `chart_table` context instead of submitting (a context switch, not a verb choice); `NONE` submits nothing. The pick is host-local presentation state and never enters a payload on any other path.

**3.9 What the Adapter publishes: `AdapterViews`.** A presentation-local, getter-only object (`src/presentation/controller/input/adapter_views.gd`, a `RefCounted` held by the composition root and handed read-only to the Avatar Presenter, HUD, Instruments, Audio and the Chart Table layer). It is **pulled**, never signalled: each changing field has a monotonic sequence counter, so a consumer running once per frame cannot miss an edge (the pattern ADR-0009 uses for Interaction's views). Fields: `context()` and `context_seq()`; `inspect_ref()` (the opaque ref of the inspected instrument; this is the registry's `inspect_card_context_signal` `{context, inspect_ref}`); `device_latch()` and `device_seq()`; `glyph_token(action, device)`; `confirm_view(out)` with `mode`, `armed`, the fill in thousandths, `target_ref` and `give_up_live`; `focus_ref()` and `self_target()`; `raw_aim()` and `aim_source()` (the local avatar's drawn cone); `cursor_px()` (the last window-pixel sample, for picking through `RenderLayout.mouse_to_world_view`); `preplay_count()` and `preplay_entry(i, out)`; `cursor_hint` is written *into* the Adapter by the layer through `set_cursor_hint(hint)`. The names above are the concepts; the accessors are written `get_<noun>` in Key Interfaces (one style, CT-54), and every field named here has one there, including `get_device_seq`, `get_focus_ref`, `get_self_target`, `get_aim_source` and `get_preplay_entry(i, out)`, which ADR-0020 (focus echo) and ADR-0029 (pre-play tokens) consume. The Adapter reads no block that is `PERCEPTION_OUT` and never requests a Sanity or Attention view (TR-pc-025, rule `CTL-08`): its facet has `perception_access` false, so a mistaken read returns an empty view and counts `PERCEPTION_READ_BLOCKED` rather than lying.

### 4. Input contexts (TR-pc-010, TR-pc-033, TR-hud-041, TR-instr-032, TR-charttable-012)

**4.1 The machine.** `InputContextMachine` (pure; Adapter-local; never persisted, never in a snapshot) holds exactly one of `GAMEPLAY`, `CHART_TABLE`, `INSPECT_CARD`, `CHAT`. **`CHAT` is VS-B: the enum value and the matrix column are reserved, the transitions are not implemented at MVP, and the matrix completeness check skips reserved columns.** Before a campaign is loaded the Adapter runs in `CHART_TABLE` with no gameplay sink, publishing the same MenuInput stream (PC-2a); the first `RUNNING` frame after a new game or load enters `GAMEPLAY`. The sim state is unchanged by a context change except through the zero intent and the cancel the Adapter submits on entry.

**4.2 The matrix is data; the transitions are code with tests.** `context_matrix.json` carries, per action family and context, one of the policies of § 3.5, copied from the GDD's context × action matrix and checked at boot for completeness (every family has a row in every **implemented** context; the reserved `CHAT` column is skipped). The context return table of the GDD is the closed set of transitions in `InputContextMachine.step`, covered one test per row:
- Entering any non-`GAMEPLAY` context submits `set_move_intent {NONE}` and clears the run latch. Entering `CHART_TABLE` also submits `cancel_channel` for a hold-mode or a toggle-mode channel (`CHAT` and the co-op overlay are VS-B, reserved: there the sim runs, so a channel the player cannot see must not keep burning). **Entering `INSPECT_CARD` does not cancel** (the Pillar 4 second-instrument check on the pipe being welded is free): the zeroed move intent is not a move, the channel continues, a held `interact` is still tracked in hold mode, and a release inside the card still cancels; `cancel` closes the card first and a second `cancel` cancels the channel.
- **Movement closes the card** (user ruling D94): a *fresh* movement input in `INSPECT_CARD` (a move key going down, or the left stick crossing `MOVE_DEADZONE` from below `MOVE_DEADZONE - MOVE_DEADZONE_EXIT`, plus `CARD_PAD_DEADZONE_MARGIN` on a pad) returns to `GAMEPLAY` and submits the first `set_move_intent` of that octant **in the same frame, in that order**. A direction already held at card entry is latched until released. A move that closes the card while a channel runs is an ordinary non-NONE intent and aborts the channel in the sim, as in `GAMEPLAY`.
- A published `loco_state` of STAGGERED, DOWNED, DEAD or IN_POD returns any context to `GAMEPLAY` on the frame the Adapter observes it (it polls `ControllerView.loco_state` once per frame; every one of those states lasts well over a frame), and the cursor hides again. `chart_table` and `request_pause` are Adapter-level and never gated by `loco_state`, so pause stays reachable in DOWNED.
- `INSPECT_CARD`: `set_aim` stays live (the lamp can hold the gauge lit), zoom is live, `chart_table` closes the card first with no pause on that press, and `CHART_TABLE` is never entered from `INSPECT_CARD`.

**4.3 Latch-until-released.** For any physical control whose press closed the layer (Resume accept, `chart_table`, `cancel`) the Adapter emits nothing from that control, to the layer or to gameplay, until it is physically released and pressed again, including across the `PAUSED -> RUNNING` resync; for a console press that opened the layer, the open press's release and repeats are latched the same way, so no `interact` edge can reach `pod_hop` until a fresh press (TR-charttable-012). The latch set is Adapter state keyed by control, cleared by the matching release record in the `EdgeRing` (for a stick- or trigger-derived control, by the derived release edge of § 3.2, since pad-axis motion produces no records).

**4.4 Cursor policy (TR-pc-033, TR-hud-041).** `CursorPolicy.mode_for(context)` is a pure table: `GAMEPLAY` and `INSPECT_CARD` -> `MOUSE_MODE_CONFINED_HIDDEN` (hidden and confined; in `INSPECT_CARD` it stays hidden and **inert**: the mouse still steers aim and picks nothing on the card, because a visible cursor would be a second pointer over the beam); `CHART_TABLE`, `CHAT` and the boot context -> `MOUSE_MODE_VISIBLE` (shown and released), with the shape taken from the layer's `cursor_hint` (DEFAULT or SELECTABLE). `EngineInputSource` applies the mode (`Input.set_mouse_mode`) on a change **and re-asserts it on every `FOCUS_IN` and on every `PAUSED -> RUNNING` transition**, because the OS may drop confinement on focus loss or alt-tab and "apply only on change" would never restore it (UNVERIFIED which platforms do; spike item (b)). HUD nodes in `GAMEPLAY` have `mouse_filter` IGNORE and `focus_mode` NONE and no gamepad focus is ever assigned to a HUD node; that node-flag half of TR-hud-041 is ADR-0030's. The cursor is hidden by mode, so no custom cursor image exists and `aim_indicator` (opt-in, off by default) is a HUD overlay.

### 5. The MenuInput stream (TR-pc-012, TR-charttable-010, TR-charttable-036)

The Adapter publishes a presentation-local event stream while the active context is `CHART_TABLE` (and in the boot context). It is a `MenuInputQueue`: a preallocated ring of parallel `PackedInt32Array` columns (`kind`, `a`, `b`, `x`, `y`; capacity `MENU_EVENT_CAPACITY`, data), so it allocates nothing per event and carries no object. Kinds are the registry's `menu_input_stream` set: `NAV_DOWN{dir, source}` and `NAV_UP{dir}` (`dir` UP, DOWN, LEFT, RIGHT; `source` STICK, DPAD, ARROW, WASD), `ACCEPT_DOWN`, `ACCEPT_UP`, `BACK`, `CLOSE`, `FOCUS_OUT`, `FOCUS_IN`, `DEVICE_CHANGED{device}` and `POINTER{kind MOVE/DOWN/UP/WHEEL, button, x, y}` (a WHEEL pointer event follows the § 3.2 wheel rule: one per `pressed == true` step, the paired release ignored). `NAV` events for a stick or a trigger are derived from `get_action_raw_strength` (§ 3.2); `DPAD`, `ARROW` and `WASD` sources come from button and key edge records. The producer-side signatures and field-index constants are in Key Interfaces.

- **Sampling and delivery.** Events are produced in the Adapter's `_process` at -100 from the ordered `EdgeRing`; the Chart Table layer, a layer 3 and 4 UI object that runs on engine time so that it responds while the kernel is `PAUSED` (ADR-0009 § 3, ADR-0008 § 6.3) and always after the Adapter in the same frame, drains the queue at the start of its update and calls `clear()`. All events of one render frame therefore arrive in arrival order before the layer's update (ADR-0030 consumes this). An undrained queue is cleared at the next Adapter frame and counted (`PC_MENU_DROPPED`). On a full queue the Adapter drops the newest event, counts `PC_MENU_OVERFLOW` and enqueues one `FOCUS_OUT` on the next frame, which every consumer treats as "release all holds and clear navigation", so an overflow can lose a press but can never wedge a hold.
- **What the Adapter owns (PC-2a (a) to (g)).** Echo suppression (echo records are ignored, § 3.2); deadzone hysteresis (a stick takes a NAV edge on crossing `MOVE_DEADZONE` from below `MOVE_DEADZONE - MOVE_DEADZONE_EXIT` and releases below that band); the cardinal-axis rule (one NAV direction at a time, the larger axis, a tie to the vertical, changing only after the stick re-passes the exit band); latch-until-released (§ 4.3); `FOCUS_OUT` releases every held menu control and the layer's hold fills abort, `FOCUS_IN` requires fresh presses; `DEVICE_CHANGED` follows the device latch (§ 3.3); the cursor hint is applied by `EngineInputSource`, its only `Input` caller. Accept is the physical controls of `interact` in this context minus every mouse button, plus Enter and Space as fixed extra keyboard accepts of this context; **a mouse button is only ever a `POINTER` event, never an `ACCEPT`**. Back is `cancel`, close is `chart_table`. Auto-repeat of a held NAV direction is the layer's (Chart Table D.9). `zoom_in` and `zoom_out` edges are never emitted here.
- **Pointer position.** `POINTER` carries the root-viewport mouse position as integer physical window pixels, forwarded unconverted: root stretch is disabled (ADR-0008 § 5), so no inverse stretch transform applies. The layer subtracts `RenderLayout.picture_rect.position` itself (the HUD origin) and maps to HUD pixels and art pixels; the Adapter's aim path subtracts the world picture's origin exactly once through `RenderLayout.mouse_to_world_aim` (§ 6.1); each consumer subtracts once and the stream value is never subtracted twice. That the unit is window pixels on a real letterboxed window is confirmed by PC-INPUT-SPIKE-1 (a) (AC-CT-135 (f)).
- **No gameplay in the stream.** It carries no command and no simulation read, and the layer submits nothing and reads no device (TR-charttable-010); the console seam (§ 3.8) is the only route from the layer's state into a payload, and it goes through the Adapter.

### 6. The aim path, the cursor and the zoom edge

**6.1 Raw aim, once per frame, from the open-loop mapping (TR-pc-031, TR-pxl-014).** In its `_process` the Adapter forms, for the local avatar, one raw aim:
1. `cursor_px: Vector2i = InputSource.cursor_px()`: the root viewport's mouse position (in virtual-cursor mode, § 6.3, the virtual position), converted once with `floori` per axis (that it is integral, or fractional under display scaling, is PC-INPUT-SPIKE-1 (a); if it is fractional, the single `floori` is the one place the cursor is quantised).
2. `world: Vector2 = pipeline.layout.mouse_to_world_aim(cursor_px, pipeline.f_q)` (ADR-0008 § 3.3: `f_q + (cursor_px - picture_centre) / k_z`, with the single picture-origin subtraction **inside** that method). The Adapter does not form `cursor_vp` itself and subtracts nothing, so the letterbox subtraction exists in exactly one place; the GDD's alternative of subtracting in the Adapter is not used.
3. `r = world - Vector2(float(px), float(py + AIM_ORIGIN_Y))` with `px = BodyView.pos_x(actor)` and `py = BodyView.pos_y(actor)` (ADR-0015's scalar getters; the committed tick's integer position, so no `floor` is needed). A `Vector2i` is built only here at the Adapter boundary, and only if a call needs one (CT-27). If `|r| < AIM_MOUSE_DEADZONE_PX` the raw aim holds. Otherwise `theta = atan2(-r.y, r.x)` and `aim_q = posmod(roundi(theta * AIM_UNITS / TAU), AIM_UNITS)`: **round once, then `posmod`** (the lower half-plane would otherwise go negative; D.7's worked values 764, 3332, 3072, 4095 are the unit-test vectors). The result is an `int`; the float never reaches `submit`.

`mouse_to_world_view`, which maps through `pose_q` and therefore includes the look-ahead `L`, **never feeds aim** (a loop: aim moves the camera, the camera moves the world point under a still cursor, which moves aim). Rule `CTL-06` (§ 8.2) bans the name inside the aim path. Because the Adapter runs at -100, before `PipelineRoot._process` at 0, the `f_q`, `layout` and `layout_version` it reads are the **previous frame's** `CAMERA_SOLVE` and `RESCALING` results and `pos_curr` is the last committed tick: one coherent snapshot, a frame old, which is the lag the GDD already costs into the recorded trade-off (a still mouse turns the beam by the geometry of the follow-lag change when a run starts or stops, bounded by the GDD's 36 px).

**6.2 Sources.** `aim_source` ∈ {AUTO, MOUSE, STICK, KEYS} is an `InputSettings` value (Settings, never sim). The right stick uses the scaled-radial deadzone (`AIM_DEADZONE`, `AIM_OUTER`, `AIM_CURVE`) and the released-stick modes of PC-5; the four arrow keys and `aim_hold` compose an octant-centre aim (`512 * o`). `live` is true for the mouse always, for a stick above its deadzone, for a key while held, and false under `FOLLOW_ALWAYS`; it travels in the payload because facing rules 4 to 6 branch on it in the sim.

**6.3 The cursor warp (a presentation-local rule; submits nothing, writes no block, reveals no sim state).** The OS cursor is hidden and confined in `GAMEPLAY`, so the Adapter may reposition it unseen:
- **Zoom edge.** The Adapter stores `cursor_off` (the cursor relative to the picture centre, in base pixels, from the frame's aim computation) every frame. A zoom step is accepted inside `PipelineRoot._process` of frame N and bumps `layout_version`. In frame N+1 the Adapter sees the version change, **uses the stored `cursor_off` as that frame's aim input instead of a fresh OS read**, and warps the hidden cursor with `Input.warp_mouse` (window pixels; the one warp call, never `Viewport.warp_mouse`, whose coordinates are viewport coordinates and equal window pixels only because the root has no stretch) to `picture_centre_new + cursor_off * k_new` (clamped to the window), so `aim_q` is unchanged within one unit while the avatar is still. The cost is that one frame of mouse motion is dropped on a warp frame (at most one rendered frame, 17 ms at 60 fps). Whether the OS position is readable in the same frame after `warp_mouse`, and whether the warp emits a synthetic motion event that would be double counted, are PC-INPUT-SPIKE-1 (b); if the warped position is readable immediately the "use the stored offset" step is dropped.
- **Fallback when `warp_cursor` is a no-op (E5).** `warp_mouse` is documented only for Windows, macOS and Linux, Wayland is doubtful, and RM-DECK is Linux. If a warp frame reads the position back more than `WARP_READBACK_TOLERANCE_PX` (a `ControllerTuning` knob, set from spike item (b)) from the target with no synthetic motion event, the Adapter latches **virtual-cursor mode** for the session: it keeps the stored `cursor_off` as a virtual cursor (the intended warp target, then advanced each frame by the summed integer `InputEventMouseMotion.relative` of that frame's pointer-move records, clamped to the window) and uses it in place of the OS read. Zoom and the handover warp then depend on no warp at all. Both modes feed the same `aim_q` computation.
- **Handover to the mouse.** While the source is STICK or KEYS the mouse takes over only after `AIM_MOUSE_TAKEOVER_PX` of accumulated cursor travel, and at takeover the cursor is first warped to the point whose direction from the aim origin is the current raw aim and whose radius is the larger of the last radius and `AIM_WARP_RADIUS_PX`, so the first mouse sample holds the aim it found. `AUTO` follows the last aim axis; a forced source ignores the others and has no handover warp.
- A **fresh** mouse motion after a focus return or a context change is not a warp trigger.

**6.4 Zoom and the pipeline root (TR-pc-032, TR-pxl-032, TR-pc-033).** `zoom_in` and `zoom_out` are the Adapter's two edge-triggered inputs (PC-16). The wheel steps latched in a frame (`pressed == true` `WHEEL_UP` / `WHEEL_DOWN` only, § 3.2) are summed to a net sign and the Adapter calls `PipelineRoot.request_zoom_step(sign)` **at most once per rendered frame** (a notched or high-resolution wheel cannot walk the ladder several steps in one frame); a pad d-pad press is the same call. The call is host-local and never a command; the rate limit and the ladder are ADR-0008's (`ZOOM_STEP_MIN_INTERVAL`, one accepted step per frame). Zoom is live in `GAMEPLAY` and `INSPECT_CARD` and ignored in `CHART_TABLE` and `CHAT`. **The pipeline root makes no `Input` read of its own.** TR-pc-032's "the Adapter hands cursor position and zoom edges to the pipeline root" is met in the form that needs no new `PipelineRoot` interface: zoom edges are the `request_zoom_step` call, and the cursor is published as `AdapterViews.cursor_px()` for any presenter that needs a pick, which converts it through the `RenderLayout` methods. The wheel burst size per frame is PC-INPUT-SPIKE-1 (c).

### 7. The Avatar Presenter

**7.1 Registration and reads.** `AvatarPresenter` registers a `PresenterDescriptor` at `PresentationStage.AVATAR` (2) with no `_process` (ADR-0009 § 3); its `present(frame)` reads `body.state` previous and current positions and the odometer, `ControllerView` (facing, `gait_effective`, `octant`, `loco_state`, `carry_ref`, `stagger_end_tick`, `t_downed`, and the chest and hand offsets through `get_chest_x/y` and `get_hand_x/y`, **read for the apex and offset terms only**), `emitter.state` (beam lit and shape) and `frame.alpha`, and places sprites with `Placement.at` and `Placement.put` (ADR-0009 § 4: interpolate, then floor per axis, so every written *sprite* position has a zero fractional part). **The beam `Light2D` is excepted from that statement (CT-29):** it sits at the foot cell, not at the chest point, and is written through `Placement.put_light_origin` (§ 7.2); the chest point is the cone apex only. **Parenting (CT-32, ADR-0034):** the Presenter attaches its node with `BodyScenes.attach_presenter(actor_id, node)` at spawn, never reparents itself, and writes position in stage AVATAR after the PROJECTOR's stage-0 `BODY_TRANSFER` reparent (`reparent(p, false)`, so the Presenter rewrites the position from the new frame in the same frame, before draw); the beam light is built and attached by the Lighting presenter through `BodyScenes.attach_light` (namespace LIGHTING, ADR-0018 § 4.1; the Avatar Presenter does not create, attach or free it, and writes only its `position` and `rotation`) and moves with the same stage-0 pass. **The Lighting presenter keeps that node disabled until this Presenter's first stage-2 position write has landed (ruling R9, ADR-0018 § 4.1); ADR-0028 must carry Lighting's create, `attach_light` and `detach` duties (forward reference: ADR-0028 is not yet written).** It draws any crewmate, local or remote, from the same views with no HOST/MIRROR branch (CR-10). For the **local avatar only** it also reads the Adapter's `raw_aim()` to rotate the beam `Light2D` (the drawn cone may lead the committed cone by at most `AIM_SEND_THRESHOLD` and one send interval, the accepted divergence of PC-5); a remote avatar's beam rotates from the replicated committed `aim_angle`, which may not be coarsened below the facing hysteresis. The pre-play tokens of `AdapterViews` drive the hand's switch animation only. The Presenter keeps its `anim_counter` private.

*Frame index.* Moving frames are `posmod(anim_counter, 8)` with `anim_counter` accumulating `dir * (cycle_pos - cycle_pos_prev)` from Body's `odometer` (D.11); the cadence test samples the Presenter once per tick through an injected `PresentFrame`, so the accumulator sees the same odometer sequence at every render rate. **The tick-stepped frames are derived from the committed tick `T`**, not from `present_time`. *Idle breathing* is free-running: `AvatarFrames.idle_frame(T)` is `posmod(floor(T / IDLE_FRAME_TICKS), 6)` with no entry tick (the GDD's `T - T_enter` needs a per-state entry tick that the block does not hold and that a catch-up frame cannot observe exactly; a breath that starts mid-cycle is invisible; GDD sync). *Work, stagger and down frames* are anchored on a sim-held entry tick through `AvatarFrames.timed_frame(T, enter_tick, ticks_per_frame, n, one_shot)`: stagger on `stagger_end_tick - STAGGER_TICKS`, down on `t_downed` (both published by the view), and the work loop on the **channel's start tick published by Interaction** (assumption on ADR-0020: the channel record carries it), so a weld animation always starts at its first frame and the GDD's work formula is unchanged. Carry-idle is the static carry frame 0 and needs no clock. Because `T` does not advance while the kernel is `PAUSED`, these freeze under pause with no special case (TR-pc-038), and because they are a pure function of committed ticks they are identical across render cadences (TR-pc-039). **This is an exception to the registry pattern `cosmetic_phase_from_tick_counter`** ("cosmetic phase uses `present_time`, never the tick counter"), which ADR-0001 and ADR-0009 own. It is a deliberate, narrow one: only `AvatarFrames`, only for the avatar's tick-stepped frames, whose per-tick output must replay byte-identically. It is a foundation ask (below) and the pattern is mechanically unlinted today (ADR-0010 § 6.6b: "No lint"), so the exception is enforced by review and by the avatar cadence test. The moving frames are driven by Body's `odometer`, a simulation value, so they need no exception; every other cosmetic (flicker, fade, the ignored-input cue) keeps `present_time`.

**7.2 Write allow-list (TR-pc-026 is ADR-0009's; the Controller's rule list is this).** The Presenter writes exactly: the sprite `frame`, `flip_h`, the integer sprite `position` (through `Placement.put` only), overlay `visible`, and **the beam `Light2D`'s `position` through `Placement.put_light_origin` at the interpolated foot cell from `body.state` plus `Placement.CELL_CENTRE` under ADR-0018 `SHADOW_ORIGIN_MODE` 0 (the chest cell under mode 1), and its `rotation` (which may be fractional)**; the beam node's `offset` is not a Presenter write (ADR-0018 `beam_offset_writer.gd`). The chest point serves only as the cone apex. CTL-11 and PRES-03 point at ADR-0018's allow-list entry for the beam light. It never writes `scale`, `rotation` (on the body), `skew`, `modulate`, `self_modulate` or any `Tween` or `AnimationPlayer` track on the body or its parents up to the avatar root (rule `CTL-11`, AC-PCI-207, PRES-03, PRES-04). It draws nothing beyond that and submits nothing; the Perception Filter may substitute the whole body for a horror rig but never edits pixels, moves the chest or hand point or touches the beam. A Horror rig that changes section calls `BodyScenes.rebind(node, body_id, section_id)` (an assumption on ADR-0023).

**7.3 The sheet and sidecar checks (TR-pc-046).** "Import fails" is read as **the asset-pipeline CI gate fails the build**, not as an engine `EditorImportPlugin` (which would put an editor-only dependency in the pipeline for one check). Two checks, one library: (1) the numeric bounds, `abs(CHEST_POINT.x) <= 2` and `abs(HAND_POINT.x) <= 12` for every sheet and row, are *named structural invariants* of the **same frozen `ControllerCatalog` table** (data under `assets/data/controller/`, manifest-listed, ruling R7) that the sim getters of § 2.6 read (CT-28: one source of the offsets; the presentation sidecar under `assets/data/animation/`, closed schema, ADR-0007 § 2.5, carries the pixel-side data and reads these offsets from that table rather than restating them) and report `PC_LINT_CHEST_POINT` and `PC_LINT_HAND_POINT` as domain-2 boot errors naming sheet and row, so the same loader fails the game, the headless tests and the offline lint (the table has no per-frame dimension, so the numeric check names no frame; the pixel check below does: a deliberate narrowing of TR-pc-046's "frame" for the numeric bounds, flagged for the registry wording, OQ-G); (2) the lamp-plate occlusion check needs pixels, so it is a content-lint rule family `AVATAR-*` in `tools/ci/content_lint.gd` (ADR-0007 § 2.2) that loads each sheet's PNG and its per-sheet lamp-plate mask with `Image` and fails with `PC_LINT_LAMP_OCCLUDED` naming sheet, row and frame when an opaque pixel lies over the mask (AC-PCI-206). **The Presenter carries no runtime clamp** (rule `CTL-11`: `clamp(`, `clampi(` and `clampf(` are banned in `src/presentation/controller/avatar/` wherever `CHEST_POINT` or `HAND_POINT` is read), so a bad table is a build failure, never a hidden correction on screen. The lamp-plate mask and the sidecar tables do not exist yet (`/asset-spec` pending); the rule is written against a fixture mask (GDD Gaps) and the file formats are the asset-spec stage's to fix.

**7.4 Footfalls (TR-audio-038).** Audio drives footfalls from the published `odometer` (Body's view), `gait_effective`, `octant` and `facing` (this ADR's view), never from the Presenter's `anim_counter`, which is private and has no getter. **8.5 The input-trace format (TR-pc-039, replay half).** An `InputTrace` (`src/presentation/controller/input/input_trace.gd`, recorded only under the dev feature tag; replayed by `FakeInputSource`) is a text file of integer records, one line per rendered frame, committed under `tests/fixtures/controller/traces/` and immutable once committed. Header: a format version and the tick-rate name. Per frame: the injected `delta_us` (ADR-0001/ADR-0005's replay injection), the integer action strengths in `ANALOG_UNITS`, the ordered edge records of that frame (`action_id`, pressed, device class, echo; key, mouse-button and pad-button records only, pad-axis motion contributes only through the action strengths), the wheel net sign (from `pressed == true` steps only), the cursor `Vector2i`, the pipeline values the Adapter read (`layout_version`, `f_q`, `k_z`, picture rect), the kernel state and `load_seq`, the focus flag and the pad-connected flag. Because every input is an int, the same trace through the same Adapter build yields the same submissions; `atan2` is not claimed bit-identical across machines, so the simulation's replay uses the **recorded submission log** (`(at_tick, type, payload, actor, correlation)`), not a re-run Adapter. **Cadence invariance (30, 60, 120, 144 fps):** device events carry the `at_tick` of the tick in whose P1 batch they must land, restricted to multiples of the least common multiple of the four frame periods in ticks (10), and the harness delivers each in the last frame before that tick, so every cadence can honour every event; the submission logs, the `controller.state` and `body.state` hashes and the Presenter's per-tick samples (frame, flip, integer position, light rotation, driven by an injected `PresentFrame` at alpha 1.0 at each tick) must match across the four cadences, across calm, tell, breach and sanity-stage read-view fixtures, and with `aim_indicator` on and off. **Block hashes are not enough (T6):** `block_hash` is taken over the snapshot payload, which normalises most per-tick Controller output (§ 2.7), so the cadence test **also compares per-tick samples of every `ControllerView` getter** (or a test-only canonical encode of the live rows) across the four cadences. **Input model for the equivalence harness (CT-49):** the Adapter's resync submission is applied in the first RUNNING tick's P2 (§ 3.6); a G4 or KRN-RST fixture that includes `controller.state` therefore either **snapshots from a rest Controller intent**, or **replays the Adapter's resync submission into the restored run**; otherwise the restored run stops until a resync while the control run keeps moving, a divergence by construction. ADR-0016 § 8 KRN-RST-05 states the same input model; ADR-0005's G3/G4 definition does not change (the note is recorded there for the harness only). Per-frame placements vary with alpha by design (ADR-0009). Hold-to-confirm timings are render-time by D.10 and are excluded from the identical claim: a confirm submit may land one frame apart across cadences.

**8.6 Budgets (TR-pc-029).** Advisory, measured by the diagnostics timer on a release export on RM-DESKTOP and RM-DECK, 120-frame warm-up, owned by the technical-director and living in `tests/performance/controller/`; a miss logs and never fails a logic test: Adapter at most 0.1 ms per frame (sampling, quantising, focus scoring over 32 candidates, submitting); `LocomotionStep` at most 0.05 ms per actor per tick against 2,000 rects (Body's, ADR-0015); `controller.step` at most 0.02 ms per actor per tick (**the ADR-0003 P3 share-ledger row for this ADR: 0.16 ms for eight actors, pending S1** (CT-20); `controller.noise` has no separate figure here, and S1 reports it as a sub-counter of the controller hooks; no new number is invented); Avatar Presenter at most 0.1 ms per avatar per frame; the `PC_*` diagnostics at most 0.01 ms per tick (dev only).

### Implementation Guidelines
- **Must** keep `Input.*`, `InputMap`, `InputEvent*`, `_input`, `_unhandled_input`, `_unhandled_key_input` and `_shortcut_input` inside the files of `input_surface.json`; **must never** read a device from the Chart Table layer, the pipeline root, HUD, Instruments or the Presenter.
- **Must** submit only from `InputAdapter._process`, in the order `set_move_intent`, `set_aim`, commands; **must never** submit from `_input`, a signal handler, `_physics_process` or while `PresentationClock.state` is not `RUNNING`.
- **Must** convert every analogue value to an `int` in `ANALOG_UNITS` and every position to `Vector2i` at the `InputSource` boundary; **must never** let a float reach `submit()`.
- **Must** build every binding, including a user-captured one (`BindingFactory.from_capture`), with `physical_keycode` for keys and `device = BINDING_ANY_DEVICE`; **must** register every action before the first poll with a **non-zero** engine deadzone; **must never** register a 0.0 deadzone, store a captured event directly, or compare `InputEvent.device` with a literal or a `DEVICE_ID_*` constant.
- **Must** add `InputAdapter` as the last child of its parent, call `get_viewport().set_input_as_handled()` only from the Adapter, and treat `InputEventJoypadMotion` as continuous-only (no edge record; stick and trigger edges derived from `get_action_raw_strength`); **must never** add an `_input` or a `set_input_as_handled()` call to a layer, HUD or the debug overlay (they consume MenuInput and `DevInputSink` through the Adapter).
- **Must** declare `InputSource` ports `@abstract`, implement all ten methods in every implementation, restate every override's return type and return on every path (`return 0` / `return Vector2i.ZERO` stubs in the Fake); **must never** write an override without a return annotation (lint `CTL-13`). No Object or Node member name is shadowed by the `get_*` accessors, `count()`, `clear()`, `setup()` or `on_result()` of the views and queues.
- **Must** send `set_move_intent` only on change or resync; **must** send `set_aim` on the D.7 predicate at most once per `AIM_SEND_INTERVAL` ticks; **must never** buffer a press (`ACTION_BUFFER_TICKS` is 0).
- **Must** attach a non-zero `correlation` to every submit and key pre-play tokens by it; **must never** pre-play an effect or the beam light.
- **Must** compute mouse aim only as `mouse_to_world_aim(cursor_px, f_q)` then round once and `posmod`; **must never** use `mouse_to_world_view` or `pose_q` for aim, and **must never** subtract the picture origin in the Adapter.
- **Must** hold the context, `last_acked`, hold timers, run latch, device latch, pre-play tokens and menu latch in Adapter classes that the Controller Sim cannot name; **must never** put any of them in `controller.state` or a snapshot.
- **Must** register `controller.state`, the four commands and the two hooks only through the `ModuleFacet.registrar` in `BOOTING`; **must** end every kernel-invoked callable in `return ctx.complete()`; **must never** write a `controller.state` field except in the Controller's own callables and the four mutators.
- **Must** keep `ControllerSchema.fields()` the single list and the agreement test green; **must never** persist a derived value or a `Vector2i`, and **must never** store the chest or hand point (they are the read-time scalar getters `get_chest_x/y`, `get_hand_x/y`).
- **Must** clear only `channel_ref`, `channel_face` and `channel_body` on a body edge; **must never** write a `body.state` field from the Controller.
- **Must** draw avatar tick-stepped loops from `AvatarFrames` on the committed tick; **must never** use that tick for any other cosmetic, and **must never** clamp a chest or hand point at run time.
- **Must** read only `locomotion_cap` and `stowed_heavy` from Suit in the Controller Sim and no Sanity, Attention or `PERCEPTION_OUT` view anywhere in the module.

## Alternatives Considered

### Alternative 1: One `Controller` node that reads devices, owns the state and draws the body
- **Description**: A single `Node2D` (or autoload) reads `Input`, holds facing, gait and carry in its own fields, and moves the sprite.
- **Pros**: Least code; no read views; no resync because there is no boundary.
- **Cons**: State lives in the scene tree, so a save cannot restore it and a MIRROR peer cannot draw a remote body from state; presentation would write the simulation (kernel H4); the simulation would depend on a `Node` (ADR-0010 `SIM-01`); input timing would be the scene's, not the tick's.
- **Rejection Reason**: Breaks three Foundation rules and the GDD's PC-1 rationale; the three-part split is the minimum that satisfies them.

### Alternative 2: Fold the Controller Sim into Ship Body (one block, one hook)
- **Description**: Body owns intent, gait, facing and position; the Controller is only an Adapter and a Presenter.
- **Pros**: One fewer block and hook; no `controller.step` -> `body.integrate` ordering edge.
- **Cons**: Intent and facing are seat semantics (what the player asked for) read by Interaction, Attention, Horror and the HUD; position is a kinematic fact. Merging them couples every reader of the first to Body's schema and its restore rules, and the GDD rejected it for that reason (PC-1 rationale).
- **Rejection Reason**: Keeping the input record apart from the integrator keeps both restore contracts small; the cost is one hook edge, which the topo-sort already handles.

### Alternative 3: The Controller owns position and calls the Layout projection
- **Description**: `controller.step` integrates the foot box against Layout's rectangles.
- **Pros**: One owner for "where the player is".
- **Cons**: Puts the Layout projection inside an input system; cannot own an actor that transfers between bodies (pod hop, detach) without learning Body's rebase rules; Horror's reuse of `LocomotionStep` would have to reach into the Controller.
- **Rejection Reason**: PC-14's reasons; the composite ship and the transfer mutators are Body's.

### Alternative 4: Per-tick held-state commands instead of change-based intents
- **Description**: The Adapter submits the current octant and aim every tick; no change filter, no `last_acked`, no resync.
- **Pros**: Self-healing; no resync rule; the simulation can never disagree with the hands for more than a tick.
- **Cons**: A steady 60 or more commands a second against `MAX_CMDS_PER_TICK` and the `var_to_bytes` size check, for a state that is constant most of the time; the command log and the replay trace would be dominated by repeats; ADR-0002's coalescing exists for exactly the bursty case, not a firehose.
- **Rejection Reason**: Change-based submission with one resync rule has the same invariant (hands equal intent within a tick of `RUNNING`) at one fiftieth of the traffic, and the five triggers are all observable.

### Alternative 5: Poll-only edges, or latch in `_unhandled_input`
- **Description**: Use `is_action_just_pressed` and `just_released` for every edge; or latch events in `_unhandled_input`.
- **Pros**: Poll-only is the simplest code and needs no ring. `_unhandled_input` leaves `Control`s first refusal.
- **Cons**: Probe 6: a same-frame press and release cannot be ordered by polling, which the latch-until-released, fresh-press and hold-cancel rules need. `_unhandled_input` misses an event a focused `Control` consumed, which would silently drop a MenuInput accept.
- **Rejection Reason**: Ordered edges from `_input`, plus a poll for continuous state, is the smallest scheme that is correct for all three; the cost is the `EdgeRing`.

### Alternative 6: A custom binding layer instead of `InputMap`
- **Description**: The Adapter matches raw `InputEventKey`/`JoypadButton` records against its own table; no `InputMap` actions.
- **Pros**: No dependence on `InputMap` device semantics (probe 3); total control of conflicts and contexts.
- **Cons**: Re-implements modifier matching, axis deadzones, echo and per-device merging that the engine already provides; the HUD, Settings and accessibility screens would each need the custom layer; Godot's merging of devices per action (a held pad `interact` survives a touch of the mouse) would have to be rebuilt.
- **Rejection Reason**: `InputMap` with runtime registration, a **non-zero** engine deadzone (a 0.0 deadzone makes a centred stick report pressed, probe 13; the gameplay deadzones are Adapter scalings over `get_action_raw_strength`, which ignores the engine deadzone) and the any-device wildcard gives the same control with the engine's matching; the pitfalls (probes 3 and 13) are closed by one factory and its tests.

### Alternative 7: Mouse aim through the camera pose (`mouse_to_world_view`) or anchored on the avatar
- **Description**: Use the full `pose_q` inverse for aim, or measure aim from the drawn avatar position.
- **Pros**: Matches picking and pings; the anchored form holds the beam rigid against the hand.
- **Cons**: `pose_q` contains the look-ahead `L`, which is a function of aim, so a still cursor would drift the beam (a feedback loop with no cursor drawn to explain it). Anchoring on the avatar puts the hidden-cursor reference up to the follow lag plus the look-ahead off the drawn avatar (GDD PC-5, ruling R4).
- **Rejection Reason**: The open-loop `f_q` form is the ruled one and ADR-0008 publishes it; the lint `CTL-06` keeps the loop open.

### Alternative 8: Identify devices by number (`device == 0`, or the `DEVICE_ID_*` constants)
- **Description**: Decide keyboard, mouse or pad from `event.device`.
- **Pros**: One integer compare.
- **Cons**: 4.7 changed the keyboard and mouse numbers and some pads already used 0 (`modules/input.md`); pads are numbered by enumeration order, so a pad that is not first matches nothing; a comparison with a constant bakes the engine's numbering into game code.
- **Rejection Reason**: Event type is stable across versions; the only legal numeric use is a variable holding the active pad's index, never a literal.

### Alternative 9: Persist the context, `last_acked` and the pre-play ledger
- **Description**: Save Adapter state so a load resumes mid-intent.
- **Pros**: A restore could keep the exact held input.
- **Cons**: The hands are the source of truth, not the save; a persisted `last_acked` would claim the simulation holds a value it just lost; the context is presentation. It would also put device state into a hashed block.
- **Rejection Reason**: The restore normalises transient fields and the resync re-sends the real hands (TR-pc-021).

## Consequences

### Positive
- Only five files can read a device; every other Controller file is pure over ints, so the Adapter's logic is unit-testable with a scripted source and replayable from a recorded trace.
- The Adapter cannot write the simulation by construction (no `ModuleFacet`, a `PresentationFacet` of class `INPUT_SURFACE`, an import-edge matrix that stops at `views/`), and the Controller Sim cannot read a device (no `Input` token is legal under `src/sim/`).
- One field list drives the block, the view and the snapshot, so the three cannot drift; a new field is one edit and one failing test until the rest follow.
- The 4.7 device-id change is closed twice: by classification on event type and by a literal-compare lint; the wildcard binding closes the pad-index hazard the probes found.
- Resync is one mechanism with five labelled causes, all observable from the clock, the result ring, the notifications and Body's view, with no kernel hook.
- Hold, toggle, pad and keyboard policies differ only in when the Adapter submits two commands the owners already define.
- Intent submission is ten to fifty times lighter than per-tick held state and stays inside the 26 commands a second budget.

### Negative
- Adapter state is lost on a restore and rebuilt from the hands; a player holding a key across a load gets one resend, at most one sub-pixel `sub` remainder, and no more.
- The Adapter reads the previous frame's `f_q` and layout (it runs before the pipeline), so aim and the zoom warp carry one frame of lag, and a warp frame drops one frame of mouse motion.
- Edge ordering needs an `EdgeRing` and a second path beside polling; the lint and fixtures to keep that honest are real work.
- The avatar's tick-stepped frames (idle, work, stagger, down) use the committed tick, an exception to a registry rule that must be defended in review and by test, because no lint can see it.
- Four Controller mutators are Class A in ADR-0003's vocabulary while the GDD says Class B; two documents must be amended.
- The Presenter and Audio share one pure function, which makes `AvatarFrames` a small cross-presentation contract that must stay stable.

## Risks
- **R1. Process order on a release export is unverified (KRN-LAT-02).** If the Adapter does not run before the driver, an intent sampled in frame N lands a tick late. *Mitigation*: shared spike item (h); the Adapter's tests assert only the order of its own calls, and a one-tick-later landing degrades latency, not correctness.
- **R2. Warp, confinement and cursor units on a real letterboxed window.** If `warp_mouse` is not visible in the same frame, or emits a motion event, the zoom edge could nudge aim by a few units. *Mitigation*: PC-INPUT-SPIKE-1 (a), (b) in a non-embedded window and on RM-DECK (Linux); the stored-`cursor_off` step already makes the warp frame independent of the OS read, and the virtual-cursor fallback of § 6.3 removes the dependence on `warp_mouse` altogether; the cursor mode is re-asserted on focus return (§ 4.4); acceptance of § 6.3 is gated on (a) and (b) only (gate G-DEVICE).
- **R3. The `-1` wildcard may not match on a real SDL3 pad.** *Mitigation*: spike (f), (g); fallback is a per-device binding rebuilt on `joy_connection_changed`, behind `BindingFactory`, with no change to callers.
- **R4. `_input` plus `get_viewport().set_input_as_handled()` could collide with ADR-0030's focus design** (a `Control` expecting `ui_accept`), and `_input` order is tree order (probe 14), so any later node that handles an event first starves the ring. *Mitigation*: the ADR-0009 `INPUT_SURFACE` last-child rule, the boot assertion and the two-node test (§ 3.1); the layer is specified to take MenuInput only (TR-charttable-010); an amendment path is open (OQ-B); no `ui_*` action is read by gameplay or MenuInput. Cross-ADR assumption on ADR-0030 and ADR-0031: no `_input` or `set_input_as_handled()` in their layers; they consume MenuInput and `DevInputSink` through the Adapter; their Controls set `focus_mode` NONE unless exempted through the OQ-B `TextEntryProbe` port.
- **R5. The registry exception for avatar tick-stepped loops** could be copied by other presenters. *Mitigation*: it is scoped to `AvatarFrames` and recorded as a foundation ask; ADR-0009's presenter tests assert that no other presenter reads `frame.T` for phase.
- **R6. PC-14 and the TR-pc-035 reading rest on ADR-0015.** If Body's transfer mutators do not reset `pos_prev` and `sub` (BM4 new side and BM5; BM3 keeps `sub` and `last_octant`), the arrival cell can be displaced. *Mitigation*: stated as an assumption on ADR-0015 (§ 1.3) and tested by the transfer property test (TR-shipbody-048); ADR-0015 and this ADR are co-accepted.
- **R7. Frame-old `f_q`.** At a high turn rate while moving, aim could trail by up to the one-frame follow change. *Mitigation*: the GDD already bounds the follow term at 36 px and measures target acquisition in the motion-integrity spike; the Adapter does not add to the bound.
- **R8. Float `atan2` is not bit-identical across machines.** *Mitigation*: not claimed; the simulation replays the recorded submission log, and the Adapter-level trace test is per-machine.
- **R9. Solo detection for `give_up` is a row count.** If co-op seats exist without avatars, the count could mis-read. *Mitigation*: OQ-C; the check moves to a kernel session fact under ADR-0032.
- **R10. Action ids in rebinding files.** A renamed action would orphan a user override. *Mitigation*: stable integer ids with an append-only ledger (ADR-0007 § 3), names are display only.
- **R11. Touch, gyro and `VirtualJoystick` (4.7) are out of scope.** A later platform could need a fourth device class. *Mitigation*: `DeviceClass.OTHER` is ignored and adding a class is an enum append plus one classifier branch.

## Performance Implications
- **CPU**: Adapter at most 0.1 ms per frame, `controller.step` at most 0.02 ms per actor per tick, Avatar Presenter at most 0.1 ms per avatar per frame, diagnostics at most 0.01 ms per tick (TR-pc-029, advisory, release export on RM-DESKTOP and RM-DECK; none measured yet). `_input` does one `InputMap.event_is_action` per registered action for key, mouse-button and pad-button events (a few dozen compares, no allocation) and none for mouse motion or pad-axis motion (which records nothing). The four point getters are O(1) integer arithmetic over `BodyView.pos_x/pos_y` and a table row, allocation-free; their cost is charged inside the caller's budget (the Emitter's `EXPOSURE_BUDGET_MS`, the Presenter's 0.1 ms), not a separate line. `controller.step` and the Adapter allocate nothing per call by construction (preallocated rings, rows, parallel arrays, a caller-owned `ChannelRef`), checked by the HOT-01 rule once the tooling lands.
- **Memory**: a handful of preallocated rings (edges, results, pre-play, menu events) sized by data; one `ControllerRow` per seat of 27 ints; `AdapterViews` and the ledgers are single objects. No per-frame allocation.
- **Load Time**: Four small tables under `assets/data/input/` and the `ControllerCatalog` data under `assets/data/controller/` (manifest-listed, ruling R7) load with the ADR-0007 pipeline at start-up; `BindingFactory` registers a few dozen actions once.
- **Network**: Intents at most 26 commands a second per player (VS-B); `controller.state` replicates as `SHARED_SUMMARY`; aim is not coarsened below the facing hysteresis.

- **OQ-F.** Whether a device-switch to a pad should also latch on a stick crossing the movement deadzone. The GDD says "last button or key"; this ADR follows it. A playtest finding of "glyphs stay keyboard while I walk with the stick" reopens it.
